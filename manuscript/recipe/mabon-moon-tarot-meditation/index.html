🌑Mabon Moon Tarot Meditation

◇Directions
Settle before your moon altar with The High Priestess card and the pomegranate placed before you. If using stones, hold the darker stone in your left hand and the lighter one in your right to embody the dual nature of the moon. Breathe deeply and attune to their energy. Meditate on the pomegranate and the myth of Persephone, the maiden drawn into the underworld, bound to return each year, giving rise to the turning of seasons; let the fruit speak to you of abundance, rebirth, creativity, and the necessity of darkness. Turn your attention to The High Priestess. Note the pillars of dark and light flanking her; she navigates both with equal comfort, and the crescent moon at her feet marks her kinship with lunar intuition. Sit with the question of what you are called to reflect on, release, or explore in this darker season. Continue resting with the imagery, allowing the moon's lesson to settle: both dark and light are necessary, and your inner knowing, like the moon, is always present to guide you. Open the pomegranate; its crimson interior speaks of blood, life, sacrifice, and abundance. Offer thanks for the harvest of the season. Count out 6 seeds and set them aside as an offering; if using juice, pour a small measure as the offering instead. Eat the remaining seeds slowly in sacred contemplation of the cycle of life and your own place within it. Close your eyes and envision your inner moon illuminating the path ahead.
◇Quantities
The High Priestess tarot card 1 pomegranate, or pomegranate seeds, or pomegranate juice 1 dark lunar stone such as black moonstone (optional) 1 light lunar stone such as moonstone or selenite (optional)
◇Description
An autumn equinox ritual meditation for the turn toward the darker half of the year. Sit with The High Priestess card and a pomegranate, optionally holding dark and light lunar stones to embody the balance of shadow and light. Reflect on what calls for release, shadow work, and inner guidance as the seasonal wheel tips into rest and introspection. The pomegranate echoes the myth of Persephone — abundance, sacrifice, rebirth — and six seeds are set aside as an offering to honor the cycle.
