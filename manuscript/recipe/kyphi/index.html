🏺Kyphi

A traditional Egyptian temple incense, aged in two stages of two weeks each, used in night rituals, to invoke Egyptian deities, and as a general magickal incense.

Quantities

4 parts Frankincense
2 parts Benzoin
2 parts Gum Mastic
2 parts Myrrh
1 part Cedar
1 part Galangal (or Ginger)
1/2 part Calamus (or Vetivert)
1/2 part Cardamom
1/2 part Cinnamon
1/2 part Cassia
1/2 part Juniper berries
1/2 part Orris
1/2 part Cypress
A few drops Lotus Bouquet
A few drops wine
A few drops honey
7 raisins

Directions

Mix the ground dry ingredients thoroughly. Let sit in an airtight container for two weeks.

In a separate bowl, mix together the lotus bouquet, wine, honey, and raisins. Add this wet mixture to the dry ingredients and blend with the hands.

Let sit another two weeks in an airtight container. Then, if desired, grind to a fine powder.

Historical Note

Kyphi is one of the most famous incenses of antiquity, burned in Egyptian temples at sunset. Ancient records describe formulas containing up to sixteen ingredients, with temple priests guarding their specific formulations. The two-week aging periods allow the ingredients to release their essential oils and harmonize.

Ancient Egyptian incense and perfume makers were masters of their craft, and a single formula could take months to complete, with each ingredient added at precisely the right moment. Even heavily simplified modern versions of kyphi remain complex and demanding to make. Egyptian Temple Incense and Egyptian Temple Floor Wash offer simpler ways to work with its botanicals.
