🍄Kombucha SCOBY

Type
Drink
Correspondences1
❤️‍🩹Healing
Plants1
Green Tea

A method to grow your own SCOBY (Symbiotic Culture Of Bacteria and Yeast) for making kombucha. The SCOBY is a living colony that ferments tea into the probiotic-rich beverage.

Quantities

  • 1 cup unflavored, nonpasteurized kombucha
  • 2 cups water
  • 1/2 cup granulated sugar (avoid alternative sugars)
  • 4 unflavored black tea bags
  • Glass mixing bowl
  • Large widemouthed jar
  • Several tea towels or closely woven fabric to cover jar

Directions

Wash all bottles, jars, and spoons. Use the hot cycle on the dishwasher to sterilize them if possible. Boil 2 cups of water and pour into a glass bowl. Add sugar and stir to dissolve. Add 4 unflavored black tea bags and allow to steep for 10 minutes before removing. Add 5 cups of cool water to bring down the temperature. Add the tea to a large widemouthed jar. Add the kombucha and give it a gentle stir. If you see wispy things floating in the storebought kombucha, include them as they will grow into your SCOBY. Cover the mouth of the jar with several layers of fabric (not cheesecloth, which allows fruit flies in). Keep the room around 70 degrees. Leave undisturbed for 1 to 4 weeks. Wash hands thoroughly before touching the SCOBY. Keep out of sunlight, in a stable temperature area, away from heating or cooling vents. Bubbles will form after a few days to a week as fermentation starts. These will join to form a thick layer that becomes the SCOBY. Wait until it is at least 1/4 inch thick. The first generation or two will look bumpy and bubbly, but will smooth out as it matures. The liquid will be too vinegary to drink but works as a starter for kombucha. It should smell tart and vinegary. If you see black or green spots or it smells cheesy, bad, or rancid, it has been contaminated and you must start over.
