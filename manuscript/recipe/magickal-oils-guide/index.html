Magickal Oils Guide

Magickal oils have been used in ritual practice for thousands of years. Ancient practitioners created scented oils by heating fragrant plant materials in oil or fat, transferring the botanical essence into the base. Today, a wide array of both genuine and synthetic oils are available for magickal work.

Why Not Make Your Own Essential Oils?

Extracting true essential oils at home is impractical for most practitioners:

  • Equipment costs are significant. Condensers, fractionating columns, and other specialized apparatus are expensive and must be adapted for distillation.
  • Large quantities of plant material are required. Extracting a small amount of oil may require hundreds of pounds of fresh petals, leaves, or roots. The plant material must also be the correct species, as certain varieties produce superior oils.
  • The process demands precision. Temperature fluctuations or missed steps compromise quality. Homemade attempts often yield disappointing results that do not capture the true scent of the plant.

For most botanical oils, purchasing high-quality products is more practical than attempting home extraction.

Buying Oils

Types of Commercial Oils

  • Genuine essential oils are extracted directly from the plant material they are named after. Lavender essential oil, for example, comes from lavender.
  • Bouquets or compounds recreate a specific scent by blending multiple true essential oils together.
  • Synthetic oils are laboratory-created fragrances. Many companies sell these without labeling them as synthetic.

Choosing Quality

Genuine essential oils are preferred for magickal work because they contain the full energetic signature of the plant. While more expensive, they are also more concentrated, so smaller amounts are needed.

Synthetics can function in magick but tend to be less potent than true botanical oils. Be cautious of "essential oil" labels on products that are actually synthetic. Purchasing from reputable suppliers specializing in aromatherapy or essential oils is the best way to ensure authenticity.

For scents unavailable as true essential oils (such as tuberose, sweet pea, or lotus), bouquet recipes can be blended from genuine oils to approximate the fragrance.

Blending Oils

Basic Method

  1. Gather the essential oils and any bouquets called for in the recipe
  2. Add 1/8 cup of carrier oil to a clean, sterilized glass jar

  2. Swirl the oils together gently in a clockwise motion. Do not stir.

Using Magickal Oils

Candle Dressing

The most common application. Rub oil onto candles before burning them in ritual. The oil's energy combines with the candle color and flame, amplified by the practitioner's visualization.

Anointing the Body

Apply oil to pulse points to absorb its energies. Love oils might be rubbed on wrists, neck, and over the heart. Courage oils can be applied before facing challenges.

Ritual Baths

Add several drops to bathwater. Immersing in the scented water brings the oil's energies within while inhaling the fragrance.

Talismans and Amulets

Anoint charms, sachets, or other magickal objects with appropriate oils to charge them with specific intentions.

Crystals and Stones

Rub oils onto quartz and other stones to enhance their energies for spellwork. The stones can then be worn, carried, or arranged in ritual patterns.

Diluting Essential Oils

True essential oils should be diluted before skin application. Essential oils are volatile, meaning they are easily absorbed through the skin and will damage tissue if applied undiluted. They should only ever be used topically as an active ingredient in a dilution with a carrier oil such as jojoba, coconut, olive, or grapeseed. As a general guideline:

5 to 7 drops per 1/8 cup carrier oil

This dilution prevents skin irritation while preserving the fragrance. Some essential oils are severe skin irritants and require extra caution or should be avoided for topical use entirely. Individual oil entries note such concerns.
