Recipe Grimoire

Potion Guide

Brews, also called potions, range from everyday herbal teas to mystical infusions. They descend from ancient magickal, ritual, and medicinal traditions, and remain just as effective today. In practical herb magick, a brew is simply an herbal infusion. Your kitchen stove works perfectly well, no forest clearing or open fire required.

Brews serve many purposes: some are consumed, others added to baths, and still others prepared to release fragrant steam that fills a space with herbal vibrations.

Choosing Your Water

Water quality matters in brewing.

Best choices:

  • Spring water
  • Distilled water
  • Rain water (collected away from polluted areas)
  • Well water from clean sources

Avoid:

  • Sea water (too mineral-heavy)
  • Mineral water (same issue)
  • Tap water (use only as a last resort due to chlorine, fluoride, and potential contaminants)

If you plan to drink your brew, distilled or spring water is always the safer choice.

Heat Sources

Any standard heat source will work:

  • Stovetop (gas or electric)
  • Fireplace
  • Outdoor fire

Note: While technically possible, microwave heating isn't recommended, it removes some of the ritual connection from the process.

Vessels and Containers

Avoid direct contact between herbs and metal during brewing. This is a longstanding principle in herbalism, with few exceptions (traditional cauldron work or double-boiler preparations).

Recommended materials:

  • Glass (especially for solar infusions)
  • Ceramic
  • Earthenware

For solar infusions, place water and herbs in a clear glass jar and set it in direct sunlight, preferably outdoors, for most of the day.

Basic Brewing Method

Unless a specific recipe states otherwise, follow this standard process:

  1. Prepare the herbs: Gather, grind, and mix your ingredients. If the brew will be consumed, use a separate mortar and pestle reserved for culinary purposes.
  2. Empower the mixture: Focus your magickal intention into the herbs as you work.
  3. Heat the water: Bring approximately two cups of water to a boil.
  4. Combine: Place about one handful of prepared herbs into a heat-proof, non-metallic container (a teapot works well).
  5. Steep: Pour boiling water over the herbs and cover with a tight-fitting, non-metallic lid. Allow to steep for approximately 13 minutes.
  6. Strain: Filter through cheesecloth or a bamboo strainer.
  7. Use promptly: Apply as directed in your specific recipe.

Storage

Brews are best used immediately after preparation. If storage is necessary, refrigerate for no more than three to four days. After this time, return the liquid to the earth and prepare a fresh batch.

Oxymels

An oxymel is an elixir made by combining herbs with vinegar and honey. The honey acts as a sweetener that can make bitter herbs more palatable. For example, Mullein is often used in oxymels to support respiratory health.

Not every herbal remedy is suitable for an oxymel. Carminative herbs used to aid digestion, particularly of fats, must remain bitter in order to stimulate bile production. Sweetening a carminative herb would negate its digestive effects.

Simples

Simples are herbal remedies that contain only one ingredient. They are easy to prepare and are generally drawn from the tonic herbs for the purpose of nourishing the body. A simple is a good starting point for anyone new to herbal preparations, as it allows you to learn how each individual plant behaves before combining them in more complex formulas.

Tonics

Tonics are liquid herbal preparations ingested for health purposes such as strengthening and healing. They can be made from roots, leaves, berries, flowers, or bark and are created by either infusing or decocting. A preservative such as brandy or another type of alcohol may be added.

The key distinction between a tonic and a tea is time and quantity. A tonic steeps for much longer, typically twenty to thirty minutes compared to five for a tea, and a significantly greater amount of plant material is used. Where a tea might call for a few teaspoons, a tonic may use a cup or more of herbs.

A Note on "Love Potions"

No brew can force someone to fall in love with you or emotionally enslave another person. Such potions don't exist. However, certain herbal preparations have traditionally been used to:

  • Relax inhibitions and ease tension
  • Mellow emotional states
  • Smooth difficulties in established relationships

A few such recipes appear in this grimoire, but understand them for what they are, gentle aids, not magickal compulsions.
