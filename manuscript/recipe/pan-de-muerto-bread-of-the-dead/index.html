🕯️Pan de Muerto (Bread of the Dead)

◇Type
Food
↗Correspondences2
🔮Divination😊Happiness
↗Materials2

↗Plants1

↗Timing1
💀Dias de los Muertos

Traditional Mexican bread celebrating the return of spirits during Día de los Muertos, stacked on home ofrendas for both spirits and the living.

Quantities

One-quarter cup lukewarm water
One tablespoon dry yeast
Four cups all-purpose flour
Three-quarter cup sugar
Two whole eggs plus five egg yolks
One-half cup melted butter
One teaspoon salt
One tablespoon anise seed
Two teaspoons nutmeg
Egg wash made from one egg white and one teaspoon melted butter
Sesame seeds (optional)
Sugar for decorating (colored sugar is most dramatic)

Directions

Proof yeast by dissolving in warm water. Add one-quarter cup of flour and blend with a spoon. Allow the mixture to rest until it doubles in volume, approximately 30 minutes. Place the rest of the flour in a large bowl, make a well in the center, and add the sugar, eggs, egg yolks, melted butter, salt, anise, and nutmeg. Beat thoroughly, then add the yeast mixture and blend. Knead on a lightly floured board until the dough becomes elastic and not sticky, approximately 15 minutes. Place the ball of dough into a greased bowl, cover with a cloth, and allow to rise in a warm spot for approximately 2 hours or until doubled in volume; check by poking your fingers into the side of the dough, and when your fingerprints stay the dough is ready. The dough may also be raised overnight, covered, in the refrigerator. Punch down the dough and knead five times without adding additional flour. Cut one-third off the dough and set it aside. Shape the remaining dough into a round loaf. Pinch off a ball from the reserved dough, form a skull with it, and place it on top of the loaf. Roll the rest of the reserved dough into a long rope, pinch it off into approximately three-inch-long pieces, and shape them into bones; these are traditionally placed onto the loaf like spokes on a wheel. Cover the loaf, place on a greased cookie sheet, and let it rise until doubled in size. Brush with the egg wash and sprinkle with sesame seeds if desired. Cover once again and let it rise once more. Preheat the oven to 350°F/180°C/Gas 4 and bake on the center rack for approximately 45 minutes or until golden brown. After the loaf has cooled on a rack, sprinkle the bread with colored sugar or decorate it with icing. Traditionally the names of departed loved ones are inscribed on the bread.

About This Recipe

Pan de Muerto celebrates the return of the spirits. Traditionally made by village bakers using lots of egg yolks and anise seeds, these round beautifully decorated breads are stacked on home ofrendas for the spirits to enjoy as well as being eaten by the living.

Regional Variations

  • Oaxaca: Loaves are sprinkled with sesame seeds representing happiness
  • Michoacán: Pieces of dough are used to form skulls and bones to ornament the top of the round loaf, which are then dusted with colored sugar

Traditionally, names of departed loved ones are inscribed on the bread.
