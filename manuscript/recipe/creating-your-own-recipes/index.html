Recipe Grimoire

Creating Your Own Recipes

Once you've worked with established formulas for a while, you may feel the urge to create something entirely your own. This is natural. Just as experienced cooks develop original dishes, the magickal herbalist often wants to craft unique blends tailored to personal needs and energies.

You don't have to create your own recipes. There are enough formulas in traditional sources to keep you busy for years. But if you want to try, this guide walks through the process step by step.

Why Create Original Blends?

Mixtures you design yourself are intimately connected to your personal beliefs and energies. They may prove more powerful simply because they come from you. Traditional recipes certainly work, but there's something uniquely satisfying about crafting your own blends and witnessing their results.

Trust your intuition when selecting ingredients and proportions. And most importantly, have fun with the process.

Step One: Define the Goal

Every mixture begins with a clear magickal intention. Ask yourself what you need this blend to accomplish:

  • Bring money into your life
  • Ease depression or anxiety
  • Attract new love
  • Draw health or vitality
  • Provide protection
  • Create peace and calm
  • Increase personal power

You might be addressing an immediate need, or you might be preparing something for future use. Either way, start with a defined purpose.

Step Two: Choose the Form

Once you know your goal, decide what kind of product to make: incense, oil, bath salt, bath herbs, tincture, sachet, ointment, or something else.

Ask yourself these questions:

Which form suits this goal best?

Some forms work better for certain intentions. A protection incense won't help you at the office or during your commute. A protective oil or amulet would be more practical.

Which procedures do I know well?

Stick to processes you have experience with. Familiarity with a technique almost guarantees better results.

Which forms produce the best results for me?

If you find combustible incense (cones, blocks, sticks) less effective than loose incense on charcoal, go with noncombustible blends. If oil-anointed candles consistently work well for you, create an oil.

Remember: While these products contain their own energies, their effectiveness largely depends on their ability to shift you into a state of ritual consciousness.

Which forms do I enjoy using?

If you hate carrying sachets, don't make one. If slipping into an herb-scented bath gets your energy flowing, create bath salts or bath herbs.

Step Three: Select Your Ingredients

Now choose which herbs, oils, or other materials to include.

Start with correspondences. Check the Correspondence Grimoire for materials aligned with your magickal goal. For a protection blend, you'd look up protective herbs, oils, and resins.

Check your supplies. Compare your list against what you actually have on hand. If you're missing key ingredients, either obtain them or find suitable substitutes.

Expand through planetary and elemental connections. If you need more options, consult the planetary and elemental associations for your goal. Protection, for example, relates to the Sun, Mars, and the element of Fire. Any herb or oil ruled by these forces could potentially work in a protection formula.

Keeping an Inventory

Maintaining lists of your supplies saves enormous time. Keep a notebook near your herb cabinet with:

  1. Herbs and botanicals you currently have
  2. Oils in your collection
  3. Processing supplies (cheesecloth, bottles, eyedroppers, cloth, cord, thread, potassium nitrate, alcohol, etc.)
  4. Wish list of items you need to obtain

Update these lists whenever you use up or acquire something. This prevents the frustrating experience of rummaging through dozens of jars trying to figure out what you have.

Even well-organized herb cabinets become cluttered over time. A simple inventory system saves you from checking every bottle.

Step Four: Narrow Your List

Once you have a preliminary list of herbs from Step Three, compare it against your stock list. Cross out any you don't currently have.

Next, determine which herbs are most favorable to the type of product you've decided to make. Some ingredients are immediately inappropriate for certain forms.

For incense: Though garlic is a fine protective herb, it's best not to use it in incense formulas. The smell would be unpleasant and counterproductive. Cross inappropriate herbs off your list.

Test by burning: If necessary, light a charcoal block, place it in an incense burner, and burn a small amount of each remaining herb. Remove any that don't appeal to you. Trust your senses. If an herb smells wrong for your purpose, it probably is.

After this refinement process, you might end up with a shorter, more focused list. For a protection incense, you might have:

  • Rosemary
  • Basil
  • Orange peel
  • Cedar
  • Frankincense
  • Cinnamon
  • Pine
  • Juniper

In a sense, the recipe has formulated itself. After determining the relative amounts of each ingredient, you could mix these herbs, empower them, and burn as a protective incense.

💡
Always Include a Resin

In general, use at least one gum resin in every incense recipe. Resins include frankincense, myrrh, benzoin, gum arabic, mastic, copal, and dragon's blood. Even if a resin doesn't appear on your correspondence lists, include one for best results.

Step Six: Record and Compound

Once you've decided on a formula, write it down immediately. Copy it on a three-by-five-inch index card or in your herbal notebook. Even if you think you'll change it later, record it! Give the blend a name as well.

Now compound the mixture:

  1. Grind herbs if necessary in your mortar and pestle
  2. Mix the ingredients together
  3. Align their energies as you blend
  4. Empower the mixture for its intended purpose
  5. Use immediately or store in a labeled bottle

You've just created a new recipe.

Creating Deity-Specific Formulas

Formulas dedicated to specific deities are created somewhat differently.

Research mythology. Check into mythology to discover which plants (if any) were used in a deity's worship. These are ritually appropriate.

Use corresponding herbs. Alternatively, use herbs and plants that relate to the deity's basic influences. For a Pele incense honoring the Hawaiian goddess of volcanoes, fiery herbs would be ideal. Though fiery Hawaiian plants would be perfect, they aren't easily obtainable elsewhere. Substitute with accessible fiery herbs that carry similar energy.

Match the deity's domain:

  • Love deities: Venus-ruled, Water-element herbs
  • War deities: Mars-ruled, Fire-element herbs
  • Wisdom deities: Mercury-ruled, Air-element herbs
  • Earth/harvest deities: Earth-element, fertility herbs
  • Sea deities: Water-element, lunar herbs

Final Thoughts

With these simple steps you can create magickal products for an endless variety of uses:

  • Rely on your inner wisdom. Your intuition knows what works for you.
  • Research. Study correspondences, mythology, and traditional uses.
  • Experiment. Try different combinations and proportions.
  • Record everything. Keep detailed notes on what works and what doesn't.

And most of all, enjoy the powers of herbs.
