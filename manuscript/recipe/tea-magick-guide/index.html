Tea Magick Guide

Herbal Tea Brewing

Dried herbs should be kept in airtight containers. Most herbs require using one teaspoon dried herbs or two tablespoons fresh herbs to one cup of water. When drinking tea for pleasure rather than medicine, adjust the herbs to suit your taste.

Infusion: Pour boiling water over the herbs and steep for ten to thirty minutes. Keep the container tightly covered. This is the standard method for most leaf and flower herbs.

Decoction: The preferred method for roots, barks, and hard nuts or seeds. Simmer the herbs in boiling water for fifteen to thirty minutes with the pot kept tightly covered.

Flavor Reference

  • Nutmeg — Spicy; don't use too much
  • Cloves — Hot, spicy, aromatic; add only a few buds
  • Cinnamon — Warming, fragrant, pleasant

🌿 Mint

  • Peppermint — Strong, calming flavor
  • Wintergreen — Woodsy mint

🍓 Tart

  • Rose hips — Refreshingly tart
  • Hibiscus — Very tart and rich in vitamin C

🍬 Sweet

  • Licorice — Sweet flavored
  • Anise seed — Licorice flavored
  • Fennel — Sweet licorice flavored

The Magickal Uses of Tea

Tea is a plant that grows in China, India, and the American South. The teas we commonly associate with our ritual cuppa all come from the same plant, Camellia sinensis. It was reputedly discovered by the Chinese emperor Shen-nong in 2737 BCE when a leaf fell into his boiling water, entrancing him with its flavor and energy.

Not everything called tea really is. Herbs and botanicals that aren't Camellia sinensis made in hot water are called tisanes. A tisane can be honeybush with cinnamon, or a cup of chamomile with honey after a long day.

Tea and Witchcraft

Incorporating tea into your magickal practice has a long history and blends two well-known disciplines: plant magick and divination. Tea is a plant of transformation with so many varieties and preparations that perform unique magickal tasks.

Though all tea comes from the same plant, the growing environment plays a large part in the outcome, as do the methods of drying and variety. The tea plant is so delicate that leaves pick up the aromas of their environment. Hawaiian tea tastes vastly different from Chinese tea because Hawaiian tea farms are often near pineapple crops, allowing leaves to mature and retain a hint of their pineapple neighbors.

Types of Tea

White Tea

Brewing: 1½ tsp dried leaves, 175°F water, steep 4-5 minutes. Water too hot or steeping too long results in bitter brew.

Season: Spring

Magickal Correspondences: Purification, aura cleansing, prosperity, fecundity, psychic protection, abundance, clarity, beginnings, beauty, childhood, blessings, connection with beneficial spirits

White tea is comprised of young leaves picked before maturity. Its delicate flavor connects it to spring's new buds and explosion of color, fertility, and life. It makes an excellent offering to various gods as it blesses and fosters connection with others, including beneficial spirits.

Green Tea

Brewing: 1 tsp dried leaves, 175°F water, steep 1-2 minutes only.

Season: Summer

Magickal Correspondences: Abundance, health, long life, banishing negative energies, love (aphrodisiac), cleansing spaces

Green tea arises from dried mature leaves. Many think green tea is too bitter or grassy, but that's usually due to improper preparation. It has a soft, vegetative flavor that pairs well with fruits, especially citrus.

Green tea's antioxidants and moderate caffeine connect it to health and longevity. The caffeine lends itself to banishing negative energies. Brew it to asperge and cleanse a home or workplace. Green tea is also an aphrodisiac used in love spells where the mild caffeination can improve circulation to vital areas.

⚠️
Note: When mild caffeination is mentioned for green tea, it is not matcha being discussed. Matcha is green tea with the entire leaf powdered and consumed, meaning all caffeine is consumed. Matcha has 3-4 times the caffeine of brewed green tea.

Oolong Tea

Brewing: 1 tsp tea, 195°F water, steep 3-4 minutes.

Season: Fall

Magickal Correspondences: Peace, inner examination, divination, centering emotions, affection, love, balance, meditation

Oolong is a partially withered tea, mildly caffeinated with a soothing flavor somewhere between green and black tea. It represents the marriage of black and green tea, making its magickal action that of love.

The ritual of brewing oolong leaves time for meditation and inner thought, making it appropriate for working magick for self-examination while brewing. The balance of green and black tea leaves also makes it perfect for finding balance in mind and heart.

Black Tea

Brewing: 1 tsp tea, 195°F water, steep 3-4 minutes.

Season: Winter

Magickal Correspondences: Banishing apathy, banishing sleepiness, overcoming directionlessness, bravery, freedom from boredom, adventure, expansive thought, prosperity

Black tea is made by fermenting green tea leaves in water for long periods. The longer the fermentation, the more caffeine. In the mountains of China where black tea was born, tea leaves would be picked, placed in a vat of water, and left in caves.

Black tea's higher caffeine content leads to feelings of bravery, freedom from boredom, and a sense of adventure. Like other stimulating substances (cinnamon, clove, cardamom), black tea can be beneficial for prosperity work.

Rooibos and Honeybush Tea

Brewing: 1½ tsp tea, 208°F water, steep 5-6 minutes. These do not get bitter if over-steeped.

Origin: South Africa (Rooibos = "red tea," Honeybush named for honey-like fragrance)

Magickal Correspondences: Health, motherly energy, fertility, prosperity, grounding, nurturing, divination (excellent base for tea leaf reading)

Rooibos (Aspalathus linearis) is left on the plant to ripen, fall, and oxidize in the sun. Green rooibos uses younger leaves not allowed to oxidize, retaining a sweeter flavor. Both plants are drought tolerant.

Honeybush (Cyclopia intermedia) was mostly unknown outside South Africa until 2001. These dark red-brown botanicals have a mild fruity flavor with earthy energies suitable for invoking motherly energy, fertility, prosperity, grounding, and nurturing.

Making a Good Pot of Tea

Start with fresh, cold water and bring it to a full rolling boil in a tea kettle. While the water heats, warm your china or ceramic teapot by filling it with hot water and then pouring it out.

Add loose tea leaves directly into the warmed pot, or place them inside a tea ball. Pour the boiling water over the leaves and let them steep for 3 to 5 minutes.

Serving

Pour the tea through a strainer into your cup, or strain it into a fresh, warmed teapot for serving. Add milk, sweetener, or lemon to taste. Cover the teapot with a cozy to keep it warm between cups.

A Note on Dosage

⚠️
Limit yourself to 2 to 4 cups per day. Tea acts as a natural diuretic, and some herbs can be harmful in large amounts. For magickal teas brewed during spellwork, drink only 1 to 2 cups during the spell event itself.

Notes on Magickal Tea Recipes

When preparing herbal tea recipes that include spoken words:

  • Words listed alongside each ingredient should be spoken aloud as that herb is added to the pot.
  • Words listed after all the ingredients should be spoken before drinking the tea.

Charging Your Tea with Intent

To turn your cup of tea into a potion, you just add magick! Whether you're using loose-leaf teas or a tea bag, the intention has to be applied.

  1. Decide on a goal. Make sure it is appropriately worded in the positive.
  2. Center your purpose in your mind. For example: "I am going to pass my English final."
  3. Gather intent and power into the palms of your hands, and use the energy to charge the tea leaves.
  4. Pour your hot water over the leaves and allow the heat of the water to charge your brew.
  5. Remove the tea after the correct steep time, and add sugar and cream as you like.
  6. Enjoy, continuing to visualize your intended outcome.

Designing a Tea Spell

To create your own magickal tea, there are a few steps to consider. Once you have the idea, experiment. Keep making, keep blending those teas. The only way you master something is by doing it. Research is lovely (and necessary), but is no replacement for getting your hands in there.

💡
Tip: Many tea shops have 1- or 2-ounce containers of loose tea leaves. With these smaller containers, you can purchase several types and flavors of tea to experiment with.

Step 1: What is your goal?

Not every tea blend needs to be magickal. Sometimes the goal is just to get your family to drink something other than a canned soda. It can be as intricate as you wish. Remember, every material that goes into your potion is magickal; it is the intent that powers that spell.

Is your goal to bring harmony to your household? Is it to have the energy to finish a project on time? Is your aim to relax after a stressful day? Deciding on the intended outcome not only cements the magick in your mind, but it also helps inform the choices you make for the rest of the work.

  • If your goal is to de-stress after a stressful day at work, you might not pick a high caffeine base like Maté.
  • If you need quick cash, you might not pick only fruits associated with love.

Step 2: Grab a notebook!

When working out this process, write it out. It puts all of the options out there and makes it easy to recreate blends that are a) effective and b) tasty!

Write down all of the things you associate with your goal. This is the free-form, brainstorming portion of the exercise. Looking at your thoughts on paper can allow you to make connections you might otherwise have missed.

Example brainstorm for Money:

Almond, Basil, Bergamot, Cedar, Clary sage, Clove, Lemon, Orange, Patchouli, Strawberry, Tangerine

Step 3: Look at correspondences

Your brainstorm list will have things that maybe wouldn't taste great, or don't agree with you for reasons of preference or allergies. Listen to the preference as much as you would allergies.

⚠️
If you genuinely object to something and decide to push the issue and include it in your working: a) you won't want to drink it and will avoid the working because of it, and b) if you make yourself do it anyway, your magick is going to be blocked because you are rejecting an ingredient therein. It's much easier and psychically gentle to pick something else.

Example: Almond is associated with abundance, fortune, money, wisdom, and prosperity, each adds a different flavor to the magick. Almond is also associated with beauty, communication, consecration, peace, and joy. An almond-based prosperity tea would encourage spiritual growth, joy, and long-term budgeting. Adding a woody, stable element will root the prosperity in the long term.

Choosing a base: If your tea hopes to stimulate the situation, black tea is an excellent choice. If you are sensitive to caffeine, rooibos and honeybush produce a caffeine-free base with mellow flavor. In the case of a caffeine issue, add a stimulating plant to your rooibos base, like cinnamon paired with a plant associated with fertility, like dried apple.

Step 4: Smell a few combinations

Before starting to blend, smell a small amount of your intended materials together. Use small dishes like custard cups. If your pieces don't work well together, you can put them back into their containers without contamination. Nothing is wasted.

Use equal amounts for smelling, each canister has different amounts with different strength scents, where equal amounts are a better measure of how they will taste together.

Step 5: Write down your findings

The thoughts that go through your head while blending and sampling should be recorded so you can more easily recall the smells, combinations, and features of each for future creations. Just because strawberry and lemon work well together doesn't mean they are suited to your immediate need for a money spell; they might be more suited to attracting a faithful and loving partner in a later tea.

Step 6: Blend your tea!

Start with equal parts of each material in the initial blending so it is easier to figure out whether it needs more or less of a certain ingredient.

⏰
Note on woody materials: They need more heat over a longer period of time to release their highest potential. They will continue to release their flavor as long as they are in the teacup. Leaving something containing clove, for instance, can quickly overpower a more delicate brew if allowed to steep too long.

Step 7: Add water!

Once you think you have the perfect ratio, write it down—something like "Test batch #1." Remember to be as detailed as possible. If you like "x" ingredient, but "y" ingredient is too powerful a flavor, you should be able to try a lesser amount for a more ideal blend.

Blend a teaspoon to a teaspoon and a half of the experimental brew with water and see how it reacts. Try it without sweetener, then try it with. Try it with creamer or your favorite cream substitute.

Step 8: Evaluate

Think of what you liked and didn't like. Not every tea is going to be a winner. There will be things you adore and things that have their place. Not every person will like everything you create, even your favorite ones. That's okay. For now, just blend for you. You can take over the world tomorrow.

Step 9: Capture your tea

The choice between loose-leaf and tea bags is an old one:

  • Loose-leaf: You can see every glorious ingredient
  • Tea bags: Quick and convenient solution
  • Fillable tea bags (tea filters): A middle ground, they allow the tea to breathe and infuse properly
  • Tea infusers: Allow the tea to fully bloom and attain its best flavor (recommended)
  • Tea balls: Can fall open, leak leaves out of the large water holes, and can burn your fingers
🏷️
Above all, label your tea. Whether you make up a name for it or just put the ingredients on a label, label everything.

Storing Your Tea

Proper storage ensures you get the best life out of your ingredients. The three most important things to remember:

Block out light

UV rays destroy the integrity of the ingredients and break down volatile oils that give your cherished blends scent and magick. They rob the tea of its color and structure. Some companies make UV-blocking tea containers; they will specifically state this and cost a bit more.

Constant temperature

Steep fluctuations in temperature can greatly reduce shelf life. High temperatures can break down the volatile oils, and freezing temperatures can break down the plant matter anchoring those oils, making your tea crumble rather than unfurl.

Intercept oxygen

Oxygen robs you of the smell of your teas, as the volatility of materials is the rate at which their oils evaporate. Every molecule that evaporates is one less that adds to your taste, aroma, and experience. Airtight containers are the way to go.
