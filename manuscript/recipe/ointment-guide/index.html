Ointment Guide

The notorious "flying ointments" of historical witchcraft (psychoactive herb mixtures suspended in fat and applied to the skin for astral travel) represent just one category of magickal salves. Practitioners have long used ointments for countless practical magickal purposes that mirror the uses of oils.

Converting any magickal oil formula into an ointment is straightforward: blend it into melted beeswax, lard, or vegetable shortening. Store finished ointments in glass or ceramic containers with secure lids, kept cool and away from light.

☠️
Warning: Some historical ointment recipes contain lethal herbs. These formulas appear here only as documentation of magickal history. Never attempt toxic preparations.

Creating Ointments

An ointment requires two components: magickal herbs or oils and a fatty base. Traditional recipes called for hog's lard due to availability, though modern practitioners achieve superior results with vegetable shortening or beeswax. The base should liquefy when heated but solidify at room temperature.

Shortening Method

  1. Melt 4 parts vegetable shortening over gentle heat, avoiding scorching.
  2. Stir in 1 part powdered dried herbs using a wooden spoon.
  3. Maintain low heat, stirring periodically, until the herb fragrance permeates the air.
  4. Filter through cheesecloth into a glass jar.
  5. Preserve with 1/2 teaspoon benzoin tincture per pint.
  6. Refrigerate for storage.

These preparations remain viable for weeks to months. Discard at first sign of mold.

Beeswax Method

This technique yields a lighter, less greasy consistency. Works best with essential oils rather than whole herbs since straining proves difficult.

  1. Shave unbleached beeswax into small pieces using a sturdy knife.
  2. Measure roughly 1/4 cup shaved wax into a double boiler arrangement.
  3. Add approximately 1/4 cup carrier oil (olive, hazelnut, or sesame work well).
  4. Stir until fully incorporated.
  5. Cool briefly until slightly thickened. This prevents essential oil evaporation.
  6. Blend in your essential oil mixture.
  7. Transfer to containers and label.

Compresses

A compress is made by soaking a clean cloth into an infusion or decoction and then applying it to the body to receive the healing benefits of the herbal preparation. Compresses can be either hot or cold and are commonly used to address pain and swelling.

To infuse a compress with magick, focus on the transference of energy and treat the cloth as a ritual object. You can charge it by placing it on your altar and exposing it to sunlight or moonlight depending on the type of effect you desire. Since a compress is reusable, think of it as one of your magickal tools dedicated to the sacred task of healing.

Liniments

A liniment is an herbal extract added to vinegar or alcohol for topical application. Liniments are used for healing purposes and are particularly effective for treating strained and stiff muscles, bruises, and sprains.

Poultices

Poultices are made by macerating fresh herbs in a mortar and pestle, gathering them into a cloth, and dipping them into hot or boiling water. Poultices are well suited for treating insect bites, skin inflammations, cuts, and scrapes.

The mortar and pestle is itself a magickal tool, representing the sacred union of the goddess and the god. Preparing a poultice can be performed as part of a ritual or spell. Consider using a mortar and pestle dedicated solely to healing work. If you also craft incense or use one for other types of spells and charms, it is worth investing in a separate set devoted entirely to healing and tonic herbs.

Charging Ointments

After cooling, consecrate the finished ointment with your specific magickal intention. This step activates the preparation for ritual work.

Application

Ointments are applied to the body to manifest magickal effects. Combine application with focused visualization, trusting the preparation to accomplish its purpose.
