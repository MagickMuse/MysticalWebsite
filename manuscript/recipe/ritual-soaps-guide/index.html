Ritual Soaps Guide

Picture yourself in a warm bath, surrounded by herb-scented water as you prepare for ritual. Incense drifts through the air while you hold your magickal intention in mind. Steam rises, carrying the essence of flowers, seeds, roots, and leaves. Then you reach for the soap and its artificial, cloying perfume shatters your concentration.

Sound familiar? While soaping is not required during magickal baths, a properly crafted ritual soap can enhance any working. Even washing your hands before ritual serves as a small act of purification that helps shift your mind into a ceremonial state.

Commercial soaps are loaded with harsh chemicals that irritate skin and carry overpowering synthetic fragrances. Occult shops occasionally stock ritual soaps of varying quality, but making your own is simple, enjoyable, and ensures you control exactly what touches your skin.

Getting Started

You will not need a cauldron bubbling over an open fire or risk burns from lye. The simplest approach uses pure, natural castile soap as a base. Castile soap (named after Castile, Spain) is typically made from coconut or olive oil and can be found at most drugstores and markets. Coconut-based varieties tend to work best.

Note: Castile soap can be drying. If this is a concern, add one to two teaspoons of apricot, almond, or coconut oil to your water before mixing, reducing the water amount slightly to compensate.

Two types of ritual soaps are covered here: solid spheres and liquid soaps.

Making Soap Spheres

Ingredients

  • 4 oz bar of castile soap
  • Slightly less than 1/3 cup water
  • 20 to 50 drops of empowered essential oils (mixed according to your intention)

Method

  1. Cut the soap into very small cubes, no larger than 1/4 inch. Smaller is better.
  2. Place the cubes in a heat-proof, non-metallic container.
  3. Heat the water until nearly boiling, then pour it over the soap. Let sit until cool enough to handle.
  4. Mix with your hands. The chips should be moist but not floating. If floating, add more soap. Let sit about nine minutes until mushy. If the cubes remain hard, gently reheat the bowl in a pan of water.
  5. Add your oils. While waiting, mix and empower your essential oils with your magickal goal. Once the water has cooled (warm water evaporates oils), add 20 to 50 drops and mix thoroughly. The scent should be strong enough to override the soap's natural antiseptic smell.
  6. Form into spheres. Divide the mixture into three or four portions and shape into balls.
  7. Wrap each sphere in a nine-inch square of cotton cheesecloth. Pull the fabric tight, gather the ends at the top, twist, and tie securely with strong string.
  8. Hang to dry in a warm place for three days or until completely hard (they will not give to finger pressure).
  9. Unwrap and use in ritual baths, or rewrap in clean cheesecloth, label, and store or gift to friends.

Making Liquid Soap (Oil Method)

Despite modern advertising treating liquid soap as a new invention, the concept is ancient. Indigenous peoples worldwide created cleansing solutions by agitating suds-producing plants in water. This method uses castile as the base.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup grated castile soap (packed tightly)
  • 3 cups water
  • 50 to 60 drops of empowered essential oils

Method

  1. Grate the soap into a large bowl and pack it into a measuring cup until you have exactly one cup.
  2. Heat three cups of water until almost boiling.
  3. Add the soap chips to the hot water, remove from heat, and whisk with a wooden utensil until completely melted.
  4. Let cool, then add 50 to 60 drops of your mixed, empowered oils. Stop when the soap is strongly scented.
  5. Transfer to a jar using a funnel, cap tightly, and shake vigorously to mix.
  6. Label and store until needed.

Making Liquid Soap (Brew Method)

You can substitute herb brews for essential oils in liquid soaps.

Method

  1. Add five to six tablespoons of mixed, dried, ground, and empowered herbs to three cups of hot water.
  2. Remove from heat, steep for 10 to 13 minutes, then strain.
  3. Gently reheat the herbal water, add one cup of grated castile soap, and whisk until dissolved.
  4. Let cool and bottle.

Note: Herb brews change dramatically when mixed with castile soap, sometimes creating unexpected scents. If the result is not appealing, add a few drops of an essential oil that matches one of the herbs to strengthen the fragrance.

Using Ritual Soaps

For liquid soaps, wet your hands and apply a few drops. The soap foams easily and leaves skin clean and scented.

For soap spheres, use them during ritual baths as you would any bar soap.

Always visualize your magickal goal as you wash. The soap is a tool; your focused intention is what powers the working.

Optional Enhancements

Some recipes call for rose water or orange flower water in place of plain water. If using these, ensure they are scented with genuine essential oils rather than synthetic fragrances.

Storage

Store unused soaps in your altar (if it has enclosed shelves or cupboards) or in your herb cabinet. Keep them labeled and accessible for ritual use.
