❤️‍🩹Making Flower Essence

Type
Elixir
Correspondences1
❤️‍🩹Healing

Flower essences capture the vibrational signature of flowers through sun-infused water and are used for emotional and mental well-being. Nontoxic and safe for internal or external use.

Quantities

  • For Mother Essence:
  • Flower blooms (enough to cover the water)
  • Spring or purified water
  • Glass bowl
  • Brandy, vinegar, or vegetable glycerin (equal amount to infused water)
  • Clean glass jar
    For Stock Essence:
  • 1-ounce amber dropper bottles
  • 5 drops mother essence per bottle
  • Water and brandy/vinegar/vegetable glycerin (equal parts to fill bottle)

Directions

When gathering blooms, avoid touching the petals. Clip into a basket. Place blooms in a bowl of spring water in full sun for 3 to 4 hours. Carefully remove blooms without touching the water. Mix the liquid with an equal amount of brandy (or vinegar or vegetable glycerin) and pour into a clean glass jar. This is the mother essence. Label, date, and store in a cool dark place. For stock essence, add 5 drops of mother essence to a 1-ounce dropper bottle and fill with equal parts water and brandy.

About Flower Essences

Flower essences work on principles similar to homeopathy, and it is believed that the energetic qualities of flower essences present themselves through their color, shape, habitat, and growing patterns. The vibrational signature of the flowers is transferred to water by steeping the delicate petals for several hours under sunlight.

Because they contain only small traces of actual physical substances and have no direct impact upon the body's biochemistry, they are nontoxic and can be used internally or externally.

Flower essences differ from essential oils in that a flower essence is an infusion of a flower's vibrational signature used for emotional and mental well-being, while essential oils contain molecular compounds transferred into the body via respiratory, olfactory, and integumentary systems.

Flower essences offer a way to work with a plant on an emotional level. They are wonderful to use before meditation, before divination, to gain self-knowledge, to facilitate self-love, or to feel a better connectedness to our natural world.

Dr. Edward Bach developed thirty-eight flower remedies in the 1930s and based his work on the four elements: the earth to nurture the plant, the air from which it feeds, the sun or fire to enable it to impart its power, and water to collect and be enriched with its beneficent magnetic healing.

Important Guidelines

Make sure you are attuned to the spirit of the plant. Commune with the flower in its natural habitat, or if the plant grows in your garden, be a part of the plant's everyday care. Always ask the plant for permission and state clearly that you are seeking the plant's wisdom. Make correct identification. If you are unsure of a plant, consult an expert or leave it be.
