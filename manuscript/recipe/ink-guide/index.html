Ink Guide

Ink has long served as a tool of magick, transforming symbols and images of our goals into visible form. These written or drawn images become focal points during ritual, helping to stir, program, and direct personal energy toward manifestation.

History

During the Middle Ages and Renaissance, many secret magickal textbooks contained sections on purifying and "exorcising" inks before use. Practitioners used these specially prepared inks to draw symbols thought to invoke or banish spiritual entities. The careful preparation was considered essential to the work's success.

Today, the magickal uses of ink have largely faded from practice. When a spell calls for inscribing symbols or images, many practitioners simply reach for a ballpoint pen and lined paper. In doing so, they miss the opportunity for deeper involvement in the ritual. Just as we create our own incenses and oils, we can also create our own inks.

The Charcoal Pen

The first "ink" was likely charcoal, and the first pen a charred stick. This primal method still works today.

  1. Burn a branch or stick until the end reduces to charcoal (not ash)
  2. Allow it to cool completely
  3. Use it as a natural charcoal pencil to trace images of your goal
  4. Create a new char-pen for each ritual
  5. Visualize your magickal need as you draw

This simple approach may be enough to spark your ability to move and direct personal power. For those who want more, creating your own magickal inks offers deeper engagement.

Tools for Ink Magick

All magickal inks work best with sharpened quills or dip pens. Dip pens are usually available at stationery and office supply stores. Practice using them before employing them in ritual work.

Making Lampblack

Lampblack is a key ingredient in several ink formulas. It is soot collected from a candle flame.

Candle Color Selection:

  • White candle for general magickal needs
  • Green candle for money inks
  • Pink candle for love inks
  • Match the candle color to your intention (see Color Correspondences)

Collection Process:

  1. Light a candle of the appropriate color
  2. Hold the back of a metal spoon's bowl in the flame, barely touching the wick
  3. After 30 to 45 seconds, the flame will coat the spoon with black soot
  4. Remove the spoon and hold it over a small bowl
  5. Carefully scrape the lampblack into the bowl using a piece of cardstock
  6. Work carefully. Lampblack is lighter than air and will scatter if disturbed
  7. Repeat 30 to 60 times (about 30 minutes to an hour of work)
  8. Visualize your magickal goal throughout the process

The longer you collect lampblack, the more ink you will be able to produce.

Invisible Inks

Invisible inks are made from common household items such as milk or lemon juice. Write with a clean dip pen on white paper and allow to dry completely. The writing will be invisible until revealed.

To Reveal:

Hold the paper carefully over a candle flame (close enough for heat but not burning) until the writing appears.

Ritual Application:

  1. Write or draw an image of your magickal goal with invisible ink
  2. Visualize with power as you write
  3. When dry, stare at the blank paper. This represents your life without your need.
  4. Hold it near the candle flame
  5. As the image slowly appears, send energy into it, knowing the need will manifest in your life as well

Using Magickal Inks

  • Written Spells: Write or draw an image of your goal on appropriately colored paper. Visualize the letters glowing with energy as you write, or the picture shimmering with power.
  • Burning Rituals: Anoint the inscribed paper with oils matching your intention and burn it to ashes. As it burns, see the energy streaming out to manifest your need.
  • Scrying: At night, burn psychism-inducing incense. Add several drops of black ink to a small round bowl of water. When the water has darkened, turn off the lights and light a yellow or white candle. Gaze into the water, relaxing your conscious mind and opening yourself to receive information about possible future trends.

Caution

Some old spells call for ink to be taken internally, such as dissolving an inscribed paper in water and drinking it. Most commercially prepared inks are poisonous, as are many homemade formulations. Do not drink or eat ink. Always apply modern safety knowledge when working with historical rituals.
