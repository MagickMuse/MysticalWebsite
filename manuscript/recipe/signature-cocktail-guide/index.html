Signature Cocktail Guide

Mixing Drinks and Friends

When grateful gatherings become selective soirées, knowing what to have on hand, how to prepare it, and how to pair it can make a party perfect. Whether you're having a casual planning session for a large group event, or throwing a huge bash to celebrate your big promotion, getting guests in the mood will guarantee they'll be talking about it for a long time.

How to Design the Perfect Signature Drink

When enjoying the presence of friends or magickal practitioners, the ability to craft a delightful cocktail is a rare treat. Anyone can follow a recipe and make the same old, tired margarita. Instead of falling back on an old, reliable cocktail, why not create something they have never seen before, and really knock their socks off?

What's the occasion?

Having the type of gathering in mind will help develop the correct feeling for your drink before you put out the wrong glassware or stumble over garnishes. Your cocktail should reflect:

  • Who you are
  • The mood of the event (if not the theme)
  • The attendees
Gathering TypeDrink Style
Staid gathering (colleagues, formal)Original takes on classic cocktails
Raucous gathering (close friends)More adventurous drinks
Intimate gathering (confidantes)Something showing your personality
Funeral or wakeNothing overly cheery or childish

An irreverent cocktail selection can encourage chatter and enliven conversation, while a new take on a classic will let people know that you have sophisticated tastes but still enjoy trying new things.

How much do you know about cocktails?

An honest assessment of your abilities is a must for this exercise.

🌱
For beginners: Try something less tricky to make but that still says you made an effort. A cocktail based on a Shrubs (Fruit Vinegar Drinks) is a great option. Something like a watermelon and prosecco vinegar shrub would pair well with a botanical gin. Vinegar-based shrubs are cooling on the body, so they will relax people in the heat and allow them to enjoy the flavors of the season.

By making your own syrup to flavor cocktails, you keep the price down and inspire people to think you're a bartending legend they knew nothing about.

What time of year is it?

Making sure your cocktail is seasonally appropriate is important. You don't want to jar guests out of their party mood.

  • Don't serve a hot tea cocktail in summer
  • Don't serve a frozen drink in a blizzard

Consider Mocktail Counterparts

As a good host, consider if your cocktail could have a mocktail counterpart for attendees who aren't drinking.

🍻
Asking "leaded or unleaded" or some variation allows guests to pick how they would like to moderate their alcohol intake without making a big deal out of it. No one owes anyone else an explanation as to why they don't drink.

Letting people know there will be mocktails and alcohol alternatives will help people who otherwise might have skipped feel welcomed.

Building Your Drink

Think in Categories, Not Singular Ingredients

This will put you on your way to making a delicious drink with your own flair.

Before you go mixing bottles of expensive liquor trying to find just the right balance, make lemonade. You will have to find just the right balance of water, sugar, and lemon. It'll teach you to have a gentle hand with sours like lemon, while feeling out the right balance of all three.
Sarah Mitchell, Lab Bar London

Basic Ratios

Shrub Cocktail:

  • 1 part syrup
  • 1 part alcohol
  • 4 parts sparkling water
  • Stir gently

Balanced Cocktail (1:1:2):

  • 1 part sweetness
  • 1 part tartness
  • 2 parts alcohol

The alcohol portion can be made of two different spirits, but try to keep them along the same lines so they don't clash on the palate. Adding triple sec to a margarita gives it jazz because orange is still within the realm of citrus. A rusty nail is a classic because Drambuie is still a whiskey at heart.

Oleosaccharum Punch:

  • ½ part oleosaccharum
  • 1 part alcohol
  • 4 parts sparkling water
  • Garnish
  • Stir gently
📏
Scaling: Recipes given in parts allow for scaling. Turn parts into ounces for one glass, or into cups for a pitcher.

Practice, Practice, Practice!

Once you have an idea of what you would like to create, allow yourself room for errors and time to perfect the drink. Have a friend come over the day before and try out variations on the theme.

For sober-friendly versions: Shrub-based drinks are already sober friendly. Just add an additional part of sparkling water where the alcohol would be.

Be Willing to Accept Feedback

Not every drink will entice every person, but if you're getting a lot of feedback, look at what you're accomplishing and how.

Creating Seasonal Cocktails for Sabbats

A method for designing drinks that honor the eight sabbats of the witch's year:

  1. Grab a notepad and pen and sit where you won't be disturbed.
  2. Label eight sections for the sabbats: Samhain, Yule, Imbolc, Ostara, Beltane, Litha, Lughnasadh, Mabon. (Adjust for other holy tides you celebrate.)
  3. Under each holy tide, list:
    • Flavors associated with the festival
    • Colors associated with the festival
    • Feelings associated with the festival
  4. Use context clues from feelings and colors for how the drink should be prepared. If the feeling is cozy, you might choose a warm cocktail like a toddy.
📝
By working out your thoughts on paper before you start to play alchemist, you will save time and money. It also gives you a chance to plan ahead if you are going to incorporate cordials, ingredients, or syrups that need to be prepared ahead of time.
