Dream Grimoire

Dream Divination

Ancestors communicate through dreams. This page covers how ancestral dreaming works, how to stimulate and record dreams, how to interpret dream symbols, and a traditional Neapolitan ritual for incubating a specific question.

Ancestors & Dreams

Dreams often begin to shift once an ancestor veneration practice is established. Follow-up dreams after rituals honoring ancestors are common. Dreaming tends to become more intense when ancestors are actively trying to reach someone. The relationship is reciprocal.

After receiving a particularly powerful dream, a small ritual of acknowledgment can be meaningful:

  • Wear a piece of clothing that appeared in the dream.
  • Visit a place of power, a river or a crossroads, and offer a prayer of thanksgiving.
  • Light a candle on the ancestor altar.

Stimulating & Recording Dreams

Dream Stimulation

Herbal teas (such as dandelion) and tinctures (such as California poppy) may help stimulate dreaming. For those who prefer not to ingest anything, one of the most effective techniques is simply committing to journaling dreams.

Keeping a Dream Journal

  • Keep a dedicated journal on the nightstand.
  • Setting an extra alarm a few hours before the usual wake time can disrupt the sleep cycle and improve dream recall.
  • When recording a dream, note: calendar date, astrological conditions, lunar phase, and (if applicable) menstrual cycle position.
  • Review journal entries regularly. Patterns emerge over time. Specific ancestors may arrive during particular lunar phases or when the moon is in a certain zodiac sign.

Bibliomancy with the Dream Journal

The dream journal can also be used for bibliomancy (book divination): say a prayer, ask a question, and flip to a random page. Interpret whatever line appears in light of that question.

Interpreting Dream Symbols

There is no universal set of dream symbols. Meaning is personal, relational, and contextual.

Using a dream dictionary:

  • Choose one book and stay with it consistently.
  • Skim through it before needing it for a specific dream. Even just reading a few entries after prayers at the altar helps open the channel between ancestors, the unconscious mind, and waking awareness.
  • Freudian psychoanalysis and Jungian psychoanalysis each produce different symbolic languages; dreamers tend to dream in the symbolic vocabulary of whatever system they are most exposed to.

Trusting your own interpretation:

External authorities are never required. Dreams often seem random or meaningless immediately upon waking, but rereading them later, especially when alert but not yet fully immersed in daily life, tends to surface intuitive connections between dream symbols and current waking circumstances.

Neapolitan Dream Incubation Ritual

In the folk tradition of southern Italy and Sicily, specific questions may be brought to ancestors or saints through ritual prayer, with the response received through an omen or a dream. The following is a Neapolitan example, courtesy of scholar and teacher Anna Scognamiglio:

  1. Make the Sign of the Cross three times.
  2. Recite the following three times, then ask your question:
"Ogge è luna, dimane è marte, 'a sciorta mia stanotte parte, parte e fa nu luongo cammino, vieneme nsuonno sciorta bella mia."

(Translation: "Today is Moon, tomorrow is Mars, my destiny tonight leaves, leaves and takes a long walk, come into my dream my beautiful destiny.")

  1. Recite three Hail Marys.
  2. Repeat steps 1–3 three times total.
  3. End with three Signs of the Cross.

At midnight, go to a window or balcony and watch for a sign from the street:

  • A dog barking: a good sign.
  • A cat meowing: a bad sign.
  • People talking: a common omen; interpret their words in light of the question.

If no omens appear, a dream will typically come that night.
