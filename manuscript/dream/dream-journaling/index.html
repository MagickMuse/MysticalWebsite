Dream Grimoire

Dream Journaling

Dreams offer direct access to subconscious wisdom, future glimpses, and problem-solving insights. Ancient Babylonians believed spirits sent dreams to humans. Egyptians considered them divine gifts. The earliest recorded dream belongs to King Thotmes IV, carved on a granite tablet between the paws of the Sphinx.

Working with your dreams strengthens your intuition and improves spellwork.

Why Keep a Dream Journal

  • Process waking experiences. Dreams often rework daily events and reveal solutions.
  • Receive warnings. Some dreams prepare you for challenges ahead.
  • Spot opportunities. Future glimpses help you recognize and act on possibilities.
  • Identify patterns. Recurring themes reveal what your subconscious is processing.
  • Build rapport with your subconscious. The more you engage, the more your dreams reveal.

How to Record Dreams

  1. Prepare your tools. Keep your book of shadows and a pen within arm's reach of your bed.
  2. Write immediately upon waking. Capture images before they fade into daily busyness.
  3. Use shorthand. Complete sentences are unnecessary. Focus on key images, feelings, and details.
  4. Suspend judgment. Record everything without filtering or analyzing.

What to Document

  • People. Who appeared? Were they familiar or unknown?
  • Setting. Where did the dream take place?
  • Emotions. What did you feel during and after the dream?
  • Standout images. What symbols or objects drew your attention?
  • Date. Always timestamp your entries.
  • Waking context. Note relevant events happening in your life at the time.

Lucid Dreaming

Lucid dreaming allows you to become aware within a dream and direct its course. Developing this skill while asleep strengthens your ability to direct your waking life with intention.
