Almond is a tree of the Rosaceae family, growing to about seven metres and prized as a garden tree for its pinky-white spring blossom. The fruit is botanically a drupe, with the kernel held inside the stone. The species is native to Western Asia and North Africa, and is now cultivated extensively throughout the Mediterranean region, Israel, and California.

Varieties

The almond tree comes in two main forms, bitter and sweet. Only the bitter variety produces an essential oil, distilled from the kernels. The kernels of both varieties also yield a separate fixed oil, commonly called sweet almond oil, which carries neither benzaldehyde nor prussic acid and is widely used as a carrier and skin oil.

Folk Tradition

Sweet almond fixed oil has long been kept in the household for medicinal and cosmetic use. It serves as a gentle laxative and has been reached for in cases of bronchitis, coughs, heartburn, and disorders of the kidneys, bladder, and biliary ducts. Applied to the body it relieves muscular aches and pains, softens the skin, and helps promote a clear complexion.

Magickal Uses

Almonds, as well as the leaves and wood of the tree, are used in prosperity and money spells.

Additionally, climbing an almond tree is said to ensure success in business ventures.

Eating almonds will cure or combat fevers as well as give the partaker wisdom. Five almonds eaten before drinking prevents intoxication.

Magickal wands are made of almond wood, for it is a plant of Air, which is the elemental ruler of the magick wand, in some traditions.

Finally, placing almonds in your pocket will lead you to treasures.

Aphrodisiac Properties

The almond is the same shape as the vesica piscis, the sacred doorway through which matter emerges into spirit. As a nut, it carries the potential for new life, making it a symbol of feminine fertility.

Hope and the Vigilant Tree

As one of the first trees to break into flower in spring, the almond stands as a natural emblem of hope and fresh beginnings. It appears many times in scripture as a sign of watchfulness and promise, a fittingness captured in its Hebrew name, shaked, meaning vigilant. The shape of its blossom is said to have inspired the Menorah, the seven-branched candelabrum that stood upon the altar of the Temple in Jerusalem and remains a central emblem of the Jewish faith. The Hebrew word luz, another name for the almond, also names a fabled City of Immortals, reached through the roots of the almond tree.

It is also held that some good comes from the nut itself: beyond the five almonds said to ward off drunkenness, the nuts were prized in India as food for the brain, even though certain strains of wild almond carry the deadly prussic acid.

Phyllis and Everlasting Love

A Greek myth makes the almond a symbol of enduring and everlasting love. Phyllis, a beautiful Thracian queen, was wed to Demophon, who was called back to Athens upon the death of his father Theseus. When a month passed with no sign of his return, Phyllis died of a broken heart and was transformed into a bare and blossomless almond tree. When Demophon at last came back, racked with guilt, he went to the tree, and in that instant its naked branches burst into pink bloom, a sign that he was forgiven.

Safety Notes

⚠️ P. dulcis, Bitter Almond: Not for internal use. Contains cyanogenic glycosides.

Meaning When Gifted

When almond blossom is gifted it carries the meaning of hope, haste, watchfulness, virginity, and a happy marriage.
