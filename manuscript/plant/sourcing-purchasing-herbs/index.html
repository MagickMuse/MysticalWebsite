Plant Grimoire

Sourcing & Purchasing Herbs

Many magickal ingredients come from distant parts of the world. You cannot grow a sandalwood tree in most climates, and purchasing herbs does not lessen their power. The global herb trade makes otherwise unavailable plant materials accessible for magickal work.

Most larger cities have at least one herb shop or health food store stocking dried herbs. Online and mail-order suppliers offer botanicals from around the world.

Evaluating Essential Oils

Be cautious when buying essential oils. If someone claims a bottle is "real jasmine oil" at an extremely low price, it is almost certainly synthetic. Even products labeled "essential" are often laboratory creations rather than true plant extracts.

Price is a useful indicator. Most genuine essential oils sell for significantly more than synthetics. Chamomile, yarrow, cardamom, neroli, jasmine, and rose are among the costliest. If a price seems too good to be true, it probably is.

Synthetics have a long history in herbal practice, but true essential oils carry plant energy that synthetics cannot replicate.

Evaluating Dried Herbs

Many stores do not refresh their stock regularly, so the rosemary you buy may be years old. When selecting dried herbs, look for:

  • Bright colors rather than faded or dull appearance
  • Few stem pieces relative to leaves and flowers
  • Fresh, strong scent appropriate to the herb

Avoid herbs that are mostly stem, show discoloration, appear insect-damaged or moldy, or have little scent when they should be fragrant.

Mail ordering makes quality assessment harder. If a supplier sends inferior botanicals, do not order from them again. Remember that suppliers depend on growers, and obtaining top-quality herbs year-round can be difficult. Use what you can find while continuing to seek better sources.
