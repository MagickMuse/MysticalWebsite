Botanical Source

Origanum vulgare of the Lamiaceae (Labiatae) family. Also called European oregano, wild marjoram, common marjoram, grove marjoram, joy of the mountain, and origanum (as the oil). The plant is a hardy, bushy perennial herb growing up to 90 centimetres high, with an erect hairy stem, dark green ovate leaves, and pinky-purple flowers. A common garden plant releasing a strong aroma when its leaves are bruised.

Distribution

Native to Europe and now cultivated worldwide, including the USA, India, and South America. Essential oil production centres on the former USSR, Bulgaria, and Italy.

Other Species

The botanical classification of the marjoram and oregano species is famously tangled, with over thirty varieties recognised, several of which yield essential oils. These include winter or Greek marjoram (O. heracleoticum), African oregano (O. glandulosum), Moroccan oregano (O. virens), Mexican oregano (Lippia graveolens or L. palmeri), Syrian oregano (O. maru), and Spanish oregano (Thymus capitatus), which is the source of most commercial oregano oil.

Folk Medicine

The "true" oregano of the herb garden carries a very ancient medical reputation. Traditional uses include digestive upsets, respiratory complaints (asthma, bronchitis, coughs), colds and flu, and inflammations of the mouth and throat. In China it is also used for fever, vomiting, diarrhoea, jaundice, and itchy skin conditions. The diluted oil has been applied externally in herbal medicine for headaches, rheumatism, general aches and pains, and to stings and bites.

Therapeutic Actions

Analgesic, anthelmintic, antirheumatic, antiseptic, antispasmodic, antitoxic, antiviral, bactericidal, carminative, choleretic, cytophylactic, diaphoretic, diuretic, emmenagogue, expectorant, febrifuge, fungicidal, parasiticide, rubefacient, stimulant, and tonic.
