Elm (Ulmus spp.) is a deciduous tree with serrated leaves and distinctive winged seeds.

Magickal Properties

  • Gender: Feminine (Cold)
  • Planet: Saturn
  • Element: Earth (source) / Water (existing)
  • Powers: Knowledge, Protection, Love

Folk Names

Elven

Magickal Uses

For love: Elm wood is used for love spells. The elf-wood wands are most powerful when used in love enchantments.

For knowledge: Elm is used in workings to acquire and protect knowledge.

Winter Solstice: In Ireland this tree is a substitute for the silver fir as ruler of the Winter Solstice.

For Elves: Elm is strongly associated with elves and elven magick.

Geographic Distribution

Elm trees are widely distributed, from as far south as Mexico, to as far north as the Himalayas. One species, the Scotch Elm (Ulmus montana) is also known as the Wych Elm. In German, however, the entire species of elms may be classed as "Hexenulme" or Witch's Elm.

The Cranky Tree

The elm has a reputation as a cranky tree, allegedly dropping branches onto people's heads deliberately. Elms can be very tall and as such served as local landmarks. Meeting under an elm was an easy direction to follow because elms were frequently the tallest, most imposing trees around.

Witches allegedly danced around elms, particularly on May Eve.

Romany Magickal Tradition

Romany magickal tradition prizes the elm as a tree of particularly powerful enchantment. Romany magick wands are traditionally crafted from elm although the wood can never be cut but only received as a gift from the tree in the form of naturally fallen branches.

(Frustrated because there is no fallen branch? If the tree wants to work with you, a branch will be available, perhaps falling directly onto your head. If not, that's your signal to look elsewhere or to be patient.)

Death and the Realms of the Dead

Elms were associated with death and passage into the realms of the Dead. Spirit guardians of burial mounds were believed to make their homes in elms. The wood was once used to craft coffins.

The Goddess Tree

Once a lofty and dignified tree that lived four hundred years or more, the elm has been so reduced by Dutch Elm disease in both Britain and the United States that it is now reckoned fortunate to reach thirty. Its older symbolism is rich all the same: groves of elms were sacred to the Goddess, the Great Mother, and some held that the first woman herself was made from the elm.

Wedded to the Vine

So large and sheltering a tree was seen as a protective force, and the elm was planted among vineyards to shade and guard the vines, so that it came to be linked with Bacchus, the Roman god of wine; the vine and the elm were, in effect, wedded to one another.

Orpheus and the Liberty Tree

The first elm was said to have sprung up where Orpheus paused to play his love Eurydice a song. In later ages the tree's great size made it a landmark and a meeting place: the famous Liberty Tree in Boston, Massachusetts, was a colossal elm beneath which the Sons of Liberty gathered from 1766 onward. Since an unauthorized meeting was a punishable offence in the years of the American Revolution, it was an easy thing to gather hidden beneath so tall a tree, and soon each of the thirteen colonies had its own Liberty Tree, until the elm itself became synonymous with independence and liberty.
