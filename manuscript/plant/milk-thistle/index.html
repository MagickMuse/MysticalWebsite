Milk thistle (Silybum marianum) is a sacred Hellenic herb of protection, purification, and Marian devotion, distinguished by its striking white-mottled leaves.

History

Native to the Mediterranean, milk thistle was known in the ancient world across a range from North Africa through the Middle East. Pliny the Elder cited it as an ingredient in a tonic to soothe and heal the liver, recommending the juice mixed with honey to carry off bile. The plant also carries a connection to the Virgin Mary in Catholic tradition: legend holds that milk from Mary's breast fell upon the thistle while she nursed the infant Christ, imbuing the plant with sacred power and giving rise to its alternate names Our Lady's Thistle and Mary's Thistle. The plant is also believed to promote lactation.

Characteristics

Milk thistle is identified by its slender stalks, prickly leaves mottled with white markings, and distinctive purple flowers rising from a round green base with spiny protrusions. The feathery purple petals bear white veins. Medicinal extracts are derived from the plant's seeds.

Magickal Properties

Gender: Feminine

Powers: Protection, Purification, Blessings, Hex Breaking

Magickal Uses

  • Protection and Purification: Milk thistle is used in protective and purifying workings. Placing a bowl of milk thistle in a room is said to cleanse and improve the energy of the space.
  • Hex Breaking: Stuffing a charm or poppet with milk thistle serves as a method for counteracting a hex or curse.
  • Blessings: The herb is used to bestow blessings, drawing on its sacred Marian associations.
