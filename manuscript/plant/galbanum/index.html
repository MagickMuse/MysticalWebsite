Galbanum (Ferula galbaniflua) is a large perennial herb of the Apiaceae family whose stems exude a fragrant oleoresin used as incense, fixative, and aromatic medicine since antiquity.

Botanical Information

  • Scientific name: Ferula galbaniflua (also classified as F. gummosa)
  • Family: Apiaceae (Umbelliferae)
  • Type: Large perennial herb
  • Description: Smooth-stemmed plant with shiny leaflets and small flowers. The stem contains resin ducts that exude a milky natural oleoresin; the dried resinous exudation is harvested by making incisions at the base of the stem.

Distribution

Native to the Middle East and western Asia. Cultivated in Iran, Turkey, Afghanistan, and Lebanon. Distillation of the oil usually takes place in Europe or the USA.

Varieties

Galbanum exists in two distinct commercial forms: Levant galbanum, which is liquid or soft and used for essential oil production, and Persian galbanum, which is solid or hard and historically used for pharmaceutical work and as resinoid fixative. Other Ferula species yield galbanum-like gum, including the muskroot. The closely related Zalou root (F. hermonic) is used in Beirut as an aphrodisiac.

Folk Medicine

Used by ancient civilisations as a ceremonial incense, in Egyptian cosmetics, and in the embalming process. Eastern herbalism employs galbanum in much the same way as asafetida: for wounds, inflammations, and skin disorders, and for respiratory, digestive, and nervous complaints.

Therapeutic Actions

Analgesic, anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, antiseptic, antispasmodic, aphrodisiac, balsamic, carminative, cicatrisant, digestive, diuretic, emmenagogue, expectorant, hypotensive, restorative, and tonic.
