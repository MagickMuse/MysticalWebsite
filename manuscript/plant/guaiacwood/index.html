Guaiacwood Essential Oil

Guaiacwood (Bulnesia sarmientoi) is a small, wild tropical tree native to South America, prized for the tearose-scented essential oil distilled from its hard, decorative wood.

Botanical Description

A small, wild tropical tree up to 4 metres high, with a decorative hard wood.

Origin & Distribution

Native to South America, especially Paraguay and Argentina. Some oil is distilled in Europe and the USA.

Related Species

Guaiacwood is distinct from guaiac gum and guaiac resin (collectively known as guaiacum), which are obtained from the related trees Guaiacum officinale and G. sanctum. The products are somewhat similar and share common properties, but they come from different trees.

Disambiguation

The Spanish synonym 'palo santo' is sometimes applied to this tree, but it should not be confused with true Palo Santo (Bursera graveolens, family Burseraceae), a different tree native to Central and South America with its own distinct aromatic and ceremonial tradition. See Palo Santo for that entry.

Folk Tradition

The wood is much used for ornamental carving. It was formerly used for treating rheumatism and gout. Guaiacum (from the related Guaiacum trees) is still current in the British Herbal Pharmacopoeia as a specific for rheumatism and rheumatoid arthritis. Valnet includes guaiacum in his 'elixirs' for gout, venereal disease, and in mouthwashes.
