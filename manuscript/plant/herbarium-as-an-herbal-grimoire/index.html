Plant Grimoire

Herbarium as an Herbal Grimoire

A magickal herbarium functions as a personal grimoire, serving as both a record and a guide to your hands-on experience with plant allies. Whether you wildcraft, grow your own herbs, or source them fresh, pressing and mounting specimens creates a tangible archive that outlasts photographs or drawings. A well-maintained herbarium can last for years and documents not just identification but your lived, working relationship with each plant. This project can be personal or collective, with covens compiling a shared volume of their combined knowledge.

Gathering Your Specimens

Supplies

  • Field notebook and pen
  • Camera
  • Garden snips, scissors, or shears
  • Clean sheets of paper
  • Heavy book (dictionary, textbook, or telephone book)
  • Gloves
  • Backpack

Process

  1. Go out into the field, or work in your garden, with all supplies in hand. For each specimen, note the date, time, and location in your notebook. Photograph the whole plant within its environment. Collect as much of the aerial portions as the current season allows: leaves, stem, flowers, and fruit. Because a plant's appearance shifts significantly across its life cycle, recording the date gives your entries context and accuracy.
  2. After cutting, layer the herbs between two clean sheets of paper and press them inside your heavy book. This begins the drying and pressing process. If you are foraging and handling unfamiliar plants, keep gloves on throughout. Continue collecting until you have a meaningful start to your collection.

Drying, Mounting, and Organizing

Additional Supplies

  • Data sheet template
  • 2 wood blocks (two-by-fours work well)
  • 2 bricks
  • Clear contact paper
  • Clear plastic sheet protectors
  • Three-ring binder

Process

  1. Back home, begin the work of identifying, labeling, and finishing the press. Handle everything carefully at this stage.
  2. Mark a corner of each drying sheet with a sequential letter. Fill out a matching data sheet for that specimen using the same letter as its identifier. Each data sheet should capture:
  • Type of herb
  • Date, time, and location gathered
  • Moon phase at time of gathering
  • Physical description at gathering
  • Uses and parts used
  1. Move the pressed herbs from the book and transfer them between two wood blocks, then weigh the blocks down with bricks to finish the pressing. A commercial flower press is an equally valid option. Budget up to a week for thorough drying.
  2. Once fully dried, transfer each specimen carefully onto clear contact paper, drying sheet included if it has not stained. Lay the contact paper over slowly and smooth out any air pockets. Trim the edges and slide the finished specimen into a sheet protector. This goes on the right-hand side of your binder. The left side holds a second sheet protector with the corresponding data sheet inside.

Organizing Your Herbarium

The binder format gives you full control over organization. Sort alphabetically, by elemental association, by magickal use, by season, or any other logic that supports your practice. Whichever system you choose, an herbarium ranks among the most meaningful grimoires a practitioner can build: a physical record of the plant world woven directly into your spiritual work.
