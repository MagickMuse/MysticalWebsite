From the Source Book

The hollyhock was brought to the United States from China and was once used as a pot herb, though it doesn't taste very good. The flowers were used as a dye when gathered in July and early August — when dried, the blossoms turn a deep purplish-black.

Medicinal uses (historical reference only):

  • Diuretic: Used medicinally for diuretic properties.
  • Mouthwash/Gums: Flowers used in infusions as a mouthwash and to treat disorders of the gums.
  • Children's worms: Root dried, ground to a powder, then boiled in wine.
  • Tonsils/Sore mouths: Leaves used as a gargle for swollen tonsils and sore mouths.

Meaning When Gifted

When hollyhock is gifted it carries the meaning of fruitfulness.
