Magickal Properties

Powers: Protection

Magickal Uses

Leaves of this plant were placed at the four quarters of a dwelling to protect it from black magick.

Boldo (Peumus boldus) is an aromatic Chilean evergreen whose dried leaves are one of South America's most respected liver and gall bladder herbs.

Characteristics

An evergreen shrub or small tree growing up to 6 metres tall, with slender branches and sessile, coarse leaves that turn a deep reddish-brown when dried. The plant bears yellowish-green fruit, and the whole plant is fragrant.

Origin & Distribution

Native to Chile and naturalized across the Mediterranean. Small amounts of essential oil are also produced in Nepal and Vietnam.

Related Species

The Australian tree Monimia rotundifolia yields a chemically similar oil and is sometimes used as a substitute. Boldo oil is also chemically related to chenopodium (wormseed) oil.

Folk Tradition

In Chile and across South America, boldo has been used for centuries:

  • The bark is used for tanning hides
  • The wood is burned for charcoal
  • The fruit is eaten fresh by locals
  • The dried leaves have long been recognised as a remedy for gonorrhoea

In Western herbalism, the dried leaves are taken for genito-urinary inflammation, gallstones, liver and gall bladder pain, cystitis, and rheumatism. The British Herbal Pharmacopoeia lists boldo leaf as a specific remedy for cholelithiasis with pain (gallstones with associated discomfort).

Safety

The dried leaves are used cautiously in traditional herbalism, but the Boldo Leaf Essential Oil is extremely toxic and should never be used in home aromatherapy or skincare.
