Botanical Information

Melissa officinalis (Lamiaceae family). Feminine flowering herb and leaf originating from Mediterranean and Europe. Also known as Bee Balm, Melissa, Sweet Melissa, Lemon Balsam, Tourengane, and Oghoul. Latin binomial melissa means honeybee, reflecting bees' profound preference for this plant.

History

Lemon balm's virtues have been documented across centuries of herbal scholarship. Ancient physicians and later Renaissance herbalists alike recorded its capacity to elevate mood, ease digestive complaints, and relieve toothaches. Among its notable champions was Hildegard von Bingen, the pioneering medieval herbalist who catalogued its many uses alongside other renowned writers of the era.

Characteristics

A perennial member of the mint family, lemon balm produces a pronounced citrus fragrance and flavor suited to culinary applications, infusions, and herbal teas. Native to the Mediterranean, it is now cultivated throughout the world. Also known as bee balm, the plant bears wrinkled leaves and tiny white flowers that may carry a faint pale yellow tone.

Botanical Description

Lemon balm is a sweet-scented herb that reaches about sixty centimetres in height, soft and bushy, with bright green serrated leaves, square stems, and tiny white or pink flowers. It is native to the Mediterranean and now grows widely across Europe, Middle Asia, North America, North Africa, and Siberia. Most commercial cultivation is rooted in France, Spain, Germany, and Russia.

Related Species

Several varieties exist, including a variegated-leaf type that is common in gardens.

Folk Tradition

Lemon balm is one of the earliest known medicinal herbs and was famously called the Elixir of Life by Paracelsus. It has long been associated with nervous disorders, the heart, and the emotions, taken for anxiety, melancholy, and to strengthen and revive the vital spirit. The herb is generally employed for digestive complaints and conditions of nervous origin such as asthma, indigestion, and flatulence, and it is reached for to help regulate the menstrual cycle and promote fertility. It is an effective remedy for wasp and bee stings. In France the leaves are still used heavily in pharmaceutical and herbal products, and the British Herbal Pharmacopoeia carries lemon balm for flatulent dyspepsia, neurasthenia, and depressive illness.

Herbal Lore

So beloved by bees that its name means honeybee. As bees constantly work, lemon balm never stops working for the practitioner wielding it. Purifying qualities so renowned it once scented wooden furniture for purification—modern lemon-scented wood polishes derive from this tradition and the aromatic principle that lemon equals clean.

Though prolific mint family member, only small white blossoms create the prized essential oil.

Magickal Uses

Lemon balm is a gentle herb of great healing power, particularly skilled at treating emotional wounds, and soothes with a compassionate, continuously working energy. It brings success, business acumen, and wealth in the manner of citrus herbs; used in new business ventures it draws steady custom, and its traditional purification associations make it well suited to blessing business spaces. Its uplifting nature encourages happiness and joy. Lemon balm is associated with fertility magick and conception work, used in dried herb and fresh flower charms and candles for that purpose.

As a lunar herb, burning dried lemon balm on incense charcoal aids in seeking audience with Artemis, Diana, Selene, or Hecate. It is used in love magick to attract a compatible partner and to mend the emotional wounds of a broken heart; carrying a sprig in the pocket draws a new lover. Lemon balm serves as a capable sedative for peace magick, banishing anxiety, grounding excitable emotions, and calmly banishing depression. It enhances ordered thinking and focus for mastering new skills. It teaches quick grounding and centering, making magickal practice more effortless and deliberate.

Lemon balm facilitates mental and emotional development and supports writing improvement lists in a journal or Book of Shadows, burned under candles for manifestation. It unlocks memories from previous lives and creates a safe space for exploring past incarnations during meditation. The herb gently encourages analysis of weighted memories and emotional trauma, proceeding with care as memories have sharp edges; it bolsters during anxiety and stress and complements therapeutic recovery work. It supports sleep magick and deep meditation, and aids partners in intimacy through anxiety and depression relief. For headaches and focus problems it acts as a talisman warding off future mental difficulties. Lemon Balm is also associated with good luck and fortune.

Magickal Teas

Lemon balm can be brewed as a magickal tea to promote health, draw love, and encourage success. Drink 1 to 2 cups only during the spell event itself.

Medicinal Uses

Lemon balm's primary medicinal quality is its calming effect through scent and ingestion. The leaves, when used aromatically or brewed into a tea, produce a gentle sedative effect that soothes anxiety, eases restlessness, and supports sleep. In herbal preparations, lemon balm is also notable for its synergistic quality: it is known to amplify and round out the effects of other herbs it is combined with, making it a valuable supporting ingredient in blended remedies. Its gentle nature makes it suitable for regular use in adults and children alike.

Parts used: leaves. Application: aromatic; internal as tea or tincture.

Seasonal Associations

Lemon balm is a summer herb aligned with Litha and Lughnasadh, carrying the magickal principle of culmination and manifesting.
