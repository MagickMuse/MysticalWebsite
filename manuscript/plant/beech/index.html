Beech (Fagus sylvatica) is a deciduous tree with smooth gray bark and edible nuts.

Characteristics

Beech woods are shady, magical places, though the trees are most often used today for hedging; the Meikleour beech hedge in Scotland, one of the tallest in the world, stands over a hundred feet high. The tree bears its nuts three at a time within an outer husk, and while they are edible they are most commonly used as animal feed. In medieval Europe pigs were turned loose in the forest to feed on the "mast," the old collective name for the beech's nuts. Like the ash, the beech often plays "queen" to the oak's "king."

Folk Tradition

Like the birch, the beech is bound up with written communication, and thin slices of its bark were once used as paper. Its name, boc in Anglo-Saxon, springs from the same root as "book," a kinship echoed in German and Swedish. It was an old custom to write a spell or magickal charm on a sliver of beech wood, then bury it in the ground and wait for the desired thing or situation to manifest itself. Ancient runic tablets were also carved from beech wood, making it one of the earliest materials for inscribed wisdom. The Franks used the beech as an oracle, listening to the wind moving through its leaves as a form of divination.

Magickal Properties

Powers: Wishes

Magickal Uses

Take a stick of beech, scratch or carve your wishes onto it, bury it in the ground and leave it there. Your wish will come true if it is to be.

Carry the wood or leaves to increase creative powers.

⚠️ Safety Note: Large amounts of beech nuts are not recommended for internal use.

Meaning When Gifted

When beech is gifted it carries the meaning of prosperity.
