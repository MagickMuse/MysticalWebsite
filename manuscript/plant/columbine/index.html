Columbine (Aquilegia vulgaris) is a graceful perennial with distinctive spurred flowers in various colors.

Magickal Properties

  • Powers: Courage, Love

Folk Names

Lion's Herb, Granny's Bonnet

Magickal Uses

For courage: Despite its delicate appearance, columbine is associated with courage and determination. The name "lion's herb" reflects this connection. Carry columbine to instill bravery and resolve.

For love: Columbine is used in love spells and sachets. The flowers are given as gifts to express affection and devotion.

The Eagle and the Dove

The columbine carries within it two birds of opposing natures. Its first name came from the Latin columba, meaning dove, since the flower's spurred nectaries resemble a ring of doves gathered head to head; its older English name, culverwort, likewise traces to words for a gathering of pigeons. Latin monks later renamed it aquilegia, from aquila, the eagle, because the curled spurs at the back of the petals call to mind an eagle's talons.

The two birds sit at odds. The eagle is flamboyant, a creature of kings, emperors, and worldly power. The dove is humble, a sign of peace and love, a quiet symbol that balances the eagle's boldness. In religious painting the columbine sometimes stands in secret for the dove, and through it for the Holy Spirit.

Magickal Symbolism

The columbine is an ethereal, intricate bloom long linked to magickal power, said to hold hidden secrets within the elaborate folds and ruffles of its petals. Its five petals form a naturally occurring pentagram, which earned it a reputation both as a flower that wards off evil and as a flower of witches. It is also pointed to as an example of the golden mean appearing in the natural world.

The plant has a medicinal side as well. It was held to be a powerful antitoxin and an aphrodisiac, and its seeds, tucked beneath the mattress, were said to cure infertility. So plainly was the flower tied to sex that it was thought improper for a man to offer a bouquet of columbine, the message being far too overt.

The aquilegia is dedicated to the Norse goddess Freya.

Caution

The seeds of columbine are poisonous and should not be ingested.

Meaning When Gifted

When columbine is gifted it carries the meaning of folly.
