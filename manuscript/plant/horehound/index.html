Horehound (Marrubium vulgare) is a bright, clean herb for protection, clarity, and banishing.

History

John Gerard noted horehound more than four hundred years ago in his Generall Historie of Plantes, praising its value as both a tonic and a topical remedy. Over the centuries, horehound has been employed in folk medicine to treat a wide range of ailments, from tuberculosis to asthma.

Characteristics

A member of the mint family, horehound is valued for its leaves and flowering tops, which have been used in folk medicine and culinary craft alike. It appears in cough drops and confections for its expectorant properties. The herb carries a pleasant taste and an appealing fragrance.

Ritual Uses

Burn to Horus, the ancient Egyptian God for whom the plant was named. Highly praised by the ancient Egyptians and Romans.

Cough tea: Brew into a strong tea for a persistent cough.

Magickal Uses

  • Protection and exorcism: Used in protective sachets; scattered to break and drive out enchantment and fascination.
  • Clarity: Drink an infusion to clear the mind and strengthen mental powers.
  • Bedside healing: Place horehound beside the patient's bed to assist healing. The botanical derives its name from Horus, Isis' son, and was highly praised by the ancient Egyptians and Romans for its healing power.
  • Grounding and centering: Horehound is a stabilizing herb well suited for grounding rituals or for bringing a practitioner back to center during intense workings.
  • Amplify scrying: Incorporate horehound into scrying practice to heighten and sharpen the working.
  • Crystal charging and purification: Fill a sachet with the dried flowers and leaves and use it to charge and cleanse crystals.
