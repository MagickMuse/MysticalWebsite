Lettuce (Lactuca sativa) cools passions, guards the home, and brings sleep.

Magickal Properties

Gender: Feminine (source: Cold)

Deities: Isis, Khonsu, Kuan Yin, Neith, Diana, Hecate, Selene/Luna, Morrigan

Powers: Psychic Vision, Prophecy, Divination, Chastity, Protection, Love Divination, Sleep

Magickal Uses

  • Sleep: Rub lettuce juice on the forehead or eat leaves to fall asleep easily.
  • Protection: Grown in the garden it protects the household; folklore warns that raising too much may cause sterility in the home.
  • Chastity and seasickness: Eat lettuce to preserve chastity and to prevent seasickness.
  • Love omen: Plant lettuce or cress seeds in the form of a loved one’s name; good sprouting is an auspicious sign for love.

From the Source Book (Wild Lettuce)

Hypnotic, narcotic, sedative — not safe unless you are a qualified herbalist. Used as a tincture or smoked. Produces a dream or trance state. Enhances the vividness of dreams.

Wild Lettuce (Opium Lettuce)

Wild lettuce (Lactuca virosa) is the "poor man's opium" of European folk herbalism, a mild sedative and vision-inducing herb cited among the ingredients of the traditional witch's flying ointment.

Magickal Uses

  • Sleep and prophetic dreams: Eaten before sleep or used in dream sachets and pillows to encourage restful sleep and vivid dreaming.
  • Chastity: Historically used to dampen sexual desire; associated with the Moon and cooling, watery energies.
  • Love divination: Carrying or burning lettuce seed in folk tradition was said to reveal whether a lover was faithful.
  • Protection: Grown in the garden or placed in the home as a protective herb.

The Witch's Flying Ointment

Wild lettuce is among the plants cited in historical flying ointment formulas from the 1500s and 1600s. Its milky latex sap — lactucarium — contains lactucin and lactucopicrin, compounds with mild sedative, analgesic, and mildly psychoactive properties, earning it the folk name "opium lettuce." In a topical preparation, lactucarium would have contributed a sedative and visionary quality, helping to ease the practitioner into the altered states associated with spirit flight.

Sacred to Min and Adonis

In ancient Egypt, wild lettuce was sacred to Min, the ithyphallic god of fertility and male sexual potency. Lettuce was grown in temple gardens as an offering to him, and its milky sap was associated with semen and generative power. In Greek tradition, Adonis was said to have been laid to rest among lettuce plants after his death, giving the herb an association with death and the underworld.

Botanical Description

Wild lettuce is a tall, branching biennial that can reach over 1.5 metres in height. Its leaves are prickly along the midrib, and the plant exudes a bitter white latex sap when cut. Small pale yellow flowers bloom in summer. It grows as a weed in waste ground, roadsides, and disturbed habitats throughout Europe and Western Asia. Cultivated lettuce (L. sativa) is a domesticated descendant that has lost most of the wild species' medicinal potency.
