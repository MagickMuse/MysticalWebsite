Broom (Cytisus scoparius) is a shrub with bright yellow flowers and thin green branches.

History

Broom became a fashionable garden plant in the mid-19th century and remained popular well into the 20th. Beyond its ornamental appeal, it had practical uses: the flowering tops flavored certain ales and were used in the tanning of leather. By the mid-20th century its reputation had grown more complicated, with some sources noting an intoxicant quality to certain preparations, though the plant's toxicity made any internal use genuinely dangerous.

Characteristics

Broom is a dense woody shrub that erupts in bright yellow flowers along vivid green stems. It grows wild along the Atlantic Coast and in parts of the Pacific Northwest, favoring full sunlight and tolerating a wide range of soil conditions. Highly ornamental but invasive in many regions, it spreads readily and produces seeds that can remain viable in the soil for years. Ornamental cultivars have been developed in shades of cream, red, and bicolor.

Name & Heraldry

The broom's long, straight branches and their many parallel shoots made the shrub a natural tool for sweeping, a use stretching back thousands of years and the origin of the word broomstick. Its Latin name, planta genista, gave rise to the royal house of Plantagenet, whose kings took the broom as their emblem.

Magickal Uses

For purification and protection: Broom is used in purification and protection spells, and is hung in the home to keep evil out. Also, an infusion of broom sprinkled through the house exorcises poltergeists.

For psychic powers: Although the infusion was once used as a drink to increase psychic powers, this can be dangerous because the plant is slightly poisonous; carry instead for this purpose.

For wind magic: To raise the winds, throw broom into the air while invoking the spirits of the Air, preferably from a mountaintop. To calm the winds, burn broom and bury the ashes.

For ritual preparation: If you do outdoor workings, sweeping the ground with broom prior to your ritual cleanses and prepares the space, if it grows nearby.

Druidic tradition: The Druids considered broom to be a tree rather than a shrub. It rules the Spring Equinox and can be substituted for furze in that role. Sacred to the Sun, the Irish called it the "physician's strength" — its diuretic shoots were a prized remedy for all surfeits and diseases of the urinary tract and kidneys. Its bright yellow flowers symbolize the young Sun of the Spring Equinox.

For aura awareness and psychic sight: Broom is associated with the ability to perceive the aura and develop psychic sensitivity. Burning the branches as incense or using an infusion of the flowering tops as an anointing oil or asperge is said to open the inner senses and support chakra awareness.

⚠️ Danger: This plant is poisonous and should not be taken internally.

Meaning When Gifted

When broom is gifted it carries the meaning of humility and neatness.
