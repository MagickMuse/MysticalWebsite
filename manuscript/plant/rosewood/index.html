Rosewood (Aniba rosaeodora) is the medium-sized tropical evergreen of the Lauraceae family whose wood chippings yield rosewood essential oil, a once-major source of natural linalol. The name 'rosewood' is also applied to several unrelated timber species; the essential oil is only distilled from this Amazonian Aniba.

Characteristics

A medium-sized, tropical, evergreen tree with reddish bark and heartwood, bearing yellow flowers. Used extensively for timber.

Origin & Distribution

Native to the Amazon region. Brazil and Peru are the main producers of rosewood oil.

Related Species

There are several species of timber all known as rosewood; however, the essential oil is only distilled from Aniba rosaeodora. French Guiana used to produce cayenne rosewood (Ocotea caudata), which is superior in quality to the Peruvian or Brazilian type. The South American Dalbergia rosewoods (notably Dalbergia nigra, Brazilian rosewood or jacaranda) are unrelated cabinet-making timbers from the Fabaceae family and are not used to produce essential oil.

Folk Tradition

Rosewood is used for building, carving, and French cabinet making. Most rosewood today goes to Japan for the production of chopsticks. Recorded actions of the oil include mild analgesic, anticonvulsant, antidepressant, antimicrobial, antiseptic, aphrodisiac, bactericidal, cellular stimulant, cephalic, deodorant, immune-system stimulant, tissue regenerator, and tonic.

Sustainability

Aniba rosaeodora is one of the trees being extensively felled in the clearing of the South American rainforests. Continual production of rosewood oil is consequently environmentally damaging, and natural linalol from rosewood is increasingly being replaced by the synthetic form.
