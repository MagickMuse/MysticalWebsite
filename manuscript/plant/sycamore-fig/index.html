The sycamore fig (Ficus sycomorus) is the tree the Egyptians honoured as their World Tree, and it should not be confused with the plane tree or the sycamore maple that share the name in other lands. It grew in profusion along the banks of the Nile, where it was known as "Pharaoh's fig," and like other World Trees it was believed that the birds perched in its branches were the souls of beings not yet born.

Folk Tradition

The Gods themselves were said to sit among its boughs, and the tree was held to be the living form of the great Goddesses Isis, Nut, and Hathor, the last of whom was honoured as the Lady of the Sycamore. The Egyptians believed that a sycamore stood on either side of the Eastern Gate of Heaven, through which the Sun God Ra passed each morning.

Because the World Tree binds together the Earth, the Heavens, and the Underworld, the sycamore was planted beside tombs, where it carried the same regenerative promise that the yew holds in the Western tradition. Coffins were fashioned from its timber, for the Egyptians believed the wood would return the body to the Mother Goddess, Hathor.
