Angelica (Angelica archangelica) is a powerful protective plant known as "Root of the Holy Ghost" with deep connections to angelic and divine protection.

History

Native to Europe, angelica has been cultivated since ancient times for its roots, leaves, and seeds, with recorded use going back to the tenth century as an aromatic, a medicinal, and a food source.

Characteristics

Angelica is an aromatic relative of parsley, recognizable by its dark green serrated leaves and spherical flower clusters rising from a single long stem. It is also related to the carrot, and its root structure reflects this, capable of reaching depths of up to ten feet. The plant is a carminative, a diuretic, and an expectorant. Roots and leaves are used in tinctures, though the fresh root carries toxic properties and must be thoroughly dried before being decocted. The essential oil has been applied topically to address joint inflammation, and the leaves are used both as a topical treatment and brewed into tea. Due to its potency, angelica is best avoided by those who are pregnant or by children.

Folk Tradition

Angelica has been praised for its virtues since antiquity. European tradition treats the herb as a heart strengthener and a stimulant for circulation and the immune system, and has reached for it for centuries to support bronchial ailments, colds, coughs, indigestion, wind, and a flagging appetite. As a urinary antiseptic it has been used for cystitis, and it has been applied for rheumatic inflammation. The British Herbal Pharmacopoeia carries angelica as a specific remedy for bronchitis associated with vascular deficiency. Over thirty species of angelica exist, and Chinese medicine alone employs at least ten of them, valued for promoting fertility, fortifying the spirit, and treating female disorders generally, with a reputation said to be second only to ginseng. Candied angelica stalks remain a familiar treat in France and Spain.

Additional Notes

If you have space for only one plant in the garden, plant the Angelica. —Harald Tietze, Herbal Teaology

It is suggested to grow angelica around your home to protect it, as one of the original epithets of the plant was "Root of the Holy Ghost." According to the herbalist Maud Grieve, author of the 1931 book A Modern Herbal, it was believed that angelica could cure the plague, after a monk saw it in a dream. It is said to protect from evil spirits and enchantment.

As indicated by its Latin binomial, angelica's heavenly connection has influenced its place in history for hundreds of years. It was dreamt as a cure for the plague by a monk who claimed he was given the plant by an angel. This supposed plague cure turned out to be correct, divine connection or no, because angelica has antibacterial properties. At the time, only two main areas remained unaffected by plague: Poland, because it gave asylum to the Jews, who had religious obligations toward bathing and therefore the bacterial growth had no grounds; and Milan, where if someone showed plague symptoms the house was burned down with the entire family inside.

Angelica is said to have sprouted from the place where the Archangel Michael first set foot on Earth from the heavens. Thus, it has a long history of protection, both from evil spirits and more down-to-earth mishaps.

The Balance of Protection

It is so protective that some warn it can protect you from good opportunities as well as keep you from any harm that may befall you. Emotional shielding can keep out dangers as well as potential loved ones. Magickal shielding can prevent beneficial energies as well as baneful ones. Protection should be encouraged and understood, without excess. There is an important line between protection and paranoia. Make sure that your interest in remaining safe does not guard you from beautiful experiences as well as horrifying ones.

Magickal Uses

For protection: Grown, the plant is protective. Use in all protection and exorcism incenses. Sprinkle the four corners of the house with angelica to ward off evil, or do this around the perimeter of the house.

For curse removal: Added to the bath, angelica removes curses, hexes, and any spells that may have been cast against you.

For gambling luck: The root was carried in the pocket as a gambling talisman among some American Indian tribes.

⚠️
Closed practice: Some accounts describe certain Native American peoples burning angelica during healing rites. These are closed Indigenous practices, offered here as cultural context rather than something to take up.

For healing: Angelica is used in healing incenses and mixtures.

For visions: Smoking the leaves is said to cause visions.

To bind someone from doing evil: Write the person's name on a piece of paper nine times, and take a pinch of angelica and a pinch of mandrake root and place them in the center of the paper. Bind it closed with twine or ribbon. Bury (or dispose of) the paper off your property.

Epidemic & Plague Use

Angelica is a primary weapon against epidemics of infectious disease. A large percentage of anti-epidemic spells feature it. Its common name derives from legend: during a terrible plague, an angel appeared to a monk in a dream and revealed this plant as the cure. It worked, and the root was named angelica — archangel root — in the angel's honor. General consensus credits Archangel Michael, though some attribute the vision to Raphael.

Angelica root may be added to Four Thieves Vinegar (in addition to the four standard thief ingredients) especially during genuine epidemic. It may also be carried as an amulet to promote healing and preserve health.

⚠️ Do not use wildcrafted angelica — it closely resembles a species of hemlock and mistakes have been made. Harvest only from a home garden where its identity is certain, or purchase from reputable vendors.

Culinary Uses

Parts used: Roots and stems

Angelica is used as a flavoring agent, particularly in candy and confections.
