Onion (Allium cepa) is a household staple of protection, exorcism, healing, money, dreams, and lust.

The Onion and Eternity

The onion was thought so powerful, as symbol and as plant alike, that Egypt once held a cult devoted to its worship and cultivation. Its circle-within-a-circle structure made it an emblem of eternity.

The Great Big Onion

In the Dharmic religions, Hindu and Buddhist alike, the onion offers a lesson in the ego: its layers peel away one by one until nothing remains at the center, so that at last there is no barrier between the self and the spirit world, and all is One. Marvin Gaye caught the same thought in the opening line of his Onion Song, "The world is just a great big onion."

Soldier's Charm and Witch's Bane

Roman soldiers carried onions for their power to ward off disease, and the plant guarded against danger in other ways too. An onion held in the hand was said to turn away snakes. Witches were believed to dislike the onion, so it was kept in the house to drive off malicious spirits and worn as a protective charm by those who did not mind the smell.

Magickal Uses

  • Window ward: Stick a small white onion full of black‑headed pins and set in a window to bar evil.
  • Household guardians: Dried flower heads are decorative protectors; onions grown in pots or garden guard the home and its people.
  • Protective blossoms: Onion flowers rise on tall, spear-like stalks that put the plant's protective power on display. Growing onions around the home forms a magickal barrier against harm.
  • Disease absorber: Halved or quartered onions are placed in rooms to absorb negativity, evil, and disease.
  • Draw out illness: Rub a cut onion on the afflicted area while visualizing the sickness leaving, then destroy the onion.
  • Colonial practice: Strings of onions hung over doorways to guard against infection; a cut onion under the kitchen sink for the same purpose.
  • Warts: Rub with onion and throw over the right shoulder; walk away without looking back.
  • Bed charm: A large red onion tied to the bedpost protects sleepers and aids recuperation.
  • Prosperity: Never throw onion skins on the ground; burn them to attract riches.
  • Dream oracle: Place beneath the pillow for prophetic dreams. Decision spell: carve each choice onto separate onions, place in the dark; the first to sprout is your answer.
  • Lust: Some authorities say eating onions “provokes to venery.”
  • Tool cleansing: Rub magical blades with cut onion to purify; throwing an onion after a bride was said to banish her tears.

Botanical Source

Allium cepa of the Liliaceae family, also called common onion or Strasburg onion. The plant is a perennial or biennial herb growing up to 1.2 metres high, carrying hollow leaves and flowering stems above a globelike fleshy bulb.

Distribution

Native to western Asia and the Middle East, with a long history of cultivation around the world, primarily for culinary use. Essential oil production centres on France, Germany, and Egypt, working chiefly with the "red" onion.

Other Species

Many cultivated forms of onion exist, including the Spanish or silver-skinned onion, the Tripoli, and the red onion.

Folk Medicine

An ancient curative agent praised by the schools of Galen and Hippocrates. High in vitamins A, B, and C and sharing many properties with its close relative garlic. Raw onion is credited with warding off colds and infections, supporting strong bones, promoting tissue circulation, and acting as a blood cleanser; the sulphur it contains is also said to keep the skin clear and well-conditioned. A reputation follows it for correcting glandular imbalance and weight problems and for improving lymphatic drainage often implicated in oedema and puffiness.

Used as a home "simple" across many conditions: as a poultice for hard tumours, for relief in suppressed gout or obstructed circulation, to soothe the nerves and induce sleep, to stimulate skin action and clear obstructions of the viscera, applied bruised to burns and scalds when no other remedy was at hand, and laid on the sting or bite of poisonous insects.

Therapeutic Actions

Anthelmintic, antimicrobial, antirheumatic, antiseptic, antisclerotic, antispasmodic, antiviral, bactericidal, carminative, depurative, digestive, diuretic, expectorant, fungicidal, hypocholesterolaemic, hypoglycaemic, hypotensive, stomachic, tonic, and vermifuge.
