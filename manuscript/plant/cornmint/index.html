Cornmint Essential Oil

A rather fragile herb of the Lamiaceae family with leafy stems up to 60 cm high, lance-shaped leaves, and lilac-coloured flowers borne in clustered whorls in the axils of the upper leaves. Cornmint is grown primarily for industrial menthol isolation and is the largest commercial source of natural menthol in the world.

Origin & Distribution

Native to Europe and parts of Asia, including Japan and China; naturalized in North America. Major producers of the oil include China, Brazil, Argentina, India, and Vietnam.

Folk Tradition (Medicinal)

Used therapeutically in many of the same ways as Peppermint; the bruised leaves are applied to the forehead to relieve nervous headache. In the East it is used to treat rheumatic pain, neuralgia, toothache, laryngitis, indigestion, colds, and bronchitis. In Chinese medicine, it is also employed for relieving earache, treating tumours, and some skin conditions.

Other Species

There are many varieties and chemotypes of this herb, which is used for large-scale oil production, including the Chinese type M. arvensis var. glabrata and the Japanese species M. arvensis var. piperascens.

Related Species

Within the Mentha genus, see also Peppermint (M. piperita), Spearmint (M. spicata), Bergamot Mint, and the broader Mint entry. The unrelated genus Calamintha is covered under Calamint.
