Sunflower is a powerful solar herb associated with fertility, wishes, health, and wisdom. These magnificent flowers that follow the sun across the sky carry strong masculine energy and are particularly potent for conception magick and truth-seeking. The seeds hold special protective powers against disease and negative energy.

Source Entry Notes

Source deities added: Apollo, Bast, Horus, Ra, Adonis.

Source powers: Protection, Fertility.

Source — Specific Uses

Flowers grown in the garden bring blessings of the sun and attract good luck, safety, and protection. Seeds are often eaten by women wanting to conceive, done during the waxing moon.

Magickal Uses

  • Fertility Enhancement: Sunflower seeds eaten by women who wish to conceive, promoting fertility and vigorous health in old age.
  • Disease Protection: Seeds worn around the neck (in bags or strung as beads) protect against smallpox and other illnesses.
  • Wish Magick: Cut a sunflower at sunset while making a wish - the wish will come true before another sunset, provided it isn't too grand or unrealistic.
  • Truth Seeking: Sleeping with a sunflower under the bed allows you to know the truth in any matter, revealing deception and hidden knowledge.
  • Virtue Enhancement: Anoint yourself with juice pressed from sunflower stems to become virtuous and morally upright.
  • Garden Protection: Sunflowers growing in the garden guard against pests and grant the best of luck to the gardener.

The sunflower's natural tendency to track the sun's movement makes it especially powerful for solar magick and following truth.

Bring sunflowers to celebrations or display them at the summer solstice to amplify joy and success. Their radiant, sun-tracking nature makes them perfect for Midsummer festivities.

Clytie and the Turning Head

The sunflower's name and nature alike bind it to the Sun, for it turns its head to follow the Sun's daily journey across the sky. The Greeks told of the nymph Clytie, who loved the Sun God Helios; when he cast her aside for another, she pined away and was changed into the sunflower, doomed ever after to gaze after her lost love. From her the flower took its shadow meaning, the heart that cannot let go.

For the solar power it was thought to hold, the sunflower crowned the heads of Roman emperors, lending the ruler the Sun's own potency, and the Christian Church later took it as an emblem of the saints, prophets, and apostles, since as the flower follows the Sun, so the faithful follow God. To the Indigenous peoples of the Americas the sunflower was sacred, woven through their celebrations and carved upon golden breastplates. In gentler guise it stands for light, hope, and innocence, and in recent times it has been adopted as a symbol of world peace. Even its seed head hides a wonder, for the florets spiral out in a perfect golden spiral, one of the cornerstones of sacred geometry.
