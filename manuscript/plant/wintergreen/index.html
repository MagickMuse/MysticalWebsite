Wintergreen (Gaultheria procumbens) is a small North American evergreen herb of the Ericaceae family, valued in folk medicine for joint, muscle, and respiratory complaints, and known as the source of a famously toxic methyl salicylate oil.

Characteristics

A small evergreen herb up to 15 cm high with slender creeping stems that send forth erect twigs bearing leathery serrated leaves and drooping white flowers. The flowers are followed by fleshy scarlet berries.

Origin & Distribution

Native to North America, especially the north eastern region and Canada. The essential oil is produced in the United States.

Other Names & Related Species

Several other Gaultheria species are also used for oil production and share similar properties. Wintergreen oil and Sweet Birch Essential Oil (Betula lenta) have historically been used interchangeably, since both are composed almost entirely of methyl salicylate and carry the same severe toxicity.

Folk Tradition

The plant has been used for respiratory conditions such as chronic mucous discharge, but it is mainly employed for joint and muscular problems including lumbago, sciatica, neuralgia, and myalgia. The dried leaf and stem are current in the British Herbal Pharmacopoeia as a specific for rheumatoid arthritis. Recognised actions include mild analgesic, anti-inflammatory, antirheumatic, antitussive, astringent, carminative, diuretic, emmenagogue, galactagogue, and stimulant effects.

Safety

The essential oil distilled from Wintergreen is toxic, irritant, and sensitizing, and is classified as an environmental hazard and marine pollutant. Methyl salicylate concentrations near 98 percent make the oil dangerous internally and externally, and the true oil is almost obsolete in commerce, replaced by synthetic methyl salicylate. See Wintergreen Oil for full oil safety guidance.
