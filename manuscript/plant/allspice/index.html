History

Its documented use stretches back to the late 1400s, when Spanish colonizers encountered indigenous Caribbean and Central American peoples already using it. Allspice is native to the Caribbean islands, Mexico, and other parts of Central America.

Characteristics

Allspice earns its name from an aroma and flavor that echoes nutmeg, cinnamon, clove, and ginger all at once. It grows on small evergreen trees and its seeds are dispersed primarily by birds. The dried berries serve as a culinary ingredient, a component in perfumery, and even a natural pesticide. It is sometimes called pimento for its visual resemblance to peppercorn. The evergreen tree belongs to the Myrtaceae family, grows up to about ten metres tall, and begins fruiting in its third year, with each fruit holding two kidney-shaped green seeds that turn glossy black as they ripen.

Magickal Properties

Powers: Money, Luck, Healing

Magickal Uses

Allspice is burned as an incense to attract money or luck, and is also added to such mixtures. Allspice is also used to promote healing.

The warm, spicy scent of allspice makes it particularly effective in money-drawing incenses and healing blends, while its fiery energy helps manifest good fortune quickly.

Allspice is considered an herb of increase above all else. The dried berries can be carried alone as a talisman or incorporated into a charm bag. In any working, pair allspice with the specific thing you wish to grow or multiply.

Love Magick

Pimento/allspice added to love sachets is a traditional use for drawing and kindling love. In culinary magick, eating allspice is said to sweeten affection and rouse desire.

Culinary Uses

Parts used: Dried berry, often crushed

A kitchen witch staple, allspice is commonly used in baking - particularly cookies and other spiced treats.

Folk Tradition

Allspice has long served as a domestic spice and a household remedy for flatulent indigestion, with external application used for neuralgic and rheumatic pain. Pimento water was traditionally used as a vehicle for medicines that ease dyspepsia and constipation, since it helps prevent griping pains.

Related Species

Four other pimento varieties grow in Venezuela, Guyana, and the West Indies, where they are used locally as spices.
