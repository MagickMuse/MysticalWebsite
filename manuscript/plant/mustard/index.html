Botanical Description

An erect annual of the Brassicaceae family, up to 3 metres high, with spear-shaped upper leaves, smooth flat pods containing about ten dark brown seeds, and bright yellow cabbage-like flowers.

Origin & Distribution

Common throughout south-eastern Europe, southern Siberia, Asia Minor, and North Africa; naturalized in North and South America. Cultivated for its seed and oil in England, Holland, Denmark, Germany, and Italy.

Folk Tradition (Medicinal)

The seeds are highly esteemed as a condiment and for their medicinal qualities. They have been used in the East and West to aid digestion, warm the stomach, and promote the appetite, and for cold, stiff, or feverish conditions such as colds, chills, coughs, chilblains, rheumatism, arthritis, lumbago, and general aches and pains.

Other Species

The Russian variety is known as brown mustard or sarepta (B. juncea). The White Mustard (B. alba) does not contain any essential oil. Also closely related is rape (B. napus) and other local species used in India and China.

Related Forms

Two distinct oil products derive from this plant: the cold-pressed culinary Mustard Seed Oil (a carrier oil, generally safe in food use) and the steam-distilled Mustard Essential Oil (one of the most toxic of all essential oils, restricted to industrial use).
