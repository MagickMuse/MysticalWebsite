Bayberry (Myrica cerifera) is an aromatic shrub prized for its waxy gray-blue berries, traditionally harvested to make fragrant candles.

History

Bayberry root bark was used in folk medicine as a healing tonic, and the plant was also applied as a poultice to treat ulcers and stomach ailments. A decoction of the bark was used as a gargle for chronic throat inflammation. Despite these traditional uses, bayberry acts as an emetic in large doses and contains tannin, making internal consumption inadvisable today. Bayberry is also given as a synonym for the wild cinnamon of the West Indies and South America, the source of bay rum.

Characteristics

A large evergreen shrub, bayberry is sometimes called southern bayberry and is closely related to the wax myrtle. Its distinctive berries are bluish-white in appearance and produce a waxy, pleasantly aromatic coating long harvested for candle-making.

Magickal Uses

Prosperity and money: Bayberry candles are burned on New Year's Eve and during the Yule season to attract wealth, luck, and blessings for the coming year. The traditional rhyme states: "A bayberry candle burned to the socket brings luck to the home, food to the larder, and gold to the pocket."

Protection: The leaves and berries are used in protective sachets and incense blends. Plant bayberry near the home to guard against misfortune.

Blessing and purification: Burn bayberry as incense to bless a new home or cleanse sacred space. Its sweet, resinous scent uplifts the spirit and invites positive energy.

Luck and good fortune: Carry dried bayberry leaves in a mojo bag to attract luck in financial matters and new ventures.

Herbal Lore

Native Americans used bayberry medicinally and introduced colonists to the practice of harvesting the waxy coating from the berries to make candles. These candles became treasured for their clean, aromatic burn and became associated with holiday blessings and prosperity magic. The tradition of burning bayberry candles at Yule and New Year persists today as a beloved custom for welcoming abundance.

Meaning When Gifted

When bayberry is gifted it carries the meaning of discipline and instruction.
