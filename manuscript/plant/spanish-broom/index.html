Spanish Broom Absolute

Spanish Broom (Spartium junceum) is a decorative Mediterranean shrub with bright yellow pea-flowers.

Characteristics

A decorative deciduous shrub growing up to 3 metres tall, with upright woody branches and tough, flexible stems. Bright green leaves and large, fragrant, pea-like yellow flowers give way to seed pods (legumes) characteristic of the family. Often grown as an ornamental in dry gardens.

Origin & Distribution

Native to southern Europe, particularly southern Spain and southern France. Now widely cultivated across Spain, France, Italy, and the United States as a garden shrub. The absolute is produced primarily in southern France.

Related Species

Spanish Broom is closely related to:

  • Dyer's Greenweed (Genista tinctoria): another yellow-flowered legume long used for textile dye.
  • Common Broom or Green Broom (Cytisus scoparius, formerly Sarothamnus scoparius): the familiar European witches' broom plant covered in the existing Broom entry.

Spanish Broom is said to be five to six times more active than common broom, and substitution between the two has historically caused serious poisonings.

Folk Tradition

The twigs and bark have been worked into strong fibre for centuries, producing cord and a coarse cloth. Branches have been used for thatching, basketwork, fencing, and, of course, the construction of brooms themselves.

Spanish broom shares its therapeutic actions with common broom, which is still listed in the British Herbal Pharmacopoeia for cardiac dropsy, myocardial weakness, tachycardia, and heavy menstruation. Spanish broom is far stronger, however, and is used only by trained herbalists when it is used at all.

Safety

The flowers contain sparteine and other alkaloids that are seriously toxic in even moderate doses. Substitution of Spanish broom for common broom in home preparations has caused poisonings, including fatalities. Use the dried plant magickally only, and never internally without professional herbalist guidance.
