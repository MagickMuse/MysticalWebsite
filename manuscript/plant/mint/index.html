Mint (Mentha spp.) is a bright Mercury herb for money, lust, healing, travel, exorcism, and protection.

Lore & History

Mint takes its name from the nymph Minthe, who in Greek mythology attempted to seduce Hades, god of the underworld. Persephone intervened and transformed Minthe into the mint plant. This myth is echoed in the plant's physical behavior: when mint is trampled underfoot, it releases its signature scent, a motif found in ancient accounts of Minthe's transformation.

Mint may have been an ingredient in kykeon, a drink consumed at the culmination of the Eleusinian Mysteries, the initiatory rites into the cult of Demeter and Persephone. The Mysteries concerned the myth of Persephone's abduction by Hades, her descent into the underworld, Demeter's search for her, and her eventual ascent back into the land of the living. Some scholars have hypothesized that the kykeon had psychedelic or entheogenic effects on initiates.

Mint was also used in Greek funerary rites, alongside rosemary and other herbs.

Dr. Alexander Cummins, in his essay "The Three Purifiers" on the herbs used for aspersing in the Grimoirium Verum, writes that spearmint, with its Venusian, airy character, represents the Queen of the Underworld, while peppermint, masculine and fiery, represents her King.

Aphrodisiac Properties

Mint owes its aphrodisiac reputation to the nymph Menthe herself, who was so alluring that Persephone transformed her into a plant out of jealousy. The intoxicating scent that rises when mint is crushed underfoot is said to be a remnant of Menthe's seductive power.

Planet: Mercury

Magickal Uses

  • Healing and head‑clearing: Long used in healing potions and mixtures; fresh leaves rubbed on the head relieve headaches. Worn at the wrist to avoid illness. Stuff a green poppet with mint and anoint with healing oils for stomach complaints.
  • Travel and lust: Used in travel spells and to provoke lust.
  • Money: Place a few leaves in the wallet or purse, or rub where money is kept, to draw prosperity.
  • Exorcism and cleansing: Sprinkle salt water with a sprinkler made of fresh sprigs of mint, marjoram, and rosemary to rid a place of evil. Fresh mint on the altar calls good spirits to aid your magick.

For offerings to helpful spirits: Mint is used as an offering to attract and honor helpful spirits.

For ancestor veneration and money: Fresh mint placed on the ancestor altar, or strewn on floors so its scent rises when stepped on, is appropriate when praying to the ancestors about money matters, particularly when seeking money quickly or protecting finances believed to be disrupted by spirits of the restless dead.

Magickal Teas

Mint can be brewed as a magickal tea to connect with devas, draw money, strengthen protection, and attract prosperity. Drink 1 to 2 cups only during the spell event itself.

Medicinal Uses

Mint is a tonic herb well suited to digestive care. It relaxes the muscles of the gastrointestinal tract, making it effective for easing bloating, cramping, nausea, and indigestion. Applied topically or inhaled, the cooling sensation from menthol compounds also makes mint useful for tension headaches and mild inflammation. As a tonic, mint is gentle enough for ongoing use and integrates easily into daily life through teas, culinary use, or simple aromatherapy. Spearmint specifically, when its essential oil is properly diluted in a carrier oil, is used topically to address stomach pain and ease nausea through both skin absorption and inhalation.

Culinary Uses

Parts used: Leaves, fresh

Mint brings fertility, money-drawing, and success energy into the kitchen. It is commonly added to salads and brewed into teas.
