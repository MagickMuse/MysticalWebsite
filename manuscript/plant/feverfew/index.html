Feverfew (Tanacetum parthenium) is a perennial herb with small daisy-like flowers and aromatic leaves.

History

Feverfew's documented medicinal use stretches back to the first century C.E., when the Greek physician Dioscorides recorded its applications. Historically it has been employed to address fevers, headaches, stomachaches, and irregular menstrual cycles. By the 1970s, it had attracted renewed attention as an alternative therapy for migraine sufferers.

Characteristics

Feverfew is a strongly aromatic perennial herb. Its leaves -- whether used fresh, dried, or powdered -- have been shown to reduce the frequency and severity of headaches, making it one of the better-documented herbal headache remedies.

Magickal Uses

For protection: Carry on the person for protection against colds, fevers, and accidents. When worn, it is thought to prevent sickness and accidents.

For purifying the home: Plant Feverfew around the house to purify the atmosphere and ward off disease.

To ward sickness: Feverfew is used to ward off illness and disease.

To ward accidents in travel: Carried for protection against accidents while traveling.

For purification: Feverfew is used as a purifying herb to cleanse spaces, tools, and practitioners before ritual work.

As a countercharm: Feverfew acts as a countercharm, working against spells, hexes, and magickal workings directed at the practitioner.

To consecrate magickal tools: Feverfew can be used in the consecration of ritual tools.

To break binding spells: Feverfew is worked into operations intended to break and dissolve binding spells.

Medicinal Uses

Feverfew has a well-established reputation as a headache and migraine remedy. A tonic prepared from the fresh leaves was the traditional delivery method, and the plant's active compounds, particularly parthenolide, are believed to reduce inflammation and inhibit compounds that trigger migraine attacks. Regular use as a preventive tonic was common in European folk medicine, with the leaves sometimes eaten fresh in small amounts between slices of bread to reduce the bitterness. It works best as an ongoing preventive rather than an acute remedy.

Part used: Leaves

Application: Internal (tonic or fresh leaf preparation)

Safety

Not recommended for internal use if pregnant or with digestive issues.

Magickal Teas

Feverfew can be brewed as a magickal tea to promote health, strengthen protection, support purification, and ward sickness. Drink 1 to 2 cups only during the spell event itself.
