Dwarf Pine Essential Oil

A pyramidal shrub or small tree of the Pinaceae family, up to 12 metres high, with black bark, stiff and twisted needles borne in clusters, and brown cones with an initial bluish hue. The species sits across the line between mountain shrub and small tree, growing low and dense at the highest elevations.

Origin & Distribution

Native to the mountainous regions of central and southern Europe. The essential oil is mainly produced in Austria (Tirol), the former Yugoslavia, Denmark, and Italy.

Folk Tradition (Medicinal)

A preparation made from the needles has been used internally for bladder, kidney, and rheumatic complaints, as a liniment for rheumatism and muscular pain, and as an inhalant for bronchitis, catarrh, colds, and similar respiratory conditions.

Other Species

There are many species of pine used to produce essential oil from their needles and wood or employed in the production of turpentine. The so-called huon pine (Dacrydium franklinii), whose essential oil is also a skin irritant, belongs to a different family altogether (Podocarpaceae) and is not a true pine. See Pine for the broader genus, Norfolk Island Pine for the unrelated Araucaria species also called "pine," and the forthcoming Scotch Pine entry for the most commonly distilled commercial pine.

Related Forms

  • Dwarf Pine Essential Oil (essential oil distilled from this species)
  • Pine Essential Oil (generic commercial pine product)
