Source — Specific Uses

Add to healing incenses and poppets, especially for curing lung diseases. A good addition to love mixtures and sachets. Like peppermint but less powerful — a milder version of mint that often works better for children.

Magickal Properties

Powers: Healing, Love, Mental Powers

Ritual Uses

Stuffed in pillows or mattresses for sleep protection; used in purification and healing rites.

Magickal Uses

  • Healing: Use in general healing spells, especially for lungs and respiratory ailments.
  • Love: Sometimes used to arouse lust and sweeten affection.
  • Mental acuity: Smell spearmint to sharpen mental powers and focus.
  • Sleep protection: Stuff pillows or mattresses with spearmint to protect while asleep.

Additional Notes

Gentler than peppermint but magically potent; substitute among common mints with care.

Season: Summer

Sabbats: Litha and Lughnasadh

Magickal Principle: Culmination and manifesting

Botanical Source

Mentha spicata of the Lamiaceae family, also classified as M. viridis. The plant is a hardy branched perennial herb with bright green, lance-shaped, sharply toothed leaves, quickly spreading underground runners, and pink or lilac-coloured flowers gathered into slender cylindrical spikes.

Distribution

Native to the Mediterranean region and now common across Europe, western Asia, and the Middle East. Introduced to the USA, where it has become a very popular flavouring. Oil production centres on the midwest USA, Hungary, Spain, the former Yugoslavia, the former USSR, and China.

Other Species

Several spearmint types are recognised, particularly in the USA, including curly mint (M. spicata var. crispa). In Russia the oil from M. verticellata is also traded as spearmint oil.

Folk Medicine

Valued worldwide as a culinary herb, a fact reflected in its many folk names. The ancient Greeks employed it as a restorative and to scent their bathwater. Distilled spearmint water is used to ease hiccough, colic, nausea, indigestion, and flatulence. Older herbal authorities describe it: "Applied to the forehead and temples, it eases the pains in the head, and is good to wash the heads of young children with, against all manner of breakings out, sores or scabs... being smelled unto, it is comforting to the head."

Therapeutic Actions

Local anaesthetic, antiseptic, antispasmodic, astringent, carminative, cephalic, cholagogue, decongestant, digestive, diuretic, expectorant, febrifuge, hepatic, nervine, stimulant, stomachic, and tonic.
