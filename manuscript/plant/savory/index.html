Summer Savory (Satureja hortensis) is the peppery annual herb of the Lamiaceae family long valued as a culinary spice and as a folk remedy for digestive, respiratory, and menstrual ailments.

Botanical Description

An annual herb up to 45 cm high with slender, erect, slightly hairy stems, linear leaves, and small, pale lilac flowers.

Origin & Distribution

Native to Europe, naturalized in North America. Extensively cultivated for its essential oil, especially in Spain, France, Yugoslavia, and the USA.

Related Species

Closely related to the thyme family, with which it shares many characteristics. Several different types of savory exist, including Satureja thymbra, found in Spain, which contains mainly thymol, and Winter Savory (Satureja montana), a related perennial species.

Folk Tradition

A popular culinary herb with a peppery flavour. Summer Savory has been used therapeutically mainly as a tea for digestive complaints including cramp, nausea, indigestion, and intestinal parasites, as well as menstrual disorders and respiratory conditions including asthma, catarrh, and sore throat. Applied externally, the fresh leaves bring instant relief from insect bites, bee stings, and wasp stings. Summer savory is described as hotter and drier than the winter kind: it expels tough phlegm from the chest and lungs, and quickens the dull spirits in lethargy. Recorded actions include anticatarrhal, antiputrescent, antispasmodic, aphrodisiac, astringent, bactericidal, carminative, cicatrisant, emmenagogue, expectorant, fungicidal, stimulant, and vermifuge.

Safety

Summer Savory Essential Oil is a dermal toxin, dermal irritant, and mucous membrane irritant due to its carvacrol content. The oil should not be used on the skin at all and should be avoided during pregnancy. The dried herb taken internally as a culinary spice or folk-medicine tea is generally safe, but the concentrated oil is not for home aromatherapy.

History

Ancient herbals frequently noted two distinct varieties of savory: summer and winter. The poet Virgil praised its virtues and encouraged planting it near beehives so its fragrance would pass into the honey. Ancient Romans used it as a seasoning substitute for salt, and by the Middle Ages its cultivation had been formally discouraged because its reputation as a potent aphrodisiac was considered a moral hazard.

Characteristics

Also called summer savory, Satureja hortensis is a highly aromatic annual member of the mint family, in contrast to the perennial Satureja montana (winter savory). It is prized as a spice for its bold, peppery flavor and is also used medicinally as a carminative, appetite stimulant, and astringent. Relatively undemanding to cultivate, it grows thin, dark green leaves and small white or pinkish-purple flowers, reaching about 18 inches in height with a preference for full sun and compost-enriched soil.
