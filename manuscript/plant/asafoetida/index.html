Asafoetida (Ferula assa-foetida) is a pungent resin known as "devil's dung" for its powerful ability to drive away evil.

Botanical Description

Asafoetida is a large, branching perennial herb that grows up to three metres tall, with a thick fleshy root system and pale yellow-green flowers. It is native to Afghanistan, Iran, and other regions of southwest Asia, where it has thrived in arid conditions for centuries.

Related Species

Several other species of Ferula yield the oleoresin known as asafetida. Tibetan asafetida, for example, is also harvested commercially, though to a lesser extent than the standard Iranian and Afghan source.

Folk Tradition

Chinese medicine has employed asafoetida since the seventh century as a nerve stimulant in the treatment of neurasthenia. It is also widely used in traditional Indian medicine, where it is believed to stimulate the brain. Across these traditions it has earned a reputation for addressing asthma, bronchitis, convulsions, coughs, constipation, flatulence, and hysteria. The foliage of the plant is eaten as a local vegetable, and the British Herbal Pharmacopoeia carries asafoetida as a specific remedy for intestinal flatulent colic.

Magickal Uses

For banishing and exorcism: Burn asafoetida to drive out evil spirits and baneful energies. The intensely unpleasant smell is believed to be as offensive to malevolent spirits as it is to humans, forcing them to flee.

For protection: Add small amounts to protective mojo bags (especially The Devil's Hand) to repel and drive away evil. The smell itself creates a protective barrier that negative forces will not cross.

As a protective amulet: Carry a small piece of asafoetida resin in a charm bag or pocket to keep harmful spirits, curses, and crossed conditions at bay. Despite its offensive smell, it is one of the most powerful protective substances in folk magick traditions.

For purification: Burn as incense (in well-ventilated spaces) to cleanse a space of lingering negative energy and malevolent influences.

Aphrodisiac Properties

Despite its folk name Devil's Dung and its powerful odor, asafoetida is used as a sexual stimulant in Ayurvedic medicine.

Additional Notes

Asafoetida has a nauseating odor that frequent users eventually become accustomed to. The smell is part of what makes it so effective at driving away negativity, as malevolent forces find it as offensive as humans do.

If you prefer not to work with asafoetida's intense smell, substitute with tobacco, valerian root, or other protective and exorcistic herbs.

Despite its pungent raw odor, asafoetida is used in Indian cooking, where it is typically fried in oil to mellow the smell before adding to dishes.
