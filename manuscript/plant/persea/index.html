Persea (Mimusops laurifolia) is a form of wild laurel that grows in arid regions and was revered by the ancient Egyptians. Its leaves served as paper for Thoth, the Measurer of Time, Scribe and Messenger of the Gods and recorder of the deeds of humankind, and for Safekh, goddess of learning, knowledge, and writing, who together inscribed the names and deeds of the great and good of the nation.

Folk Tradition

Once a person's name was written upon its leaves, that person held the security of eternal life, and so the persea became a symbol of lasting fame.
