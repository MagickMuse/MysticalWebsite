Dragon's Blood is a popular incense resin resembling red chalk, commonly obtained from two main species: Dracaena draco and Dracaena cinnabari. Though many varieties of Dracaena produce this fragrant resin, these two are the most common sources.

Botanical Information

Species: While multiple species produce dragon's blood resin, the two primary sources are Dracaena draco and Dracaena cinnabari.

Appearance: The resin resembles red chalk and has a distinctive bright red color.

Scent Profile: Both produce a similarly colored sap with musky, warm notes and a hint of floral. D. cinnabari has slightly more pronounced floral notes than D. draco.

Growth: Both trees take over ten years to produce their signature red sap. D. cinnabari is the slower growing of the two varieties.

Conservation Status: Due to overharvesting and habitat loss, both species of Dracaena are on the threatened list. Use responsibly and seek sustainably sourced resin when possible.

Magickal Properties

  • Powers: Love, Protection, Exorcism, Potency

Magickal Uses

For love: Dragon's blood is burned to draw back a loved one who has strayed. Despite its fearsome name, dragon's blood is used extensively in love magick.

For protection and exorcism: A pinch of dragon's blood added to other incenses increases their potency and power. It is used in protection rituals, and when carried or used in sachets it protects the wearer.

For potency: Dragon's blood under the bed restores potency to men. The bright red resin is used to increase the power of spells and rituals.

For talismans and sigils: Dragon's Blood ink is an excellent tool for drawing talismans and sigils. Simply dissolve a small amount of the powder in a little alcohol to create a potent magickal ink.

For life cycle and changes: Dragon's blood is used in workings related to life cycle transitions and transformation/changes.

Sangre de Drago (Croton lechleri)

A distinct plant also known as dragon's blood is Croton lechleri, a flowering plant native to South America. Its dark red resinous sap is the source of the name sangre de drago, meaning "blood of the dragon." Unlike the Dracaena species, Croton lechleri has a long history of ethnomedicinal use and is credited with several documented healing properties.

Medicinal Properties

The resinous sap has been used traditionally to treat diarrhea, insect bites, and inflammations. Laboratory studies indicate potential to inhibit cancer cell growth, and clinical trials have shown efficacy in easing AIDS-related diarrhea. Animal studies suggest it can reduce wound healing time. Croton lechleri dragon's blood is considered low in toxicity and is generally regarded as safe for use.

Magickal Uses

This form of dragon's blood is used as incense, as an ingredient in spells and charms, and as a red color additive for magickal inks used in grimoires and written workings. Note that artificial dragon's blood and commercial "dragon's blood ink" products are often unrelated to the genuine resinous sap.

Working with Sangre de Drago

  • Bath: Crush 1 tablespoon of dried resin and combine with 1/2 cup Epsom salts and 1/4 cup sea salt. Dissolve in bathwater for a cleansing and fortifying dragon's blood bath.
  • Incense powder: Combine dragon's blood resin with copal in a mortar and pestle and crush into a powder. Break up larger pieces first if needed. Burn on a charcoal disc in a fireproof vessel such as a cauldron.
  • Sachet: Add whole pieces of dragon's blood resin to a sachet or mojo bag and carry for ongoing magickal support.
