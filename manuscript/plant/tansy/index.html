Tansy (Tanacetum vulgare) is the hardy perennial wayside herb of the Asteraceae family long valued in folk medicine but yielding a high-thujone essential oil that is unsafe for home aromatherapy.

Botanical Description

A hardy perennial wayside herb up to 1 metre high with a smooth stem, dark ferny leaves, and small, round, brilliant yellow flowers borne in clusters. The whole plant is strongly scented.

Origin & Distribution

Native to central Europe and naturalized in North America, now found in most temperate regions of the world. The essential oil is mainly produced in France, Germany, Hungary, Poland, and the USA.

Related Species

Tansy is closely related to the medicinal herb Feverfew (Tanacetum parthenium), as well as the marigolds and the wider daisy family.

Folk Tradition

Tansy has traditionally been used to flavour eggs and omelettes. It has a long history of medicinal use, especially among the Romani people, and is regarded as something of a cure-all. It was used to expel worms, to treat colds and fever, to prevent possible miscarriage, and to ease dyspepsia and cramping pains. Externally, the distilled water was used to keep the complexion pale, and the bruised leaves were employed as a remedy for scabies, bruises, sprains, and rheumatism. It was also used generally for nervous disorders and to keep flies and vermin away. The flowers are still current in the British Herbal Pharmacopoeia as a specific used externally for worms in children. Recorded actions include anthelmintic, anti-inflammatory, antispasmodic, carminative, diaphoretic, digestive, emmenagogue, febrifuge, nervine, stimulant, tonic, and vermifuge.

Safety

Tansy essential oil is an oral toxin, poisonous due to its very high thujone content (66 to 81 per cent), and an abortifacient. The oil should not be used in aromatherapy, internally or externally, and must be avoided during pregnancy.

History

Tansy carries a long history in folk medicine as an anthelmintic herb, meaning it was used to expel internal parasites. It contains the toxic compound thujone and is not recommended for consumption in large quantities. Always consult an herbal practitioner before internal use.

Characteristics

Strongly aromatic with characteristically bright yellow flowers, tansy grows up to three feet tall. Native to Europe, it is widely cultivated across the United States as well. The dried leaves and flowers are used in infusions and historically as a culinary herb, particularly to flavor cakes and sweets prepared during spring rites.

Magickal Uses

  • Fever relief: A bit of tansy placed in the shoes is used to help cure persistent fevers.
  • Longevity: Carried to lengthen lifespan and promote longevity.
  • Banishing spell: To remove a person or situation from your life, place a photograph or symbolic representation of it on a black cloth upon your altar. Cover it with tansy flowers and allow them to wilt naturally. Once fully dried, crush the flowers and burn them on a charcoal disc along with an effigy or written symbol of what you wish to banish.

Medicinal Uses

Tansy's essential oil, when properly diluted in a carrier oil, functions as an effective topical insect repellant. It contains thujone and camphor-like compounds that insects find strongly aversive, and it was historically rubbed on the skin or packed into doorways and windowsills to deter flies, fleas, and other pests. This was a common use in European households before the advent of modern pesticides. Dilution is essential; undiluted tansy oil is irritating to the skin and toxic in larger amounts.

Part used: Essential oil (diluted)

Application: Topical only

⚠️
Tansy is toxic internally and not safe for consumption. Not recommended during pregnancy under any circumstances. Use only properly diluted oil on the skin, and avoid contact with eyes or mucous membranes.
