Hemp (Cannabis sativa) was once widely used in love, vision, and healing magick before legal restrictions curbed its ritual use.

Folk Tradition

Hemp is the common name for all the species of cannabis. Both Cannabis sativa and Cannabis indica were smoked across many countries long before the Conquistadors encountered tobacco, and the plant's ubiquity shows in its many names: in India it is bhang or ganja, from the Sanskrit bhanga and ghanyika; in the Near East it is hashish, from an Arabic word for hemp and the root of the word assassin; in South Africa it is dakka; and the South American marijuana was heard by the Conquistadors as Mary Jane. Nearly everywhere it is simply called weed.

Its value as a narcotic has long overshadowed its usefulness as a fabric. Hemp's role as a ritual substance is well documented, with evidence reaching back to Neolithic times, and in 2003 a mummified shaman was uncovered in China alongside a leather basket holding fragments of seeds and leaves. Such practice often involved burning the flowering tops to induce a trance. In more recent times the Rastafarian movement adopted hemp as a sacred herb, depicting the cannabis leaf in the red, black, and yellow of its colors and holding that the herb purifies the soul, clears the mind, and brings peace. The Mahayana Buddhist tradition reveres it as well, linking it to the Buddha, who is said to have lived on a single hemp seed each day as he journeyed toward enlightenment.

Magickal Uses

  • Crystal ball divination: One old recipe calls for burning hemp and mugwort while gazing into a crystal ball.
  • Love spells: Figures in many love spells as an aphrodisiac and intoxicant; the effect varies with the individual. Its action is almost entirely on the higher nervous centers, altering perception and time sense.
  • Love and divination: Long used in love spells and love divinations, including the infamous Hempseed Spell to reveal one's future spouse.
  • Vision and scrying: Added to vision and scrying incenses; traditionally burned with mugwort before a magick mirror to open psychic sight.
  • Meditation: Included in meditation blends to calm the mind and deepen focus.
  • Traditional scourging: In China, hemp scourges were used as imitation snakes beaten around the beds of the sick to drive out illness‑causing demons.
  • Traveler’s lore: Venerated figure Al Khidr is sometimes syncretized in traditions that also revere green, growing plants like hemp.
