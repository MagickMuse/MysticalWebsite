Darnel (Lolium temulentum) is the infamous biblical "tare", a poisonous grass weed that mimics wheat until harvest, cited among the ingredients of the traditional witch's flying ointment and one of the most feared agricultural pests of the ancient world.

⚠️
TOXIC: Darnel seeds contain alkaloids from a symbiotic fungus that cause intoxication, dizziness, trembling, vomiting, and in large doses, convulsions and death. Do not ingest.

Magickal Uses

  • Illusion and confusion: Associated with deception and disguise, used in workings intended to confuse enemies or veil one's own activities.
  • Binding and cursing: Historically used in folk cursing; scattered in an enemy's path or mixed into binding workings.
  • Reversal: The same properties that make darnel dangerous can be turned toward undoing deceptive workings directed at oneself.

The Witch's Flying Ointment

Darnel is listed among the plants cited in historical flying ointment formulas from the 1500s and 1600s. Its seeds contain the alkaloids loline and others produced by an endophytic fungus, which are known to cause intoxication, visual disturbances, and trembling, effects consistent with the trance states associated with spirit flight.

The Biblical Tares

Darnel is almost certainly the plant referred to as "tares" in the Parable of the Tares in the Gospel of Matthew (13:24–30), in which an enemy sows tares among a farmer's wheat. The parable was possible because darnel is virtually indistinguishable from wheat until the seed heads form, a quality that made it uniquely feared. Consuming bread contaminated with darnel flour caused "tare drunkenness," a recognized affliction throughout the ancient and medieval world.

Botanical Description

Darnel is an annual grass that grows to about 60–90 cm tall, closely mimicking wheat and rye in its vegetative stages. Only at seed set does it become distinguishable, its seeds are smaller, more rounded, and deeply embedded in the spike rachis. It was one of the most common and destructive weed species in grain fields before the advent of modern seed-cleaning technology.
