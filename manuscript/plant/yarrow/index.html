History

Yarrow is one of the great healing herbs of northern and western Europe, with a parallel history of use among Native American peoples. In Chinese tradition, yarrow stalks were used in place of coins in the divination practice of the I Ching. According to legend, the warrior Achilles was bathed in a yarrow infusion at birth, granting him protection in battle, and he carried yarrow with him to treat wounded soldiers in the field. European colonists brought yarrow across the Atlantic, where it was eventually adopted by Native Americans who prepared it as a tea to treat menorrhagia. In an irony fit for legend, Achilles himself eventually died from a cut to the heel, bleeding out from the one wound his own healing herb could not seal.

The scientific name Achillea millefolium comes from Achilles, the Greek hero of the Trojan War. His mother, a sea spirit, taught him both enchantment and healing, and he is said to have carried yarrow to magickally shield and heal the warriors he commanded. Yarrow is a powerful styptic that stops bleeding when used therapeutically. As a spiritually protective plant, it is believed to grant its bearer something close to the magickal invulnerability and near immortality of Achilles.

Characteristics

Yarrow is easily recognized by its clusters of small white flowers atop straight stalks, with grayish-green, deeply serrated leaves that have a soft, feathery appearance. It has long been used to treat nosebleeds and wounds, and yarrow tea is a traditional remedy for reducing fevers.

Specific Uses

Used in divination spells and to consult the I Ching. Sleep with yarrow under the pillow for dreams of a future spouse. Has the power to keep a couple together for seven years. Worn as an amulet, wards off negativity. Sometimes added to exorcism incenses. In ancient China, considered a sacred plant with spiritual qualities.

Ritual Uses

Bunches hung over the bed or used in wedding decorations ensure a love lasting at least seven years. Incorporated into a bridal bouquet, yarrow ensures seven years of happiness.

Infant Protection

Tying yarrow to a baby's cradle protects the child from fairies, malevolent magick, and harmful spirits.

Magickal Uses

  • Protection and courage: Worn for protection; held in the hand to stop fear and grant courage.
  • Injury prevention and healing: Yarrow stalks carried in a charm bag guard against injury and hasten healing.
  • Love and friendship: Used in love spells; carrying yarrow attracts love, friends, and distant relations you wish to contact.
  • Psychic powers: Flowers made into an infusion and drunk to improve psychic ability.
  • Exorcism: Used to drive out evil and negativity from persons, places, or things.
  • Home banishing: Decorate the home with boughs of fresh yarrow to banish sadness and negativity.
  • Baldness lore: Washing the head with a yarrow infusion will prevent baldness (tradition says it will not cure baldness once begun).
  • Divination and prophecy: Yarrow has long been used as a divinatory herb. Its prophetic powers are especially cited in predicting the outcomes of romantic couplings and matters of the heart.

Bloodwort, Truth, and the Threshold

The folk names bloodwort and nose-bleed point to a real virtue behind the legend of Achilles, for yarrow truly does help staunch bleeding thanks to the blood-clotting power of its leaves. Young girls once used its flowers as a tool of divination to learn their true loves, and the dried stalks are cast in the Chinese I Ching to read the future. Yarrow made a fitting instrument for such work because it was held a sacred plant of truth, and its stalks grow long and dry well besides. The plant guards as surely as it foretells: its leaves were strewn across the threshold to turn back evil and stop any witch in her tracks, and so it became a token of protection, sometimes laid in a cradle to keep the baby safe.

Meaning When Gifted

When yarrow is gifted it carries the meaning of healing, comfort, and a cure for heartache.
