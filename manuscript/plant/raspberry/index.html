History

Raspberries are thought to have been in use since at least the first century. The Roman agricultural writer Palladius mentions the discovery of raspberry seeds in the fourth century. Through the medieval era, raspberries were a luxury reserved for the wealthy. Commercial cultivation is believed to have begun in western Europe during the seventeenth century.

Characteristics

Considered native to Europe though also believed to have origins in Asia, raspberry is now widely cultivated across the world. The leaves — not the fruit — are most prized for their therapeutic value: used in poultices and teas, they are an astringent tonic associated with women's health, credited with easing menstrual discomfort and reducing morning sickness during pregnancy. The plant is relatively easy to grow, tolerates both cool and warm climates, requires full sun, and fruits in summer.

Magickal Uses

Hang brambles at doors and windows for protection; this use was especially common during mourning, to prevent the spirit of the deceased from returning. Serve the berries to attract and maintain romantic love. Wear or carry the leaves in a charm bag to speed labor and ease the pains of pregnancy and childbirth. Because the berries are densely packed with seeds, dried raspberries are added to fertility charm bags. A simple love potion can be made by soaking the fresh berries in red wine. As a herb strongly aligned with feminine health and the female body, raspberry is a natural ally in workings centered on women's power, cycles, and well-being.

Aphrodisiac Properties

The raspberry's deep red color, abundance of tiny seeds, and resemblance to a nipple all contribute to its aphrodisiac reputation.

Ghost Prevention

Hanging raspberry vines over the entrances of a deceased person's former home prevents their ghost from reentering.

Additional Notes

Red brambles create both physical and spiritual barriers; gentle Venusian protection for the home.

Magickal Teas

Raspberry leaf can be brewed as a magickal tea to receive Fairie blessings, promote health, draw love, and strengthen protection. Drink 1 to 2 cups only during the spell event itself.
