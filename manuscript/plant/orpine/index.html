Orpine is a fleshy, drought-tolerant perennial herb of the family Crassulaceae. Its thick, succulent leaves store water, giving rise to its folk names Live-Forever and Livelong, a quality that made it a natural symbol of endurance and vitality in folk magic across Europe.

Folk Magic & Tradition

Orpine is one of the seven herbs of Saint John, gathered at midnight on Midsummer's Eve or at noon on Midsummer's Day, when their protective power is said to be at its height. Dried and hung in the home, they guard against fire.

Under the name Midsummer Men, orpine was used in love divination. Pairs of orpine stems were hung in the rafters on Midsummer's Eve: if both stems bent toward each other, the love match would be true; if one withered or turned away, the omen was poor.

Because orpine stays alive long after being cut, it was traditionally brought indoors and kept as a charm for longevity and against illness.

Botanical Notes

  • Family: Crassulaceae
  • Height: 30 to 60 cm
  • Flowers: Clusters of small pink to red-purple blooms, appearing late summer to early autumn
  • Leaves: Broad, flat, fleshy, blue-green
  • Habitat: Rocky slopes, dry grasslands, woodland edges across Europe and temperate Asia

Culinary Use

Young leaves and shoots are edible raw or cooked, with a mild, slightly mucilaginous flavor. They have been eaten in salads and cooked as greens across parts of Europe.
