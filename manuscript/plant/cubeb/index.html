Cubeb (Piper cubeba), also called Tailed Pepper or Java Pepper, is an evergreen climbing vine reaching up to 6 metres in height with heart-shaped leaves. The plant looks much like its relative the black pepper vine; what distinguishes cubeb is that its fruits keep their peduncles attached even after drying, giving each berry a small "tail" and the spice its common name.

Botanical Information

Scientific name: Piper cubeba

Botanical family: Piperaceae

Type: Spice / Fruit / Seed (climbing vine)

Origin: Native to Indonesia. Cultivated throughout Southeast Asia and across southeast Africa, where it is typically interplanted with coffee.

Distribution

Cubeb is native to Indonesia, which remains the principal source of both the dried berries and the essential oil distilled from them. Cultivation has spread throughout southeast Africa, where the vine is most often grown in mixed plantings alongside coffee crops.

Other Species

Cubeb is closely related to several other peppers in the Piperaceae family:

  • Piper nigrum (the common black pepper plant)
  • Piper augustifolium (South American matico)
  • Piper crassipes (false cubebs, an Indonesian relative frequently used as an adulterant for true cubeb berries)

Folk Medicine

Cubeb berries have served as a domestic spice across their growing range and have a long history in folk pharmacy. The seeds are best known for their action on the genito-urinary tract, with traditional applications including:

  • Genito-urinary infections (gonorrhoea, cystitis, urethritis, abscess of the prostate gland, leucorrhoea)
  • Digestive upsets
  • Respiratory complaints, including chronic bronchitis

The seed exerts a local stimulating effect on the mucous membranes of both the urinary and respiratory tracts. Powdered cubeb has also been documented as roughly 90 percent clinically effective in treating amoebic dysentery.

Therapeutic Actions

Antiseptic (pulmonary and genito-urinary), antispasmodic, antiviral, bactericidal, carminative, diuretic, expectorant, and a general stimulant.
