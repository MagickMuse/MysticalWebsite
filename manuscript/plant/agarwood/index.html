Agarwood (Aquilaria agallocha), also known as wood aloe, lignaloes, oriental lignaloes, wood aloes, and lignum aloes, is a fragrant tree native to India prized for its aromatic resin.

Magickal Properties

Gender: Masculine

Planet: Venus

Element: Water

Powers: Love, Spirituality, Protection, Consecration, Success, Prosperity

The odor of agarwood is described as a rich combination of ambergris and sandalwood, making it highly prized for incense blending.

Magickal Uses

For incense: Wood aloe is primarily used in incenses of protection, consecration, success, and prosperity. Its rich scent creates sacred atmosphere for ritual work.

For spiritual work: The combination of earthy and ethereal notes makes it excellent for meditation, prayer, and connecting with higher realms.

For Jupiter and Venus incenses: The wood is especially prized for use in Jupiter and Venus incenses. Combined with musk and myrtle, it serves as a burnt offering to the Goddess.

Aloes Wood and Aloe Vera

Similar botanical names have long caused confusion between two very different plants. The aloes named in the Bible is believed to be this mysterious wood of Southeast Asia, not Aloe vera, a native of Zanzibar. Aloe vera means "true aloe," a name born of this very mix-up. Both plants offer profound gifts, but they are far from identical.

Aloes wood, also called agar, oud, and laos, is a treasured, prized wood that was rare even in biblical times, once valued as highly as ambergris and musk. It is a potent aphrodisiac and psychic enhancer. Its name laos should not be confused with laos root, which is Galangal, also known as Courtcase root.

Additional Notes

If agarwood is unavailable, substitute with the same amount of sandalwood sprinkled with a few drops of synthetic ambergris for incense use. This approximates the unique scent profile.
