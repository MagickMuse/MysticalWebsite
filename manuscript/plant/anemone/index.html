Magickal Uses

Gather the blossoms when first seen in the spring, wrap them up in a red cloth and wear or carry to prevent disease. Grow red anemones in the garden to protect both it and the home. Use the blossoms in all healing rituals.

The spring timing of anemone collection aligns with the season of renewal and new beginnings, making it particularly potent for health and protection magick.

Symbolism & Mythology

The name anemone descends from the Greek anemos, meaning wind, a fitting title for so fragile and short-lived a bloom. Ovid wrote that the flower was born of the wind and carried away by it, and the plant came to stand for the fleeting nature of life itself. For this reason anemones were a favored flower in funeral wreaths.

A Greek myth ties the bloom to grief and love alike. When Aphrodite mourned the death of her beloved Adonis, the tears she shed fell to the earth and rose again as anemones. Her sorrow soon passed and she took a new lover, so the flower also speaks to the passing, ephemeral side of love.

Because the early Christians took any red flower as an emblem of the blood of Christ, the red anemone in particular gathered associations of pain and suffering.

Meaning When Gifted

When anemone is gifted it carries the meaning of brevity and a wish to be left alone.

  • Pasque Flower: Unpretentious, you have no claims
  • Windflower: Sincerity, a symbol of love
