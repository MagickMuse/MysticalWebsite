Ginkgo (Ginkgo biloba) is one of the oldest living tree species on Earth, a solitary survivor from the Paleolithic era prized in both traditional medicine and magickal practice for its associations with longevity, wisdom, and vitality.

History

Ginkgo is among the oldest living trees in the world, having endured since the Paleolithic era. Individual trees are believed to live for thousands of years, with some specimens estimated to be 2,500 years old. Ginkgo holds an important place in traditional Chinese medicine, where it has long been valued for its reputed effects on memory and sexual vitality.

Characteristics

Ginkgo trees are remarkably resilient, tolerating cold climates and the environmental stress of urban settings with ease. In England, it is commonly called the "maidenhair tree" due to the resemblance of its leaves to the maidenhair fern native to western Europe. The tree is most easily recognized by its distinctive fan-shaped, notched leaves. The nut of the ginkgo is traditionally used to support virility and has also been employed as a remedy for asthma.
