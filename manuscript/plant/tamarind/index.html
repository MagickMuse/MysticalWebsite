Tamarind (Tamarindus indica) is a long-lived tropical tree of the legume family, grown across Africa, India, and South Asia for its tart, pod-borne pulp. For all the everyday value of its fruit, the living tree carries a heavy weight of superstition wherever it grows of its own accord.

Folk Tradition

Throughout parts of India, Sri Lanka, and Thailand the tamarind is regarded with deep unease. It is thought either to mark a place where bad spirits dwell or to give shelter to such evil entities itself, so that the tree is something to be avoided; even its scent and its shade are counted dangerous, and its timber is feared as holding the very essence of these baleful influences. The likely root of the dread is plain enough to the gardener, for the tree secretes an acid through the hours of darkness that leaves the ground beneath it barren, so that little will grow in its shadow.

In the Kitchen

Despite the fear that surrounds it, the tamarind's bitter fruit has a cooling quality and is an essential note in the spiced cookery of India, so that people have always been willing to brave the tree's dark reputation in order to gather it.

Safety

The pulp is widely eaten and generally safe; the wariness attached to the tree is a matter of folklore rather than toxicity.
