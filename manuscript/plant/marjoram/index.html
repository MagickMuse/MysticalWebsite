Marjoram (Origanum majorana; O. vulgare) protects, brightens, heals, and draws love and money.

Magickal Uses

Marjoram is added to all love charms and used in love spells, as well as added to food to strengthen love. Small pieces placed in every room of the house provide protection, renewed monthly. An infusion of marjoram, rosemary, and mint sprinkled around the home produces protective vibrations and is also used to cleanse objects. Grown in the garden, marjoram shields against evil. Violets mixed with marjoram are worn in winter as an amulet against colds, and marjoram is given to the depressed to bring happiness. It is added to money mixtures and sachets to draw prosperity. Marjoram is woven into marriage spells and wedding charms to bless unions with lasting joy.

Culinary Uses

Parts used: Leaves

Marjoram is used in similar ways to basil in the kitchen, bringing love, marriage blessings, and joy to dishes like pesto, sauces, soups, stews, and salads.

Botanical Source

Origanum majorana of the Lamiaceae family, also classified as Marjorana hortensis and known as knotted marjoram. The plant is a tender bushy perennial (cultivated as an annual in colder climates), reaching about 60 cm with a hairy stem, dark green oval leaves, and small greyish-white flowers gathered in clusters or "knots." The whole plant is strongly aromatic.

Distribution

Native to the Mediterranean region, Egypt, and North Africa. Major essential oil production now centres on France, Tunisia, Morocco, Egypt, Bulgaria, Hungary, and Germany.

Other Species

The marjorams and oreganos are subject to considerable taxonomic confusion. Most commonly encountered relatives include pot or French marjoram (Origanum onites, also Marjorana onites), a hardier and more spreading plant; Spanish marjoram or oregano (Thymus mastichina); and wild or common marjoram (Origanum vulgare), the species used to produce so-called "oregano oil."

Folk Medicine

A traditional culinary herb and folk remedy. The ancient Greeks employed marjoram in fragrances, cosmetics, and medicines, and the name oregano descends from a Greek phrase meaning "joy of the mountains." Marjoram is described as soothing, fortifying, and warming, and has long been valued for digestive and menstrual problems as well as nervous and respiratory complaints. Older herbal authorities describe the plant as "comforting in cold diseases of the head, stomach, sinews and other parts, taken inwardly or outwardly applied... helps diseases of the chest, obstructions of the liver and spleen." It is also recommended for muscular and rheumatic pain, sprains, strains, stiff joints, and bruises.

Therapeutic Actions

Analgesic, anaphrodisiac, anti-oxidant, antiseptic, antispasmodic, antiviral, bactericidal, carminative, cephalic, cordial, diaphoretic, digestive, diuretic, emmenagogue, expectorant, fungicidal, hypotensive, laxative, nervine, sedative, stomachic, tonic, vasodilator, and vulnerary.
