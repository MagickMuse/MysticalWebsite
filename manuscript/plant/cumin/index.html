Cumin (Cuminum cyminum), known across different traditions as Comino, Jeera, Cummin, or Roman Caraway, is a small, delicate annual herb of the Apiaceae family. The plant grows to about 50 cm in height with a slender stem, dark green feathery leaves, and small pink or white flowers that ripen into oblong seeds. Those seeds are the part of the plant that carries cumin's characteristic warm, musky-spice flavour and aroma.

Botanical Information

Scientific name: Cuminum cyminum

Botanical family: Apiaceae (Umbelliferae)

Type: Herb / Seed / Spice (annual)

Height: Approximately 50 cm

Origin: Native to upper Egypt. Cultivated since ancient times throughout the Mediterranean (especially Spain, France, and Morocco), as well as across India and the former USSR.

Distribution

Cumin is one of the oldest cultivated spices in the Mediterranean and West Asian world. It originated in upper Egypt and has been farmed across the Mediterranean basin, the Indian subcontinent, and the former Soviet Union since antiquity. Today the bulk of the essential oil is produced in India, Spain, and France.

Other Species

Cumin is closely related to Cilantro (Coriandrum sativum) and shares many of its therapeutic properties.

Folk Medicine

Cumin is a traditional Middle Eastern spice and one of the foundational ingredients of curry. Although Western herbalism has largely set it aside, it remains a major remedy in Ayurvedic medicine, where it is used principally as a general stimulant and for digestive complaints including:

  • Colic
  • Sluggish digestion
  • Dyspepsia

Therapeutic Actions

Anti-oxidant, antiseptic, antispasmodic, antitoxic, aphrodisiac, bactericidal, carminative, depurative, digestive, diuretic, emmenagogue, larvicidal, nervine, stimulant, and tonic.
