Melilotus Absolute

Melilotus (Melilotus officinalis) is a sweet-scented yellow- or white-flowered legume of the Fabaceae family whose dried flowers smell strongly of new-mown hay.

Magickal Properties

Gender: Cold

Planet: Mercury

Element: Earth/Air

Deities: Hermes, Thoth, Anubis, Odin, Maat

Powers: Intellect, Eloquence, Creativity

Magickal Uses

  • Potpourri and perfume: Its sweet odor increases when dried. Put in potpourris, sweet bags, and perfumes.
  • Parts used: herb.

Characteristics

A bushy perennial herb reaching up to 1 metre high, with smooth erect stems, trifoliate oval leaves, and small sweet-scented white or yellow flowers. The scent of the flowers becomes stronger on drying.

Origin & Distribution

Native to Europe and Asia Minor. Other similar species occur in Asia, the USA, and Africa. The flowers are mainly cultivated in England, France, Germany, and the former USSR.

Related Species

Several similar species exist, including Melilotus arvensis, whose oil is also used in perfumery and flavouring work.

Folk Tradition

On the Continent, the leaves and shoots are used for sleeplessness, thrombosis, nervous tension, varicose veins, intestinal disorders, headache, earache, and indigestion. As an ointment or plaster, melilot is applied externally for inflamed or swollen joints, abdominal and rheumatic pain, bruises, cuts, and skin eruptions. Recorded actions include anti-inflammatory, antirheumatic, antispasmodic, astringent, emollient, expectorant, digestive, insecticidal against moth, and sedative.

Safety

The plant is high in coumarins, including melilotic acid and orthocoumaric acid. Coumarin was banned from flavourings in 1953 in some countries, including the USA, because of toxicity. Some coumarins are also known to be phototoxic.
