Cajuput (Melaleuca cajuputi) is a tall Southeast Asian evergreen whose leaves are distilled for an aromatic essential oil.

Characteristics

A tall evergreen tree growing up to 30 metres high, with thick, pointed leaves and small white flowers. The flexible trunk is wrapped in a whitish, spongy bark that flakes off easily in papery sheets. In Malaysia it is called caju-puti, meaning "white wood," for the pale colour of the timber.

Origin & Distribution

Grows wild across Malaysia, Indonesia, the Philippines, Vietnam, Java, Australia, and other parts of southeastern Asia.

Related Species

Several other Melaleuca species yield cajeput-style oils, most notably M. quinquenervia (niaouli). Cajuput sits inside the broader Melaleuca group, closely related to Tea Tree (M. alternifolia) and Rosalina, with eucalyptus and clove as more distant cousins in the Myrtaceae family.

Folk Medicine

Held in high regard across the East, where the leaves and oil are used locally for colds, headaches, throat infections, toothache, sore and aching muscles, fever (including cholera), rheumatism, and skin diseases.

In Western herbalism only the essential oil is used, known for producing a sensation of warmth and quickening the pulse. It has been a steady remedy for chronic laryngitis and bronchitis, cystitis, rheumatism, and as a vermifuge to expel roundworm.
