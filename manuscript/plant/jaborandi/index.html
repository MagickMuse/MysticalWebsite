Jaborandi Oil

Jaborandi (Pilocarpus jaborandi) is a small Brazilian shrub of the Rutaceae family whose leaflets yield pilocarpine, a powerful glandular stimulant.

Characteristics

A woody shrub reaching up to 2 metres high, with smooth greyish bark, large brownish-green leathery leaves dotted with prominent oil glands, and reddish-purple flowers.

Origin & Distribution

Native to Brazil. Related species occur in Paraguay, Cuba, the West Indies, and Central America.

Related Species

Many unrelated plants in the Rutaceae and Piperaceae families are sold under the name jaborandi, including Piper jaborandi, which has caused lasting confusion about the true botanical source of the oil. Other Pilocarpus species used commercially include maranham jaborandi (Pilocarpus microphyllus), ceara jaborandi (Pilocarpus trachylophus), and aracti jaborandi (Pilocarpus spicatus).

Folk Tradition

Jaborandi induces salivation and stimulates most gland secretions, and was at one time used to promote hair growth. Older herbal sources record its use for psoriasis, prurigo, deafness, chronic catarrh, tonsillitis, and dropsy. Its recorded actions include antiseptic, diaphoretic, emmenagogue, galactagogue, and nerve stimulant.

Safety

The leaf and its derivatives are toxic when taken orally and irritating to skin. The plant is abortifacient and must not be used during pregnancy.
