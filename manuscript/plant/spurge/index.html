Spurge (Euphorbia spp.) is a large and diverse genus of plants united by their acrid, toxic white latex sap — cited among the ingredients of the traditional witch's flying ointment for its powerful skin-penetrating and vesicant properties.

⚠️
POISON: All spurges produce caustic white latex sap. Skin contact causes irritation and blistering; eye contact can cause temporary blindness. Do not ingest. Handle with gloves.

Magickal Properties

Planet: Saturn / Mars

Element: Fire

Powers: Purification, Protection, Exorcism, Banishing

Magickal Uses

  • Purification and exorcism: Historically burned (with caution — fumes are toxic) or placed around thresholds to drive out evil and purify a space.
  • Protection: Caper spurge (E. lathyris) planted in gardens as a deterrent — folk tradition holds it repels moles, insects, and malevolent influences alike.
  • Banishing: Used in workings intended to drive away persistent negative presences or unwanted people.

The Witch's Flying Ointment

Spurge appears among the plants named in historical flying ointment formulas from the 1500s and 1600s. Its inclusion is almost certainly related to the powerful skin-penetrating properties of euphorbin, the active compound in the latex sap. As a vesicant (a substance that causes blistering and increased skin permeability), spurge sap would have dramatically enhanced the absorption of other active compounds in a topical preparation — effectively acting as a pharmacological delivery mechanism for the other ingredients.

Botanical Description

The Euphorbia genus contains over 2,000 species, from tiny annual weeds to large cactus-like succulents. The species most commonly associated with European herbalism and witchcraft are the herbaceous spurges of fields and woodlands — sun spurge (E. helioscopia), petty spurge (E. peplus), caper spurge (E. lathyris), and wood spurge (E. amygdaloides). All share the characteristic acrid white latex sap.

Historical Use

Despite its toxicity, spurge was used medicinally as a purgative, a wart remover, and a treatment for skin conditions. Modern research has confirmed that ingenol mebutate, derived from petty spurge, is an effective treatment for actinic keratosis — a precancerous skin condition — and is now used in pharmaceutical preparations.
