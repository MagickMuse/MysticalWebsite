Catnip (Nepeta cataria) is a perennial herb with heart-shaped serrated leaves and small white or lavender flowers.

History

While cats have long had a celebrated affinity for this herb, catnip also has a well-established place in folk medicine as a carminative used to ease digestive discomfort. Its medicinal and magickal uses have been documented among ancient Romans, Indigenous Americans, and European colonists alike.

Characteristics

A common perennial, catnip spreads readily along roadsides and footpaths. Its flowering tops are best known for inducing a state of playful euphoria in cats. Pleasant-tasting and mildly sedating, it is also used as a sleep aid. Originally native to Europe, Asia, and Africa, it has since naturalized across much of the world.

Magickal Properties

  • Powers: Cat Magic, Love, Beauty, Happiness

Folk Names

Cat, Catmint, Catwort, Field Balm, Nepeta

Magickal Uses

For warriors and fierceness: Catnip was once chewed by warriors before battle to inspire fierceness and courage.

For magickal books: Dry large leaves to use as bookmarks in magickal books.

For cat magic: Large cats as well as small ones are attracted to this plant. Give catnip to your cat to create a psychic link between the two of you.

For love: Catnip is used in love sachets, usually in conjunction with rose petals. If you hold catnip in your hand until it is warm, then hold anyone else's hand, they will forever be your friend, as long as you keep the catnip you used for the spell in some safe place.

For beauty and happiness: Grown near the home or hung over the door, catnip attracts good spirits and great luck. It is also used in spells designed to enhance beauty and happiness.

For familiars: Catnip strengthens the bond with cat familiars and is used in workings involving cat magics.
