Devil's Shoestring (Viburnum alnifolium) is a powerful protective root used extensively in hoodoo and folk magick traditions.

⚠️ Hoodoo is a closed practice meant only for those within the originating culture.

Magickal Properties

Gender: Masculine

Planet: Saturn

Element: Earth

Powers: Protection, Binding, Job Security, Luck

Magickal Uses

For binding evil: Devil's shoestring is named for its ability to bind up evil forces, trip up malevolent spirits, and tie down harmful magick. The root literally ties up negative influences, preventing them from reaching you.

For protection: Carried in mojo bags (especially The Devil's Hand) to bind, scare off, and drive away evil. Nine pieces are traditionally used in protective charms.

For job security: Place pieces of devil's shoestring in your shoes or carry in your pocket to keep steady employment. The binding power keeps you tied to your job and prevents you from being "tripped up" by workplace difficulties.

As a protective amulet: Wear devil's shoestring root around the neck or carry in a charm bag for continuous protection against harmful spirits, spells, and crossed conditions.

Against poison: Wear a whole, uncut devil's shoestring around the neck to protect against poison.

For gamblers' luck: Rub devil's shoestring directly onto lottery tickets, racing forms, and other gambling cards and papers to draw fortune.
