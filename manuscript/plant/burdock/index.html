Burdock (Arctium lappa) is a large, biennial herb cultivated for centuries in Asia, where its roots are prized as food. Throughout much of the rest of the world, it is treated as a common weed.

History

The name burdock has roots in both Greek and Celtic language. The genus name Arctium derives from the Greek word for "bear," while the species name lappa comes from the Celtic word meaning "to seize" -- a fitting nod to the plant's notorious seeds.

Characteristics

Burdock is a nutritive and remarkably versatile herb. Nearly every part of the plant -- roots, seeds, leaves, and flower stems -- has been used in folk medicine to support the immune system, and all are rich in vitamins and minerals. Its most distinguishing feature is its seed pods, which cling aggressively to clothing, fur, and skin and are notoriously difficult to remove. These clingy burrs are the real-world inspiration behind the invention of Velcro. As a biennial, burdock completes its life cycle over the course of two years.

Magickal Uses

For protection: Cast around the home to ward off negativity. Add to protection incenses and use in such spells. Gather burdock roots in the waning Moon, dry and then cut them into small pieces. String these on red thread (like beads) and wear for protection against evil and negativity. Sprinkle in the corners of your magick room to ward off negativity.

For purification: Used as a purifying agent in incense and herbal waters. Can be steeped in the mop water used for cleaning floors as a purification measure.

For healing: The leaves of the burdock, when laid to the soles of the feet, help to cure gout.

Magickal Teas

Burdock root can be brewed as a magickal tea to reinforce protection, aid in purification, and ward negativity. Drink 1 to 2 cups only during the spell event itself.
