Silver Fir (Abies alba) is a relatively small coniferous tree with a regular pyramidal shape and a silvery white bark, grown chiefly for timber and as a Christmas tree.

Origin & Distribution

Native to north European mountainous regions; cultivated mainly in Switzerland, Poland, Germany, France, Austria, and especially the former Yugoslavia.

Related Species

Oils distilled from the twigs and needles of various members of the coniferous families Abies, Larix, Picea, Spruce, Pinus, and Tsuga are all commonly called fir needle oil, so the specific botanical name matters. Other notable Abies species include Canadian Balsam (A. balsamifera), the Siberian fir (A. siberica) which is the most popular fir needle oil in Europe and the USA due to its fine fragrance, and the Japanese firs A. mayriana and A. sachalinensis.

Source Book Notes

  • Birth tree of Northern Europe: A flaming fir candle was whirled three times around a birthing bed soon after delivery to bless and protect mother and child.
  • Protective smoke: Burning silver fir needles throughout childbirth keeps mother and child protected.
  • Sacred to the Moon Goddess: In Greece this tree was sacred to the Moon Goddess.
  • Celtic year: Silver Fir ruled the first day of the Celtic year — December 24.
  • Druantia, Queen of the Druids: There was a Gallic Fir Goddess, Druantia, called "Queen of the Druids," who was the mother of the tree calendar.
  • Associated deities: Artemis, Selene, Athena, Druantia.

Folk Tradition

Silver fir is highly esteemed on the Continent for its medicinal virtues and its fragrant scent. It is used mainly for respiratory complaints, fever, and muscular and rheumatic pain.

Meaning When Gifted

When silver fir is gifted it carries the meaning of time.
