Tea tree is a potent healer and protective herb used in both practical and magickal applications.

History

Despite sharing a name, tea tree has no botanical connection to the Camellia sinensis plant that produces black, white, and green tea. Its common name originates from 1770, when sailors from Captain James Cook's crew brewed an infusion from its aromatic leaves after anchoring off the coast of New South Wales. Indigenous Australians had long before discovered its value as a topical antiseptic. During World War II, tea tree oil found industrial use as a lubricant in munitions factories, and its antimicrobial properties helped reduce infections among workers injured by machinery.

Characteristics

Tea tree is most commonly encountered as a distilled essential oil obtained by steam-processing the leaves. A powerful antiseptic, it is used topically to treat fungal infections and repel insects. Its volatile oil must always be diluted before skin application — undiluted tea tree oil is potent enough to cause significant skin damage.

Magickal Properties

Element: Fire

Powers: Healing, Protection

Magickal Uses

  • Protective mirror charm: Place a mirror on your windowsill and charge it with a single drop of tea tree oil to create an effective ward that reflects and deflects negative energy.
  • Space protection: Bundle tea tree leaves and hang them around a dwelling or room to establish a protective boundary and purify the space.
  • Healing work: Draw on tea tree's powerful antiseptic and healing energy in workings focused on recovery, cleansing, and restoration of health.

Beauty & Skincare

Tea tree is a powerful antiseptic herb widely incorporated into skincare products for blemish-prone complexions. Its antimicrobial properties are effective against acne-causing bacteria and support the skin's ability to heal from minor wounds and irritation. Always dilute tea tree oil before skin application, as the undiluted essential oil is potent enough to cause significant irritation.
