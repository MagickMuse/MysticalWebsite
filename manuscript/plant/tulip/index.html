Additional Notes

The tulip's chalice shape holds blessings, making it an excellent vessel for collecting positive energy and intentions. Fill a tulip with morning dew for especially potent blessing water.

Folk Medicine

Tulip flowers have been used as a poultice for bites, stings, itching, and general skin irritation. Taken internally, they have served as a diuretic and an antiseptic, useful for coughs and colds. Red and purple tulip petals were once used as blush or lip tint.

Fairies and Elves

Tulips are beloved flowers to fairies and elves, who protect both the blooms and those who grow them. The flower carries currents of love and renewal, and a tulip kept in the garden or on the altar invites the small folk to watch over the household.

Origin of the Name

The name Tulip derives from Tulipan, a word for turban, owing to the turban-like form of the tulip's flower.

Dragon Tears and the Ottoman Tulip

Tulips were prized blooms among the Ottoman Empire, and a folk tale walks alongside that reverence. Dragons once dwelled throughout the land of the Ottomans, witnessing battle after battle in which young soldiers were killed. The Turkish dragons were of a gentle nature, and after each battle they would weep over the corpses of the slain. It is said that the very first tulip sprouted from a spot where the dragon's tears fell.

Tulip Mania

Tulip bulbs were introduced to Europe in 1562, when a Flemish merchant found them among a shipment of cloth, mistook them for onions, ate half, and planted the rest in his garden bed. By the spring of 1563 those bulbs were blooming, and the neighborhood took notice. By 1634, full speculative fever had broken out around the tulip trade, with people spending one hundred thousand florins to acquire forty bulbs. In 1635, a single bulb of Admiral Liefken sold for 4,400 florins, Admiral Van der Eyck for 1,260, Viceroy for 3,000, and the highly sought Semper Augustus reached 5,500. For scale, one thousand pounds of cheese went for 120 florins at the time, and an entire suit of clothing could be purchased for 80.

Thumbelina

In an old fairy tale, a woman who longed for a child went to a witch who told her to plant a little seed of barley corn in a flowerpot. The woman tended the pot, and out of it grew a little plant that looked like a tulip. She kissed the red and yellow petals, and at her kiss the flower burst open. In the middle of the blossom, on the green velvety petals, sat a tiny girl scarcely half a thumb in height, and so they called her Thumbelina. A polished walnut shell served as her cradle, blue violet petals as her mattress, and a rose leaf as her coverlet. By day she sailed across a bowl of water on a great tulip petal, rowing herself with two white horsehairs for oars, and sang in a voice softer and sweeter than any that had been heard before.

Varieties

There are nearly three thousand different types of tulips, in nearly every color of the rainbow. Botanists have tried to create a blue tulip, but have only succeeded in shades of purple.

Meaning When Gifted

When a tulip is gifted it carries the meaning of ardent love.

  • General Tulip: Perfect lover, fame, the emblem of Holland
  • Red Tulip: "Believe me," a declaration of love
  • Variegated Tulip: Beautiful eyes
  • Yellow Tulip: "There's sunshine in your smile"
