Botanical Information

Jasminum officinale (Oleaceae family). Feminine flowering vine and bush originating from West Asia, South Asia, and East Asia. Night-blooming flowers picked by hand for use in teas, perfumes, and magickal work.

Herbal Lore

One of the most recognized florals across cultures. Pale blossoms open at night, preserving fragrant oils from daytime heat while attracting nocturnal pollinators like moths and beetles. This night-blooming habit creates strong associations with moon goddesses, darkness, and lunar energies. Jasmine green tea preserves the delicate flavor through traditional drying methods with fresh flowers.

Historical cost made jasmine exclusive to royalty and clergy, cementing associations with wealth, prosperity, and spiritual connection. Traditional use in love spells, particularly "Come to Me" blends, spans centuries across magickal traditions.

Botanical Source Detail

  • Scientific name: Jasminum officinale
  • Family: Oleaceae
  • Type: Evergreen shrub or vine reaching up to ten metres high
  • Description: Delicate bright green leaves and star-shaped, intensely fragrant white flowers.
  • Synonyms: Jasmin, jessamine, common jasmine, poet's jessamine

Distribution

Native to China, northern India, and west Asia. Cultivated through the Mediterranean region, China, and India depending on the exact species. The concrete (the precursor of the absolute) is produced in Italy, France, Morocco, Egypt, China, Japan, Algeria, and Turkey; the absolute is mainly produced in France.

Other Species

Many species of jasmine are used in medicine and perfumery. Beyond the common jasmine, the most widespread varieties are the royal or Italian jasmine (J. grandiflorum), grown in the Mediterranean region, and its Eastern counterpart J. officinale var. grandiflorum or J. auriculatum. J. sambac is the variety whose dried flowers flavour jasmine tea, and whose blossoms are used in Chinese herbal medicine for conjunctivitis, dysentery, skin ulcers, and tumours.

Folk Medicine

In China the flowers of J. officinale var. grandiflorum are used to treat hepatitis, liver cirrhosis, and dysentery, while the flowers of J. sambac address conjunctivitis, dysentery, skin ulcers, and tumours. The root is used for headaches, insomnia, dislocation pain, and rheumatism. In Western herbal tradition the common jasmine was held to warm the womb and facilitate birth, to ease cough and difficulty of breathing, to disperse crude humours, and to suit cold and catarrhous constitutions but not hot ones. It was also used for hard, contracted limbs and for problems of the nervous and reproductive systems.

Therapeutic Actions

Mild analgesic, antidepressant, anti-inflammatory, antiseptic, antispasmodic, aphrodisiac, carminative, cicatrisant, expectorant, galactagogue, parturient, sedative, and uterine tonic.
