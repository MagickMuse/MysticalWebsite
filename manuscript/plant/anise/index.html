Botanical Description

Aniseed is an annual herb that grows under a metre tall, with delicate, finely cut leaves and small white flowers carried in umbels. The plant is native to Greece and Egypt and is now cultivated mostly in India and China, with smaller production in Mexico and Spain.

Related Species

Several chemotypes of aniseed exist depending on the country of origin, and the plant should never be confused with star anise, which belongs to a completely different family.

Folk Tradition

Aniseed is widely used as a domestic spice, with its volatile oil providing the basis for most of its medicinal reputation. It has been reached for in cases of dry irritable coughs, bronchitis, and whooping cough, and the seed has historically been added to smoking mixtures. Aniseed tea is taken for infant catarrh, flatulence, colic, and griping pains, as well as for painful periods and to encourage breast milk. In Turkey, the popular alcoholic drink raki is made from the seed.

Magickal Properties

Powers: Protection, Purification, Youth

Magickal Uses

Fill a small pillowcase with anise seeds and sleep on it. This will ensure that you have no nightmares. Use in protection and meditation incenses. Fresh anise leaves placed in a room will drive off evil, and they are sometimes placed around the magick circle to protect the magician from evil spirits. It also averts the evil eye.

Anise seed is also used in purification baths, especially with bay leaves. It is used to call forth spirits to aid in magickal operations, and a sprig hung on the bedpost will restore lost youth.

Anisette Liqueur

Anisette is a liqueur distilled from anise (Pimpinella anisum). It gained popularity following the ban of absinthe as its taste is somewhat similar, although its alcohol content is much lower. Like wormwood, anise guards against malevolent magick.

Anisette was particularly beloved by spiritualists because it is believed to be particularly appealing to dead souls. This makes it a popular component of séances, necromantic summoning spells, and ancestral offerings. Anisette allegedly serves as an invitation, summoning ghosts and ancestors to visit.

A glass of anisette, served neat or diluted with water, is often incorporated into séances, Dumb Suppers, or ancestral altars. Like absinthe, anisette is often diluted with water before use.

Spirit Water is a further dilution used specifically for beckoning spirits. Add a tablespoon or splash of anisette to a glass of spring water and place it on an altar to beckon the spirits.

Anisette, like absinthe, was often identified as a woman's drink. True anisette is a distilled drink. Commercial anisettes are readily available, and homemade infusions of anise are easily concocted.

Aphrodisiac Properties

Aniseed aids digestion and sweetens the breath, which could explain why the Romans considered it a useful ingredient for seduction.

Amulet Uses

Anise seed carried as an amulet offers protection against poverty.

Anise and Star Anise

True anise and Star Anise have little in common botanically, yet magickally both induce and enhance psychic power. Ground together and burned, they make a double dose of psychic power (see Double Anise Psychic Incense).
