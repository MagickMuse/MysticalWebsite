Basil (Ocimum basilicum) is a fragrant culinary herb with powerful magickal properties, especially for love and protection.

History

Basil has been cultivated and revered for four to five thousand years. In ancient Greece it was considered a royal herb, a reputation likely tied to its name: the Greek word basileus means king. In Hinduism, basil holds a sacred place and is treated as a divine plant. The Greek Orthodox tradition uses basil as a purifying herb in the preparation of holy water. Across parts of Europe, basil has also served as a sacred offering to the dead, with bundles sometimes placed in the hands of the deceased to help ensure safe passage into the spirit realm. The name basil shares its royal Greek and Roman roots with the Basilisk, and the herb was once believed able to counteract that creature's venom.

Characteristics

Basil belongs to the mint family and is both highly aromatic and visually appealing, used as an ornamental as well as a culinary herb. It is a low-growing perennial with glossy light green leaves and small white flowers. Basil thrives in warm, dry conditions.

Botanical Varieties

Basil's many varieties have been cultivated for cuisine, medicine, and ritual all over the world. Two principal chemotypes dominate essential oil production:

  • French (or "true" sweet) Basil: the European-grown chemotype valued for its delicate scent and broad therapeutic use. Linalool-rich and gentle, this is the type favored in aromatherapy.
  • Exotic Basil: a tropical chemotype grown chiefly on the Comoro Islands and Madagascar (sometimes called Comoran or Reunion basil). It is high in methyl chavicol, has a coarser scent, and is reserved for fragrance and flavoring rather than therapeutic use.

Other beloved cousins include Holy Basil (Ocimum sanctum, the sacred tulsi of Indian tradition), Bush Basil (O. minimum), Camphor Basil (O. kilimanjaricum), Fever Plant (O. viride), East Indian or Shrubby Basil (O. gratissimum), and the Hairy Basil (O. canum) of East Africa, whose oils (rich in methyl cinnamate or camphor) are produced in West and East Africa, India, the West Indies, and Indonesia.

Magickal Uses

The scent of fresh basil causes sympathy between two people, which is why it is used to soothe tempers between lovers. Add to love incenses and sachets, and rub fresh leaves against the skin as a natural love perfume. In Eastern Europe, a young man would love any woman from whose hand he accepted a sprig of basil.

For love divination, place two fresh basil leaves on a live coal. If they lie still and burn quickly to ashes, the marriage will be harmonious. If there is crackling, the relationship will have quarrels. If the leaves fly apart with fierce crackling, avoid the relationship. To test fidelity, lay fresh basil on a person's hand; by folk tradition it will wither immediately if they have been unfaithful.

Basil brings wealth when carried in pockets and attracts customers to businesses when placed in cash registers or on doorsteps. Strewn on floors, basil ensures no evil can live where it lies. Use in exorcism incenses and purification baths. Small amounts placed in each room bring protection. Witches traditionally drank basil juice before magical flight.

⚠️ In quantities above average food use, not recommended for pregnant or nursing mothers, infants, or toddlers.

Additional Notes

Basil's documented use spans over 5,000 years, originating in India and spreading westward to the Mediterranean. Its sacred status in both Hindu tradition (dedicated to Vishnu) and Western love magic demonstrates its cross-cultural spiritual importance.

A medieval Italian tale tells of Isabetta, whose beloved Lorenzo was murdered by her brothers. His ghost revealed his burial place, and in her grief, Isabetta buried his severed head in a pot of basil, watering it with her tears until she died of sorrow. This story established basil's enduring association with grief, love, and remembrance.

In courtship traditions, young men wore basil sprigs in their hats to signal matrimonial intentions. Basil leaves were also used to test virginity and fidelity, withering immediately in the hands of the unfaithful.

When visiting a new home, gift a potted basil plant to bless the dwelling with love, prosperity, and protection. Fresh-cut basil kept on the counter serves dual purposes: culinary readiness and continuous spiritual protection through its natural exorcism properties.

Love Manifestation Ritual

Plant 9 basil seeds in soil and tend them daily with conversation. Share your day, emotions, hopes, and dreams with the growing plants. This practice demonstrates to love deities that you possess both readiness for love and the capacity to nurture a relationship. The spell works by signaling availability rather than targeting specific individuals, honoring free will while opening pathways for compatible partnerships.

Child Blessing

During naming ceremonies and wiccanings, use fresh basil leaves to sprinkle blessed water upon children, invoking love, protection, and prosperity for their lives.

Medicinal Uses

Basil is a tonic herb, meaning it can be used regularly over long periods without adverse effects. Its primary medicinal contribution is digestive support: basil helps calm the stomach, ease cramping, and encourage healthy gut function. It has also been used historically as a mild antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory agent, and the leaves have been applied to help heal wounds. Because tonic herbs work gradually, basil is most effective when incorporated consistently into daily life rather than taken in isolated doses.

Culinary Uses

Parts used: Leaves, fresh or dried

Basil is a cornerstone of kitchen witchery, bringing peace, happiness, and protective energy to meals. Used in pesto, sauces, soups, stews, and salads.

Moon Magick

Key themes for lunar workings: Love, protection, wealth.

Royal Herb of Personal Power

The name basil comes from the same Greek root words as "royal" and "king." Basil stimulates personal power, and its fresh leaves and essential oil are both used in psychic power spells (see Regal Basil Power Oil and Royal Wreath of Psychic Power Oil). The essential oil smells very different from the fresh leaves, but it too possesses profound gifts.
