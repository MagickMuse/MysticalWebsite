Daffodil (Narcissus pseudonarcissus) is a spring-blooming bulb with trumpet-shaped yellow or white flowers.

Magickal Properties

Planet: Venus

Deities: Persephone

Powers: Love, Luck, Fertility

Magickal Uses

For love: Daffodils are used in love spells and carried to attract love. The flowers placed in a room bring good luck in love.

For luck: Wear a daffodil next to the heart to draw good fortune.

For fertility: Associated with spring's return and new life, daffodils are used in fertility workings and to celebrate renewal.

Safety Notes

⚠️ Poison: All parts of the daffodil are toxic if ingested. Handle with care and do not consume. Narcissus species must never be consumed in cooking or teas, and daffodils are known to shorten the life of other cut flowers when arranged with them.

Connection with the Dead

Underworld transition: The heavy scent of daffodils carries a sedative quality and assists in the transition to the Underworld.

Speaking with the departed: Use daffodils to connect with those who have passed away.

Pioneer graves: In the foothills of California, remote pioneer cemeteries left over from the Gold Rush hide among the pines. On many of these graves, bright yellow daffodils bloom every spring. In much of Celtic fairy mythology, the Otherworld and the Underworld are one and the same.

Self-Love and Resilience

Self-love: Daffodils are used in rituals for self-love and healing.

Resilience: Because daffodils naturalize, returning year after year and spreading on their own, they carry a quality of resilience. Employ them magickally to overcome obstacles and to find your place in the world.

Cup of Tears

The cup at the center of the flower is said to hold the tears of Narcissus, the woodland spirit whose myth gives the genus its name. Where his body sank into the ground, the narcissus flower bloomed the following spring. In some tellings the flowers spring directly from his corpse.

Wreaths of the Fates and Eumenides

The Fates wore wreaths of narcissus. The Greeks twined the white stars of the odorous blossoms among the locks of the Erinyes, deities of revenge also known as the Furies. Crowns of narcissus were woven in honor of these vengeful gods and placed upon the heads of the dead.

National Flower of Wales

The daffodil is the national flower of Wales, an honor it may owe to a tangle of language. In Welsh the leek and the daffodil share the same name, and the leek was famously worn by Welsh soldiers as a mark of recognition in a battle against the Saxons, so the bright spring flower came to share in the distinction.

Funeral Flower of Egypt

In ancient Egypt the daffodil was a favored bloom for funeral wreaths, and its bulbs were laid over the mouth, nose, and eyes of the mummy, a sign of the deep sleep that eating the bulbs was known to bring.

Related Species

Daffodil (Narcissus pseudonarcissus) is the most common member of the Narcissus genus, which contains more than fifty species and an endless number of hybrids. Narcissus jonquilla, called jonquil or jonquil daffodils, bears smaller double flowers in greater numbers. Paperwhites, often referred to as narcissus, are the most fragrant of the bulbs and are commonly forced in pebbles indoors around the holidays. All daffodils and jonquils are narcissus, but not all narcissus are daffodils or jonquils.

Caution

Many witches consider daffodils a powerful ingredient in love spells and beauty work, but caution that the practitioner should not go too far.

Meaning When Gifted

When daffodil is gifted it carries the meaning of regard and the sense that "you're the only one," along with unrequited love.
