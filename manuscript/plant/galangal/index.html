Galangal (Alpina officinalum or A. galanga) is a rhizome closely related to ginger, with a spicy, aromatic flavor and pungent scent.

Magickal Properties

Gender: Masculine

Planet: Mars

Element: Fire

Powers: Protection, Lust, Health, Money, Psychic Powers, Hex-Breaking

Magickal Uses

For protection and luck: Galangal has been used for many different magical needs. Worn or carried it protects its bearer and draws good luck. The root is aromatic and used in potpourris, incenses, and perfumes; its odor and taste are similar to ginger.

For knowledge and guardian angels: Galangal is used in rituals for knowledge and for conversation with guardian angels.

For money: Placed in a sachet of leather with silver it brings money.

For hex-breaking: Powdered galangal is burned to break spells and curses.

For lust: It is also carried or sprinkled around the home to promote lust.

For psychic powers and health: Worn, galangal aids psychic development and guards the bearer's health.

Substitution: If galangal is unavailable, ginger, which is part of the same family, can be substituted.

Botanical Description

A reed-like plant of the Zingiberaceae family, reaching about 1 metre in height, with irregularly branched rhizomes that are red or brown on the outside and light orange within.

Origin & Distribution

Native to south east China, especially the island of Hainan. Cultivated in China, Indonesia, Thailand, and Japan.

Folk Tradition (Medicinal)

Used as a local spice, especially in curries, and employed in perfumery in India. The root is current in the British Herbal Pharmacopoeia, indicated for dyspepsia, flatulence, colic, nausea, and vomiting.

Related Species

Similar species grow in Malaysia, Java, India, and elsewhere. Closely related to Ginger (Zingiber officinale) and to the large galanga (Galanga officinalis). Should not be confused with the dried rhizomes of Kaempferia galanga, known as kentjoer, which are used in Malaysia for medicinal purposes and for flavouring curry. See also Galangal Essential Oil.
