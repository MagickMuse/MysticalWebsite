Dill (Anethum graveolens) is an annual herb with feathery leaves and umbrella-like flower clusters.

Magickal Properties

Gender: Masculine

Planet: Mercury

Element: Fire

Powers: Protection, Money, Lust, Love

Magickal Uses

Against witchcraft: The dried ripe fruits were used by medieval magicians in spells and charms against witchcraft.

For home protection: Tie up the dried seed heads and hang them as protection for your home and property. Use the seeds in protection sachets and incenses.

Anise confusion: Some mistake dill for anise, whose flower cluster also has an umbrella shape. Anise, however, has a distinctively different odor.

Folk medicine — digestive cordial: Use the seed to make a cordial to relieve digestive problems, flatulence, headaches, and insomnia. In medieval times seeds were chewed to freshen the breath and allay hunger pains. Dill water was given to babies for colic.

For protection: Dill, when hung at the door, protects the home. It is also used in protection sachets, and when placed in the cradle, protects children.

For money: Use dill in money spells.

For love and lust: Add to love mixtures and food to cause lust. The smell of dill is said to stimulate lust.

To prevent hexes: No one can hex you when you carry a bag of dill over the heart.

Culinary Uses

Parts used: Seeds and feathery leaves

Dill brings good fortune and protective energy to the table. It is commonly used in soups, stews, and savory dishes.

Botanical Description

Dill is an annual or biennial herb up to 1 metre high with a smooth stem, feathery leaves, and umbels of yellowish flowers followed by flat small seeds.

Origin & Distribution

Native to the Mediterranean and Black Sea regions; now cultivated worldwide, especially in Europe, the USA, China, and India. Dill seed oil is mainly produced in Europe (France, Hungary, Germany, England, Spain); dill weed oil is mainly produced in the USA.

Related Species

Indian or East Indian dill (A. sowa) is widely cultivated in the east, especially in India and Japan. A commercial oil is produced from its seed which has a different chemical composition and contains 'dill apiol'.

Folk Tradition

Dill has been used since the earliest times as both a medicinal and culinary herb. In Germany and Scandinavia it is used with fish and cucumber, and the seeds baked in bread. In the west and east, dill is used as a soothing digestive aid for indigestion, wind, colic, and similar complaints, especially in children, a use still current in the British Herbal Pharmacopoeia.

Meaning When Gifted

When dill is gifted it carries the meaning of lust.
