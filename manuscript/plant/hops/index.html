Hops (Humulus lupulus) are soothing cones whose gentle spirit brings rest, supports healing, and opens the way between worlds.

History

Best known for their role in brewing ales and beer, hops have been in use across Europe since the eleventh century. Their primary importance to brewing was as a natural preservative and a prized flavoring agent. Commercial cultivation in the United States began in Massachusetts in 1791.

Characteristics

Hops is a perennial plant capable of growing back year after year when the root is left undisturbed. The bines — the climbing stems — can reach lengths of one and a half to two feet. The plant is most recognizable by its yellowish-green cones. Medicinally, hops has been used to stimulate appetite, increase urination, and relieve indigestion, as well as to address bladder infections and intestinal cramping.

Magickal Properties

Gender: Masculine (source: Hot)

Powers: Healing, Sleep

Magickal Uses

  • Sleep: A pillow stuffed with dried hops brings sleep and rest. A tenth-century Saxon prescription recommends this practice for sound, restful sleep.
  • Healing: Used in healing sachets and incenses.
  • Trance magick: Hops is used in trance workings to shift consciousness and access in-between states of awareness.
  • Sabbat incense: Burned as incense, hops creates a between-the-worlds state of consciousness, making it a fitting burnt offering or altar offering for sabbat rituals.

Additional Notes

Prayers for Physical Healing

Burn hops over incense charcoal while the smoke lifts your prayers for the health and healing of you, your friend, or loved one. Consider adding other herbs to improve the smell of the incense as well as magickal function:

  • Clove for luck and happiness
  • Cedar for physical strength
  • Rosemary for joy and harmony

Relaxation

Hops are a powerful sedative; they can provide a restful respite during trying times. This is one of the reasons beer is associated with relaxing after a stressful day or strenuous labor—it is physically as well as emotionally sedating.

Hops Infusion:

  • 1 teaspoon dried hop flowers
  • 8 ounces boiling water

Let steep for 5 minutes, then strain out the plant material. Sweeten if desired; hops are bitter. Slowly sip the infusion while breathing deeply of the aroma. Feel yourself relaxing, lowering your shoulders, and releasing tension in the body.

Safety: Not recommended internally if you have depression.

Magickal Teas

Hops can be brewed as a magickal tea to bring comfort, promote good health, encourage relaxation, and support overall well-being. Drink 1 to 2 cups only during the spell event itself.

Botanical Source Detail

  • Scientific name: Humulus lupulus
  • Family: Moraceae
  • Type: Perennial creeping, twining herb growing up to eight metres high
  • Description: Bears male and female flowers on separate plants. Dark green, heart-shaped leaves and greeny-yellow flowers. The cones, called "strobiles," contain glandular hairs in which a volatile oil called lupulin forms.
  • Synonyms: Common hop, European hop, lupulus

Distribution

Native to Europe and North America. Cultivated worldwide, especially in the USA (California and Washington), Yugoslavia, and Germany. The essential oil is mainly produced in France, the UK, and Germany.

Related Species

Botanically related to the common stinging nettle (Urtica dioica) and to the fig (Ficus carica).

Folk Medicine

Best known as a nerve remedy for insomnia, nervous tension, and neuralgia, and also for sexual neurosis in both sexes. Supports female oestrogens and is useful for amenorrhoea (heavy periods). The traditional hop pillow exploits the heavy aromatic odour for direct action at the olfactory centres; the volatile aromatic component is the most active fraction. Hops have also been used for heart disease and stomach and liver complaints, including bacterial dysentery. In China the herb is used for pulmonary tuberculosis and cystitis. Hops remain current in the British Herbal Pharmacopoeia as a specific for restlessness with nervous headaches and indigestion.

Therapeutic Actions

Anodyne, aphrodisiac, antimicrobial, antiseptic, antispasmodic, astringent, bactericidal, carminative, diuretic, emollient, hypnotic, nervine, sedative, soporific, and possessing oestrogenic properties.
