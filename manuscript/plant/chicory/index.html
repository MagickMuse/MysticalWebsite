Chicory (Cichorium intybus) is a hardy plant with bright blue flowers that open in the morning.

History

Native to Europe, chicory carries a particular association with New Orleans -- a city with deep roots in folk magick and witchcraft. It has been employed in traditional medicine as a poultice for inflammatory conditions and has long served as a culinary green.

Characteristics

A perennial member of the daisy family also known as succory, chicory grows to around three feet in height. Its roasted root is a well-known additive to coffee, and its leaves appear in salads and cooked dishes. Striking blue flowers bloom along its stems and can be spotted growing wild throughout much of North America.

Magickal Properties

  • Planet: Sun
  • Powers: Removing Obstacles, Invisibility, Favors, Frugality

Folk Names

Blue Sailors, Succory

Magickal Uses

For removing obstacles: Chicory is used to remove obstacles from one's path. Carry the root or anoint the body with chicory juice.

For invisibility: According to old grimoires, if chicory is gathered with a golden knife in silence at noon or midnight on St. James' Day (July 25), it will make the bearer invisible and open locked boxes.

For frugality: The dried, roasted root is used as a coffee substitute and magically promotes thrift and careful spending.

For love: Chicory is used in love magick, particularly when emotional balance is needed. If one finds themselves becoming too consumed by or preoccupied with a love interest, carrying chicory can help restore a sense of center and perspective.

Meaning When Gifted

When chicory is gifted it carries the meaning of frugality.
