Eucalyptus (Eucalyptus globulus) is a tall evergreen tree with aromatic leaves and distinctive peeling bark.

Magickal Properties

  • Gender: Feminine
  • Planet: Moon
  • Element: Water (source: Air)
  • Powers: Healing, Protection
  • Entities: Apollo, Thoth, Brigid

Folk Names

Blue Gum Tree, Stringy Bark Tree

Magickal Uses

For healing: The leaves are stuffed into healing poppets and small pillows and carried to maintain good health. The oil of the leaves is a powerful antiseptic that can be used to sterilize instruments. It also helps fight off colds and flu germs if burned in an aromatherapy vessel. Ring blue candles (existing entry says green — source says blue) with the leaves and burn for healing vibrations. To relieve colds, ring green candles with the pods and leaves and burn completely while visualizing the person as completely healthy.

For protection: The leaves and pods are also strung and hung around the house for protection, and the leaves carried for the same purpose.

For reversing harm: Eucalyptus has powerful reversing properties. Carry eucalyptus leaves in a reversing mojo bag (such as A Reversing Hand) to turn spells and harm back upon the sender. The leaves symbolically reverse the direction of negative energy.

As a protective amulet: String eucalyptus leaves and pods together and wear as a necklace or carry in a charm bag for protection against psychic attack and to reverse any harmful magick sent your way back to its source.

Botanical Information

  • Scientific name: Eucalyptus globulus var. globulus
  • Family: Myrtaceae
  • Type: Tree (leaf and flower used)
  • Height: up to 90 metres
  • Description: A tall evergreen. Young trees bear bluish-green oval leaves; mature trees develop long, narrow, yellowish leaves, creamy-white flowers, and a smooth pale-grey bark often coated in a white powdery bloom.

Distribution

Native to Tasmania and mainland Australia. Most commercial oil today is produced in Spain and Portugal, with additional cultivation in Brazil, California, Russia, and China. Very little oil now comes from its native range. Wood is also harvested for timber, especially in Spain.

Other Species

There are over 700 eucalyptus species, of which at least 500 yield an essential oil. Source-listed varieties fall into three broad chemical categories:

  • Medicinal (cineol-rich): Blue Gum (E. globulus), Blue Mallee (E. polybractea), Narrow-Leaved Peppermint (E. radiata var. australiana), Gully Gum (E. smithii).
  • Industrial (piperitone- and phellandrene-rich): Peppermint Eucalyptus (E. piperita), Grey Peppermint (E. radiata var. phellandra), Broad-Leaved Peppermint (E. dives var. Type).
  • Perfumery (citronellal-rich): Lemon-Scented Eucalyptus (E. citriodora).

Folk Medicine

A traditional household remedy across Australia. Leaves and oil are used for respiratory ailments such as bronchitis and croup, and dried leaves were smoked like tobacco for asthma. Also used for feverish conditions (malaria, typhoid, cholera) and skin problems including burns, ulcers, and wounds. Aqueous extracts are applied to aching joints, bacterial dysentery, ringworms, and tuberculosis. Both western and eastern herbal traditions employ it for similar reasons.

Therapeutic Actions

Analgesic, antineuralgic, antirheumatic, antiseptic, antispasmodic, antiviral, balsamic, cicatrisant, decongestant, deodorant, depurative, diuretic, expectorant, febrifuge, hypoglycaemic, parasiticide, prophylactic, rubefacient, stimulant, vermifuge, vulnerary.
