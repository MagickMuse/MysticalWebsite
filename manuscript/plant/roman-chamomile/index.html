Botanical Information

Roman Chamomile (Chamaemelum nobile) belongs to the Asteraceae family. This flowering herb is native to Europe, Western Europe, and the Mediterranean region.

Characteristics

Roman chamomile grows as a low, stocky perennial reaching about 25 cm in height. Its hairy, heavily branched stems half-spread or creep along the ground, the foliage is feathery and finely divided, and the white, daisy-form flowers are noticeably larger than those of the German variety. Crushed or brushed against, the entire plant releases an unmistakable apple-sweet aroma.

Distribution

Native to southern and western Europe and naturalized in North America. Cultivated in England, Belgium, Hungary, the United States, Italy, and France. Of the four chamomile varieties native to the British Isles, Roman chamomile is the only one used therapeutically.

Related Species

Distantly related to Moroccan Chamomile (Ormenis multicaulis), which is marketed under the chamomile name but is botanically and chemically distinct. The Moroccan oil is chemically and olfactorily different from both German and Roman chamomile and is not a substitute for either. See also German Chamomile (Matricaria chamomilla) and Moroccan Chamomile Essential Oil.

Folk Tradition

This herb has held a medical reputation across Europe, and especially the Mediterranean, for over two thousand years, and it remains in widespread use. It was employed by the ancient Egyptians and the Moors, and it was one of the Saxons' nine sacred herbs, who called it "maythen." It was also called the "plant's physician," because it was held to promote the health of nearby plants. It is current in the British Herbal Pharmacopoeia for the treatment of dyspepsia, nausea, anorexia, vomiting in pregnancy, dysmenorrhoea, and specifically flatulent dyspepsia associated with mental stress.

Dual Nature and Associations

Chamomile holds two valid magickal associations depending on how you work with the plant. The bright, sunny appearance of its flowers and apple-like scent create solar associations linked to Apollo. However, the plant's watery, cooling nature and healing properties also connect it strongly to lunar energy and Hypnos. Both associations are supported by research and tradition.

Symbolism & Mythology

The chamomile flower draws its name from a Greek word meaning apple, a nod to the distinct apple scent the plant gives off, especially when its foliage is crushed. The Egyptians held it sacred to the Sun God Ra, and the Teutons in turn dedicated it to their own Sun God, Balder. Its protective reputation runs deep as well: a wreath of chamomile hung in the home is said to guard the house against thunder and lightning.

Financial Magick

Chamomile flowers have long been associated with financial prosperity. Gamblers traditionally washed their hands in chamomile infusions to attract money. The plant is added to love oils to draw a mate who is generous with their finances.

Cooling and Empowering Properties

In herbal medicine, chamomile is recognized as a cooling herb. It subdues hot flashes, excitability, and hysteria. Beyond its cooling nature, chamomile is profoundly empowering. It strengthens the emotionally weak and soothes heated tempers, anxiety, and heightened emotional states.

Magickal Uses

Burn chamomile flowers over incense charcoal for quick purification. Drink chamomile tea while grounding and centering to prepare for magickal work. Wash hands and feet in strong chamomile tea (no sugar) before job interviews, sales meetings, or loan proposals to attract good luck. Keep a sachet of chamomile (preferably purple) at your workspace to deflect bickering. Add Rowan to the sachet to be rewarded for maintaining peace. Fill a muslin tea bag with chamomile flowers and place inside your pillowcase to banish nightmares. Drinking chamomile tea before bed also prevents nightmares. Carry a chamomile sachet in your pocket to draw money for needed physical goods. Drink chamomile tea before difficult conversations to soothe anxiety while maintaining both boldness and tenderness.

Infant Protection

Bunches of chamomile hung above a baby's crib are used as a protective charm, shielding the child from harmful influences and malevolent spirits.

Magickal Teas

Chamomile tea serves as a magickal brew to promote calmness, deepen meditation, attract prosperity, and support purification. Drink 1 to 2 cups only during the spell event itself.
