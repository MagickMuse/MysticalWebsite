Iroko

◇Notes/Warnings
The iroko tree is considered sacred in many African traditions. Approach its use with respect for its cultural and spiritual significance.

Iroko (Milicia excelsa, formerly Chlorophora excelsa) is a large West African hardwood tree held sacred throughout sub-Saharan Africa. Also known as the African oak or Nigerian teak, it is one of the most spiritually significant trees on the continent.

Sacred Status

The iroko tree is venerated across many African cultures as a dwelling place of spirits and a source of immense protective power. Its aura of holiness survived the Middle Passage and is honored within African Diaspora traditions including Yoruba-derived religions. In some diaspora traditions, the ceiba (silk cotton tree) serves as a substitute when iroko is unavailable.

Magickal Uses

Wood from the iroko tree is used as a protective amulet, particularly for newborns and children. A piece of iroko wood hung over a cradle or sleeping area is believed to shield the child from the Evil Eye, malevolent spirits, and harm.
