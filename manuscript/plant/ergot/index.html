Ergot (Claviceps purpurea) is a parasitic fungus that grows on grains, especially rye. The blackish growths (sclerotia) affixed to rye grain are identified in folklore as the Rye Mother's iron nipples, which she gives children to suck so they'll die.

Source Book Notes

This fungus is a disease that attacks grasses, especially rye and wheat. It contains LSD and was used for its hallucinogenic properties in the Eleusinian Mysteries.

⚠️ The author does NOT recommend its use.

The Rye Mother's Long Arm

Folk names like Mutterkorn (Mother Corn), Roggenmutter (Rye Mother), Roggenwolf (Rye Wolf), Tollkorn (mad corn), and Seigle ivre (drunk rye) reflect understanding of ergot as the Rye Mother's dangerous power. The blackish sclerotia on grain are identified as her iron nipples.

Dual Nature: Poison and Medicine

Ergot produces ergotamine alkaloids with both medicinal and toxic properties. In controlled doses it treats hemorrhage, hastens childbirth, and relieves migraines. Midwives historically used it for difficult labors and abortion, though dosage control is extremely difficult. When ergot-contaminated grain is baked into bread, it causes ergotism (St Anthony's Fire): gangrenous ergotism (limb atrophy, burning sensations) in Western Europe or convulsive ergotism (hallucinations, violent cramps, psychosis) in Central/Northern Europe. Effects include infertility, miscarriage, tremors, and death. When heated, ergotamine transforms into LSD—first synthesized by Dr. Albert Hofmann in 1938.

Historical Significance

Ancient Mesopotamians and Greeks documented ergot's effects on fertility. In communities with traditional midwives and shamans, ergot was a known visionary tool. Where witch-hunters eliminated these practitioners, knowledge was lost, leading to epidemics blamed on witchcraft—some historians suggest ergot poisoning caused witch panics including Salem. Major outbreaks occurred in Western Europe during the 1430s-1480s and 1660-1739, and as recently as 1951 in Pont St Esprit, France (6 deaths, 130+ hospitalized). Ergot was officially recognized for medicinal use beginning in 1582, admitted to London Pharmacopoeia in 1836.

Warnings

⚠️ EXTREME DANGER: Ergot is deadly poisonous and cannot be safely self-administered. Requires expert knowledge to use medicinally. The Rye Mother's folklore reflects real danger, her "iron nipples" (ergot growths) kill those who consume them. Knowledge suppression historically led to epidemics and witch panics, creating a vicious cycle of fear and ignorance.
