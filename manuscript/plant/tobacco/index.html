Magickal Uses

  • Purification: Burned as incense to clear spaces of negativity and spirits.

The Sacred Smoke

Among the medicine men of the Americas, tobacco was holy long before Western colonizers ever met it. In their training, initiates drank tobacco juice to call up visions, and the juice was squirted into the eyes to grant the gift of second sight. Smoking was held an even deeper magick than drinking, for it brought the smoker into direct contact with the spirit world, and to pass the pipe around a circle was to seal the unity of the group in a shared vision. The rite opened with the calumet offered first to the sky and then to the earth, after which smoke was breathed toward the four cardinal points to honour the spirits of the directions and the elements, and the smoke was blown over people to heal their ills and to lend them strength.

The ceremony was conducted with great solemnity, in keeping with the sacredness of the herb. When tobacco reached Europe in the sixteenth century, however, it was stripped of that reverence: people fell quickly into addiction, tobacco drinking became a fashion, and by 1610 London alone held as many as seven thousand tobacco shops. Like many of the most sacred plants, once meant for rare and careful use, tobacco is fiercely poisonous, and the vast toll of deaths from its overuse may be read as a sign of how little respect has been paid to a sacred gift of the natural world.
