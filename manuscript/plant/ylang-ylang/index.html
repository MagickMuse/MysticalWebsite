Ylang Ylang (Cananga odorata var. genuina) is a tall tropical tree of the Annonaceae family.

Description

A tall tropical tree up to 20 metres high, with large, tender, fragrant flowers that can be pink, mauve, or yellow. The yellow flowers are considered best for the extraction of essential oil.

Origin & Distribution

Native to tropical Asia, especially Indonesia and the Philippines. Major oil producers are Madagascar, Reunion, and the Comoro Islands.

Folk Tradition

In Indonesia, the flowers are spread on the beds of newly married couples on their wedding night. In the Molucca Islands, an ointment is made from ylang ylang and cucuma flowers in a coconut oil base for cosmetic and hair care, skin diseases, to prevent fever including malaria, and to fight infections. In the Victorian era the oil was used in the popular hair treatment Macassar oil for its stimulating effect on the scalp and its encouragement of hair growth. The oil was also used to soothe insect bites and is reputed to have a regulating effect on cardiac and respiratory rhythm.

Other Species

Cananga odorata yields two commercially distinct varieties: ylang ylang (C. odorata var. genuina), the refined variety profiled here, which is fractionally distilled to yield a graded series; and cananga (C. odorata var. macrophylla), the heavier, less-refined sister sold as a single complete oil. See Cananga and Cananga Essential Oil.
