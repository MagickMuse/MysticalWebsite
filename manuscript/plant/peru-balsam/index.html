Notes/Warnings
The crude balsam (not the oil) is a common contact allergen and may cause dermatitis. Those sensitive to it may also react to benzoin resinoid, known as cross-sensitization.

Magickal Properties

Powers: Success, Control

Magickal Uses

Burn Peru Balsam as incense in success spells. Its warm, rich, vanilla-like aroma makes it an excellent solar incense and a powerful ally in workings for achievement, authority, and control.

Botanical Description

A large tropical tree growing up to 25 metres high, with a straight smooth trunk, beautiful foliage, and very fragrant flowers. Every part of the tree contains a resinous juice, including the fibrous fruit. The balsam itself is a pathological product, drawn from exposed lacerated wood after strips of bark have been removed. It is a true balsam, collected as a dark brown or amber semi-solid mass.

Family: Fabaceae (Leguminosae)

Related Species

Myroxylon frutescens and guina-guina are close relations, as is Tolu Balsam (Myroxylon balsamum var. balsamum), from the same parent species.

Folk Tradition

Traditionally Peru Balsam was used to stimulate the heart, raise blood pressure, and reduce mucous secretions, making it a remedy for asthma, chronic coughs, and bronchitis. It was also widely employed for rheumatic pain and a long list of skin troubles, including scabies, nappy rash, bedsores, prurigo, eczema, sore nipples, and wounds, and was credited with destroying the itch acarus and its eggs.

Aromatherapy Use

  • Skin Care: Dry and chapped skin, eczema, rashes, sores, wounds.
  • Circulation, Muscles & Joints: Low blood pressure, rheumatism.
  • Respiratory System: Asthma, bronchitis, coughs.
  • Immune System: Colds.
  • Nervous System: Nervous tension, stress. Like other balsams, it has a warming, opening, comforting quality.

Other Uses

The balsam is extensively used in tropical medicinal preparations and appears in pharmaceutical products such as cough syrup. It serves as a fixative and fragrance component in soaps, detergents, creams, lotions, and perfumes; the oil is often preferred in perfumery to avoid resin deposits and discolouration. It is also used in most food categories, including alcoholic and soft drinks.
