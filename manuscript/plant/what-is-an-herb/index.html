Plant Grimoire

What Is an Herb

At its most basic, an herb is a plant whose fragrance or taste lends itself to cooking, healing, or perfumery. The term generally refers to the aboveground portions of the plant, mainly leaves and flowers, though definitions shift depending on how the plant is viewed and used.

In horticulture, an herb has a soft stem with less cellulose than other plants. Cellulose forms the rigid outer wall of the plant cell. Herbaceous plants are most often valued for their leaves and flowers, though all parts of a plant can serve healing, culinary, and magickal purposes. Any nonwoody, vascular plant could meet this definition.

🌿
Herbs vs. Spices: Spices fall under the broader umbrella of herbs but are differentiated by their origin in subtropical temperate zones. Spices are typically pictured as ground powder made from dried seeds, bark, or roots of aromatic and savory plants.

Key Herbalism Terms

  • Annuals: Plants with a growth cycle lasting only one growing season. They germinate, mature, flower, and die within a year, requiring replanting each season.
  • Binomial: The two-part Latin name identifying each plant. The genus name appears first, categorizing shared characteristics. The species name follows, singular and specific. For example, basil is Ocimum basilicum.
  • Forage: The act of locating and harvesting wildcrafted herbs in their natural environment.
  • Hardiness Zone: One of eleven geographical regions in the United States delineating climate conditions that affect herb cultivation. Each adjacent zone differs by approximately ten degrees.
  • Hydroponics: A soil-free method of plant cultivation where nutrients dissolve into circulating water and plants grow directly from the solution.
  • Organic: In cultivation, this means plants are raised without additives such as pesticides or fertilizer.
  • Perennial Herbs: Herbs that grow back year after year without replanting.
  • Wildcrafted: Herbs that grow in nature without human cultivation.

Ways to Work with Plants

Divination and Observation

  • Watch plant behavior for omens and signs
  • Press flowers in your book of shadows

Spell Components

  • Gather loosened leaves and petals for magick potions
  • Use plant matter in amulets and talismans
  • Add plant matter to incense and candles

Healing and Wellness

  • Blend herbs for poultices and healing teas
  • Make fragrant potpourris for closets and drawers

Living Plant Magick

  • Place live plants throughout your home or yard to encourage personal growth and well-being

Plants as Spell Components

If you think of a spell as a magick recipe, you begin to understand why the components matter so much. A good cook knows that the key to great food lies in the quality of the ingredients and how they are combined. The same holds true for spells.

  • Measure correctly: Proportions matter
  • Add at the right time: Timing affects potency
  • Allow time to work: Magick needs space to unfold

Anything that builds the energy of the working toward the right outcome qualifies as a good spell component. All ingredients must mesh on a metaphysical level.

✨
Remember: The witch themselves is the key component of any spell, adding a word, a touch, or a wish.

Building Your Plant Collection

You do not need to acquire everything at once.

  1. Select a few staples suited to the kinds of spells you want to cast
  2. Add more over time as your practice expands, just as a cook builds a spice collection
  3. Keep notes on what works and what does not as you become more proficient
