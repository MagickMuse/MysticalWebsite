History

Marshmallow has served as both a remedy and a food source since ancient times. Pliny the Elder praised its healing efficacy, claiming that a spoonful of mallows could protect a person from all disease for a day. Arabic physicians prepared poultices from the leaves to treat inflammation. The genus name derives from the Greek althainein, meaning "to heal," a fitting origin for a plant long prized for its restorative properties.

Characteristics

Marshmallow grows best in moist and sandy soil, reaching heights of two to five feet with full sunlight. It is a gelatinous herb with a characteristically slimy texture. Medicinal applications range from laxatives to skin lotions, with uses extending to soothing skin irritations and even serving as a natural toothpaste.

Magickal Uses

Althea has long been used in protection rites, and it also is a good psychic power-stimulator. Burn as incense for this purpose, or carry in a sachet.

Althea is also known to be a good "spirit-puller," i.e., it brings good spirits in during rituals when placed on the altar. This is a popular voodoo practice.

The marshmallow root creates a bridge between the physical and spiritual realms, making it valuable for both protective and divinatory work.

  • Handfasting and Sex Magick: Marshmallow is a traditional herb for handfasting ceremonies and sex magick, and is considered an aphrodisiac.
  • Fidelity: The herb is used in workings intended to inspire loyalty and fidelity in a partner.
  • Cleansing and Protection: Marshmallow is incorporated into cleansing rituals and protective workings.
