Atlas Cedar (Cedrus atlantica) is a pyramid-shaped evergreen of the Algerian Atlas mountains.

Characteristics

A pyramid-shaped evergreen of majestic stature, growing up to 40 metres high in the wild. The wood itself is hard and strongly aromatic, owing to the high percentage of essential oil it carries.

Origin & Distribution

Native to the Atlas mountains of Algeria. The essential oil is now produced principally in Morocco, with smaller production across other parts of North Africa.

Related Species

Believed to have originated from the famous Lebanon cedars (C. libani), which still grow wild in Lebanon and on the island of Cyprus. Closely related to the Himalayan deodar cedar (C. deodara), which yields a similar essential oil. Note that some so-called cedarwood oils are produced from the Juniperus genus, such as Virginian and Texas cedarwood, which are not true cedars.

Folk Tradition

The oil from Lebanon Cedar was possibly the first essential oil ever extracted, used by the ancient Egyptians for embalming, cosmetics, and perfumery. It was an ingredient in mithridat, a renowned classical poison antidote that was used for centuries. The Lebanon cedar was prized as a building wood, and its odour repelled ants, moths, and other harmful insects, as does the oil from the Atlas cedar.

In the East, the oil has long been used for bronchial and urinary tract infections, as a preservative, and as an incense. It is still used as a temple incense by the Tibetans, and is employed in their traditional medicine.
