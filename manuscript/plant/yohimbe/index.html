History

Yohimbe was discovered by German missionaries and brought back to Europe, where it was marketed as a sexual stimulant. In its native West Africa, it had long been used as a remedy for fevers and leprosy.

Characteristics

Native to Africa, yohimbe is an evergreen tree that can grow up to 90 feet tall. It is found in Nigeria, Cameroon, and the Democratic Republic of the Congo. The plant dilates the blood vessels of the skin and mucous membranes, with the effect of lowering blood pressure — making self-administration inadvisable. It is still sold commercially despite safety concerns. Traditional preparation calls for a decoction of the bark, strained and sweetened before drinking.

Magickal Uses

  • Love and lust: The infusion is drunk as a "lust" potion and the powdered herb is added to love mixtures.
  • Aphrodisiac and desire: Yohimbe is best known as a potent aphrodisiac with a strong reputation for igniting desire — the bark in particular is associated with love magick.
  • Herbal charms and incense: It is not necessary to consume the bark to draw on its energy — yohimbe can be incorporated into herbal charms or burned as incense to carry its arousing, desire-kindling properties into any working.
