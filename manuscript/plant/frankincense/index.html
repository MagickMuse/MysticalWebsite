Frankincense (Boswellia spp., especially Boswellia sacra) is a fragrant resin obtained from trees of the Boswellia genus, used since ancient times in religious and magical ceremonies. The Boswellia sacra tree is notably hardy, growing readily in difficult conditions, including directly out of solid rock. The resin is harvested by slashing the bark and allowing the sap to seep out and harden into rounded lumps known as tears.

Magickal Properties

  • Deities: Ra, Baal
  • Powers: Protection, Exorcism, Spirituality

Folk Names

Olibanum

Lore & History

Frankincense was one of the incenses burned by the ancient Hebrews in various sacrificial rites. The sweet-smelling smoke symbolized the divine name and was emblematic of prayer and humanity's connection to the divine. In the New Testament, frankincense was one of the gifts brought by the Magi to the infant Jesus alongside myrrh and gold, which some interpret as an allusion to his eventual role as a sacrificial figure. It has remained a core component of Catholic ritual to this day.

Agrippa wrote that frankincense is ruled by the sun and included it in his recipe for a fumigation to be used in solar workings.

Science lends support to the plant's solar, uplifting reputation: a 2008 study by researchers from Johns Hopkins University and the Hebrew University in Jerusalem found that incensole acetate, a chemical constituent of frankincense, lowered anxiety in mice. The implication is that frankincense may function as an effective natural antidepressant.

Ritual Uses

Burned at sunrise by the ancient Egyptians to honor Ra. Still used in church incenses.

Magickal Uses

For elevation and cleansing: Burn to raise vibrations, purify spaces, and drive out negativity.

For protection and exorcism: Add to blends for warding and banishing.

For visions and meditation: Burn to induce visions and aid deep meditation.

For luck and growth: Add to sachets to draw luck and spiritual growth.

For sacredness: Frankincense is used to consecrate sacred spaces and objects, and to honor the sacred in ritual.

Protective incense blend: Mix with cumin and burn as a powerful protective incense useful for general workings.

Egyptian Incense recipe: Frankincense, benzoin, and aloe wood.

Church Incense recipe: 10 oz frankincense, 4 oz powdered benzoin, 1 oz storax.

Substitution: Rosemary may be used if frankincense is unavailable.

Ancestor Veneration

Frankincense makes a powerful regular offering to the ancestors, particularly for those with Mediterranean heritage. It can also be burned specifically in times of hardship, or when seeking the ancestors' help in battling anxiety or depression, given its mood-lifting chemical properties.

To emphasize its solar qualities, burn frankincense immediately after sunrise on a Sunday.

Botanical Description

Frankincense (also known as olibanum or gum thus) is a handsome small tree or shrub with abundant pinnate leaves and white or pale pink flowers. It yields a natural oleo gum resin collected by making incisions into the bark; at first a milky-white liquid appears, which then solidifies into tear-shaped amber to orange-brown lumps between the size of a pea and a walnut.

Origin & Distribution

Native to the Red Sea region; grows wild throughout north-east Africa. The gum is mainly produced in Somalia, Ethiopia, China, and south Arabia, then distilled in Europe and, to a lesser extent, India.

Related Species

Other Boswellia species also yield olibanum gum, such as the Indian variety B. serrata. Constituents vary according to type and locality. The species Boswellia carteri is also commonly cited as a primary source of frankincense alongside B. sacra.

Folk Tradition

Used since antiquity as an incense in India, China, and the west by the Catholic Church. In ancient Egypt it was used in rejuvenating face masks, cosmetics, and perfumes. It has been used medicinally in the east and west for a wide range of conditions including syphilis, rheumatism, respiratory and urinary tract infections, skin diseases, as well as digestive and nervous complaints.

A Tree of Resilience

The Boswellia is a remarkably tough tree, taking root where there seems to be no soil at all and even drawing itself up out of bare stone by means of a sucker-like hold, and for this it has become an emblem of resilience. Its resin is gathered by slashing the bark so that the sap bleeds and hardens into the rounded lumps known as tears. The rising smoke is held to carry the thoughts and prayers of the faithful up to the heavens, and the fragrance itself was taken as a sign of life, mixed with oils to anoint newborn children. Paired with the myrrh used to embalm the dead, frankincense stands at the beginning of life as myrrh stands at its end, each a token of spiritual transformation.
