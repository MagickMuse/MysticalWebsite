Paulownia (Paulownia tomentosa), called the foxglove tree in the West for its large violet flowers, guarded the north gate of the elements for the ancient Chinese. Because the north is the traditional abode of winter and the dead, the tree came to symbolize regeneration and rebirth.

Folk Tradition

Its timber was made into drums beaten to follow the course of the Sun, and into the drums of war, and the Chinese ideogram for the paulownia, tong, carries the same meaning as drumbeat. In Japan the paulownia is the princess tree, once planted to herald the birth of a baby girl, the tree's growth mirroring the child's until, when she was ready to marry, it was felled to make her wedding chest, marking her passage from one life to another.
