Holly (Ilex aquifolium or I. opaca) is a par‑excellence protective evergreen of luck, lightning‑warding, and winter rites.

Magickal Properties

Powers: Protection, Anti‑Lightning, Luck, Dream Magick

Magickal Uses

  • House and land ward: Planted around the home it protects against evil spirits, poison, and mischievous sorcerers. Sprigs were thrown at wild animals to pacify them.
  • Blessing newborns: Holly water (infused or distilled) is sprinkled on newborn babies to protect them.
  • Luck (especially for men): Carried to promote good luck. Traditionally paired with ivy, holly is the “male” plant.
  • Saturnalia and Winter Solstice: Boughs were used for decorating and as gifts during the Saturnalia and the Winter Solstice.
  • Anti-poison and anti-evil: Planting holly near a house or farm was believed to repel poison and defend against lightning and evil.
  • Yule blessing: Hung around the house for good luck at Yule.
  • Wish dream: After midnight on a Friday, gather nine smooth holly leaves in silence. Wrap in white cloth with nine knots and place under the pillow to make dreams come true.

Additional Notes

Holly is associated with the God of Winter and Yule traditions.

Eternal Life and the Holly King

Like other evergreens the holly stands for eternal life and immortality, while its scarlet berries speak of life, vitality, and blood, and in Christmas songs those red berries are named the blood of Christ the redeemer. The masculine holly and its female counterpart the ivy are both welcomed into churches, unlike the mistletoe, whose pagan roots are harder to disguise. The custom of bringing holly boughs indoors in the depths of winter goes back to the older belief that its prickly leaves sheltered the fairy folk, glad of a warm refuge in the cold, and the Romans too brought it in during the December Saturnalia. Planted close to the home it guarded house and household from evil, the spiny leaves of the male tree being a sign of protection, while holly with smoother leaves was held more female. Its very name is traced to the Teutonic goddess Hole, mother of all unborn children and the one who named them, though the tree was so sacred that it was also called the holy tree. The Holly King, a giant figure built of holly and bearing a holly club, is the seasonal counterpart of the Oak King and the guardian of the midwinter solstice. In the Ogham alphabet the holly carries the letter T, named Tinne, and it is ruled by the element of fire.
