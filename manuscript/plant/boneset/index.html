Boneset (Eupatorium perfoliatum) is a tall herb with white flower clusters used in traditional medicine.

History

Boneset earned its common name not from broken bones but from breakbone fever, a dengue-like illness whose deep, aching bone pain inspired the name. Indigenous peoples across North America relied on the plant long before European contact, primarily to ease fevers and encourage sweating as part of the healing process. Their knowledge passed into colonial folk medicine, and by the 19th century boneset was among the most widely used plants on the continent. It was officially recognized as a drug well into the early 20th century before modern pharmaceuticals gradually replaced it around 1950.

Characteristics

Boneset is a perennial member of the daisy family that favors damp meadows and forest edges throughout North America, growing as tall as five feet. Its most striking feature is its paired leaves, which appear to be pierced by the hairy central stem. In late summer it produces flat-topped clusters of small white tube-shaped florets that attract butterflies and other pollinators. Despite the name, boneset has no particular history of treating broken bones.

Magickal Uses

For protection: Boneset is used in protective magic. Sprinkle an infusion around the house to drive away evil.

For exorcism: The herb is burned to drive out unwanted spirits and negative energies from a space.

For binding and poppets: Boneset is traditionally used to stuff poppets and voodoo dolls in binding and binding-related workings.

For endings and release: Boneset supports workings of dissolution and parting, helping bring situations, partnerships, and life phases to a natural close. It is well suited to rituals of letting go, separation, and the intentional severing of ties that no longer serve.

Medicinal Uses

Boneset is a stimulating herb with a long history in North American folk medicine. It was particularly valued for relieving the deep, aching bone pain associated with influenza and other fevers, which gave the plant its common name. It was a staple in the plant medicine practices of many Indigenous communities long before it entered the broader folk herbal tradition. Like all stimulating herbs, boneset is used in short courses for specific conditions rather than as an ongoing supplement, and its internal use requires care.

Magickal Teas

Boneset tea can be brewed as a magickal infusion to call upon devas, support divination, strengthen protection, and ward negativity. Drink 1 to 2 cups only during the spell event itself.
