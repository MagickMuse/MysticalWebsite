Ginseng (Panax quinquefolius) is a slow-growing perennial plant native to North America, highly prized for its human-shaped root with powerful magical and medicinal properties.

Medicinal Uses

Ginseng is one of the most widely used adaptogenic herbs in the world, with a history spanning thousands of years across both East Asian and North American traditions. Asian ginseng (Panax ginseng) and American ginseng (Panax quinquefolius) are distinct species with somewhat different properties, but both are valued for their ability to support vitality, endurance, and resilience under stress. As an adaptogen, ginseng helps the body manage fatigue and recover more effectively rather than artificially stimulating performance. It has been used as a longevity tonic, an immune supporter, and a remedy for general depletion, particularly in older adults or those recovering from illness.

Folk Tradition

In the East, and in China above all, ginseng is revered as the very elixir of life. Its botanical name, panax, acknowledges its reputation as a cure for all ills. Chinese myth tells of one of the Eight Immortals who ate a ginseng root fully two feet long, and the plant is spoken of as godlike. Symbolically it stands for longevity, vitality, vigor, strength, and clarity of mind.

Magickal Uses

For attraction and protection: The root is carried to attract love, as well as to guard one's health, to draw money, and to ensure sexual potency. Ginseng will also bring beauty to all who carry it.

For banishing evil: Burn ginseng to ward off evil spirits and to break hexes and curses.

For lust: A tea of ginseng is used as a powerful lust-inducing drink, whether alone or mixed with other like herbs.

For wishes: Hold a ginseng root in your hands, visualize your wish into the root, and throw it into running water. Or, carve your wish onto a root and toss into the water.

Aphrodisiac Properties

Like ginger, ginseng root often takes on a humanoid shape, which has long reinforced its association with virility and desire. Its sharp, stimulating taste adds to the sensory appeal.

As mandrake substitute: Ginseng can be a substitute for the mandrake.

For psychic stimulation: Taken in syrup form or as a tea, ginseng stimulates the psychic centers. Root is highly regarded in Chinese medicine as a cure-all and rejuvenator.
