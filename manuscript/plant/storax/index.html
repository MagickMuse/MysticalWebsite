This page covers Levant storax (Liquidambar orientalis), the resin meant when spells call for liquidambar or storax. For the American tree and its witch burrs, see Sweetgum.

Storax (Liquidambar orientalis), also called Levant Styrax or Oriental Sweetgum, is the aromatic balsam of a deciduous Asia Minor tree of the Hamamelidaceae family, source of a sweet-balsamic essential oil long valued in perfumery and folk medicine.

Botanical Description

A deciduous tree up to 15 metres high with purplish-grey bark, leaves arranged into five three-lobed sections, and white flowers. The styrax is a pathological secretion produced by pounding the bark, which induces the sapwood to produce a liquid from beneath the bark. It hardens to form a semi-solid greenish-brown mass with a sweet balsamic odour.

Origin & Distribution

Native to Asia Minor. It forms forests around Bodrum, Milas, Mugla, and Marmaris in Turkey.

Related Species

Levant Storax is very similar to the American Styrax, Sweetgum (Liquidambar styraciflua) or red gum, which produces a natural exudation slightly darker and harder than the Levant type. Many other types of styrax exist, including Styrax officinale, which produced the styrax of ancient civilizations. Styrax benzoin is the botanical name for Benzoin, with which Storax shares similar qualities.

Folk Tradition

In China, Storax is used for coughs, colds, epilepsy, and skin problems including cuts, wounds, and scabies. In the West, it has been recommended as a remedy for catarrh, diphtheria, gonorrhoea, leucorrhoea, and ringworm. A syrup made from the bark of the American styrax is used for diarrhoea and dysentery in the western USA. Recorded actions include anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, antiseptic, antitussive, bactericidal, balsamic, expectorant, nervine, and stimulant.

Safety

The essential oil distilled from the crude balsam is non-toxic and non-irritant, but may cause sensitization in some individuals. Storax is frequently adulterated, so low-priced product is usually an imitation.

Source — Specific Uses

The balsam obtained from the wood and inner bark is used in perfumery and incense. Blends well with oriental (spicy, woody, earthy) scents and serves as a fixative for florals.

⚠️ Historical note: The storax of the ancients is not this tree (Liquidambar orientalis), but Styrax officinale, a close relative of benzoin.

Magickal Properties

Storax has been used in incense and perfumery since ancient times. It carries purifying and spiritually elevating properties, making it valuable in ceremonial magic and temple incense. The balsam helps create sacred space and enhances meditation.

Ritual Uses

Burn as part of planetary and ceremonial incense formulas. Add to purification blends. Use in temple incense for spiritual elevation. Due to rarity, Gum Arabic or Benzoin can substitute in most recipes.

A Strengthening Resin

Liquidambar resin provides protection on its own and is also believed to magickally enhance and strengthen the powers of any other botanical it is blended with.
