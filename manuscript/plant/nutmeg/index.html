Characteristics

Nutmeg comes from an evergreen tree up to 20 metres high with greyish-brown smooth bark, dense foliage, and small dull-yellow flowers. The fleshy fruit contains a seed (the nutmeg) wrapped in a bright red, netlike aril or husk known as Mace.

Origin & Distribution

Native to the Moluccas and nearby islands, the tree is now cultivated in Indonesia, Sri Lanka, and the West Indies, especially Grenada. The essential oil is also distilled in the USA and Europe from imported nutmegs.

Related Species

Indonesia and Sri Lanka produce the so-called East Indian nutmeg, which is considered superior, while Grenada produces the West Indian nutmeg.

Folk Tradition

Nutmeg and mace are widely used as domestic spices in both East and West, and have been used for centuries as a remedy mainly for digestive and kidney problems. In Malaysia they are used during pregnancy to strengthen and tone the uterine muscles. Grated nutmeg with lard is used for piles. A fixed oil of nutmeg is also used in soap and candle making. Nutmeg is current in the British Herbal Pharmacopoeia for flatulent dyspepsia, nausea, diarrhoea, dysentery, and topically for rheumatism. Recorded actions include analgesic, anti-emetic, anti-oxidant, antirheumatic, antiseptic, antispasmodic, aphrodisiac, carminative, digestive, emmenagogue, gastric secretory stimulant, larvicidal, orexigenic, prostaglandin inhibitor, stimulant, and tonic.

Botanical Information

Myristica fragrans (Myristicaceae family). Masculine seed, spice, fruit tree originating from Southeast Asia. Used far longer than recorded history. Already used for hundreds of years in ancient Egyptian embalming by the time records were written.

Herbal Lore

Ancient history in magickal and medicinal use. During Middle Ages, mixed with fatty ointment as cure for digestive ailments. As warming herb, associated with magick of love, sex, and fidelity across cultures.

Ritual Uses

Nutmeg is carried for good luck and used in workings for calm, rational thought. Place it in dream pillows to banish harmful dreams and stimulate beneficial ones. Its warming nature makes it well suited to prosperity work: use in money charms, abundance sachets, and financial manifestation. Nutmeg carries energies of loyalty and fidelity, making it useful in charms for faithful friendships, marriages, and business partnerships. It provides physical and emotional fortitude; carry a whole nutmeg or its powder in strength charms, or encircle family photos with powdered nutmeg for protective health charms.

Nutmeg expands awareness past physical boundaries for meditation and astral work, and was historically burned in temples as divinatory incense. Use it in scrying, divination rituals, and oracular practices. It enhances mental focus and creative flow. Scatter nutmeg around the home or make a protective room spray to break hexes and crossed conditions. Burn it on Thursdays for justice in legal matters, or on Saturdays to banish unjust lawsuits. It also facilitates visionary states and trance work alongside movement practices and drumming.

The dried kernel of the seed is the nutmeg; the dried arils surrounding it are mace. Both appear in potpourris and perfumes. In large doses, nutmeg is narcotic. Hollowed-out nutmegs filled with mercury and carved are traditional good luck charms. Oil of nutmeg rubbed lightly on the temples is said to induce sleep and aid meditation.

Amulet Uses

A whole nutmeg carried as an amulet brings good luck in gambling.

Moon Magick

Key themes for lunar workings: Luck, money, wishes, health, fidelity.

Culinary Uses

Parts used: Seeds, often dried and ground into powder

Nutmeg is a beloved baking spice, bringing psychic enhancement, happiness, and love into kitchen creations.

Seasonal Associations

Nutmeg is a winter herb aligned with Yule and Imbolc, carrying the magickal principle of repose and rest.
