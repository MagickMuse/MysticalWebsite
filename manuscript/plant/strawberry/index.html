Botanical Information

Fragaria virginiana, F. × ananassa (Rosaceae family). Feminine fruit originating from various regions. The common garden strawberry enjoyed throughout North America is known as the Virginia strawberry. The larger variety grown for its size, aroma, and appearance is a hybrid offspring of the Virginia species, bred in France. There are over twenty varieties of Fragaria, and their offspring number collectively in the thousands.

Herbal Lore

When hulled and sliced, these delicate and easily bruised fruit (technically they are not a true berry) resemble a lovely, red heart. That is no coincidence, as these delectable, bite-sized members of the rose family are associated with Venus, as are all stone fruits. The strawberry is seen as the union of opposites: fruit with visible seeds, as well as the womb-shaped void inside the fruit itself.

Ritual Uses

Attraction

Signal your intention to a potential romantic interest by sharing this juicy berry with them. Muddle strawberries with lime to attract a love interest who is happy, loyal, and respects you. For a sexy roll in the hay, chop apples and strawberries, add a dash of cinnamon, and top with sparkling apple cider in a champagne flute.

Beauty

Eating these berries when in season provides omega-3 and omega-6 fatty acids and vitamin C. Vitamin C reduces redness in skin and has mild acid content. Due to their natural acids, strawberries make a lovely face mask with oatmeal for balancing oil and dry factors in skin. The omega fatty acids benefit hair, nails, and more. Offer a handful of berries to Venus, Aphrodite, Freya, or Isis, and keep a handful for yourself.

Mental Flexibility

The omega fatty acids are also great for keeping your brain in shape. These oils found in large quantities in fish oils are commonly suggested supplements for attention span and mental acuity. This is not simple money. For true blessings of good fortune, leave a bowl of ripe, beautiful strawberries for Fortuna (Tyche in Greece) and ask that she look well upon you. This daughter of Jupiter (Zeus in Greece) will be especially happy if you do it on her father's day, Thursday.

To attract a new friend, sit down with a small bowl of strawberries and a candle (best on a Friday) and call out to Philotes, the Greek goddess of social intercourse, friendship, and affection. As the daughter of Nyx (the goddess of night), she is intimately aware of how loving friendships do in dark times. Light a candle in her honor and gift her half your berries. Peacekeeping, whether for romantic, platonic, or familial relationships, is a necessary skill. To inspire peace among loved ones, wash and bless a bowl of strawberries, then serve them as a treat for everyone. With the hull (or the top leaves) serve with homemade whipped cream. Without the hull, consider dusting them with sugar and serving over pound cake for the fullness of love and emotional vulnerability.

Joy

Make a simple syrup by boiling one cup water, adding one cup sugar, and whisking until clear. Next, muddle 5 (the number associated with Venus) strawberries and add them to the syrup. Flavor seltzer (creativity, spontaneity) with it for a joyous pick-me-up.

Aphrodisiac Properties

Like the raspberry, the strawberry's vivid red color, abundance of tiny seeds, and resemblance to a nipple all contribute to its aphrodisiac reputation.

Sacred Fruit of Heaven and Earth

The strawberry was counted among the fruits of the Elysian Fields, the resting place of blessed souls, and it gathered a rich Christian symbolism besides. Its three-part leaf was read as the Holy Trinity, its white flowers as the purity of the Virgin and the innocence of Christ, and its red flesh, borne without thorn or pip and eaten whole, as good deeds and the blood of Christ, which is why it is so often painted growing at the Virgin's feet. Yet in its fruiting the strawberry speaks just as plainly of fecundity and sensuality, and so it came to stand for both spiritual growth and bodily desire, the sacred and the profane entwined.

An old legend warned mothers whose children had died not to gather strawberries before Midsummer Day, for that is the day the spirits of the dead children go strawberry picking, and the fruit must be left for them. Because of its bright red colour the berry was also held a token of vitality, and it was said that eating plenty of strawberries would grant long life, an old wives' tale that modern research, finding the fruit rich in anti-carcinogenic compounds, has gone some way toward bearing out.
