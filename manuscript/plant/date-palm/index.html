Date Palm (Phoenix dactylifera; P. roebelenii) is an honored fertility tree whose abundant fruiting inspires rites of potency and protection.

Magickal Properties

Deities: Taht (Thoth), Apollo, Artemis, Hecate, Isis, Ra

Powers: Fertility, Potency

Magickal Uses

For fertility and potency: Dates are eaten to increase fertility; palm leaves or date pits are carried by men to restore or strengthen sexual potency.

For weather ward: Where the palm grows it protects the area from inclement weather.

For house protection: A palm leaf hung near the entrance keeps evil and uncanny beings from entering.

From the source book: Gender: Hot | Planet: Jupiter | Element: Water | Deities: Ishtar, Ashtoreth, Isis, Venus, Astarte, Aphrodite, and all mother goddesses | Powers: Love, Protection. The birth tree and symbol of motherhood in the more southerly latitudes. It is a water tree, as it grows by the sea.

The Tree of Civilization

For the Assyrians and the Babylonians the date palm was the equivalent of the World Tree. Its versatile fruit was a staple of the diet thousands of years ago and remains important today, eaten on its own, cooked in countless recipes, or even made into a sparkling drink, and the tree also yields cosmetics, animal feed, shade, and timber. Little wonder it was held a sacred gift from God. Its cultivation reaches back as far as six thousand years.

Sacred to Three Faiths

The date palm is an important symbol within Islam, Christianity, and Judaism alike. It was the leaves of the date palm that lined Christ's route into Jerusalem before his crucifixion, and the same leaves are woven into the palm cross. The shape of the tree so impressed the architects of Egypt that they raised a stylized version of it in their monumental columns.
