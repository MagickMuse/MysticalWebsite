Creeping Cinquefoil (Potentilla reptans) is a five-petaled wayside herb of protection, luck, and love — closely related to tormentil and sharing its reputation as a powerful magickal herb, cited among the ingredients of the traditional witch's flying ointment.

Magickal Properties

Gender: Masculine

Planet: Jupiter

Element: Fire

Powers: Money, Protection, Love, Sleep, Prophetic Dreams, Luck

Magickal Uses

  • Five-finger symbolism: Each leaf of cinquefoil has five leaflets, representing love, money, health, power, and wisdom. Carrying it is said to attract all five.
  • Protection: Hung over doorways and windows to protect the home; infused in washes to cleanse and shield people and spaces.
  • Love: Added to sachets, bath water, and charm bags to draw love and strengthen romantic bonds.
  • Sleep and dreams: Placed beneath the pillow or used in dream sachets to encourage prophetic sleep.
  • Court cases: Known in hoodoo and folk practice as "Five Finger Grass" — carried to court or petition work to make judges and juries favorable.

⚠️ This is a Hoodoo-attributed practice. It should only be practiced by those within or trained in that tradition.

The Witch's Flying Ointment

Creeping cinquefoil is listed among the plants cited in historical flying ointment formulas from the 1500s and 1600s. Unlike the outright poisons on the list, cinquefoil likely functioned as a carrier and activating herb — its role was energetic and symbolic as much as pharmacological. Its five-petaled flower and five-part leaf made it a potent symbol of wholeness and multidirectional power in folk magick.

Botanical Description

Creeping cinquefoil is a low-growing perennial that spreads by long, slender runners — hence "creeping." It thrives in dry grasslands, roadsides, and waste ground across Europe and Asia. The bright yellow five-petaled flowers bloom from late spring through summer. The plant resembles a small wild strawberry in growth habit and belongs to the same rose family.

Relation to Other Cinquefoils

Creeping cinquefoil is closely related to tormentil (Potentilla erecta) and silverweed (Potentilla anserina). The cinquefoil family shares many properties across species; in many folk traditions the name "five-finger grass" is applied interchangeably to several Potentilla species.
