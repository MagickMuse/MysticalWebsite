Magickal Properties

Planet: Sun, Jupiter

Deities: Dagda, Dianus, Jupiter, Zeus, Thor, Pan, Cernunnos, Herne, Janus, Rhea, Cybele, Hecate, Brighid

Powers: Strength, Courage, Longevity, Protection, Good Fortune

Magickal Uses

  • Strength and courage: Carry oak leaves or acorns to increase personal strength and bravery.
  • Protection: Hang oak branches in the home to guard against lightning and evil; plant an oak near the house for protection.
  • Longevity: Acorns carried preserve youth and attract long life.
  • Good fortune: Oak is incorporated into luck and prosperity workings.
  • Fertility: Acorns are potent fertility charms.

Additional Notes

The oak is a Druid favorite, sacred across Celtic traditions. The traditional Yule log is ideally made of oak. Acorns symbolize fertility and are carried to preserve youth and attract prosperity. Mistletoe growing in oak is considered especially potent for magickal workings.

If oak grows with ash and hawthorn, fairies are said to dance among them. This sacred triad marks places where the veil between worlds is thin.

When crafting a scribe for rune candle work, oak increases the power of favorable, memorable, or particularly revealing rune casts. If your reading was significant or you want to amplify its energy, use an oak scribe to carve the runes into your candle.

Father of the Forest

The oak is the Father of the Forest and King of all Trees, holding this high place across the temperate world. A tree of great longevity, it may take sixty years to bear its first fruit and then stand for centuries, an imposing figure of generational continuity in the land. Sacred and sky-reaching, it was held to channel the messages of the gods: the famous oak grove at Dodona in Greece housed the birds that bore the gods' messages through its priestesses, and one Dodona oak was felled to make the prow of the Argo, whose carved figurehead could whisper to Jason and warn him of danger. At Hebron too there was a sacred oak grove where Abraham received the messages of God, and before temples were raised by human hands the gods were worshipped beneath oaks. The tree is especially sacred to the Druids, who take their name from duir, the name of the oak, from which the word door also descends, for the oak is a door to another world, the Sidhe, the unseen Celtic realm that runs alongside our own. Oak, or Duir, is the seventh letter of the Ogham alphabet.

The oak stands for strength (the Latin robur, root of robust, is also the word for oak), power, longevity, and protection, and it is a benevolent ally to animals and people alike; the oaks that built the ships which repelled the Spanish Armada show its protective might, and beams from those very ships survive in old houses still. The oak gall, a little ball that grows where a wasp attacks the tree, serves a particular divination to learn whether a child has been bewitched: three galls are dropped into a bowl of water set beneath the cradle, and if they float all is well, but if they sink the child must be freed from enchantment by other means. Because its great size draws lightning, the oak gained a name for governing the weather, deepening its tie to the gods who rule storm and lightning. Its fruit, the acorn, gives nourishment and carries its own rich meaning, for the nut in its cup was a sign of the phallus and of birth and the womb, and a necklace of acorns was once a tell-tale mark of a witch.

Magickal Teas

White oak bark can be brewed as a magickal tea to support fertility, honor or invoke the Green Man, promote health, and strengthen protection. Drink 1 to 2 cups only during the spell event itself.

Moon Magick

Key themes for lunar workings: Protection, health, money, healing, potency, fertility, luck.
