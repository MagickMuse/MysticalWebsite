History

Native to Africa, aloe's reputation as a healing plant spans thousands of years. It held sacred status in ancient Egypt, where it was depicted on temple walls and incorporated into religious symbolism. Cleopatra is said to have made it a staple of her beauty regimen, and Alexander the Great reportedly used it to treat injured soldiers in the field.

Characteristics

Aloe is a succulent with thick green or green-and-white variegated leaves edged with small spines. The leaves and the gel inside them are the most commonly used parts. The gel is widely applied to burns and skin irritations and appears in cosmetic products from shampoo to toothpaste. The plant is also edible, and its sap is known to soothe upset stomachs.

Magickal Uses

The aloe, a popular house plant, is also protective. It guards against evil influences and prevents household accidents. In Africa the aloe is hung over houses and doors to drive away evil, as well as to bring good luck.

Aloe vera leaves, full of healing and soothing gel, are shaped like spears. Keep living aloe plants on the altar for spiritual protection, particularly when working with volatile entities or dangerous spirits.

In Mexico, large wreaths made of whole garlic bulbs strung on wire are festooned with pictures of saints, packets of magickal herbs, lodestones, rock salt, pine nuts as well as clumps of freshly cut aloe. These are hung up in the home for protection, luck, money, and so on.

Aloe vera flower remedy counters psychic depletion after a long illness or personal crisis. Add it to the bath or take it internally according to the manufacturer's instructions.

In funerary tradition, aloe vera planted at a gravesite is believed to soothe the deceased, ease feelings of loneliness or abandonment in the afterlife, and prevent the soul from longing for or clinging to the living.

Additional Notes

There are over five hundred cultivated aloe species that have reputed medical benefits, and more; however we are most interested in its reputation as a protection plant.

The watery flesh of this succulent aligns it with the energy of the Moon and the sign of Cancer. Think of it as washing away anything aligned against you.

The gel of the aloe may wash away the pain of burns, but the prickly leaves mean that it is fully able to protect itself and your emotional needs.

Beauty & Skincare

Aloe is one of the most widely applied botanical ingredients in skincare. Its cooling gel soothes overheated, irritated, or sun-stressed skin on contact. Topical preparations ease inflammation and support the skin's recovery from sun overexposure and minor burns.

Aloe Vera and Aloes Wood

Aloe vera, a native of Zanzibar, is used for healing and protective magick. Its name means "true aloe," given to set it apart from the aloes of the Bible, which is believed to be the Southeast Asian wood now known as Agarwood. The two plants both offer profound gifts but are far from identical.
