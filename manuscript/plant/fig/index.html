The Fica / Fig Hand

The fig lends its name to both a gesture and a family of amulets known as the fica or fig hand: a closed fist with the thumb pushed between the first two fingers. The gesture simultaneously represents the sexual act and evokes the shape of a pregnant woman. It is one of the oldest protective gestures in Mediterranean tradition, deployed against spiritual attack, the Evil Eye, and any threatening force. Physical fig hand amulets are equally widespread, and the material they are carved from determines their specific purpose. A fig hand carved from red coral functions as a men's amulet protecting the genitals, sperm, virility, and general procreative capacity.

Fig (Ficus carica) is a deciduous tree producing sweet, fleshy fruits that have been cultivated since ancient times.

Source Book Notes

  • Sacred to the Goddess in Crete: This tree was sacred to the Goddess; wreaths of fig leaves were worn during Saturn rituals and were traditional New Year's gifts.
  • For strength and swiftness: Greek athletes ate figs to increase strength and swiftness.
  • Aphrodisiac: Figs are said to be an aphrodisiac.
  • Conflict: Existing entry lists Gender as Feminine; source lists Hot.

Magickal Uses

For fertility and potency: Carve small phallic images from fig wood and carry. Eating fresh figs also promotes fertility.

For divination: Write a question on a fig leaf. If it dries slowly the omen is good; if it dries quickly the answer is unfavorable.

For home blessings: A fig tree grown indoors is protective and brings good luck. In the bedroom it aids sleep; in the kitchen it ensures no one goes hungry.

For attraction: Give a fig to charm a person (if they like figs).

Aphrodisiac Properties

The fig's plethora of tiny seeds symbolize fertility, while its moist, plump flesh carries a sensual, feminine quality. The fruit's association with desire is ancient and widespread.

For safe travel: Place a fig branch before the door before a journey for safe return.

The Tree of Life

In all its many varieties and wherever it grows, the fig is revered as a Tree of Life, a sign of abundance, plenty, and peace. The tree under which the Buddha gained enlightenment was a fig, the Ficus religiosa, kin to the banyan and the Bodhi, and to those figs that in Africa are said to house the souls of the ancestors. For Muslims the fig is a tree of great intelligence, its awareness but a single step below that of an animal, and so the fig is also bound to knowledge, which is why Adam and Eve reached for fig leaves once they knew themselves naked. The tree's milky, seed-laden sap and the shape of its many-seeded fruit lend it a strong link to male fertility and sexuality, the same root from which springs the old fig hand gesture. An Indian invocation called the assathva honours the tree: the magician sits in its shade, recites a poem in its praise, then walks around it in multiples of seven circuits, at least eighty-four times in all, before bathing and putting on fresh clothes.

Vulva Symbolism and Protection

The fig is one of the traditional symbolic stand ins for the female genitals, the most protective of all images. Display fig imagery in the home or wear it as jewelry to borrow that protective power. See Vulva.
