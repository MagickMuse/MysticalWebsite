Olive (Olea europaea) heals, blesses, and protects; its oil lights temples and anoints for recovery and peace.

Magickal Properties

Planet: Sun

Deities: Athena, Apollo, Irene, Minerva, Ra

Powers: Healing, Peace, Fertility, Potency, Protection, Lust

Magickal Uses

  • Headache charm: Write Athena’s name on an olive leaf and press it to the head to cure headaches.
  • Healing oil: Olive oil is long used as an anointing oil to aid healing.
  • Peace and blessing: Olive leaves scattered or placed in rooms spread peaceful vibrations throughout the area.
  • Fertility and potency: Eating olives ensures fertility and sexual potency in men; also lust‑inducing. Athenian brides wore olive crowns for fertility.
  • House ward and anti‑lightning: A branch hung over the door guards from evil; one on the chimney wards off lightning.
  • Luck: Olive leaves worn bring good fortune.
  • Psychic restoration: Olive flower remedy counters psychic depletion after a long illness or personal crisis. Add it to the bath or take it internally according to the manufacturer's instructions.

From the source book: Symbolizes fruitfulness, peace, and happiness; the oil represents goodness and purity. Olive oil was burned in sacred temple lamps. Associated with the Spring Equinox, where boughs were used in Greek spring festivals. Sacred to the young Sun. Its wood was used for statues of deities. Olympic game victors were crowned with olive wreaths.

Athena's Gift and the Branch of Peace

The olive was Athena's gift to Athens, the goddess of wisdom winning the privilege of having the city named for her; the rival gift, a spring offered by Poseidon, ran salt and undrinkable. From this comes an old charm: to cure a headache, write the name of Athena on an olive and hold it to the head, and the goddess will take the pain away. The olive branch has long stood for peace and hope, for when Noah loosed a dove from the Ark it returned with a sprig believed to be olive, a sign that land and hope were both at hand, and to this day to offer an olive branch is a universal token of peace and the wish for harmony. Olive oil, vital to peoples who seldom ate meat for its rich nourishment, made the tree sacred wherever it grew, and in former times to harm an olive was a punishable offence. Because olives reach a great age, and because olive wood is an antitoxin with protective power, the tree is a symbol of longevity, and in an echo of the laurel crown courageous Roman soldiers were given crowns of olive wood to mark their invincibility in battle. The olive is the most important of all trees in Islam and a symbol of the Prophet Mohammed, each of its leaves said to bear one of the names of God, and because its oil is burned in lamps it is also a symbol of light, a practical use whose meaning attests to the tree's sacred nature.

When an olive branch is gifted it carries the meaning of peace.
