Passion Flower (Passiflora incarnata) is a calming vine of peace, love, and gentle spirit — carrying Christian crucifixion symbolism since the early 1600s and prized for its tranquilizing properties.

History

Though unknown during biblical times, passionflower was interpreted by Spanish explorers who encountered it in Peru as a living symbol of the Passion of Christ. By the early 1600s, its distinctive blossom had been associated with the crown of thorns, the hammer and nails of the crucifixion, and the loyal apostles. The five petals and five sepals — ten parts total — were said to represent ten of the twelve apostles, with Peter and Judas excluded for their betrayal and denial. Passionflower's calming reputation earned it a place in the National Formulary in the early twentieth century, and it has since found its way into products as varied as herbal remedies and chewing gum.

Characteristics

Passionflower is a perennial climbing vine whose exotic blossoms are short-lived, remaining open for only about a day. The plant requires full sun to partial shade, moist and well-drained soil, and a structure to climb. It is a heavy feeder, benefiting from the addition of compost to the soil.

Magickal Properties

Planet: Venus

Powers: Peace, Sleep, Friendships

Magickal Uses

  • Peace in the home: Placed in the house to calm problems and troubles and bring peace.
  • Friend‑drawing: Carried to attract friends and enhance popularity.
  • Sleep aid: Placed beneath the pillow to aid sleep.
  • Love and drawing affection: As a clinging vine, passionflower is used in love magick to attract and hold affection, drawing connections that hold fast.
  • Uplifting the spirit: Its calming nature allows passionflower to gently lift the spirit without agitation, making it useful in workings centered on gentleness, emotional ease, and inner peace.

Beyond the Passion

The plant's Latin name reads as suffering flower, a strange title for so flamboyant a bloom until you recall that the Spanish, pressing into the Inca lands in the sixteenth century, took it as a sign from God that converting the native peoples was sanctioned. They read the gothic blossom as a living emblem of Christ's Passion: the thorn-like crown at its center, the ten white petals for the faithful apostles, the styles for the nails of the crucifixion, and the five stamens for the wounds He endured.

The flower has older powers beneath the Christian reading. Passiflorine, a narcotic drawn from the plant, brings on heavy hypnosis and sleep, and long before it entered the medicine chest the passion flower was taken by shamans who believed it opened communion with the unseen world of spirits and Gods. In present-day Japan the blossom has come to stand as a symbol of love between men.

Meaning When Gifted

When passion flower is gifted it carries the meaning of faith and religious fervor.
