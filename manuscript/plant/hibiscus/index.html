Hibiscus (Hibiscus rosa-sinensis) stirs desire, blesses unions, and aids divination.

History

Hibiscus is native to the Far East and is believed to have originated in India or China. It was introduced to Europe in the sixteenth century and has since spread throughout the world, where it is cultivated for use in food and beverages.

Characteristics

Hibiscus is an annual herb that bears striking red flowers and can reach heights of four to five feet. It has recognized culinary applications — the young flowers are used in sauces, jellies, jams, and elixirs. Infusions brewed from the blossoms are also widely consumed as a tea.

Magickal Properties

Gender: Feminine

Planet: Venus

Element: Water

Powers: Lust, Love, Divination

Magickal Uses

  • Lust tea: Red hibiscus flowers are brewed into a strong red tea for lust. This drink is traditionally forbidden to women in Egypt for this reason.
  • Love magick: Blossoms added to love incenses, sachets, and wedding wreaths in tropical rites.
  • Divination: Flowers floated on water for scrying in some Pacific traditions.
  • Choice divination: Designate cut blossoms to represent a person or situation, particularly when choices must be made. Float the flowers on a bowl of water and observe.
  • Invoking passion: The colorful flowers of hibiscus, with their tart but pleasant flavor, are incorporated into workings that call forth passion and stoke desire.
  • Offering of beauty: Hibiscus blossoms are considered an appropriate offering of beauty in ritual and devotional practice.
  • Courage: Hibiscus carries an association with the acknowledgment of courage and is used in workings that honor bravery or summon inner strength.

Meaning When Gifted

When hibiscus is gifted it carries the meaning of being consumed by love and of delicate beauty.
