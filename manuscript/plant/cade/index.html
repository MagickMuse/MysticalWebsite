Cade Oil

Cade (Juniperus oxycedrus) is a large prickly Mediterranean juniper whose smoky, leathery tar oil has been a staple of European dermatology for centuries.

Characteristics

A large evergreen shrub or small tree growing up to 4 metres tall, with long, sharp, dark green needles and brownish-black berries about the size of hazelnuts. The leaves are noticeably more rigid and pointed than those of common juniper.

Origin & Distribution

Native to southern France, now common throughout Europe and North Africa. The famous cade tar is produced mainly in Spain and the former Yugoslavia (today's Croatia, Serbia, Bosnia, and surrounding region).

Related Species

Cade is one of many commercially used junipers. Its close relatives include:

  • J. communis: produces the bright, gin-scented juniper berry oil. See the existing Juniper entry.
  • J. virginiana: produces Virginian cedarwood oil.
  • J. smreka: a Yugoslavian species whose fruits and twigs yield a regional oil.

Folk Tradition

In France and across continental Europe, cade has long been used to treat cutaneous diseases: chronic eczema, parasitic skin infections, scalp disease, hair loss, and slow-healing skin troubles. The smoky tar was applied as an antiseptic wound dressing and dabbed onto aching teeth as an old remedy for toothache.
