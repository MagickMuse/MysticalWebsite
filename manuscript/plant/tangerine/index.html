Tangerine and Mandarin (Citrus reticulata) come from a small evergreen tree of the Rutaceae family with glossy leaves, fragrant flowers, and fleshy fruit. The terms mandarin, tangerine, and satsuma are used somewhat interchangeably for cultivars of the same species, though their oils represent distinct chemotypes.

Characteristics

A small evergreen tree reaching up to 6 metres in height. The tangerine is larger than the mandarin and rounder in shape, with a yellower skin closer to the original Chinese type.

Origin & Distribution

Native to southern China and the Far East. The tree was brought to Europe in 1805 and to America forty years later, where it was renamed the tangerine. The mandarin is produced mainly in Italy, Spain, Algeria, Cyprus, Greece, the Middle East, and Brazil. The tangerine is produced in Texas, Florida, California, and Guinea.

Related Cultivars

Many cultivars exist within Citrus reticulata. The names tangerine, mandarin, and satsuma are applied somewhat interchangeably across them, although they yield oils with quite different chemistry and scent.

Folk Tradition

The name comes from the fruit being a traditional gift to the Mandarins of China. In France, mandarin is regarded as a safe children's remedy for indigestion, hiccoughs, and similar complaints, and is also given to the elderly to help strengthen the digestive function and the liver. Recorded actions include antiseptic, antispasmodic, carminative, digestive, mild diuretic, mild laxative, sedative, digestive and lymphatic stimulant, and tonic.
