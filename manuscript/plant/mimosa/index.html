Mimosa (Acacia dealbata) cleanses, comforts, and opens the dreaming gate.

Magickal Properties

Gender: Feminine

Planet: Saturn

Element: Water

Powers: Protection, Love, Prophetic Dreams, Purification

Magickal Uses

  • Purification: Scatter to purify a place.
  • Love and healing: Used in love work and healing rites.
  • Prophetic dreams: Placed beneath the pillow to dream true.
  • Baths and uncrossing: A bath (or infusion sponged on the body) destroys hexes and protects from future harm.

Botanical Source

Acacia dealbata of the Mimosaceae family, also classified as A. decurrens var. dealbata and known as Sydney black wattle. The tree reaches about 12 metres, bearing greyish-brown bark with irregular longitudinal ridges, delicate foliage, and clusters of ball-shaped fragrant yellow flowers.

Distribution

Native to Australia and naturalized in North and Central Africa. Brought to Europe as an ornamental in the early nineteenth century, it now grows wild on the continent. Concrete and absolute production is concentrated in southern France and Italy.

Other Species

The genus Acacia contains many close relatives. The East African type (A. arabica) is very similar. The florist-shop "mimosa" is A. floribunda. Brazilian mimosa or sensitive plant (Mimosa humilis) yields a homoeopathic tincture used for swelling of the ankles. Mimosa is also closely related to cassie.

Folk Medicine

The bark, traded as "wattle bark," carries a leather-like odour and astringent taste, with up to 42 percent tannins along with gallic acid. The tanning industry relies on it extensively. Medicinally the bark is used in parallel to oak bark, particularly as a specific for diarrhoea and as an astringent gargle and ointment. Black catechu (A. catechu), a related extract, appears in the British Herbal Pharmacopoeia as a specific for chronic diarrhoea with colitis.

Therapeutic Actions

Antiseptic and astringent.
