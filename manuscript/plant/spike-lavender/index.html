Spike Lavender Essential Oil

An aromatic evergreen sub-shrub of the Lamiaceae family, growing up to 1 metre high with lance-shaped leaves that are broader and rougher than those of Lavender. The flower is more compressed and of a dull grey-blue colour.

Origin & Distribution

Native to the mountainous regions of France and Spain. Also found in North Africa, Italy, the former Yugoslavia, and the eastern Mediterranean countries. Cultivated internationally; the oil is mainly produced in France and Spain.

Other Species

There are many different chemotypes of spike lavender. The French spike oil is reputed to have a more delicate, aromatic scent than the Spanish variety.

Folk Tradition (Medicinal)

Culpeper recommended spike lavender for ailments including pains of the head and brain proceeding from cold, apoplexy, falling sickness, the dropsy, sluggish malady, cramps, convulsions, palsies, and faintings, while warning that the oil of spike is of a fierce and piercing quality and should be used carefully, with a very few drops sufficient for inward or outward maladies. The preparation oleum spicae was made by mixing one part spike oil with three parts turpentine and was used for paralysed limbs, old sprains, and stiff joints; it was also said to encourage hair growth. Spike lavender is current in the British Herbal Pharmacopoeia, indicated for flatulent dyspepsia, colic, depressive headaches, and the oil (topically) for rheumatic pain.

Related Species

See Lavender (true lavender, Lavandula angustifolia) and Lavandin (L. x intermedia, the hybrid of true lavender and spike lavender) for the other commercial lavender oils.
