Elecampane (Inula helenium) is a tall perennial herb with large yellow daisy-like flowers.

Magickal Properties

  • Gender: Masculine
  • Planet: Mercury
  • Element: Air
  • Powers: Love, Protection, Psychic Powers

Folk Names

Elf Dock, Elfwort, Horseheal, Scabwort, Velvet Dock, Wild Sunflower, Yellow Starwort

Magickal Uses

General cure-all: In ancient times elecampane was regarded as a general cure-all.

Love potions and sachets: It was used in love potions, sachets, and incense, usually mixed with vervain and mistletoe.

For love: Sew elecampane into pink or red cloth and wear or carry to attract love.

For protection: The herb protects against evil spirits when worn or carried, and when burned, the smoke drives away harmful energies.

For psychic powers: Use elecampane in incense blends to enhance psychic abilities and promote clairvoyant visions.

For scrying: Elecampane holds a special affinity for scrying and sharpens psychic perception. Burn it alongside divination by crystal ball, magick mirror, or any other scrying method.

Botanical Information

Scientific name: Inula helenium

Botanical family: Asteraceae (Compositae)

Type: Flower / Herb / Root (perennial)

Height: Up to 1.5 metres

Description: A handsome perennial with a stout, soft-haired stem, oval pointed leaves that are velvety on the underside, large yellow daisy-like flowers, and substantial fleshy rhizome roots.

Origin: Native to Europe and Asia; naturalized in North America. Cultivated in Belgium, France, and Germany, as well as China and India.

Distribution

Elecampane grows wild across Europe and temperate Asia, with a long-established presence in North America since colonial introduction. Cultivation is concentrated in Belgium, France, Germany, China, and India. The essential oil is produced almost entirely in southern France from imported roots.

Other Species

The genus Inula is broad and contains several aromatic species:

  • Inula crithmoides (golden samphire)
  • Inula graveolens / I. odora (sweet inula), the practical aromatherapy oil of the genus and a noted mucolytic that avoids the sensitization problems of true Elecampane
  • European and Asian variants of I. helenium itself differ slightly, with the Asian forms carrying a harsher scent

Folk Medicine

Elecampane is a herb of ancient medical repute. The candied root was once sold as a sweetmeat, and the plant remains an important spice, incense, and medicine throughout the east. Both Western and Eastern herbalism work with it, most often as a tea, for:

  • Respiratory complaints, including asthma, bronchitis, and whooping cough
  • Disorders of digestion, the intestines, and the gall bladder
  • Skin disorders

The root remains current in the British Herbal Pharmacopoeia as a specific remedy for irritating cough and bronchitis. Elecampane root is also the richest known source of inulin.

Therapeutic Actions

Alterative, anthelmintic, anti-inflammatory, antiseptic, antispasmodic, antitussive, astringent, bactericidal, diaphoretic, diuretic, expectorant, fungicidal, hyperglycaemic, hypotensive, stomachic, and tonic.

Other Uses

Alantolactone, the principal sesquiterpene lactone of the root, is used in Europe as an anthelmintic and is also a strong bactericide. The essential oil and absolute serve as fixatives and fragrance components in soaps, detergents, cosmetics, and perfumes, and as flavouring ingredients in alcoholic beverages, soft drinks, and dessert foods.
