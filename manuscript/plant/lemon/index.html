Botanical Information

Citrus limon (Rutaceae family). Masculine fruit tree originating from South Asia and Southeast Asia. Takes over 1,300 lemons to make one pound of essential oil from the peels.

Characteristics

A small evergreen tree reaching up to 6 metres in height, with serrated oval leaves, stiff thorns, and very fragrant flowers. The fruit ripens from green to yellow.

Origin & Distribution

Native to Asia, probably east India. The lemon now grows wild throughout the Mediterranean region, especially in Spain and Portugal. It is cultivated extensively worldwide in Italy, Sicily, Cyprus, Guinea, Israel, and across South and North America, including California and Florida.

Related Species

About forty-seven varieties have been developed in cultivation, including the Java lemon (Citrus javanica). The lemon is closely related to lime, cedrat (citron), and bergamot.

Folk Tradition

The juice and peel are widely used as a domestic seasoning. The fruit is highly nutritious and rich in vitamins A, B, and C. In Spain and other European countries, lemon is treated as something of a cure-all, especially against infectious illness, and was used historically for fevers including malaria and typhoid. It was carried specifically for scurvy on English ships at sea. Taken internally, the juice is considered invaluable for acidic disorders such as arthritis and rheumatism, and of great benefit for dysentery and liver congestion. Recorded actions include anti-anaemic, antimicrobial, antirheumatic, antisclerotic, antiscorbutic, antiseptic, antispasmodic, antitoxic, astringent, bactericidal, carminative, cicatrisant, depurative, diaphoretic, diuretic, febrifuge, haemostatic, hypotensive, insecticidal, rubefacient, tonic, and vermifuge. The oil also stimulates white corpuscles.

Herbal Lore

Lemon is a high-vibration citrus with a battery-like energetic charge from its natural acid content. The bright, clean essence clears mental cobwebs and amplifies magickal workings. Traditional use in love magick, purification rituals, and friendship spells spans many cultures.

Magickal Uses

Lemon juice mixed with water cleanses second-hand magickal objects and dispels lingering vibrations. Adding juice to bathwater at the full Moon produces purification, while dried peel creates protective boundaries around property. The natural acid in lemon acts as a battery for spells; adding it to mojo bags before sealing, or dressing them weekly with lemon, maintains their working power and increases the efficacy of all magickal operations.

Lemon soothes nervous agitation and provides clear, focused thinking, making it a useful aid in meditation and decision-making work. A slice placed under a visitor's chair ensures lasting friendship, eases loneliness, and increases confidence and social happiness. A lemon tree grown from the seed of an eaten lemon makes a potent gift of affection. In candle spells lemon draws financial abundance and material manifestation, and its bright essence restores vitality quickly. Dried flowers and peel in love sachets attract devoted partners and raise vibrations of readiness for new relationships.

Lemon's bright energy helps contact the faerie folk; work with care and respect. Adding lemon to water strengthens scrying and divination work, and lemon is associated with water magick and spirit communication. It increases joy, lightens mood, and relieves stress. Its reputation extends to longevity magick, keeping spells active long after other ingredients fade. Lemon also aids the processing and release of painful experiences, dissipates depression, and opens the soul to releasing old patterns. Studding a green lemon with colored pins and hanging it serves as a charm for blessings and luck.

When lemon blossom is gifted it carries the meaning of fidelity in love and a promise to be true.
