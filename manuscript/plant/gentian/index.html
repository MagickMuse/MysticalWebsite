Gentian (Gentiana lutea) is a tall perennial herb with large yellow flowers, prized for its bitter root with magickal properties.

History

Gentian root has been in medicinal use for more than two thousand years and receives mention in Pliny the Elder's Naturalis Historia. The plant is thought to take its name from Gentius, an ancient king of Illyria who ruled from 181 B.C.E. until his defeat by the Romans in 168 B.C.E. and who is credited with discovering the root's medicinal applications.

Characteristics

Gentian is a tall, flowering herb with a sturdy stem and clusters of bright orange and yellow flowers. Native to central and southern Europe, it grows in alpine and subalpine meadows. The root is the part used medicinally and magickally, and it requires several years to reach full maturity. Gentian root is perhaps most widely known today as the primary bittering agent in Angostura bitters.

Magickal Properties

Powers: Love, Power

Folk Names

Bitter Root, Yellow Gentian, Hochwurzel

Magickal Uses

For love: Gentian is added to love baths and sachets. Chips of the dried root can also be blended into an incense mixture and burned to draw love, or the root can be carried in a sachet or charm pouch for the same purpose.

For power enhancement: When used in any incense or sachet gentian adds a great deal of extra power.

For hex breaking: It is also used to break hexes and curses.

Healing Magick

For magic in abating a chronic illness or recovering from a lengthy one, enclose a piece of gentian root in a red cloth and carry it on your person as a talisman.

Culinary & Craft Uses

Gentian is the traditional bitter herb — Angostura bitters are made with gentian root. The intensely bitter flavor makes it essential for crafting homemade bitters, but requires a very light hand.

Meaning When Gifted

When gentian is gifted its meaning varies by type.

  • Fringed Gentian: Intrinsic worth, "I look to heaven," fall
  • Closed Gentian: Sweet be thy dreams
