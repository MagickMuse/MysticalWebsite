Magickal Uses

For psychic vision and divination: Burn cascarilla bark as incense to open psychic sight and enhance divinatory work. The agreeable, musk-like aroma creates a receptive atmosphere for scrying and trance.

For magick: Cascarilla is a general enhancer of magickal workings when burned as incense. Note: inhaling the smoke in quantity can cause mild intoxication — this practice is not recommended.

Cascarilla (Croton eluteria) belongs to the Euphorbiaceae family. Synonyms include cascarilla, sweetwood bark, sweet bark, Bahama cascarilla, aromatic quinquina, and false quinquina.

Description

A large shrub or small tree up to 12 metres high, with ovate silver-bronze leaves, pale yellowish-brown bark, and small white fragrant flowers. It bears fruits and flowers all year round.

Distribution

Native to the West Indies, probably the Bahama Islands. Found growing wild in Mexico, Colombia, and Ecuador. The oil is mainly produced in the Bahamas and Cuba; some distillation also takes place in America, France, and England from imported bark.

Other Species

An essential oil is also distilled locally from other Croton species. White, red, and black cascarillas are also found in commerce.

Folk Tradition

The bark is used as an aromatic bitter and tonic for dyspepsia, diarrhoea, dysentery, fever, debility, nausea, flatulence, vomiting, and chronic bronchitis. The leaves are used as a digestive tea and for flavouring tobacco. The bark also yields a good black dye.

Cascarilla Bark Essential Oil

For the steam-distilled oil drawn from the dried bark, see Cascarilla Bark Essential Oil.

Disambiguation

Not to be confused with the eggshell-based protective powder used in Hoodoo and Lukumi practice, which is also called "cascarilla" or "cascarilla powder" but has no botanical relation to this tree. See Cascarilla Powder.
