Buchu (Agathosma betulina, also Barosma betulina) is a small aromatic shrub from the Cape of Good Hope.

Characteristics

A small shrub with simple, wrinkled leaves about 1 to 2 cm long, alongside much smaller bright green leaves with finely serrated margins. Delicate stems bear small five-petalled white flowers. The whole plant carries a strong, aromatic, blackcurrant-like odour.

Origin & Distribution

Native to the Cape of Good Hope in South Africa, where it now grows wild throughout the country. Dried buchu leaves are exported to the Netherlands, the United Kingdom, and the United States.

Related Species

More than twelve so-called Barosma species grow in South Africa. The three considered "true" buchus are:

  • B. betulina (the species covered here): yields oils high in diosphenol.
  • B. crenulata: yields oils high in pulegone, a toxic constituent.
  • B. serratifolia

Folk Medicine

Locally in South Africa, the leaves are used as an antiseptic and to ward off insects. In Western herbalism, buchu leaves are taken for infections of the genito-urinary system, including cystitis, urethritis, and prostatitis. The plant is listed in the British Herbal Pharmacopoeia.

Magickal Properties

  • Planet: Moon
  • Powers: Psychic Powers, Prophetic Dreams

Folk Names

Bookoo, Bucoo, Buku, Oval Buchu, Short Buchu, Sab, Pinkaou

Magickal Uses

For ritual incense: Buchu leaves are burned in ritual incense for their powerful aroma and to raise energy.

For divination: An infusion of buchu, when drunk, enables one to foretell the future.

For prophetic dreams: Frankincense mixed with buchu is burned directly before retiring to produce prophetic dreams. Only a small amount should be burned, and this must be in the bedroom.

Safety

Not recommended internally during pregnancy or while nursing. While the dried leaves used as tea or tincture are considered safe in moderation, the Buchu Essential Oil is of questionable safety due to its high diosphenol content and should not be used in home aromatherapy.
