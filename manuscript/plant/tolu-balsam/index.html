Magickal Uses

Mix with other herbs or rub on candles in spells for success. Burn as incense — its sweet, aromatic odor resembling vanilla or benzoin makes it a natural solar incense for workings of achievement and control.

Tolu Balsam (Myroxylon balsamum var. balsamum) is a tropical South American tree whose fragrant resin is used in pharmacy and perfumery.

Characteristics

The Tolu tree grows tall and graceful, closely resembling its sister, the Peru Balsam tree. Harvesters carve V-shaped incisions into the bark and sapwood, sometimes after the trunk has been beaten or scorched. The tree exudes a sticky pathological resin that flows out as a fragrant liquid before hardening into an orange-brown brittle mass.

Origin & Habitat

Native to South America, with the finest crude balsam coming from Venezuela, Colombia, and Cuba. The tree is also cultivated in the West Indies.

Related Species

The Tolu tree is one of two named varieties of Myroxylon balsamum. Its sister variety, var. pereirae, is the source of Peru Balsam. The two trees look similar and yield related but distinct balsams, each with its own scent profile and chemistry. Many other South American hardwoods produce balsam-like resins, though Tolu and Peru remain the two "true" balsams of commerce.

Folk Tradition

Tolu Balsam has long been valued for its soothing effect on the chest. Traditional remedies use it for chronic congestion, lingering coughs, laryngitis, and croup, and it still appears as a flavoring and gentle expectorant in cough syrups and lozenges. Combined with benzoin in compound tinctures, it is applied to cracked nipples, lips, cuts, and bedsores to support healing.
