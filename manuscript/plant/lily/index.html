Lily (Lilium spp.) wards spirits, breaks bewitchment, and brings signs.

Ghost Magick

Tiger lilies planted near doors and windows are believed to prevent the entry of ghosts into a home.

Lilies kept in the home and garden will not repel or banish ghosts, but they are believed to keep any resident ghosts well-behaved, preventing them from causing harm or mischief.

Magickal Properties

Deities: Venus, Juno, Nephthys, Kwan Yin

Powers: Protection, Breaking Love Spells

Magickal Uses

  • Spirit ward: Plant lilies to keep away ghosts and evil, protect against the evil eye, and deter unwanted visitors.
  • Break love spells: Wear or carry a fresh lily to break love spells cast involving a specific person.
  • Divination of crime: To gain clues in solving a crime committed within the past year, bury an old piece of leather in a bed of lilies.
  • First bloom omen: The first white lily of the season brings strength to the one who finds it.

Additional Notes

White lilies are connected to death and the afterlife, often appearing at funerals and memorials. This association makes them powerful for ancestor work and communication with those who have passed.

Safety: Some species are poisonous; do not use internally. Lilies are highly toxic to cats.

A Family Too Large for One Name

It is almost unfair to give a single section the title of Lily, because the lily family itself is vast. The Madonna lily, native to the Middle East and the Balkans, is the one most often meant in old European herbals and the one most often pictured in religious art. The Asiatic lilies come in every shade from pure white through pale yellow to deep maroon. Smaller, related species are native to the Americas, from the prairies of Minnesota down through the foothills of California. Of those many American plants, nearly all have edible roots. California in particular holds an unusual abundance and variety of bulbous-rooted lily relatives, more than almost anywhere else in the world. Some of those roots are foods of Indigenous tradition and should be treated with care if you are not from those communities. ⚠️ Closed-practice cautions apply to harvesting and ceremonial use of native California lily relatives without invitation from the people who hold those teachings.

Madonna and Meadow

The Madonna lily, also called meadow lily or simply white lily, is the lily of the Annunciation, the lily of the resurrection, and the lily of weddings and funerals across centuries of Christian art. Its bulbs have been used for centuries in folk medicine, ground with honey and pressed as a topical paste against infections, snake bites, bruises, blemishes, and wrinkles. Older treatments boiled the bulbs to make a remedy for corns and calluses. Newer research is examining the plant for antidiabetic and anti-inflammatory action. Magickally, the Madonna lily anchors purity, immortality, and resurrection, and is the lily of choice for any rite of return to life.

A Sacred Trinity of Flowers

Along with the rose and the lotus, the lily forms a sacred trinity of the most important flower symbols in the world. Not all lilies are white, but paleness is so bound to the flower that we still call a thing lily white, and from that whiteness comes its oldest meaning: purity, innocence, and virginity.

Virgin, Fertile, and Mother at Once

The Angel Gabriel is said to have appeared to Mary carrying a lily, and the flower has belonged to the Virgin ever since. Yet the lily is not only chaste. The shape of its petals and the phallic pistils standing erect from its center make it a symbol of sexuality and reproduction as well, so that a single bloom holds both male and female generative parts. This is why, in images of Mary, the lily can mean virginity, fertility, and motherhood all at once.

The Goddess, Lilith, and Eostre

The lily is the flower of the Goddess in whatever form she takes. The Babylonian goddess Lilith, named in legend as the first wife of Adam before the Church recast her as a demon, takes her name from the lily, the Lilu. The flower is sacred too to Astarte, who in parts of Europe was called Eostre, the root of our word Easter. The Easter lily, then, is a quiet survival of a far older devotion.

The Fleur-de-lys

The fleur-de-lys, the flower of the lily, is a stylized emblem that grew from both the lotus and the lily. It stands for the Trinity and for the Triple Goddess, gathering up all the other meanings of the lily, and it was used so widely in France that it became the badge of royalty and nobility.

The Calla and the Day Lily

The calla lily in particular is a flower of resurrection, which is why it is carried at funerals and carved on gravestones. The day lily keeps a different lesson: each bloom opens for a single day and is gone, and so it stands for a carefree nature that does not cling to what passes.

The Lily Belonged First to Hera

The lily was, before it was the Madonna's flower, the flower of Hera. The myth gives the origin: Zeus tricked his wife into nursing the infant Heracles so that her milk would make him immortal. When she woke and tore him from her breast, the milk scattered in two directions. What rose to the sky became the Milky Way. What fell to earth sprouted as lilies. Every white lily carries both halves of that story, the goddess's stolen milk and the goddess's tearing rage, the route to immortality and the cost of it. For this reason the lily is at home in fertility work as well as resurrection work, and the two are not as far apart as they sound.

Self-Resurrection and Abundance

Fresh, fragrant lilies on the altar mark any working that asks something to come back to life: a self that was lost, a body that was harmed, a project that was abandoned, a stream of money that dried up. The flower's two lineages, milk-born and resurrection-born, both feed the work. Use white lilies for purity and resurrection, yellow for joy, orange or maroon for fertility and abundance, and trust the scent itself to carry the magick whether you speak it or not.

Tiger Lilies and Leopard Lilies

Tiger lilies are bold, glorious, spotted, and unmistakable in the garden. The tiger lily most often sold in catalogs and stores is the Asiatic variety, brilliant orange with little dark spots that recall the coat of an Asian tiger. Many other tiger lilies are native to the Americas, ranging from deep orange to pale yellow, all with the signature dark spots and the prominent stamens that lean out of the bloom. These American spotted lilies are sometimes called leopard lilies. Among the American natives, the Humboldt lily is the rare one, tall and golden-orange and spotted, growing in the foothills of California and rewarding any sighting with a sense of luck.

The Flower Garden That Could Talk

In one famous fairy-tale flower garden, the tiger lily is the speaker. The garden is enchanted, the flowers are bossy, and the tiger lily has the loudest voice in it. When the small wandering girl asks why the flowers in her own garden cannot talk, the tiger lily answers that in most gardens the beds are made too soft, so the flowers stay asleep. The tiger lily silences the daisies, snaps at the violet, and gets the rose to behave. As a piece of flower lore it is half joke and half serious correspondence work, casting the tiger lily as the watch-flower of any enchanted garden: vivid, fearless, and uninterested in being polite.

Color and Meaning

The color of a lily shapes its meaning and its use. A white lily stands for purity and is the standard wedding and funeral flower in the Western tradition, marking both the beginning of a vowed life and the end of one. Yellow lilies can mean joy or, in older flower-language books, can mean a lie or a falsehood, so they are double-edged and ask for clear intention when used in spellwork. Orange and red lilies lean toward passion, fertility, and bold abundance. Pink lilies lean toward gentleness, prosperity, and admiration. Tiger and leopard lilies, with their spots, lean toward courage, ferocity, and protection of the home.

A Note on Cats

Lilies of the Lilium and Hemerocallis genera are profoundly toxic to cats. A small amount of pollen on a cat's fur, licked off during grooming, can cause kidney failure. If you share your home with a cat, keep cut lilies out entirely or use only the silk versions for visual altar work, and use the scent through essential oil at heights the cat cannot reach.

Meaning When Gifted

When lily is gifted it carries the meaning of majesty and honor and purity of heart.

  • Orange Lily: Hatred, dislike
  • Tiger Lily: Wealth, pride, prosperity
  • White Lily: Virginity, purity, majesty, "it's heavenly to be with you," youth
  • Yellow Lily: "I'm walking on air," falseness, gaiety, gratitude
