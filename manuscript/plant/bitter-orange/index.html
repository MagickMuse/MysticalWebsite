Bitter Orange

Notes/Warnings

An evergreen tree of the Rutaceae family up to 10 metres high, with dark green glossy oval leaves (paler beneath), long but not very sharp spines, a smooth greyish trunk and branches, and very fragrant white flowers. The fruits are smaller and darker than those of Orange. Bitter orange is well known for its resistance to disease and is often used as rootstock for other citrus trees, including sweet orange itself.

Origin & Distribution

Native to the Far East, especially India and China, but well adapted to the Mediterranean climate. Also grows abundantly in the USA (California), Israel, and South America. Main producers of the essential oil include Spain, Guinea, the West Indies, Italy, Brazil, and the USA.

Folk Tradition (Medicinal)

“Oranges and lemons strengthen the heart, are good for diminishing the coagulability of the blood, and are beneficial for palpitation, scurvy, jaundice, bleedings, heartburn, relaxed throat, etc. They are powerfully anti-scorbutic, either internally or externally applied.” The dried bitter orange peel is used as a tonic and carminative in treating dyspepsia.

In Chinese medicine the dried bitter orange, and occasionally its peel, are used in treating prolapse of the uterus and of the anus, diarrhoea, and blood in the faeces. Ingestion of large amounts of orange peel in children, however, has been reported to cause toxic effects.

Three Oils From One Tree

Bitter orange is unusual in that three commercially distinct essential oils are produced from different parts of the same tree:

  • Bitter Orange Essential Oil — cold expressed from the outer peel of the almost ripe fruit
  • Petitgrain Essential Oil — distilled from the leaves
  • Neroli Essential Oil — distilled from the blossom

A terpeneless oil version of the peel oil is also produced for industrial use.

Other Species

There are numerous different chemotypes of bitter orange according to location. Oils from Spain and Guinea are said to be of superior quality. See Orange (Citrus sinensis) for the closely related sweet citrus species, and Bergamot Orange for another distinct Citrus aromatic.
