Bay Laurel (Laurus nobilis) is an evergreen tree with aromatic leaves used extensively in cooking and magickal practices.

Characteristics

A handsome evergreen tree growing up to 20 metres high, with dark green glossy leaves and small black berries. Bay laurel is often cultivated as an ornamental shrub for its dense aromatic foliage. Native to the Mediterranean, it is now extensively cultivated for its leaves and berries across France, Spain, Italy, Morocco, the Balkans, China, Israel, Turkey, and Russia, with most essential oil production centered in the Balkans.

Related Species

Several unrelated trees share the common name "Bay" and are sometimes mistaken for true bay laurel. Californian Bay (Umbellularia californica) and West Indian Bay (Pimenta racemosa, the source of bay rum) yield very different oils. The Cherry Laurel (Prunus laurocerasus) shares the laurel name but is poisonous and should never be substituted for true bay in cooking, medicine, or ritual.

Lore & History

Bay laurel was used to fashion wreaths in ancient Greece that symbolized high status. These wreaths were originally given as prizes to athletes at the Pythian Games, but their use spread to victorious generals, poets, and musicians. The symbolism of victory and triumph carried over into Roman culture and persists today. "Laurel" is the source of the word laureate (someone who has received high honors for a creative or technical achievement) and the expression "to rest on one's laurels."

In the ancient world, bay laurel was particularly associated with the Greek sun god Apollo, who ruled over prophecy and trance. He presided over the Temple at Delphi, where his oracle (also called a pythia or sibyl) would fall into trance and deliver prophecy. Some ancient sources claim the oracle entered trance through the use of bay laurel, though it is unclear whether these sources were referring to the same plant known by that name today.

Ghost Magick

A wreath of fresh bay laurel leaves posted on entrance doors signals to ghosts that they are not welcome and may not enter.

Magickal Uses

Sacred to Apollo and the Sun, laurel garlands were given to poets, heroes, and others who excelled in courage, service, and the creation of beauty. The priestesses of the Triple Goddess and the Delphic oracles chewed the leaves for prophecy, and bay is used in clairvoyance and wisdom brews as well, though its taste is strong. Bay leaves placed beneath the pillow induce prophetic dreams, and burning them causes visions. The ancient priestesses also inhaled their fumes to induce a prophetic state, and bay leaves are used as decorations at Yule.

Bay is a protection and purification herb par excellence. Wearing it as an amulet wards off negativity and evil; burning or scattering it during exorcism rituals clears the space; placing it in windows protects against lightning; and hanging it up prevents poltergeists. Place at least one bay leaf in each corner of every room to create an aura of protection. A sprig of bay is used to sprinkle water during purification ceremonies. The tree planted near the home protects inhabitants against sickness, and bay leaves mixed with sandalwood burned together remove curses and evil spells. Bay is also used in love charms, perfumes, sachets, and incenses of power.

To ensure a love will stay, couples break off a twig from the tree and split it in two, each keeping a half. Bay leaves give strength to those engaged in wrestling and athletic sports if worn during competition. They are a symbol of immortality and counteract the negativity and restriction of Saturn's influence. Wishes written on bay leaves and then burned are said to come true, and a bay leaf held in the mouth wards off bad luck.

Daphnomancy

Bay laurel has a deep association with the Oracle of Delphi. The pythonesses of Delphi were said to deliver their prophecies with bay leaves clenched between their teeth, and the fumes they inhaled to induce visionary states were widely believed to contain bay laurel as a key component, though the full formula has never been identified. The oracle's association with bay runs even deeper through the myth of Daphne, a pythoness said to have been transformed into the very first bay laurel tree while fleeing Apollo. In this myth the tree offered Daphne an avenue of escape when she was fleeing rape.

A second oracular shrine, named for Daphne and located near Tarsus, received its oracles by dipping bay leaves or branches into a sacred spring. This shrine, set within celebrated groves, was an early target of Christian suppression. The practice of bay laurel divination, known as daphnomancy, survived independently.

To perform daphnomancy, hold bay leaves in your hand while concentrating on your question. Then either place a single leaf onto a glowing charcoal or toss several into an open flame. Leaves that crackle loudly and burn brightly signal a favorable outcome. Leaves that sputter, resist burning, or extinguish the flame signal that plans should be reconsidered or postponed. Flames that shoot cleanly and directly upward from the leaves indicate strong auspicious conditions.

Ancestor Veneration & Mediumship

Bay laurel is a popular choice for fumigations when working with the ancestors, especially during mediumship or divination sessions. The leaves smolder well and can be burned on their own. They can also be crushed and burned alongside frankincense, which shares bay laurel's solar attributes.

The dried leaves are large and flat enough that short petitions can be written on them. They are especially suited to petitions related to developing ability as a medium or diviner, or petitions addressed specifically to the ancestors.

The Greek Magical Papyri include several spells calling for bay laurel. In particular, PGM I. 262-347 describes creating a protective charm by tracing magical characters onto a seven-leafed sprig of laurel and invoking Apollo. This can be adapted by placing seven bay laurel leaves inscribed with magical symbols or petitions in an oil lamp and burning it during prayer.

Amulet Uses

A bay leaf carried as an amulet provides protection against spells and evil.

Folk Medicine

Both the leaves and the berries were once used for a wide range of afflictions, from hysteria and colic to indigestion and loss of appetite, as well as to promote menstruation and to bring down fever. Internal use has fallen out of favor due to bay's mildly narcotic properties, but a fixed oil expressed from the berries is still applied to sprains, bruises, and earache.

Culinary Uses

Bay leaves are a kitchen staple for flavoring soups and stews, infusing slow-cooked dishes with depth and warmth.

Moon Magick

Key themes for lunar workings: Protection, strength, prophecy, healing, purification, wisdom, success.

The Crown of Victory

The leaves that crown victorious leaders and great men are those of the bay laurel, the same tree that gives the kitchen its bay. Sacred to the Greek god Apollo, the laurel stands for the courage and wisdom that victory demands, and, like other evergreens, for longevity and immortality, all the more reason it was chosen for the victor's wreath. To chew or burn its leaves was said to grant second sight and prophecy, and so the oracular priestesses, the Pythonesses of Delphi, used it before they spoke their prophecies. In China too the laurel is a sign of immortality, and the sacred hare that lives in the Moon is said to pound its leaves into the drug that grants immortality to any bold enough to take it. The thought of fame and everlasting life lingers even in the tree's name, for Britain's Poet Laureate is named for the laurel.
