Henna (Lawsonia inermis) is a sacred dye plant whose cooling virtue heals, guards, and draws affection.

Folk Tradition

The dye is made from the ground leaves of the shrub, yielding a reddish-orange stain that women have used to color hair and skin for thousands of years, in equal measure for adornment and for ritual. These designs are called mehendi, and their patterns carry a rich symbolic language all their own. Henna symbols painted onto houses, and onto doorways in particular, are said to shield the home from the evil eye, while the dye is also marked on the foreheads of cattle and daubed onto tombs to turn away malevolent spirits.

Magickal Uses

  • Headache relief: Place henna upon the forehead to ease pain.
  • Love magnetism: Worn near the heart, henna attracts love.
  • Apotropaic: Protects from illness and the evil eye; traditional protective body art marks the skin with its blessing.
  • Sleep: Fill a dream pillow with powdered henna to promote deep, restful, restorative sleep.
  • Incense: Used in exorcism and uncrossing incense blends.
