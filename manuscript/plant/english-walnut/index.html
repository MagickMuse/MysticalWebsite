Source — Specific Uses

J. regia is native to Greece and Asia. Considered a food of the gods. Carry the nut in its shell to promote fertility, strengthen the heart, or ward off rheumatism.

The English or Persian walnut (Juglans regia) is the walnut of Old World myth and witch-lore, distinct from the American black walnut. In Celtic symbolism every nut-bearing tree is sacred, for the nut is held to guard a hidden, secret knowledge, and the walnut is no exception.

Folk Tradition

In Greek myth Artemis, gifted with second sight, was transformed into a walnut tree, so that the tree and its fruit became sacred to the Goddess and to the arts of prophecy. Darker tales tell of walnuts that harboured she-devils, who would whisper obscenities and lewd suggestions to those who passed beneath; the Church of Santa Maria del Popolo in Rome is said to stand where one such foul-mouthed tree was felled when its profanities grew unbearable.

Because the walnut allows few other plants to grow beneath it, it earned a reputation, especially in Italy, as the tree of witches. Country wisdom held that a walnut yields a richer crop of nuts if it is beaten, and that a heavy crop foretells a hard winter to come.
