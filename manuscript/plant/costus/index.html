Costus (Saussurea costus) is a tall perennial herb native to the Himalayas, prized for its aromatic root.

Botanical Information

Costus grows at high altitudes in India, Pakistan, and China. The plant produces purple flowers, but it is the thick, woody root that is harvested for magickal and medicinal use. The root has a strong, complex scent that is woody, earthy, and slightly animal-like.

Related Species

Closely related to Elecampane (Inula helenium), whose roots are also used to produce an essential oil.

Folk Tradition

The root has been used for millennia in India and China for digestive complaints, respiratory conditions, as a stimulant, and for infection including typhoid and cholera. It is also used as an incense.

Magickal Properties

Costus root has been used in ceremonial magic and planetary workings since antiquity. Its deep, grounding scent makes it valuable in incense blends designed for general magickal workings and spirit communication. The root is traditionally burned to consecrate ritual spaces and enhance the power of other ingredients in a blend.

Ritual Uses

Burn as part of planetary incense formulas. Add to incense blends for consecration and general magickal work. The root can be ground and mixed with other resins for a rich, complex smoke.
