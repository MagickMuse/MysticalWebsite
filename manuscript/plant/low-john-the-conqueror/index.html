Low John the Conqueror (Trillium erectum), also called Bethroot or Wake Robin, is a North American woodland perennial used in Hoodoo and folk magick as a root charm for victory and good fortune.

Not to be confused with High John the Conqueror (Ipomoea jalapa), which is a far more aggressive, conquering root. Low John works in the quieter registers: romantic success, interpersonal harmony, and legal victories of a gentler, more relationship-based nature. It is carried as a charm rather than consumed or burned.

Low John is traditionally placed in Notre Dame Water overnight to charge before carrying. A piece of root is also carried as a charm to draw success in all things — success, prosperity, and luck in any endeavor.

⚠️
Closed Practice Notice: Low John root use is central to Hoodoo, a closed African American folk magick tradition. Please research and respect the cultural context.

Magickal Correspondences

  • Primary use: Romantic and interpersonal victory, legal success, all-purpose good fortune
  • Tradition: Hoodoo (closed practice)
  • Form: Carried whole as a root charm
  • Home Peace: Hanging Low John root within the home stimulates family peace, happiness, and harmony, allowing its influence to radiate throughout the space.
