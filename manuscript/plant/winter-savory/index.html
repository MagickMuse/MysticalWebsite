Winter Savory is a bushy perennial subshrub of the Lamiaceae family, native to the Mediterranean region.

Characteristics

The plant grows up to 40 centimetres high with woody stems at the base, linear leaves, and pale purple flowers.

Origin & Distribution

Native to the Mediterranean region. Now found throughout Europe, Turkey, and the western Caucasus. The essential oil is mainly produced in Spain, Morocco, and the western Balkans.

Other Names & Related Species

The creeping variety S. montana subspicata is also a well-known garden herb. See also Savory (Satureja hortensis).

Folk Tradition

Winter savory has been used as a culinary herb since antiquity, much in the same way as summer savory. It was used as a digestive remedy especially good for colic, and in Germany it is used particularly for diarrhoea. When compared against many varieties of thyme, rosemary, and lavender, research has shown the net superiority of the antimicrobial properties of essence of savory.

Safety

The essential oil should not be used on the skin at all and is not for home aromatherapy use.
