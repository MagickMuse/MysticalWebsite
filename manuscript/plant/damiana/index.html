Damiana (Turnera diffusa) is a small shrub with serrated leaves and small yellow flowers.

History

Damiana arrived in American commerce in 1874, introduced as an aphrodisiac by a pharmacist who promoted it as a pelvic toner, virility amplifier, and fertility aid. Its reputation as an herb of sexual enhancement outlasted the eventual discovery that its chemical properties have no measurable impact on sexual function. What the plant does contain are complex volatile oils, which give it its distinctive taste and aroma -- both qualities long associated with sensuality.

Characteristics

Native to Mexico, damiana is a leafy, aromatic shrub. Its leaves have been used in tinctures, blended into tonic preparations alongside other ingredients, and brewed as infusions.

For lust: Damiana is used in lust infusions and spells. It is also burned to produce visions.

For love: When mixed with equal parts of mint and burned on charcoal it can help bring prophetic dreams of a future love.

For the Goddess: Damiana is associated with Goddess offerings and workings.

For summoning affection: Damiana leaves are used in charms intended to draw the object of affection closer.

For existing relationships: Damiana acts as a magickal restorative, helping to rejuvenate connections that have grown stagnant or distant.
