Rosehip (Rosa canina and related species including R. rugosa, R. acicularis, and R. cinnamomea) is a gentle talisman herb of love, peace, and serenity.

History

Rosehips became particularly prized during World War II, when the berries' extraordinarily high vitamin C content made them a valued substitute for citrus fruits that had become scarce.

Characteristics

Rosehips are the small, red, oblong fruits produced by the dog rose, the Japanese rose, and several related species. They appear after the blossom has faded, on plants that can grow up to five feet tall. Used in capsules, extracts, teas, and purées, rosehips are most widely valued for their vitamin C content. They also appear in jellies and syrups.

Magickal Uses

Dried rosehips strung on a thread and worn as a necklace or carried serve as a magickal talisman for attracting love and affection. Rosehips are also used in workings aimed at cultivating inner calm and outer harmony, making them well suited to peace spells and objects of serenity.

Magickal Teas

Rose hips can be brewed as a magickal tea to aid divination, draw love, strengthen protection, and support psychic healing. Drink 1 to 2 cups only during the spell event itself.
