Copaiba Balsam Essential Oil

Botanical Description

A wild-growing tropical tree reaching up to 18 metres high, with thick foliage and many spreading branches. The natural oleoresin occurs as a physiological product from various Copaifera species. Despite the common name, it is not considered a true balsam.

Family: Fabaceae (Leguminosae)

Related Species

Several Copaifera species yield an oleoresin, and the trade names reflect where the resin is harvested:

  • Maracaibo Balsam: the Venezuelan type, with a lower essential oil content.
  • Para Balsam: the Brazilian type, with a high essential oil content (60 to 80%) and the form most commonly used for distillation.

Folk Tradition

The oleoresin has been used for centuries in Europe in the treatment of chronic cystitis and bronchitis. It also has a long history of use for piles, chronic diarrhoea, and other intestinal problems.

Aromatherapy Use

  • Digestive System: Intestinal infections, piles.
  • Respiratory System: Bronchitis, chills, colds, coughs.
  • Genito-Urinary System: Cystitis.
  • Nervous System: Stress-related conditions.

Other Uses

The oleoresin appears in pharmaceutical products, especially cough medicines and diuretics. Both the oil and the crude balsam are extensively used as a fixative and fragrance component in all types of perfumes, soaps, cosmetics, and detergents. The crude balsam is also used in porcelain painting.
