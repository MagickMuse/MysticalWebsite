Yellow Flag (Iris pseudacorus) is the marsh-dwelling iris of purification, vision, and liminal watery spaces — a plant cited among the ingredients of the traditional witch's flying ointment and the likely origin of the fleur-de-lis symbol.

⚠️
CAUTION: Mildly toxic. Do not ingest. Handle the rhizome with gloves as the sap can irritate skin and eyes.

Magickal Properties

Gender: Feminine

Planet: Moon / Venus

Element: Water

Deities: Iris (goddess), Juno, Hera

Powers: Purification, Wisdom, Faith, Valor, Protection, Divination

Magickal Uses

  • Purification: Root added to purification baths and washes; dried root burned as incense to cleanse sacred spaces.
  • Divination: Associated with the goddess Iris, messenger between worlds; used in scrying and oracle work.
  • Protection: Planted at boundaries and thresholds to guard against negative forces; the fleur-de-lis is a protective symbol derived from this plant.
  • Wisdom and vision: The root's mildly psychoactive properties connect it to visionary work in small, controlled use.

The Witch's Flying Ointment

Yellow flag is among the plants cited in historical flying ointment formulas from the 1500s and 1600s. Its aquatic habitat placed it firmly within the symbolic world of Saturn and Water — liminal, dangerous, and associated with boundaries between worlds. The rhizome contains irisin and other compounds with mild psychoactive and emetic properties.

The Fleur-de-Lis

The famous fleur-de-lis, one of the most recognizable heraldic symbols in Western history, is widely believed to depict the yellow flag iris rather than a lily. The plant grew abundantly in the marshes along French rivers, and its stylized form became the symbol of the French royal house, representing purification, light, and perfection. The name itself is often translated as "flower of life," and irises are placed on graves as a symbol of life across many cultures, even as they carry their older associations with death and the Underworld.

The Threefold Flower

The three falling leaves of the iris flower are traditionally said to represent faith, wisdom, and valor. The plant carries these three virtues whenever it is brought into ritual, whether as a fresh bloom, a dried root, or as a planted boundary at a threshold.

Botanical Description

Yellow flag is a vigorous perennial that colonizes the margins of rivers, ponds, and marshy ground. It produces bright yellow flowers in early summer, with the distinctive falls and standards of the iris family. The rhizome is thick, creeping, and brownish-red inside — it was historically used as a source of black ink and tannin for curing leather.
