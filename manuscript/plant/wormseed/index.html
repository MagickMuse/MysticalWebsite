Wormseed Essential Oil

Wormseed is a hairy, coarse, perennial wayside herb of the Chenopodiaceae family, native to South America.

Characteristics

The plant grows up to 1 metre high, with a stout erect stem, oblong-lanceolate leaves, and numerous greenish-yellow flowers, the same colour as the leaves.

Origin & Distribution

Native to South America. Cultivated mainly in the eastern and southeastern United States, with additional production in India, Hungary, and across the eastern European range.

Other Names & Related Species

The parent plant, C. ambrosioides, is also used to produce an essential oil with similar properties. There are many members in the Chenopodium or Goosefoot family, such as Good King Henry (C. bonus-henricus), a European variety whose leaves were eaten like spinach. The so-called Russian wormseed oil, or wormseed Levant (Artemisia cina), is quite different from the American type, although it is also used as an anthelmintic. It is extremely toxic and contains mainly cineol.

Folk Tradition

⚠️ Wormseed has a long history of use by Indigenous peoples of the Americas as an anthelmintic, and several Indigenous tribes of the eastern United States used the whole herb decocted to ease painful menstruation and other female complaints. These applications are sacred to those traditions and should only be practiced by those within the originating culture.

Apart from being used to expel roundworm, hookworm, and dwarf tapeworm, the herb has also been employed for asthma, catarrh, and other chest complaints, and to treat nervous disease. In China it is used to treat articular rheumatism.

Safety

Extremely toxic. Cases of fatal poisoning have been reported even in low doses, and effects can be cumulative. Causes dizziness and vomiting in concentration.
