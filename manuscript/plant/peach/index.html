Peach (Prunus persica) sweetens love, expels evil, extends life, and divines thieves.

Magickal Properties

Gender: Feminine

Planet: Venus

Element: Water

Powers: Love, Exorcism, Longevity, Fertility, Wishes

Magickal Uses

  • Love: The fruit eaten to induce love; peach pie served to a desired one may help win their heart; also eaten to gain wisdom.
  • Exorcism and healing: Branches used in China to drive off evil spirits and root out illnesses; children wear a peach pit about the neck to keep demons away.
  • Longevity: Carry a bit of peach wood to increase life span; some traditions seek even immortality.
  • Fertility and divination tools: Japanese rites use peach branches as divining and magickal wands to increase fertility.
  • Protective tree: The peach is the primary protective tree of Chinese metaphysics, holding the place that Rowan holds in Northern Europe and Tamarisk in the Middle East.
  • Carved wood and pits: Peach wood or a dried peach pit carved into protective shapes is carried in a charm bag (see Carved Peach Wood Protection Charm), or carved into a padlock and hung on red cord (see Carved Peach Pit Padlock Charm).
  • Blossom door guard: Set blooming peach blossoms at the door to guard the household and keep evil from entering.

The Peach of Immortality

In China the peach is a symbol of immortality. Stories tell of a mythic garden kept by a beautiful woman, Siwang Mu, the Royal Lady of the West, where grew the Peach Tree of the Gods, which bloomed once every three thousand years and three thousand years after bore the Fruit of Eternal Life. To some Chinese secret societies the peach is an emblem of the immortality won through certain initiatory practices, and because it was held to confer eternal life the fruit belonged to the Eight Immortals of Taoism and counts among the Three Blessed Fruits of Buddhism. The tree itself is the Tree of Life. Wands of peach wood were thought especially powerful, as were the calligraphic brushes used in divination to write the ideograms that foretell the future, much of whose magick came from the peach wood. In both China and Japan the peach is said to guard against lightning, and figurines carved from its wood were set over doors at New Year to protect the house from that threat. The Shou, the emblem of immortality often woven into rugs and wall hangings, is based on the shape of a peach stone.

Vulva Symbolism and Protection

A deeply cleft peach is one of the traditional symbolic stand ins for the female genitals, the most protective of all images. Display peach imagery in the home or wear it as jewelry to borrow that protective power. See Vulva.

Meaning When Gifted

When peach blossom is gifted it carries the meaning of "I am your captive."
