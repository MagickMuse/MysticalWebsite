Bergamot Orange (Citrus bergamia) is a small Italian citrus tree. The fragrant peel is used for essential oil production and as a flavouring in Earl Grey tea.

Characteristics

A small evergreen tree growing to about 4.5 metres, with smooth oval leaves and small round fruits that ripen from green to yellow. The fruit looks like a miniature orange but is too sour to eat. Its value lies entirely in the aromatic peel.

Origin & Habitat

Originally native to tropical Asia, bergamot is now extensively cultivated in Calabria in southern Italy, where the bulk of the world's bergamot oil is produced. It is also grown commercially on the Ivory Coast.

Other Names

The tree took its modern name from the Italian city of Bergamo in Lombardy, where its oil was first sold and traded. Citrus aurantium subsp. bergamia is the other valid scientific name.

Despite the shared common name, bergamot orange should not be confused with Wild Bergamot or Bee Balm (Monarda didyma / Monarda fistulosa), an unrelated North American flowering herb, nor with Bergamot Mint (Mentha citrata), an aromatic mint hybrid. All three smell vaguely similar but have entirely different chemistry, properties, and uses.

Folk Tradition

In Italian folk medicine, the oil from the peel was used for centuries to treat fever (including malaria) and intestinal worms. The fruit barely registers in the folk medicine of any other country, yet its perfume is famous worldwide. Bergamot is the classic citrus heart of eau-de-cologne and the unmistakable scent of Earl Grey tea.
