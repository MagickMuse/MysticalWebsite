Enchanter's Nightshade (Circaea lutetiana or Circaea alpina) is Circe's plant; the enchanter in question is the Greek witch-goddess Circe.

Overview

This is Circe's plant; the enchanter in question is the Greek witch-goddess Circe. Other names for it include Walpurgis Herb, Great Witch Herb, Sorcerer of Paris (the Trojan prince, not the city), Paris Nightshade, Magic Herb, and Great and Common Witch's Herb.

In German, it's called Hexenkraut (Witches' Herb); its Anglo-Saxon name was Aelfthone, as it was believed to counter elf-derived illnesses.

Toxicity

Despite its common folk name, it is not as toxic as other plants nicknamed Nightshade, such as Black Nightshade, Deadly Nightshade or Russian Nightshade.

Two Species

There are two species of Enchanter's Nightshade:

Circaea lutetiana

The most common—Circaea lutetiana—is from Eurasia. It grows best near streams and damp, marshy places, often associated with witchcraft and magic.

Circaea alpina (Circe of the Alps)

A variation of the species prefers higher altitudes. Alpine Enchanter's Nightshade (Circaea alpina) is also called Circe of the Alps.

Magickal Uses

Both plants are associated with:

  • Hexes
  • Binding charms
  • Love spells

Evidence of Witchcraft

Enchanter's Nightshade was one of the plants whose possession was sufficient evidence to warrant accusation of witchcraft.
