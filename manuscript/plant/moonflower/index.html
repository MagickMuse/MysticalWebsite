History

Moonflower (Ipomoea alba) is a fast-growing tropical vine native to Central and South America. It is closely related to the morning glory (Ipomoea purpurea) and to the Aztec ritual plant ololiuqui (Ipomoea tricolor), and shares with them the presence of ergine (d-lysergic acid amide, LSA) and related ergoline alkaloids in its seeds — compounds with psychoactive properties used historically in Mesoamerican ceremonial contexts. The plant spread widely through trade and cultivation and is now grown in gardens throughout the world.

The moonflower is defined by its timing. Where the morning glory opens with the sun, the moonflower does the opposite: its large, pure white flowers remain tightly coiled through the day and unfurl as evening falls, releasing a sweet, faintly citrus fragrance that carries on the night air. They remain fully open through the night and close again by morning, belonging entirely to the hours of darkness.

Characteristics

Moonflower is a vigorous climbing vine that can grow 10–15 feet or more in a season. Leaves are large and heart-shaped; flowers are 4–6 inches across, pristine white with a faint green star-shaped outline at the center. It prefers full sun during the day, warm temperatures, and support to climb — a trellis, fence, or pentacle armature in a window frame. It blooms from midsummer through first frost.

Toxic Profile

All parts of the plant are toxic if ingested, particularly the seeds, which contain ergine and related compounds. Symptoms of ingestion include nausea, confusion, and hallucinations. Handle seeds with care; do not use internally.

Magickal Uses

  • Nocturnal altar: The signature plant of the Night Blooming Living Altar — trained over a pentacle armature in a window, it creates a living protective ward that glows white by moonlight through the night hours.
  • Protective charm: A moonflower pentacle in a window casts a protective charm over the home. Recite a warding verse at each new moon to reinforce it.
  • Moon magick: Conduct lunar workings by moonflower light during full moon nights when the blossoms are open. The flowers serve as natural moon-mirrors.
  • Psychic work: The vine's connection to Mesoamerican visionary traditions — via its ergoline relatives — gives it associations with psychic opening, dream work, and boundary-crossing. Work with the living plant or dried flowers for this purpose; do not ingest.
  • Liminal rites: Because it blooms only between darkness and dawn, moonflower is ideal for workings at the veil — Samhain, dark moon nights, and any ritual of passage or threshold-crossing.

The Witch's Flying Ointment

Moonflower shares its botanical family with several plants historically connected to altered states and the witch's craft. Though not itself a traditional flying ointment herb, its ergoline-containing seeds place it in proximity to the psychoactive species used in Mesoamerican shamanic practice. It is included here as a plant of the liminal and the nocturnal — not for internal use, but for its symbolic and energetic resonance with the tradition of night-working plants.
