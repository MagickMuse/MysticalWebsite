Peony (Paeonia officinalis) is a powerful guardian and exorcist, long worn to ward malice and night terrors.

Magickal Properties

Planet: Sun

Powers: Protection, Exorcism, Healing

Magickal Uses

  • Worn for warding: Guards the body, spirit, and soul; placed in the home it wards off evil spirits; planted in the garden it protects against evil and storms.
  • Child’s talisman: Seeds or roots hung around a child’s neck protect from mischievous fairies and imps; roots carved into small “piney beads” and strung as an amulet.
  • Anti‑incubus: Peony roots worn with coral and flint keep away the incubus.
  • Exorcism and lunacy: Used in exorcisms; the root is carried to cure lunacy. The root should only be gathered at night when its seeds are said to shine with an eerie light.
  • Substitute: Peony root is sometimes substituted for mandrake in workings.
  • Every part protects: Peonies have been held magickal from China to ancient Greece, and every part of the plant offers protection.
  • Carved root charms: Dried roots carved into sacred, auspicious shapes are carried for protection (see Carved Peony Root Protection Charm).
  • Seed necklace: Dried seeds strung on red thread and worn as a necklace ward off evil (see Red Thread Peony Seed Necklace).

Paeon, Healer of the Gods

The peony takes its name from Paeon, the Greek god of healing and of light, whose name also carries the meaning of a hymn of thanksgiving. Paeon was said to have used the flower's roots to heal the wounds of Hercules, and from this the plant won its long reputation as a healer, a power later vouched for by Asclepius and by Galen.

Like the mandrake, the peony's root was thought to take a human shape and to stand for the body of a man, and like the mandrake it was said to scream when torn from the earth, the cry being fatal to any who heard it. The only safe way to lift the root was to tie it to a dog and lure the animal forward with a piece of meat set just out of reach, so that it pulled the root free in its struggle. The gathering had to be done by the light of the Moon as well, for otherwise a woodpecker might fly down and peck out the harvester's eyes. A thing so dangerous to win was held to carry great power, and the peony root delivered: it was counted a cure for epilepsy, and bead necklaces strung from the root were still worn against fits into the early twentieth century. The seeds, gathered at night like the root, give off a faint phosphorescent glow that marked them as magickal, and because whatever heals also guards, the woody parts of the plant were carved into charms and amulets.

Meaning When Gifted

When peony is gifted it carries the meaning of shame, of a happy marriage, of compassion, and of bashfulness.
