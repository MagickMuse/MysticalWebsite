Water Hemlock (Cicuta maculata) is widely considered the most violently toxic plant native to North America, cited among the ingredients of the traditional witch's flying ointment.

☠️
DEADLY POISON: Considered the most violently toxic plant in North America. Do not touch, ingest, or burn. Fatal in small amounts. Symbolic reference only.

The Witch's Flying Ointment

Water hemlock is one of the plants named in historical accounts of the witch's flying ointment, documented in sources from the 1500s and 1600s. Its extreme toxicity and aquatic habitat connected it to Saturn and the liminal world of marshes and mires — thresholds between the living world and the realm of spirits. It appears alongside poison hemlock, belladonna, henbane, and mandrake in these formulas.

Botanical Description

Water hemlock is a perennial herb that grows in wet meadows, marshes, and along stream banks. It bears clusters of small white flowers in a compound umbel — superficially resembling edible plants like wild parsnip and wild carrot, which has led to many fatal misidentifications. The root, when cut, exudes a yellow resinous sap containing cicutoxin, one of the most potent convulsant toxins known.

Historical Lore

Native American healers were well acquainted with water hemlock and used it in carefully controlled external applications for specific ailments, though internal use was universally recognized as lethal. European herbalists distinguished it from poison hemlock (Conium maculatum) based on habitat and root structure, but both were known to kill rapidly.

Safety

Do not ingest, burn, or handle. No practical magickal application is possible or safe. For research and symbolic reference only.
