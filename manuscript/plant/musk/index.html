Ambrette is an evergreen shrub of the Malvaceae family, growing to about 1.5 metres and bearing large single yellow flowers with a deep purple centre. The seedpods are five-cornered pyramidal capsules holding greyish-brown, kidney-shaped seeds that carry a distinctive musky scent. The plant is native to India and is now cultivated widely across the tropics, including Indonesia, Africa, Egypt, China, Madagascar, and the West Indies. Distillation of the seed oil is generally carried out in Europe and America.

Related Species

A close relative, Abelmoschus esculentus, known as okra, is grown largely in Istanbul as a demulcent. A separate variety is found in Martinique, where the seeds carry a more delicate scent.

Folk Tradition

The seeds are widely used as a domestic spice across the East and have a long history as a stimulant and digestive aid for indigestion, cramping, and nervous dyspepsia. Chinese medicine uses them to treat headache. In Egypt the seeds sweeten the breath and are emulsified with milk for itch. Arab tradition mixes the seeds with coffee.
