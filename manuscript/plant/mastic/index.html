Mastic (Pistacia lentiscus) empowers manifestation and sight. It is a member of the Anacardiaceae family.

Characteristics

A small bushy tree or shrub reaching up to 3 metres high. The trunk produces a natural oleoresin: incisions are made in the bark to collect the liquid oleoresin, which then hardens into brittle pea-sized lumps.

Origin & Distribution

Native to the Mediterranean region, including France, Spain, Portugal, Greece, and Turkey, and also found in North Africa. Most mastic is produced on the Greek island of Chios. Smaller quantities are produced in Algeria, Morocco, and the Canary Islands.

Related Species

Mastic belongs to the same family as Peruvian pepper or Peruvian mastic, the Pepper Tree (Schinus molle). Mastic resin resembles the resin sanderach, but unlike sanderach it can be chewed rather than turning to powder.

Folk Tradition

In the East, mastic is used in the manufacture of sweets and cordials. It is still used medicinally for diarrhoea in children and is chewed to sweeten the breath. The oil was used in the West in a similar way to turpentine, sharing many of the properties of coniferous turpentines, and was formerly greatly used in medicine. Recorded actions include antimicrobial, antiseptic, antispasmodic, astringent, diuretic, expectorant, and stimulant.
