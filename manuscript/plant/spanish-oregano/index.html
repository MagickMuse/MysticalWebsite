Spanish Oregano Essential Oil

A perennial creeping herb of the Lamiaceae family with a woody stem, small dark green leaves, and pink or white flowers borne in clusters. Despite its common name, Spanish oregano is botanically a thyme, and it is the source of most commercial "oregano oil" on the market.

Origin & Distribution

Native to the Middle East and Asia Minor; grows wild in Spain. The essential oil is produced mainly in Spain, Israel, Lebanon, Syria, and Turkey.

Folk Tradition

According to Mrs Grieve, the properties and oil of Spanish oregano (Thymus capitatus) are similar to those of Thyme (T. vulgaris); it also shares many qualities with Oregano, also known as wild marjoram (Origanum vulgare).

Botanical Identity

Although this herb is strictly a thyme, it serves as the source for most so-called oregano oil sold commercially. For closely related plants in the same aromatic family, see Thyme (Thymus vulgaris), Oregano (Origanum vulgare), and Marjoram (sweet marjoram). Savory sits in an adjacent genus and shares chemistry through the source's synonym Satureja capitata.
