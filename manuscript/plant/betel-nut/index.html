The betel nut is the seed of the areca palm (Areca catechu), a tree that grows throughout the warmer regions of the Far East, from India across to the Melanesian Islands. People have chewed it as both a stimulant and a narcotic for thousands of years; the earliest written mention appears in a Chinese manuscript dated to around 1000 BC.

Preparation

The nut is wrapped in a leaf of the betel pepper vine along with a pinch of shell lime, and the bundle, known as a betel quid, is chewed as a stimulant. In parts of Vietnam, the time it takes to chew one quid, roughly three to four minutes, was once used as a measure of time.

Folk Tradition

In India the areca palm is held sacred, and its leaves and nuts are used to adorn statues of the gods in Hindu ceremonies.

Magickal Uses

The betel nut is a symbol of love and marriage, carried into wedding rites and worked into love potions, and it is also used as an aphrodisiac. This may grow from a legend in which a young man was transformed into an areca palm and his wife into the betel pepper vine that climbs it.
