History

By the sixth century, saffron was already prized as an aphrodisiac, a stimulant, and a vehicle for spiritual elevation. A fifteenth-century imam described it as the scent of the seventh heaven — a declaration that cemented saffron's identification with the highest planes of spiritual attainment and the fragrance of paradise itself.

Mythology

The Greeks told a myth to explain the origin of saffron's color. Krokos was a handsome mortal youth and a friend of Hermes. When Hermes accidentally killed him, three drops of blood fell upon the crocus flower and became the stamens that yield saffron. The crocus itself was held sacred in ancient Crete and was dedicated to the goddesses Demeter and Persephone.

Characteristics

Saffron stands only about six inches tall and flowers in autumn. Its long leaves, typically blue or purple, wrap themselves around the three delicate stigmas from which the spice is harvested. Grown from seed, saffron takes three years to reach its first bloom and requires a sheltered position in sandy, well-drained soil.

Source — Specific Uses

The flower stigmas and styles (called threads) produce a vivid yellow dye, are used in perfumery, and have medicinal uses. Gives off a distinctive aromatic odor. This herb is so expensive that dried marigold petals may be substituted for it in any spell.

Magickal Properties

Deities: Eros, Ashtoreth

Powers: Love, Healing, Happiness, Wind Raising, Lust, Strength, Psychic Powers

Ritual Uses

Baked into ritual cakes; worn or infused in historical rites for joy, prophecy, and protection from intoxication.

Magickal Uses

  • Love and lust: Add to love sachets; eaten to raise lust.
  • Healing and divination: Use infusion for hand-washing before healing; drink sparingly for foresight.
  • Joy and protection: Wear a chaplet to prevent inebriation; keep in the home to deter lizards.
  • Weather magic: Employed to raise winds.
  • Angelic protection: Saffron's celestial associations — its golden color, its fragrance likened to heaven — give it a natural connection to angelic energy. Use it in workings of divine protection, guarding, and warding off evil.
  • Incense for angelic invocation: Sprinkle saffron powder over a hot coal and burn it as incense when calling upon angelic presence or seeking higher spiritual guidance.

Additional Notes

Use sparingly; not recommended internally during pregnancy.

Colour of the Sun and of Wisdom

Among the costliest spices on earth, saffron owes its worth to the painstaking harvest of the tiny stigmas of a particular crocus. Its vivid yellow ties it firmly to the Sun and, through that solar light, to wisdom, and it carries the many associations of the colour that bears its name. The saffron robes of Buddhist monks are a defining part of their identity, and in Hindu tradition a saffron paste is used to mark the followers of certain castes and divisions.

Meaning When Gifted

When crocus is gifted it carries the meaning of cheerfulness and gladness, with a gentle reminder not to abuse it.
