Hyssop can be brewed as a magickal tea to aid purification and ward negativity. Drink 1 to 2 cups only during the spell event itself.

Botanical Description

An attractive perennial, almost evergreen subshrub of the Lamiaceae family, growing up to 60 cm high with a woody stem, small lance-shaped leaves, and purplish-blue flowers.

Origin & Distribution

Native to the Mediterranean region and temperate Asia, and now grows wild throughout America, Russia, and Europe. Mainly cultivated in Hungary and France, and to a lesser degree in Albania and the former Yugoslavia.

Other Species

There are four main subspecies of hyssop, but H. officinalis is the main oil-producing variety. The variant H. officinalis var. decumbens is less toxic than many other types and is well suited to aromatherapy use.

Folk Tradition (Medicinal)

Hyssop has an ancient medical reputation and was used for purifying sacred places and as a strewing herb. It is used principally for respiratory and digestive complaints, and externally for rheumatism, bruises, sores, earache, and toothache. It is also used to regulate blood pressure, as a general nerve tonic, and for states of anxiety or hysteria. It is current in the British Herbal Pharmacopoeia as a specific for bronchitis and the common cold.

Related Species

Distinct from hedge hyssop (Gratiola officinalis), which is still used in herbal medicine but belongs to an entirely different family. The hyssop mentioned in the Bible probably does not refer to this herb but to a form of wild marjoram or oregano, possibly Origanum syriacum. See also Hyssop Essential Oil.
