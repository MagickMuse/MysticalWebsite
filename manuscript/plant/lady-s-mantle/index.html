Lady's Mantle (Alchemilla vulgaris) gathers dew and gathers hearts, carrying associations with the Virgin Mary, the Egyptian goddess Sekhmet, and a storied place in European alchemical history.

History

Native to Europe, lady's mantle has been found growing as far north as the Arctic Circle. During the Middle Ages, it was employed in alchemical attempts to transmute base metals into gold. The name "lady's" honors the Queen of Heaven, the Virgin Mary, written as a possessive rather than a plural. The plant also holds a connection to the Egyptian goddess Sekhmet. Its broad leaves, likened to a lion's paw, inspired the French name pied-de-lion and the New Latin leontopodium, drawn from the Greek leon (lion) and podion (foot).

Characteristics

The broad, fan-shaped green leaves bear a striking resemblance to a draped cloak or mantle. Both leaves and roots have served as remedies for diarrhea, bloating, menopausal symptoms, and inflammation. Root decoctions have been used to treat ulcers, while a tea made from the leaves functions as a gargle for sore throats.

Magickal Uses

  • Love: Use the herb in love spells and sachets.
  • Strength and Concealment: Lady's mantle can be used psychically to invoke inner strength or to shield oneself from unwanted attention.
  • Dew Magick and Alchemy: Dew collected from the broad leaves is considered particularly potent. Traditionally called "pearls that falleth in the night," this gathered dew is believed to carry concentrated magickal power. The foliage gives a subtle influence to the dew that collects in its cup-shaped leaves — alchemists gathered this dew for preparing the philosopher's stone.
