Rhodiola (Rhodiola rosea) is an arctic adaptogen of energy, courage, and love magick — its rose-scented root used to amplify power and invoke virility.

History

Rhodiola has been used for hundreds of years, first documented by the Greek physician Dioscorides. Known in the first century as rodia riza, the root was employed to treat anxiety, fatigue, and a range of other ailments. The Swiss taxonomist Carolus Linnaeus later gave the herb its current binomial name in the eighteenth century.

Characteristics

A perennial flowering herb, rhodiola is classified as an adaptogen — a plant that supports the body's ability to manage and adjust to stress. It is also known as arctic root and golden root. When the stem is cut, it releases a fragrance closely resembling that of a rose. Its use is comparable to ginseng, and it is most commonly prepared as an infusion. A cluster of greenish-yellow flowers crowns the top of its sturdy stalk.

Magickal Properties

Gender: Feminine

Powers: Energy Amplification, Courage, Strength, Love, Fertility, Virility, Consecration

Magickal Uses

  • Energy amplification: Including rhodiola root in a charm or spell packet increases the overall power of the working. It acts as a magickal amplifier, intensifying whatever intention surrounds it.
  • Courage and strength talisman: The root carried on its own functions as an herbal talisman for courage, fortitude, and physical and spiritual strength.
  • Love and virility: Rhodiola is used in love magick with a particular focus on fertility and virility, making it well suited to workings that call for passionate, embodied energy.
  • Consecrating a sacred space: To create a flower water for consecrating a sensual or ritual space, heat distilled water and steep the flowers and cut stems for several hours. Transfer to a spray bottle and add a tablespoon of witch hazel or grain alcohol to preserve it, or refrigerate for short-term storage.
