📅Spring Equinox

Gorse (Ulex europaeus) is a thorny evergreen shrub with bright golden-yellow flowers, valued for its protective properties and ability to attract wealth.

Magickal Properties

Powers: Protection, Money

Folk Names

Broom, Frey, Furze, Fyrs, Gorst, Goss, Prickly Broom, Ruffet, Whin

Source Book Notes

  • Spring Equinox: Furze rules the Spring Equinox, when it blooms and attracts the first bees of the year. Its golden flowers symbolize the young Sun in spring.
  • Sacred to Jupiter: It is sacred to Jupiter, god of shepherds.
  • Powers: Protection and battle.
  • Associated deities: Jupiter (Zeus), Ares (Mars), Thor, Horus.

Magickal Uses

For protection: Gorse is a good protectant against evil. In Wales hedges of the prickly gorse are used to protect the home against fairies, who cannot penetrate the hedge.

For money magick: Gorse is also used in money spells; it attracts gold.

Symbolism and Folk Tradition

Gorse, also called furze, is the seventeenth letter of the Celtic Ogham tree alphabet, where it is named Onn. It flowers in great profusion, even through winter in some places, and so stands as a symbol of fertility, while its bright yellow blooms are a cheering sight that represents the Sun. The shrub belongs to the element of fire: its spiny thorns resemble flames, and the dry woody stems hold so much oil that they catch light quickly and burn fiercely. Cattle were once herded through bonfires of gorse before being turned out to summer pasture, securing protection for the season. Those same vicious prickles lend the plant its warding power, and in Wales, where gorse thrives in poor soils and bad weather, country dwellers planted it around their homes and farmsteads, where it served at once as a stock-proof barrier and a ward against witches.

Meaning When Gifted

When furze is gifted it carries the meaning of love for all occasions.
