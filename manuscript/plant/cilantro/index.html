Coriander is the seed of the cilantro plant (Coriandrum sativum), a versatile herb with powerful magickal properties centered on love, protection, and prosperity. This plant from the Apiaceae family produces seeds with a warm, herbal scent that has been used in magickal practice for millennia.

History

Native to the Mediterranean region, coriander is one of the oldest documented herbs, with evidence of its use stretching back to around 5000 B.C.E. Seeds have been recovered from ancient ruins across several cultures, including the tomb of the Egyptian pharaoh Rameses II, where coriander seeds were placed among funerary offerings. Its feathery leaves resemble parsley, and its distinctively potent aroma and flavor have made it a beloved yet divisive culinary herb across traditions.

Folk Medicine

A traditional infusion of coriander seed has long been used to ease children's diarrhoea, digestive upsets, griping pains, anorexia, and flatulence. In Chinese medicine, the whole herb is applied to dysentery, piles, measles, nausea, toothache, and painful hernia.

Distribution

Coriander is native to Europe and western Asia and is naturalized in North America. It is now cultivated worldwide, with most of the essential oil produced in the former USSR, Yugoslavia, and Romania.

Therapeutic Actions

Coriander is recognized as analgesic, aperitif, aphrodisiac, anti-oxidant, anti-rheumatic, antispasmodic, bactericidal, depurative, digestive, carminative, cytotoxic, fungicidal, larvicidal, lipolytic, revitalizing, stomachic, and a stimulant of the cardiac, circulatory, and nervous systems.

Characteristics

Both the leaves and seeds of coriander have served as remedies for digestive complaints including colic, nausea, and flatulence. An annual herb, its growth cycle runs from March through September, with the best leaf harvest falling in late spring through autumn. The entire plant is edible, making it one of the more versatile culinary herbs.

Herbal Lore

Love and Fertility Magic

The lore of coriander frequently centers on its reputation in the realm of love, lust, and sex. Coriander seeds were frequently crushed and added to wine to act as a lustful potion, designed to inflame the loins of anyone who drank it. The seeds were not only to arouse desire in the intended target but also had a reputation for making sex last longer, and helping the coupling be healthy and fertile.

Pregnant women were told to eat coriander seeds so that they would bear intelligent children.

Domestic Harmony

Wives would place coriander seeds near the hearth to release their scent, thereby increasing the peace in the home, protecting it from harm, and arousing the man of the house.

Aphrodisiac Properties

Coriander seeds have a long history as an aphrodisiac. They were traditionally crushed and added to wine to inflame desire, and were reputed to help couplings last longer and prove more fertile.

Magickal Uses

Culinary Uses

Coriander is a well-known addition to many styles of food, with Italian and Mexican being the most notable. The seeds carry their magical properties into food preparation.

Elemental Work

Coriander connects strongly to fire element work. Burn coriander seeds or herb on incense charcoal to invoke fire energy.

Lunar Work

Despite its solar and martial associations, coriander makes an effective base for lunar incenses and essential oil blends when working with moon goddesses.

Home Protection

Place coriander seeds near the hearth or entrance to protect the home from harm and increase domestic peace.

Samhain Offerings

Coriander's association with immortality in Chinese medicine makes its seeds appropriate as offerings to the dead at Samhain. Place them on an ancestor altar or include them in Samhain rituals honoring those who have passed.

Fragrant Seeds and Immortality

Coriander seeds become very fragrant when dried and are used extensively in perfumery and incense. The longer they are kept, the more aromatic they become. In China, it was believed that these seeds offer immortality.

Love Sachets and Charms

This plant has long been used in love sachets and charms, making it one of the primary magickal love herbs.

Love Charms and Fidelity

Coriander seeds are included in love charms to attract a new love interest, encourage fertility, and protect the fidelity of an existing relationship. Sew seeds into a red pouch and carry it in a pocket or wear it on the body.
