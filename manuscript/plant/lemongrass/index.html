Botanical Information

Cymbopogon citratus (Poaceae family). Masculine grass herb and leaf originating from South Asia and Southeast Asia. Also known as West Indian Lemongrass and Oil Grass. Called "Indian melissa" in Ayurvedic medicine for its cooling, pain-relieving, and stress-reducing properties.

Characteristics

A fast-growing, tall, aromatic perennial grass reaching up to 1.5 metres in height. The plant produces a dense network of roots and rootlets that rapidly exhausts the soil.

Origin & Distribution

Native to Asia. The West Indian lemongrass is probably native to Sri Lanka and is now cultivated mainly in the West Indies, Africa, and tropical Asia, with Guatemala and India among the main oil producers.

Related Species

There are several varieties of lemongrass, of which the West Indian type (Cymbopogon citratus) and the East Indian type (Cymbopogon flexuosus, also Andropogon flexuosus, Cochin lemongrass, native lemongrass, British India lemongrass, vervaine Indienne, France Indian verbena) are the most common. The East Indian variety is native to east India around Travancore and is now mainly cultivated in western India. Chemotypes within each variety are also quite pronounced.

Folk Tradition

Lemongrass is employed in traditional Indian medicine for infectious illness and fever. Modern research carried out in India shows that it also acts as a sedative on the central nervous system. It is used as an insecticide and as a flavouring for food. After distillation, the exhausted grass is fed locally to cattle. Recorded actions include analgesic, antidepressant, antimicrobial, anti-oxidant, antipyretic, antiseptic, astringent, bactericidal, carminative, deodorant, febrifuge, fungicidal, galactagogue, insecticidal, nervine, sedative on the nervous system, and tonic.

Herbal Lore

Potent ally for intuitive work including card reading, divination, scrying, and tea leaf reading. In hoodoo tradition, essential for deep cleansing and uncrossing work. Main ingredient in van van oil for removing obstacles and in floor washes to keep evil away. Dried lemongrass carried in mojo bags keeps loved ones safe from physical harm.

Lemon-scented family member shares magical properties with other lemon-scented herbs across traditions.
