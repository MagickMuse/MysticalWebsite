A strongly aromatic herb of the Asteraceae family, growing up to 0.6 metres high with a much-branched stem that is woody at the base. The brightly coloured, daisy-like flowers become dry as the plant matures yet retain their colour.

Origin & Distribution

Native to the Mediterranean region, especially its eastern part and North Africa. Cultivated mainly in Italy, the former Yugoslavia, Spain, and France.

Other Species

There are several other Helichrysum species, including H. arenarium (commonly found in florist shops), H. stoechas (also used to produce an absolute), and H. orientale (grown for its oil).

Folk Tradition (Medicinal)

In Europe it is used for respiratory complaints such as asthma, chronic bronchitis, and whooping cough; also for headaches, migraine, liver ailments, and skin conditions including burns, allergies, and psoriasis. Usually taken as a decoction or infusion.

Related Species

Distinct from Life-Everlasting (Gnaphalium obtusifolium and related species), a North American plant of a different genus that shares the common name everlasting. See also Helichrysum Essential Oil.

Meaning When Gifted

When helichrysum, the everlasting flower, is gifted it carries the meaning of never-ceasing memory and agreement.
