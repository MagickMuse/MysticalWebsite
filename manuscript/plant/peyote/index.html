Peyote (Lophophora williamsii) is a small, spineless desert cactus revered as one of the holy plants of Mexico and used for thousands of years as a gateway to other worlds.

Characteristics

A small, low-growing, spineless cactus of the desert. Its crown bears soft rounded sections, and from the side grows the protuberance harvested as the peyote button, the part that is consumed.

Origin & Distribution

Native to the deserts of Mexico and the southern reaches of North America, where it grows close to the ground in arid, stony country.

Folk Tradition

The gathering of peyote is itself a sacred undertaking. For thousands of years the shamans of the region have made a ritual hunt for the cactus, and the search, the taking of the button, and the visions that follow can unfold over several days. The peyote button carries mescaline and other psychoactive compounds that bring vivid hallucinations, and through them the seeker is said to travel into other dimensions, to speak with animals, and to meet the world in a heightened state of awareness. The plant itself is understood as a doorway into another world. The hunt and its visions were recorded at length by Carlos Castaneda in his Don Juan books, though many now regard those accounts as more literary than factual.

Peyote is powerfully psychoactive and carries real physical and legal risks. It has only ever belonged within supervised, ceremonial settings inside the cultures that hold it sacred.
