Petitgrain

◇Notes/Warnings
Essential oil distilled from the leaves and twigs of the bitter orange tree (Citrus aurantium). Shares botanical origins with neroli (from the flowers) and bitter orange (from the peel). Used in perfumery and aromatherapy; uplifting and clarifying.

🌸Astarte Oil

Petitgrain refers to the leaves and twigs of Bitter Orange (Citrus aurantium var. amara), the same tree that yields Neroli from its blossoms and bitter orange oil from its peel. It belongs to the Rutaceae family.

Characteristics

Petitgrain is harvested from the leaves and twigs of the bitter orange tree, an evergreen of the Rutaceae family. The leaf-and-twig material yields the standard petitgrain oil; an absolute called petitgrain sur fleurs is also produced from leaf-and-flower water.

Origin & Distribution

Native to southern China and north-east India. The best-quality petitgrain oil comes from France, with good-quality oil also produced in North Africa, Paraguay, and Haiti from semi-wild trees.

Related Species

A type of petitgrain is also produced in small quantities from the leaves, twigs, and small unripe fruit of the Lemon, Orange, Untitled, and Bergamot Orange trees.

Folk Tradition

At one time the oil used to be extracted from the green unripe oranges when they were still the size of a cherry, which is the origin of the name petitgrains, or 'little grains.' Petitgrain is one of the classic ingredients of eau-de-cologne. Recorded actions of the oil include antiseptic, antispasmodic, deodorant, digestive, nervine, stimulant, stomachic, and tonic.
