Bergamot, Wild (Monarda fistulosa) is an aromatic flowering herb native to North America.

History

Bergamot is cultivated across North America, Central America, Europe, and Asia. Before European colonization spread it widely, Indigenous peoples knew it as Oswego tea and used the flowers and leaves both as a remedy and a beverage. That tradition continued throughout and beyond the colonial period. The plant is also commonly called beebalm, scarlet beebalm, and crimson bee balm.

Characteristics

Grown widely throughout North America, bergamot is frequently planted to attract bees and butterflies, which are drawn to its citrus-like scent. Its long, hollow, pipe-shaped flowers also attract hummingbirds. The herb is broadly used as a flavoring agent and brewed into teas.

Magickal Uses

Wild Bergamot can be used to bring clarity and good working order to the surface of any situation. It helps to clear confusion and bring understanding to complex matters.

Use in sachets and incense for workings of friendship and health. Wild bergamot also makes an excellent herbal tea, with a slightly spicy, orange-like flavor. The scent of bergamot gives Earl Grey tea its distinctive flavor.

Medicinal Uses

Bee balm has been used in North American folk and Indigenous medicine as a digestive remedy. A tea brewed from the fresh or dried leaves helps calm an unsettled stomach and is particularly effective for reducing nausea and vomiting. Its active compounds overlap with thyme and oregano, giving it mild antimicrobial properties as well. Short-course use as a tea is the most common traditional preparation.

⚠️ Safety Note: Not recommended for internal use during pregnancy.
