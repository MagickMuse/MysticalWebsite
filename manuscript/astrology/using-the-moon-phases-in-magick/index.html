Using the Moon Phases in Magick

Once you know the Moon’s phases and the kind of spiritual tone each one carries, you can start applying that rhythm to your practice. You might time a single spell to the phase that best supports it, or work with one intention across the whole lunation through small, phase-matched actions.

Ways to work with the lunar cycle include:

  • Casting spells when the phase naturally supports the outcome
  • Reinforcing a goal through brief meditations in multiple phases
  • Scheduling tarot or oracle readings when insight is easier to access
  • Doing supporting work to keep momentum moving toward a longer-term desire
  • Using the waxing and waning rhythm as a mirror for personal reflection, healing, and manifestation

As you build familiarity, you can also choose to create a different working for each phase, letting the Moon shape the entire month of magick.

Special Lunar Occasions

Beyond the regular cycle, certain lunar alignments add an extra layer of intensity. When the Sun, Moon, and Earth line up in particular ways, you may experience events like eclipses, supermoons, blue moons, and black moons.

Older cultures often treated these events with awe and anxiety, weaving stories that explained the sudden shift in the sky as omens, warnings, or signs of divine power. Even with modern astronomy clarifying what is happening, these moments can still feel charged and uncanny. In magickal practice, they are often approached as times when change accelerates and themes become harder to ignore.

Eclipses

An eclipse occurs when the alignment of the new or full moon is close enough that either the Moon obscures the Sun (solar eclipse) or Earth’s shadow darkens the Moon (lunar eclipse). Eclipses are commonly framed as energetic shake-ups that can trigger major inner and outer shifts.

Eclipses often arrive in pairs and can create a concentrated stretch of intensity sometimes called eclipse season. Many practitioners find that eclipses highlight life themes connected to the Sun and Moon as symbols: identity, purpose, and the conscious self (Sun) alongside intuition, emotion, and the subconscious (Moon).

Because eclipses occur in specific zodiac signs, they can emphasize different lessons depending on where they land for you. Their influence may echo for months, especially when a later lunation in the same sign completes or clarifies what was set in motion.

Solar Eclipses

Solar eclipses happen when the Moon moves between Earth and the Sun, blocking some or all of the Sun from view. They occur only at the new moon. Although each eclipse is brief, it can coincide with a broader period of shifting energy, and it is often paired with a lunar eclipse near a surrounding full moon.

Because the Moon’s shadow is relatively narrow, any given solar eclipse is only visible from certain parts of the world. Even so, many practitioners treat the energetic tone as collective and noticeable whether or not the eclipse is visible locally.

Types of solar eclipses include:

  • Partial: The Moon covers part of the Sun, leaving a bright crescent visible.
  • Annular: The Moon crosses the Sun when it is farther from Earth, so a ring of sunlight remains visible.
  • Total: The Moon fully covers the Sun for a short span of time.

In magickal terms, solar eclipses are often approached as amplified new-moon portals, closing one chapter while pushing a new direction into motion. They can bring closure, realizations, and decisive pivots, along with a sense of being re-aimed toward a truer path.

If you choose to work with a solar eclipse, focus on intention-setting and practical alignment. Vision boards, guided meditation, crystal healing, sound work, and action planning can help you meet the moment with clarity. Many practitioners also release what is ready to end just before the point of exact alignment, then commit to the new direction as the portal opens.

Lunar Eclipses

A lunar eclipse happens when Earth moves between the Sun and the full Moon, casting its shadow across the lunar surface. From the night side of Earth, the Moon may darken or shift in color, sometimes taking on a deep red tone.

Lunar eclipses involve two layers of Earth’s shadow:

  • Penumbra: the outer shadow
  • Umbra: the darker central shadow

Types of lunar eclipses include:

  • Penumbral: The Moon passes through the penumbra only, creating a subtle dimming or slight change in tone.
  • Partial: Part of the Moon enters the umbra, and the shaded area can appear red, orange, brown, or yellow.
  • Total: The entire Moon moves through the umbra. Because it also crosses the penumbra on the way in and out, this is the longest form of lunar eclipse and can last for a couple of hours.

Magickally, lunar eclipses are often treated as intensified full moons. Emotions can feel amplified, and situations may come to a head quickly. Some practitioners avoid outcome-driven spellwork during these windows because the energy can feel erratic. Others use the potency for major manifestation or for work that requires a decisive turning point.

Because the full moon is already a time of culmination, a lunar eclipse can accelerate endings and reveal what has been hidden. Old wounds, repeating patterns, and unspoken truths may rise to the surface, creating vulnerability alongside a strong opportunity for healing. This is also a potent time to check in with your path: what is reaching completion, what needs to be released, and what kind of transformation is asking to begin.

Many practitioners also find psychic sensitivity heightened during a lunar eclipse. Divination, spirit communication, and reflective ritual can feel especially vivid, provided you stay grounded.

Supermoons and Micromoons

The Moon's orbit around Earth is elliptical, so its distance from us changes throughout each cycle. The closest point is called the perigee, and the farthest is the apogee.

When a full moon lines up at or near perigee, it is known as a supermoon or perigee moon. This happens roughly three to four times per year. A supermoon can appear up to fourteen percent larger and about thirty percent brighter than a micromoon (a full moon at apogee). The stronger gravitational pull at perigee can also produce higher-than-usual tides.

Many practitioners describe supermoon energy as amplified and electric, with heightened emotions and sharper psychic impressions. Manifestation work, lunar devotion, and full moon rituals may feel especially potent. When perigee coincides with a new moon instead, some astrologers call it a super new moon, and new moon themes like intention-setting and fresh starts may feel more pronounced.

A micromoon, by contrast, falls at apogee. The full moon energy is still present but may feel softer or more subdued. Micromoons are not usually treated as a major factor in practice, but paying attention to how the distance lands for you can deepen your sense of connection to the lunar cycle.

If you notice a pull at the supermoon, try tracking perigee timing during other phases. You may find that orbital distance adds a subtle charge to whatever phase it overlaps with.

Blue Moons and Black Moons

Because the lunar cycle lasts about 29.5 days, slightly shorter than most calendar months, an extra full or new moon occasionally fits into one month or season. A repeated full moon is called a blue moon, and a repeated new moon is called a black moon.

There are three ways to define them:

  • Astronomical: When a season holds four full or new moons instead of the usual three, the third is the astronomical blue or black moon. This happens roughly every two to three years.
  • Calendrical: The second full or new moon within a single calendar month. This is the most commonly referenced definition in modern usage.
  • Astrological: The second full or new moon within a single zodiacal season. Not formally standardized, but it follows the same principle of repeated lunar energy within a defined window.

Some practitioners treat these moons as no different from any regular full or new moon, while others see them as amplified windows for revisiting intentions, getting a second chance on workings that did not land, or riding a wave of rare and concentrated energy. A blue moon is often associated with luck, success, and prophecy, while a black moon is linked to deep truth, shadow work, and long-range manifestation.

Each type of blue or black moon lends itself to a slightly different approach: calendrical ones suit self-made success and monthly goal-setting, astrological ones invite you to revisit the themes of a particular sign, and astronomical ones extend the window for working with seasonal and lunar energy together.

Monthly Moons

Each lunation through the year carries a traditional name tied to the season's character, from the animals and weather patterns to the harvests and shifts in light. Many of the names in common use today trace back to Algonquian peoples in North America, later adopted and adapted by colonial farmers. Others have Celtic, English, or broader Indigenous roots, and cultures around the world have developed their own names reflecting local seasonal rhythms.

A few things worth knowing as you explore them:

  • These names usually apply to the entire lunation, not just the full moon night. The whole cycle from new to full to dark carries the month's signature energy.
  • Because the lunar cycle runs about 29.5 days, slightly shorter than most calendar months, the same name can land in different months from year to year. The Harvest Moon, for instance, belongs to whichever full moon falls closest to the autumn equinox, so it may arrive in September or October. Some years also hold thirteen lunations instead of twelve.
  • Many of these names come from Indigenous traditions. If you work with them, take time to learn their origins and show genuine respect for the peoples and practices they come from. You might also research moon names local to the land where you live, and as your practice deepens, consider creating your own personal moon names that reflect your relationship with each season.

Connecting with the monthly moons is one of the simplest ways to align your magick with the turning year. Each lunation's themes, combined with the seasonal energy around it, offer a natural framework for timing spells, rituals, and reflective work.

Moon Sign Magick

As the Moon orbits Earth, she passes through sections of the sky associated with each zodiac sign, spending roughly two to three days in each one before moving on. In astrology, these transits are called moon signs, and in witchcraft they offer another layer of timing and intention for your spellwork.

The Moon holds a central place in Western astrology. She is associated with the classical element of water and represents the realms of feeling (both psychic and emotional), the subconscious, nurturing and being nurtured, daily habits, adaptability, and the most intimate parts of the self. Through the Moon, you can map your emotional and intuitive landscape, access the past, uncover repeating patterns, connect with ancestors, and bring hidden truths to the surface. In a birth chart, the Moon can reveal how you were raised and nurtured, and in turn how you nurture others.

Because the Moon shifts form slightly every day yet follows predictable patterns, she mirrors our own routines and our capacity to change. This makes moon magick especially effective for habit work: small, repeated shifts that build toward lasting transformation.

The Moon Through the Elements

As the Moon moves through each sign, she expresses her power through that sign's traits and themes. A quick elemental overview:

  • Fire signs (Aries, Leo, Sagittarius): The Moon's energy feels spontaneous and warm. This time favors vitality, courage, and self-expression.
  • Water signs (Cancer, Scorpio, Pisces): The Moon is in her home element and feels supportive and nurturing. Emotions flow freely, and connecting to feeling and intuition comes naturally.
  • Earth signs (Taurus, Virgo, Capricorn): The Moon's energy slows and steadies. Use this time to engage the physical senses, build structure, and promote long-lasting results.
  • Air signs (Gemini, Libra, Aquarius): The Moon illuminates the mind and inspires ideas. Communication, learning, and intellectual magick thrive here.

New Moons, Full Moons, and Signs

Because the new moon aligns with the Sun, it always falls in the same sign the Sun currently occupies. The full moon sits opposite the Sun, so it always lands in the opposite sign. If the Sun is in Libra, for example, the new moon will be a Libra new moon and the full moon will be in Aries. The Moon passes through all twelve signs during each lunation.

With a working knowledge of what the Moon means in each sign, you can specialize your full moon and new moon rituals, or combine sign energy with phase energy for precise timing. A first quarter moon in Taurus, for instance, carries a different charge than a first quarter moon in Sagittarius. The more you layer these rhythms together, the more nuanced and potent your practice becomes.

Your Moon Sign and Magick

Just as you have a sun sign, you also have a moon sign: the zodiac sign the Moon was in at the time of your birth. Your moon sign can reveal how you were nurtured or raised, how you prefer to be nurtured, and how you tend to nurture those closest to you. It can offer insight into your emotional and subconscious landscape, your sense of feeling, and perhaps even your soul, helping to guide and illuminate your purpose.

When it comes to magick, knowing your moon sign is not essential. But if you are able to access your birth chart (many free resources exist online), you may gain insight into what kind of magick is most comforting, healing, and nurturing for you personally. It can reveal the aspects of your intuitive and emotional nature that especially empower your lunar spells.

Astrology has many moving parts, and your moon sign is just one facet of a much larger puzzle. But reflecting on your own moon sign can be illuminating as you consider moon spells and how the Moon in each sign impacts you.

Eclipse Magick: To Do or Not to Do?

Eclipse magick is often treated with caution. Some practitioners avoid spellwork aimed at controlling outcomes during eclipses, describing the atmosphere as volatile or unpredictable. Others deliberately work with eclipse energy because it can feel amplified and catalytic.

A flexible approach is to prioritize practices that support clarity and realignment:

  • Meditation and grounding
  • Cleansing and energetic reset
  • Healing rites and restorative baths
  • Divination for insight, not certainty

If the energy feels chaotic, it can be wiser to focus on release and reflection rather than pushing the current. If the themes feel clear and you feel steady, you may choose to engage the power directly. In all cases, let your intuition guide the choice, and keep your work centered on alignment and healing.
