The Planetary Seal of the Sun

Seal Symbolism

The Sun's seal contains elements linking to the rotation and spin of the sun. Sharp edges extending from the design evoke solar flares, while the right side of the seal remains open, leaving room for manifestation to occur. At the center is a spiral of force emanating outward clockwise, calling to mind the number 6, which is the number of Sol.

Powers of the Sun

The Sun governs ego, inspiration, true will, great gains, health, peace, youth, resurrection, and promotions. Its radiant energy fuels vitality, creative power, and the manifestation of one's highest purpose.

Applications of the Seal

The Sun's seal can be used in five primary ways:

Illumination: Achieve Knowledge and Conversation of the Holy Guardian Angel. Work to discover and align with your true will, the divine purpose that guides your spiritual path.

Financial: Generate ideas and inspiration for new business ventures. Without an idea, there is no product, the Sun sparks the creative vision needed for enterprise.

Love: Add the Sun's radiance to your aura. Create a glamour that makes you more visible to your target than anyone else, drawing their attention with magnetic presence.

Wrath: The Sun applied out of balance can burn out your enemies with fiery wrath. Direct solar force as a weapon when necessary, overwhelming opposition with raw power.

Healing: The Sun should be central to any overall healing efforts. Its life-giving energy restores vitality, strengthens the body, and renews the spirit.

Ceremonial Planetary Seals of the Sun

In the ceremonial tradition, these sigils are arranged within a double-ringed circle. The Seal is positioned at the top center, the Intelligence beneath it, and the planetary symbol to one side or below for visual balance. Gold is the traditional material for inscribing the Sun's ceremonial seals. The Intelligence is regarded as benevolent in nature, while the Spirit is regarded as malevolent. For this reason, only the Seal and Intelligence are incorporated into ceremonial magick circles.

The Sun in Myth and Symbol

The Sun is usually cast as the masculine force that balances the feminine Moon, yet many traditions honor Sun Goddesses alongside Sun Gods. For a great many peoples the sheer glory of the Sun makes it the physical manifestation of the Godhead or Supreme Being, and therefore beyond any single sex. The Sun also stands for the Eye of God, seen for example in the sun-like rays of the All Seeing Eye of the Masonic tradition. Some tribal peoples regard the Sun as the good, right eye of the Divine Being and the Moon as the bad, left eye.

To our ancestors, when the Sun slipped over the western horizon it passed into the Land of the Dead, so they believed it could take life as well as give it, a truly Godlike power. The Aztecs called this vanished aspect the black Sun, a malevolent force. More often, though, the heat and light of the Sun are seen the world over as celestial gifts granted to the Earth and all the life it shelters.

The Sun is personified in many symbolic forms, including the petals of flowers, above all the sunflower, and the unruly mane and golden color of Leo the Lion, the sign it rules. The eagle, called the lion of the skies, represents the glory of the Sun in the form of a bird. For the Druids the stag stood for the Sun, and in alchemy gold is the metal of the Sun.
