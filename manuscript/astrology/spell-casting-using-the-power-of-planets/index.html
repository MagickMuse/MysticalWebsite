Spell-casting Using the Power of Planets

Working with Stones in Spellcraft

Stones do not have to be faceted or expensive. They do not even have to be tumbled smooth, large, or of the best quality. Some stones are multi-functional, so you don't need a different one for each spell unless you are trying to do more than one spell at a time, which isn't advised. Handle several stones of a kind to see which feels right to you. You can keep them in a plastic bag with a label until you can differentiate between the types.

Most stones can, and should, be rinsed in water to cleanse them of all vibrations except yours. One exception is calcite, which is too soft to endure water. Cleanse calcite by surrounding it with quartz crystals for twenty-four hours.

When your spell is completed and you empty the used herbs on the ground, pick up and cleanse the used stones again. Stones shouldn't be wasted; they are not an infinite resource.

Stones make charm bags and poppets more powerful. You can go only by the color of the stones if you wish, but more energy is drawn to your project if certain stones are used. The planetary list on each planet's page will help you decide on the weekday — and hour of the day — to further fine-tune your working.

In magickal and astrological tradition, the luminaries (sun and moon) are classified as planets alongside other heavenly bodies, despite differing from modern astronomical definitions. Both astrology and magick operate as geocentric systems, perceiving everything as revolving around Earth even when this is not literally the case.

Every day, hour, topic, and category carries a planetary correspondence. This applies across Western, Chinese, and Hindu magickal systems, though their respective astrological frameworks differ. The Western system derives from Babylonian astrology, which heavily influenced Egyptian and Greek traditions.

Every planet possesses astrological and magickal influence (ancient Greek and Roman curse tablets fell under the dominion of Saturn, for instance), but the planets most strongly identified with magick are Earth and the Moon.

Earth

Where the Moon represents psychic ability, Earth represents general vitality. Both are required for optimal magick power. According to many worldwide traditions, Earth is the material from which humans are created. Earth is our mother: we are born from her body and returned to her to await rebirth while the corporeal body decays and becomes part of Earth once more, in a never-ending cycle. Without extended, consistent physical contact with Earth, vitality and magick power suffer and diminish.

Earth is also very literally the dirt underfoot. Dirt empowers both practitioners and spells. Various traditions pursue physical healing by packing patients into heated sand. Moor mud detoxifies the body. Dirt from shrines and saints' tombs is perceived as exceptionally charged with power. Bits of dirt are carried as talismans and sprinkled over items to empower and revitalize them.

The most famous magickal dirt comes from the Roman Catholic shrine at Chimayo, New Mexico, though the dirt's miraculous reputation predates Catholicism in the area. Thousands converge on Chimayo annually for its healing properties.

Certain magickal traditions rely on the power of dirt more than others. The Afro-Caribbean tradition of Palo Monte utilizes more types of dirt than perhaps any other, with spells requesting specific amounts of dirt from locations like police stations, courthouses, and hospitals. This magickal tradition dates back at least to ancient Mesopotamia.

Most traditions utilize certain specific types of magickal dirt, including:

  • Crossroads dirt
  • Footprint dirt
  • Graveyard dirt

Inherent energy is transmitted through contact with the dirt itself.

The Moon

The Moon is considered the ruler of all magickal arts, and magick is timed to the phases of the Moon:

  • Waxing Moon: Spells intended to increase something (like money) or initiate something new (a fresh romance) are coordinated with the waxing moon. Begin these spells on or just after the New Moon.
  • Waning Moon: Spells intended to decrease something (like debt) or cast for the purpose of banishing are coordinated with the waning moon. Begin these spells on or just after the Full Moon.
  • Dark of the Moon: The few days where the Moon is not visible, just prior to the rebirth of the New Moon, are controversial. Some traditions avoid casting spells during this period. Others, particularly those devoted to Dark Moon spirits like Hecate or Lilith, consider this a period of profound magickal power.

In Arabic folk custom, whatever you find yourself doing at the moment you first glimpse the brand new Moon is considered the right thing for you to do.

Exposing magickal tools, charms, and preparations to the beams of the Full Moon provides enhanced magickal empowerment, though this does not eliminate the need to charge items with personal energy.

Magick worked directly with the Moon (rather than simply timing by phase) is traditionally women's magick. A trinity exists between the Moon, women, the ocean, and by implication all water. The Moon profoundly affects menstrual cycles and the tides. Since women have a natural affinity for the Moon, they may more readily draw on its power.

Most lunar deities are female, including Artemis, Diana, Hecate, Selene, Lady Chang'o, and Ix Chel. In ancient Egypt, however, the Moon was perceived as male precisely because of its demonstrated influence over women's fertility.

Drawing Down the Moon

This term refers to specific Wiccan spiritual rites in which the Goddess is invited to enter the body of her priestess and speak through her. Some traditions also practice a parallel Drawing Down the Sun ritual, invoking male divine energy. However, Drawing Down the Moon is a very ancient term predating modern Wicca that may refer to various rites depending on the magickal tradition, most especially the process of creating moon-infused waters.

Charm Bags & Poppets

Choose the herbs and stones that you feel good about using and are within your budget. The power of your intent will be what makes them all work together to fulfill your wish.

Charm bags and poppets don't have to be very large. In fact, small is often better. You can pin a small charm bag to the inside of your bra or carry it easily in your pocket. A poppet can fit just as easily into a drawer — that way no one sees it and thinks you are up to some negative mischief. Fill most of the poppet or charm bag with cotton balls or quilt batting; you will be putting a lot of your intent energy into the spoonful of herbs you add.

Poppets

The poppet is primarily used for healing on yourself or others. You can draw on facial features. Poppets are stuffed with appropriate herbs and a stone (or stones) placed inside where the illness is. The poppet requires two cut pieces of muslin or fabric — sew up the sides first and leave a small opening to stuff in the herbs and stones, then finish sewing the poppet shut.

Charm Bags

Sew three sides shut, stuff in the herbs and stones, then sew the packet closed. You can also use fabric glue instead of sewing. The charm bag needs only one piece of fabric cut — it will be bent in half, the two sides sewed or glued together, and then the top closed after the herbs are inside.

You can choose different herbs and stones than those suggested, and you can change any chant or make up your own. The most important part is to be clear in your intent and concentrate on it very hard while holding the finished poppet or charm bag.

The ancient Greeks called this technique "drawing down the moon" or the "Thessalian Trick." Thessalian witches were believed to hold great power and metaphysical knowledge, and this was considered one of their most important formulas.
