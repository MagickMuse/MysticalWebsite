Pole Star

Type
Stars/Asteroids
Also called
Polaris, North Star, Nail of Heaven

In the northern hemisphere the Pole Star is the fixed center of the skies, the hub of the great turning wheel of the Heavens and, in effect, the astral image of the Axis Mundi. Its constancy has made it a welcome sight to sailors, nomads, and travelers for thousands of years, a symbol of safety and security in unfamiliar terrain.

Finding it is simple enough. There is a distinctive group of seven stars shaped like a great ladle, called the Plough in the UK and the Big Dipper in the US, and known to astronomers as Ursa Major. The two everyday names say a good deal about the two cultures. If you draw an imaginary line joining the two stars at the front of the ladle's bowl and carry it on for about five times the gap between them, you arrive at the Pole Star.

In myth the Pole Star is often set at the center of the celestial universe, directly above the corresponding center of the Earth. For the peoples of central India, Asia Minor, and Central Asia it marks the position of the highest peak of the symbolic world mountain, and the altars in their holy buildings are likewise oriented to the north. It is seen as the ultimate throne of God in his Heaven, and the Samoyeds call it the nail of Heaven. Some Native Americans see it as a hole in the sky, one of three holes joining the three worlds. For Muslims the Ka'aba, the holiest site in Mecca, lies in line with the Pole Star, connecting the Earth to the Heavens.
