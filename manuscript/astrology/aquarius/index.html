True to Your Sign

Aquarius holds the eleventh house of the zodiac and falls between January 20 and February 18. As an air sign ruled by Saturn and Uranus and symbolized by the water carrier, this sign carries an idealistic, intellectual, unconventional, and independent current with generous and altruistic instincts. The shadow side leans toward unpredictability and emotional detachment.

Birthstones

Birthstones are crystals traditionally associated with each zodiac sign based on ancient correspondences. However, modern jewelry store lists vary wildly and often bear little resemblance to historical traditions. Rather than relying on inconsistent commercial lists, select stones by color affinity and elemental alignment. This approach produces more effective magickal results.

For Aquarius, choose light blue stones such as Aquamarine or Blue Lace Agate. These align with Aquarius's airy, intellectual nature and visionary energy.

The Eleventh Sign

The eleventh sign of the zodiac is the water carrier, yet it belongs to the element of air, a paradox that runs through everything Aquarius touches. It is a sign ruled by air but bound by a mystical attachment to water, and its emblem is a figure forever pouring out a stream that is more than simple liquid.

The Water Carrier

The Greeks and Persians knew the constellation as the Water Pot. For the Babylonians the cluster was a goddess, and to the Romans it was Juno, while the Akkadians called it Ku ur Ku, the Seat of the Flowing Waters, or else Rammanu, the god of the storm. The sign is most often shown as a human figure, of either gender, pouring water from a vessel. In the Tarot the Aquarian archetype appears in two places: as Temperance, a winged woman who circulates water between two jugs, and as the Star, a naked young woman pouring water from two jugs onto the earth. Those twin streams reach back to the Egyptian image of the sign, a god who likewise pours two streams of water.

The Glyph

The sigil for Aquarius all but explains itself, a set of wavy lines standing for water. Their zig-zag shape, though, casts water as an active principle, a life force much like electricity. In the Star card the figure stands within the very water she pours, a hint that this is something more than a liquid, and the two streams in the sigil point to the same idea, water as one of the elements yet charged with another quality. Homeopathy leans on the power of water to dilute a remedy and still strengthen its essence, and that seemingly magickal property may hold a clue to the meaning of the two lines.

The Air of the Water Carrier

Before the discovery of Uranus, Aquarius answered to Saturn, the planet that lends gravity and a certain weight. Uranus adds something else to the Aquarian psyche, a gift for innovation and a reputation for eccentricity, so that Aquarius is the sign of the Mad Professor. Ruled by air yet drawn to water, the Aquarian cares little for material things and far more for the mind and the spirit, seeking to quench the thirst of the soul before that of the body, which lends the sign a measure of emotional detachment. The air element makes it intuitive and prone to sudden leaps of deductive reasoning.

The Age of Aquarius

Much has been written about the Age of Aquarius, but what does it actually mean? The ages of the zodiac run backward, so the age we are now leaving is that of Pisces, which in fact follows Aquarius on the zodiacal wheel. The exact moment of entering this new age is hard to fix, though each age lasts some 2,160 years; some place the turning point at 1962, others as late as 2377. It is worth noting that the preceding Piscean age was for many tied to Christianity, whose symbol was the fish, an emblem still in use two thousand years after Christ walked the Earth. The Aquarian Age is said to be the dawning of a time of change, bringing freedom in every sense, humanitarianism, and a raising of consciousness and awareness. Fittingly, the glyph itself carries a clue to one of the new age's planetary concerns, the element of water.
