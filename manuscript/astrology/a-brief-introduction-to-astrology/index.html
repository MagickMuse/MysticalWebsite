Astrology Grimoire

A Brief Introduction to Astrology

Astrology views humanity as shaped not only by genetics and environment, but also by the configuration of the solar system at the moment of birth. The Sun, Moon, and planets act as fundamental life forces, both the substance we are made of and the tools we live by. Their influence shifts according to their zodiacal position and the angles they form with one another.

A Long Lineage

Astrology is among the oldest surviving occult sciences. Sophisticated systems flourished in Babylonian, Egyptian, and Aztec cultures, with substantial records surviving from each. For centuries in the West, astrology was a respected method of divination, supported and consulted by royal courts. The rise of empirical science in the seventeenth century pushed it toward the realm of superstition, but it never lost its following entirely. Today it remains widely practiced across every walk of life.

The Horoscope

Popular astrology centers on the horoscope, a chart mapping the positions of the planets, the Sun, the Moon, and the stars at the moment of a person's birth. Reading that chart interprets how those celestial placements bear on human affairs.

The Sun passes through the twelve signs of the zodiac across the year. Someone said to be born under Pisces was born during the Sun's transit through the zone of the zodiac named for the constellation Pisces. Each of the twelve signs carries its own personality traits, and the element tied to a sign's ruling planet (fire, water, earth, or air) shapes how that Sun sign expresses itself. The daily positions of the other planets layer further influence on top.

Decanates

As the Sun moves through a single zodiac sign over the course of a month, it crosses three decanates, each about ten days long. Every decanate adds the influence of an additional planet and sign on top of the basic Sun-sign reading, refining the interpretation for an individual birthday.

An Aries born in the third decanate (April 10 to 21), for example, picks up the influence of Sagittarius, the third decanate sign, and its ruling planet Jupiter. The ancient Egyptians considered decanates as important as the Sun signs themselves.

Progressions

Progressions are another widely used predictive technique. With this method, the Sun is treated as moving about thirty years through each sign of the zodiac. Over an average lifespan a person's progressed Sun typically travels through three or four signs. Each shift from one sign to the next marks a turning point, often coinciding with a significant change in circumstance or outlook.

A Scorpio born on November 9, for instance, has a progressed Sun that moves into Sagittarius around age thirteen, into Capricorn around age forty-three, and into Aquarius around age seventy-three.

Fixed Stars

Specific fixed stars tied to particular days of the year exert further influence on a birthday reading. Decanates, progressions, and fixed stars can sound intimidating, but the calculations are usually folded into pre-built birthday profiles, so the reader simply consults the result.

What the Research Suggests

Skeptics argue that the link between planetary position and human destiny is unproven. Recent research on the season, and even the month, of birth complicates that dismissal.

In the early 1970s, a study compiled from the British population census found that architects tended to be born in spring, secretaries in summer, miners in fall, and electricians in winter. The same researcher asked astrologers to predict which signs would dominate two professions, nurses and labor union officials, then ran a large survey to test the prediction. Nurses skewed toward Taurus, Cancer, Virgo, Scorpio, and Pisces, while labor union officials clustered under the remaining signs, matching the astrologers' calls.

Other work has focused on the Sun itself. Ultraviolet radiation is thought to cause genetic changes in the developing foetus that may shape later life and personality, which would help explain why people born around the same time of year often share traits and trajectories.

Analysis of European birth records has found that month of birth measurably affects lifespan. The chance of living past one hundred ran up to 16 percent higher than average for those born in December, and 23 percent lower for those born in June. A separate study reached a similar conclusion, with December births living roughly three years longer on average.

One leading hypothesis is that December babies were conceived in March, possibly avoiding the most damaging solar radiation peaks early in pregnancy. By this argument, the radiation experienced at conception can influence personality and health for decades, which would make where someone is born at least as important as when.

Selected Findings

Happiness. People born in June, July, and August tend to be happier on average. People born in December, January, and February tend to be more pessimistic.

Personality. A study of two thousand people found that women born between February and April were more likely to be novelty seekers than those born in October. Men born in spring tended to be more impulsive, while those born in winter were more inclined to introspection.

Intelligence. A long-term study of twenty-one thousand children found seasonal variations in intelligence, weight, height, and head size. Winter-born children tended to be larger and more academically inclined than summer-born children. A separate study, however, found female students born in spring and summer achieved better exam marks than those born in fall and winter.

Health. Women born in fall reported the fewest menopausal symptoms, while those born in spring reported the most. Winter births carry a greater risk of heart disease, and other work has linked winter birth to a higher risk of obesity.

The Planets

Since the earliest times people have held that the planets of the solar system press a steady, unrelenting influence upon the affairs of the world and of each person within it. What sets a planet apart from a star is its motion, for a planet travels in an orbit around a star; the word itself comes from the Greek planaomai, meaning wanderer.

By tradition there were seven planets, a count that fell into neat agreement with the seven days of the week, the visible colors of the rainbow, the seven magickal metals, the seven heavens, the seven stages of the alchemical Great Work, and the Seven Deadly Sins. Each planet carries the name of a god, and the clearest sign of their influence is read in the zodiac. In the Kabbalah the planets are named as spheres, and they too fall under the care of angels.

The Music of the Spheres

The very building of the musical scale is owed to the planets. Legend tells that the Muses brought music into being by listening to the note each of the seven planets sang as it turned in its celestial orbit, and the harmonies they made together were believed to hold the cosmos itself in being.
