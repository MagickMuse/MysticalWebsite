The Zodiac

Notes/Warnings
Also called the Great Wheel or, in Hindu tradition, the Rashi Chakra. A general overview of the zodiac as a system; the twelve individual signs are covered in their own entries.
Type
Western Zodiac
Also called
The Great Wheel; Rashi Chakra (Hindu tradition)

Although the early Church set out to stamp on anything that looked pagan or godless, belief in the zodiac not only survived but flourished. The reason is simple enough: the wish to glimpse the Book of Fate has always run deep, however firm a person's trust in the will of God or Allah.

The Zodiac and Religion

The early priesthood did try to dismantle astrological theory, since a system that fixed human destiny in the stars seemed to deny both divine intervention and the teachings of Christ. Yet Arab scholars, drawn to Ptolemy's Tetrabiblos, were deep in the study of the heavens at the same time. They built on Greek astronomy and astrology and devised instruments, the astrolabe among them, that could gauge the height of the stars and the spaces between them.

Christianity, meanwhile, was passing through a restless age. When the Second Coming foretold for the year 1000 failed to arrive, blind faith no longer felt sufficient, and learning became precious. The Crusades carried Eastern knowledge westward, and the new schools of Spain became crucibles where Sufi mysticism, Kabbalah, mathematics, and theology mingled freely. In the thirteenth century Thomas Aquinas wove Christian mystery together with the philosophy of Aristotle and a strong thread of Sufi thought. The broader, more open outlook this encouraged left a door ajar for astrological ideas, among them the image of the cosmos as a ladder rising between Earth and Heaven, its rungs tended by angelic powers who governed the stars and planets, which in turn swayed every earthly element.

The ambivalence ran high then, as it still does. Some Christians condemned the zodiac as devilry while others welcomed it as part of a grand design ordained by God. Cathedral architects of the thirteenth and fourteenth centuries, taken with the freshly arrived astrological notions, drew on them when planning major buildings, Chartres among them; the signs were lovely and stirring, and took readily to a Christian gloss even where that gloss lay thin.

The Celestial Mirror

Just as people once held the human body to be a small copy of the universe, shaped in the likeness of God, it followed that the zodiac, a band of constellations each with its own story and meaning, was a mirror in the sky reflecting the great moments of life below. If the patterns formed by the planets could be read, they might serve as a tool of divination for foreseeing what was to come.

It is easy to overlook that the signs have drifted over the roughly three thousand years since they were first marked out, each now shifted by about a month, so that a person counted today under one sign would, by the original reckoning, have fallen under the one before it, with some overlap at the edges.

The great wheel of the zodiac, known in Hindu tradition as the Rashi Chakra, has been read in many ways. To the Egyptians it mirrored the sacred Nile set in the heavens. To Zoroastrians it stood for the twelve chiefs of the sun god Ahura Mazda. The Akkadians, around 2000 BC, saw it as the furrow cut by the great bull god El as he ploughed steadily through the year.

The Cycle of Life

Whatever one makes of it, the zodiac's reach is wide, and almost everyone knows what it represents; even a confirmed skeptic usually knows their own sign and its character. Rulers and heads of state have often kept personal astrologers, some openly and some quietly. Each sign answers to one of the four elements and is ruled by a planet, and each governs a host of things besides, from flowers, trees, and herbs to cities, countries, metals, colors, and parts of the body. Every sign carries a core meaning, and together they trace a sequence of unfolding steps. The signs are usually listed from Aries onward, but because the zodiac turns like a wheel, it has no true beginning and no end.
