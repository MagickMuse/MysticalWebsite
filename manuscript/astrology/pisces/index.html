True to Your Sign

Pisces holds the twelfth house of the zodiac and falls between February 19 and March 20. As a water sign ruled by Jupiter and Neptune and symbolized by the two fish, this sign carries an intuitive, creative, imaginative, and spiritual current. The shadow side leans toward dreaminess, impracticality, and impressionability.

Birthstones

Birthstones are crystals traditionally associated with each zodiac sign based on ancient correspondences. However, modern jewelry store lists vary wildly and often bear little resemblance to historical traditions. Rather than relying on inconsistent commercial lists, select stones by color affinity and elemental alignment. This approach produces more effective magickal results.

For Pisces, choose sea-green and blue stones such as Aquamarine, Blue Lace Agate, or seafoam-colored stones. These resonate with Pisces's watery, intuitive nature and connection to the ocean depths.

The Twelfth Sign

Pisces is the last sign of the zodiac, rising in the sky just before the Vernal Equinox and the start of spring. As the closing sign it stands for the idea of rebirth, and it is fitting that its fish should come from the sea, that great womb of renewal.

The Fish

Both the Greeks and the Persians called this constellation the Fish, while the Babylonians knew it as Two Tails and split the star cluster into two separate shapes, Simmah the swallow and Anunitum the goddess. The symbol for Pisces is traditionally a pair of fish swimming in opposite directions, their mouths nonetheless joined by a cord. That cord is faint within the actual constellation yet still visible; the Babylonians called it Riksu and the Arabians Al Risha, and it is a vital part of the sign that has long carried esoteric significance.

The Glyph

The sigil for the fish comes from Egyptian sources and has scarcely changed since, unlike the glyphs of other constellations. The original Egyptian sign showed the two opposing curves, but kept them separate; the connecting line was added in the fourteenth or fifteenth century, giving the symbol its present H shape.

The Water of the Fish

Though some modern readings of Pisces leave out the cord, the fact that the fish are joined rather than parted matters a great deal, a fine demonstration of how a seemingly simple line within a symbol can hold so much meaning. The duality of the fish is well known, even if it signifies different things, such as the soul that craves the stimulus of the material world and the spirit that longs to leave such things behind, neither of which can survive without the other. This spirit and soul significance carries into seventeenth-century alchemical writings. Because of their tie to water, fish are symbols of the Goddess and the feminine aspect, and some say the fish swimming in opposite directions point to the supposed female difficulty in reaching a decision, or to the experience, shared by both sexes, of being pulled in two directions at once. Since the fish is so closely aligned with the Christian faith, the Pisces symbol is sometimes found in churches where no other astrological imagery appears, and Christ was born at the start of the Age of Pisces. Those born under the sign are said to be dreamy and impractical, deep thinkers drawn to psychic matters.
