True to Your Sign?

Cancer (June 21 to July 22)

The fourth sign of the zodiac, Cancer is a water sign ruled by the Moon. Its symbol is the crab. Ruled by the Moon, these people are often highly creative and intuitive. Sensitive captures the core of the Cancer temperament. The shadow side shows in oversensitivity and insecurity. Their empathy and good sense of humor win them many admirers, though their moodiness can be confusing.

Intuition, psychic, nurture, family focused.

Birthstones

Traditionally, birthstones are crystals associated with zodiac signs based on ancient correspondences. However, modern jewelry store lists vary wildly and often bear little resemblance to historical traditions. Rather than following inconsistent commercial lists, you'll achieve more powerful results by choosing stones that match your sign's elemental color.

Cancer color: Sea-Green

Sea-green stones align with Cancer's watery, intuitive, nurturing energy. Choose from Green Beryl for emotional wisdom and calm, or Green Turquoise for protection and lunar connection.

Sacred Creature

The crab is the creature assigned to Cancer, the only astrological sign belonging exclusively to the moon. Those born under Cancer are believed to resemble crabs in temperament. In Mediterranean regions, crabs and crayfish are intensely affiliated with the moon and considered lunar foods that transmit the moon's effects and impart psychic power.

The Crab

The constellation we call Cancer has not always worn the shape of a crab, yet whatever creature has stood for it, the shell has remained a constant. Early European astrologers drew it as a crayfish, while the Egyptians saw the scarab beetle, a sacred emblem of death and rebirth and the sign of the god Khepera. Even so, the words for the constellation in Persian, Greek, Sanskrit, and Babylonian all carry the meaning of crab. This may have been the most significant figure in the whole zodiac, for it holds more fixed stars within its reach than any other sign. The Maya were not alone in expecting the world to end when the planets gathered here; the Romans, too, held that Juno had set Cancer in the heavens to mark the end of all things whenever she chose to bring it about.

The Glyph

The well-known "69" shorthand for Cancer invites several readings. It is built from two matching parts set in opposite positions, and to some this see-saw shape mirrors the up-and-down moods often linked to the sign. Since the constellation rises just after the summer solstice, as the seasons turn, the paired spirals can be taken to mark that change. Others see two spermatozoa meeting to form a single seed, tying the sign once more to conception and birth, or read the figure as a pair of breasts in keeping with its themes of nurture and fertility. Still others see fixed stars of the constellation itself, one turned upward and one down, an image of the principle "as above, so below."

Qualities

Cancer arrives just as the seasons shift and the Sun's climb tips over into decline. A lunar sign of the water element, it is held to be passive, home-loving, imaginative, romantic, and at times self-absorbed. The Moon's influence lends a vivid imagination and a depth of inner life, both fitting the sign well. Every creature ever tied to this constellation shares a single feature, the shell, an emblem of self-protection that speaks to the guarded, withdrawn streak in some born under Cancer. The sign rules the very parts that such a shell would shield: the heart, the lungs, and the stomach.
