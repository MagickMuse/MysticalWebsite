Harvest Moon

◇Date Range
September or October (NH) / March or April (SH)
◇Notes/Warnings
The Harvest Moon is not tied to a specific calendar month. It is the full moon closest to the autumn equinox and can fall in either September or October.

The Harvest Moon is the full moon that falls closest to the autumn equinox, and because the lunar and calendar cycles do not perfectly align, it can arrive in either September or October. This makes it unique among the monthly moons: it belongs to the season rather than the month, and in years with thirteen full moons, it stands as the extra lunation that rounds out the cycle.

This is the peak of harvest season. Historically, the Harvest Moon's light gave agricultural workers precious extra hours to gather the bounty before the cold set in. For practitioners, the energy is the same: abundance is at its fullest, and the work now is to collect, appreciate, and share what has grown. The celebration of Mabon often falls near this lunation, and together they invite reflection on the balance between light and dark, effort and reward, and what has been sown versus what has been reaped.

With one final harvest still ahead, manifestation remains possible, but the momentum is shifting inward. This is the turning point where outward effort gives way to inner contemplation. The Harvest Moon asks you to take stock honestly: what did your intentions produce, and what still needs tending before the year's cycle closes?

Magickal Workings

Celebrate and give thanks for the bounty. Pumpkin is a seasonal staple and endlessly versatile in both kitchen witchery and spellcraft. Prepare a pumpkin dish or a spiced drink as an offering or a ritual centerpiece. Share the harvest with friends, family, ancestors, land spirits, and moon deities. Cast spells for abundance that will carry you through the winter months. Reflect on what you are grateful for and what you still wish to manifest, then begin the inward turn. The Harvest Moon is both a culmination and a threshold, so honor both the fullness of what you have gathered and the quieter wisdom that comes with letting the season change.
