Horseradish

Horseradish Oil is the steam-distilled essential oil of the macerated root of Armoracia rusticana. It is one of the most hazardous essential oils on record and has no place in home aromatherapy or therapeutic practice.

Safety

Do not use Horseradish Oil in home aromatherapy, externally or internally. It is an oral toxin, a dermal irritant, and a mucous membrane irritant. The vapour itself produces a tearing effect on contact. Avoid during pregnancy and nursing.

Extraction & Characteristics

The oil is produced by water and steam distillation from broken roots that have been soaked in water. A resinoid or concrete is also produced by solvent extraction. The finished oil is a colourless to pale yellow mobile liquid with a sharp, potent odour and a pronounced tear-producing effect. Its dominant constituent is allyl isothiocyanate at around 75 percent, with phenylethyl isothiocyanate forming only when the plant is bruised or crushed.

Where You Will Encounter It

Horseradish oil appears almost exclusively in the food industry, used in minute amounts as a flavouring in seasonings, ready-made salads, condiments, and canned products.

Magickal Use

Work with the Horseradish root rather than the distilled oil. The root retains the plant's stimulating action without the hazards of the concentrated oil.
