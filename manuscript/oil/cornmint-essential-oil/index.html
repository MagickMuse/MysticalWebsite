Notes/Warnings
The source explicitly recommends against aromatherapy use of cornmint oil; use Peppermint Essential Oil instead, which is not fractionated and has a more refined fragrance. Cornmint

A colourless or pale yellow liquid steam distilled from the flowering herb of Cornmint (Mentha arvensis). The oil carries a strong, fresh, bitter-sweet minty odour somewhat like peppermint. The commercial form is almost always dementholized, since the raw oil contains so much menthol that it is otherwise solid at room temperature.

Extraction & Characteristics

Steam distilled from the flowering herb, then dementholized for commercial use. Principal constituents are menthol (70 to 95 percent) and menthone (10 to 20 percent), with pinene, menthyl acetate, isomenthone, thujone, phellandrene, piperitone, and menthofuran among others. Constituents vary according to source.

Therapeutic Uses

The source provides no aromatherapy or home-use indications for cornmint oil and explicitly recommends Peppermint Essential Oil in preference, since cornmint is fractionated for the menthol industry and does not carry the more refined fragrance of true peppermint. For folk medicinal applications of the plant itself (Western, Eastern, and Chinese traditions), see the Cornmint plant entry; those uses describe the herb in herbal preparation, not the distilled essential oil.

Safety

Non-toxic and non-irritant except in concentration; may cause sensitization in some individuals. Menthol is a dermal irritant. The oil also has a cytotoxic action. Use peppermint in preference for any aromatherapy application.

Where You Will Encounter It

Used in some pharmaceutical preparations, such as cough lozenges, herb teas, and syrups, mainly in the form of menthol. Extensively employed in soaps, toothpastes, detergents, cosmetics, perfumes, and especially industrial fragrances. Used by the food industry, especially for flavouring confectionery, liqueurs, and chewing gum. However, its primary commercial purpose is the isolation of natural menthol.
