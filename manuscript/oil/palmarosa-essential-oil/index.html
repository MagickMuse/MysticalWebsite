Palmarosa Essential Oil (Cymbopogon martinii) is a unique oil with a distinctive scent that combines citrus and rose notes. This unusual aromatic profile makes it versatile in magical blending.

Scent Profile

A surprising combination of citrus brightness and rose floral depth. The scent is fresh yet romantic, making it easy to incorporate into many different types of blends.

Magickal Uses

The rose-like qualities make palmarosa naturally suited for love workings. Add to love blends or dilute and wear to attract romantic attention.

Palmarosa's gentle nature supports healing work. Include in healing formulas or anoint healing candles with diluted oil.

Botanical Source

Palmarosa (Cymbopogon martinii var. martinii) of the Graminaceae family. Also classified as Andropogon martinii and known by trade names motia, Indian rosha, East Indian geranium, and Turkish geranium. The plant is a wild-growing herbaceous perennial with long slender stems and terminal flowering tops; its grassy leaves carry a strong fragrance.

Distribution

Native to India and Pakistan and now cultivated across Africa, Indonesia, Brazil, and the Comoro Islands.

Other Species

A member of the same family as lemongrass and citronella. Closely related to gingergrass, a different chemotype designated C. martinii var. sofia; gingergrass is considered an inferior oil, though in some parts of India the two grasses are distilled together. Several chemotypes exist depending on source, and the cultivated varieties are considered of superior quality.

Folk and Historical Use

The trade names "Indian geranium" and "Turkish geranium" once applied to palmarosa oil and date to the period when the oil was shipped from Bombay to Red Sea ports and carried partly overland to Constantinople and Bulgaria, where it was often used to adulterate rose oil.

Therapeutic Actions

Antiseptic, bactericidal, cicatrisant, digestive, febrifuge, hydrating, stimulant (digestive and circulatory), and tonic.

Extraction & Characteristics

The essential oil is produced by steam or water distillation of the fresh or dried grass. It presents as a pale yellow or olive liquid carrying a sweet, floral, rosy, geranium-like scent.

Principal Constituents

Geraniol dominates, alongside farnesol, geranyl acetate, methyl heptenone, citronellol, citral, dipentene, and limonene, among others.

Safety

Non-toxic, non-irritant, and non-sensitizing.

Aromatherapy Uses

Skin care: Acne, dermatitis, minor skin infections, scars, sores, and wrinkles. Valuable for face, hands, feet, neck, and lip treatments; moisturizes the skin, stimulates cellular regeneration, and regulates sebum production.

Digestive system: Anorexia, digestive atonia, and intestinal infections. Older aromatherapy literature describes it as acting on pathogenic intestinal flora, particularly the colibacillus, Eberth bacillus, and dysentery bacillus, supporting the restoration of normal intestinal mucosa.

Nervous system: Nervous exhaustion and stress-related conditions.

Blending Partners

Cananga Essential Oil, Geranium Essential Oil, Oakmoss Absolute, Rosewood Essential Oil, amyris, Sandalwood Essential Oil, Guaiacwood Essential Oil, cedarwood, and floral oils.

Other Uses

Used extensively as a fragrance component in cosmetics, perfumes, and especially soaps owing to its excellent tenacity. Limited use as a flavouring agent, including in tobacco. Also a commercial source for the isolation of natural geraniol.
