Guaiacwood Essential Oil is produced by steam distillation from the broken wood and sawdust of Guaiacwood (Bulnesia sarmientoi), a small wild tropical tree native to Paraguay and Argentina.

Extraction & Characteristics

The oil is a yellow, amber, or greenish soft or semi-solid mass with a pleasant, tearose-type fragrance and sometimes an unpleasant smoky undertone. Some oil is distilled in Europe and the USA.

Principal Constituents

Guaiol (42 to 72 per cent), bulnesol, bulnesene, guaiene, patchoulene, and guaioxide, among others.

Folk Tradition

The wood is much used for ornamental carving. Guaiacwood was formerly used for treating rheumatism and gout. Guaiacum, the gum and resin of the related trees Guaiacum officinale and G. sanctum, is still current in the British Herbal Pharmacopoeia as a specific for rheumatism and rheumatoid arthritis. Valnet includes guaiacum in his 'elixirs' for gout, venereal disease, and in mouthwashes.

Aromatherapy & Home Use

Circulation, muscles, and joints: Arthritis, gout, rheumatoid arthritis.

Blending

Guaiacwood blends well with Geranium Essential Oil, Neroli Essential Oil, Oakmoss Absolute, Rose Oil, Costus Essential Oil, Sandalwood Essential Oil, Amyris Essential Oil, spice oils, and woody-floral bases.

Disambiguation

The Spanish name 'palo santo' is sometimes applied to Bulnesia sarmientoi, but this is a separate species from the true Palo Santo (Bursera graveolens, Burseraceae). The two trees come from different botanical families and produce different oils. See Palo Santo Essential Oil for that distinct entry. Guaiacwood is also distinct from guaiac gum and guaiac resin (guaiacum), obtained from Guaiacum officinale and G. sanctum, though they share some common properties.

Other Uses

The fluid extract and tincture are used in pharmacology, mainly as a diagnostic reagent in blood tests. Guaiacwood is also used as a fixative and fragrance component in soaps, cosmetics, and perfumes.

Safety

Non-toxic, non-irritant, non-sensitizing.

Therapeutic Applications

  • Arthritis: Apply via massage, skin oil/lotion, compress, or bath.
  • Gout: Apply via massage, skin oil/lotion, or bath.
