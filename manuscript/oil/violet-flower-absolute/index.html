Violet Flower Absolute is solvent-extracted from the fresh blossoms of Violet. The result is a yellowish-green viscous liquid with a sweet, rich, floral fragrance, characteristic of the fresh flowers and treasured in fine perfumery.

Other Names & Related Species

Known as English violet, garden violet, blue violet, and sweet-scented violet. There are over 200 species of violet; the main types cultivated for aromatic extraction are the Parma and Victoria violets. Not to be confused with the unrelated African Violet, which belongs to a different family entirely. Pansy is a related Viola. The companion Violet Leaf Absolute is extracted from the leaves of the same plant.

Origin & Distribution

Native to Europe and parts of Asia, and cultivated in gardens worldwide. The aromatic crop is mainly grown in southern France around Grasse, and to a lesser extent in Italy and China, for perfumery use. The plant itself is small and tender, with dark green heart-shaped leaves, fragrant violet-blue flowers, and an oblique underground rhizome.

Folk Tradition

Both the leaf and flowers have a long tradition in herbal medicine, mainly for congestive pulmonary conditions and sensitive skin conditions, including capillary fragility. Mild pain-killing properties are reported, probably due to the presence of salicylic acid, as in aspirin. The flowers are still used to make a syrup of violet that serves as a laxative and colouring agent. The dried leaf and flowers are current in the British Herbal Pharmacopoeia as a specific for eczema and skin eruptions with serous exudate, particularly when associated with rheumatic symptoms.

Extraction & Characteristics

A concrete and absolute are produced by solvent extraction from the fresh flowers. The flower absolute is a yellowish-green viscous liquid with a sweet, rich, floral fragrance, characteristic of the fresh blossoms. Both leaves and petals contain nonadienal, parmone, hexyl alcohol, benzyl alcohol, ionone, and viola quercitin, among others. Reported actions include mild analgesic, anti-inflammatory, antirheumatic, antiseptic, liver decongestant, diuretic, expectorant, laxative, soporific, and circulatory stimulant.

Therapeutic Uses

  • Skin care: acne, eczema, refines the pores, thread veins, wounds.
  • Circulation, muscles, and joints: fibrosis, poor circulation, rheumatism.
  • Respiratory system: bronchitis, catarrh, mouth and throat infections.
  • Nervous system: dizziness, headaches, insomnia, nervous exhaustion. The scent was traditionally believed to comfort and strengthen the heart.

Blending

Violet flower blends well with Tuberose Oil, Clary Sage Essential Oil, Boronia Absolute, Tarragon Essential Oil, Cumin Essential Oil, Hops Essential Oil, Basil Essential Oil, Hyacinth Absolute, and other florals.

Safety

Non-toxic and non-irritant, but possible sensitization in some individuals. Patch test before topical use, and avoid on broken or sensitive skin.

Where You Will Encounter It

Used in high-class perfumery work, and occasionally in flavouring, mainly confectionery.

Therapeutic Applications

  • Catarrh: Apply via massage, vaporizer/diffuser, or gentle inhalation.
  • Bronchitis: Apply via massage, vaporizer/diffuser, or gentle inhalation.
  • Rheumatism: Apply via massage, compress, or bath.
  • Acne: Apply via massage, skin oil/lotion, flower water, bath, inhalation (steam), or neat application.
  • Dry & Sensitive Skin: Apply via massage, skin oil/lotion, flower water, or bath.
  • Eczema: Apply via massage, skin oil/lotion, flower water, or bath.
  • Low Blood Pressure: Apply via massage or bath.
  • Poor Circulation: Apply via massage or bath.
  • Scars & Stretch Marks: Apply via massage or skin oil/lotion.
