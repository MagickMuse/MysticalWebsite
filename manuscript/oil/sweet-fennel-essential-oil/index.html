Sweet Fennel Essential Oil is steam-distilled from the crushed seeds of Foeniculum vulgare var. dulce, the cultivated variety also known as Roman, garden, or French fennel. Sweet fennel is the variety preferred in aromatherapy and perfumery work, having little or none of the harsh fenchone note found in its bitter relative, and is non-sensitising on the skin.

Botanical Information

  • Scientific name: Foeniculum vulgare var. dulce
  • Family: Apiaceae (Umbelliferae)
  • Origin: Thought to have originated on the island of Malta, introduced by monks or crusaders thousands of years ago. Now grown principally in France, Italy, and Greece.
  • Source of plant's oil: Crushed seeds
  • Evaporation: Top to middle note

Extraction & Characteristics

Method: Steam distillation from crushed seeds.

Appearance and scent: A colourless to pale yellow liquid carrying a very sweet, anise-like aroma with a slightly earthy-peppery undertone.

Principal Constituents

Anethole at 50 to 60 per cent, plus limonene, phellandrene, pinene, anisic acid, anisic aldehyde, camphene, and other minor components. Sweet fennel oil contains little or no fenchone, the harsh-noted ketone that distinguishes the bitter variety.

Safety

Non-irritant, relatively non-toxic, and non-sensitising on the skin. Narcotic in large doses; use in moderation. Do not use during pregnancy and do not use if epileptic.

Therapeutic Actions

Aperitif, anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, antiseptic, antispasmodic, carminative, depurative, diuretic, emmenagogue, expectorant, galactagogue, laxative, orexigenic, circulatory stimulant, splenic, stomachic, tonic, and vermifuge.

Aromatherapy Uses

  • Skin care: bruises; dull, oily, or mature complexions; pyorrhoea.
  • Circulation, muscles, and joints: cellulitis, obesity, oedema, rheumatism.
  • Respiratory system: asthma, bronchitis.
  • Digestive system: anorexia, colic, constipation, dyspepsia, flatulence, hiccough, nausea.
  • Genito-urinary system: amenorrhoea, insufficient milk (in nursing mothers), menopausal problems.

Source-Named Blending Partners

Geranium Essential Oil, Lavender Essential Oil, Rose Oil, and Sandalwood Essential Oil.

Other Uses

In pharmaceutical products, sweet fennel is the main fennel oil used and is known as "codex" fennel oil, appearing in cough drops, lozenges, carminatives, and laxative preparations. It is extensively used as a flavour ingredient across all major food categories, in soft drinks, and especially in brandy, liqueurs, and other alcoholic beverages. The oil also features in soaps, toiletries, and perfumes.
