Plant1
Asafoetida

Asafetida essential oil is steam-distilled from the oleoresin of Asafoetida. The oleoresin itself is collected by making incisions into the root and above-ground parts of the plant; the milky juice is allowed to leak out and harden into dark reddish lumps, which are then scraped off, gathered, and distilled. Absolutes, resinoids, and tinctures are also produced from the same source. The finished oil is a yellowy-orange liquid carrying a bitter, acrid taste and a strong, tenacious odour reminiscent of garlic, with a sweet balsamic note hiding underneath. Principal constituents include disulphides, notably 2-butyl propenyl disulphide, alongside monoterpenes, free ferulic acid, valeric acid, and traces of vanillin.

Aromatherapy Use

Asafetida oil is reached for primarily across two systems of the body. For the respiratory system, the volatile oil is expelled through the lungs, which makes it well suited to asthma, bronchitis, and whooping cough. For the nervous system, it eases fatigue, nervous exhaustion, and stress-related conditions. Reported actions are antispasmodic, carminative, expectorant, hypotensive, and stimulant. Animals are repelled by its odour, which has historically given asafetida a place in protective and pest-deterrent applications as well.

Other Uses

Asafetida is now rarely used in pharmaceutical preparations, though it was formerly applied as a local stimulant for the mucous membranes. It still sees occasional use as a fixative and fragrance component in perfumes, particularly rose bases and heavy oriental compositions. It is widely employed in food, mostly in condiments and sauces.

Therapeutic Applications

  • Nervous Exhaustion: Apply via massage, bath, or vaporization.
  • Nervous Tension and Stress: Apply via massage, bath, or vaporization.
  • Bronchitis: Apply via massage, vaporizer/diffuser, or gentle inhalation.
  • Asthma: Apply via massage, vaporizer/diffuser, or gentle inhalation.
  • Whooping Cough: Apply via massage or steam inhalation.
