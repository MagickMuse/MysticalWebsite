Opopanax Essential Oil is the soft, sweet-balsamic aromatic of Opopanax (Commiphora erythraea), the East African oleogum-resin tree closely related to myrrh and now generally known in trade as bisabol myrrh.

Extraction & Characteristics

Two aromatic products are made from the crude oleogum resin. The essential oil is produced by steam (or water) distillation and is an orange, yellow, or olive liquid with a sweet-balsamic, spicy, warm, animal-like odour. Unlike myrrh, it does not carry a medicinal note, and it resinifies on exposure to air. The resinoid is produced by solvent extraction and is a solid dark mass with a warm, powdery, sweet-balsamic, rooty odour. The crude resin contains resins, gums (50 to 80 percent), and essential oils (10 to 20 percent), notably the sesquiterpene bisabolene and sesquiterpene alcohols.

Therapeutic Uses

Recorded actions are antiseptic, antispasmodic, balsamic, and expectorant. Specific aromatherapy and home-use applications are not detailed for opopanax beyond a general note that they may be similar to those of myrrh.

Blending

Opopanax blends well with Untitled, Coriander Seed Essential Oil, Rock Rose Essential Oil, Bergamot Essential Oil, Myrrh Oil, Frankincense Essential Oil, Vetivert Essential Oil, Sandalwood Essential Oil, Patchouli Essential Oil, mimosa, Fir Needle Essential Oil, and Neroli Essential Oil.

Safety

Opopanax is frequently adulterated, as it is more expensive than hirabol myrrh. The commercial resinoid is usually mixed with a solvent such as myristate because it is otherwise unpourable at room temperatures.

Where You Will Encounter It

Opopanax is used as a fixative and fragrance component in high-class perfumery. It is also used in liqueurs to lend body and add winelike notes.
