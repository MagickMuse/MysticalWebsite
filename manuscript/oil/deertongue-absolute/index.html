A dark green, heavy, viscous oleoresin extracted from the dried leaves of Deerstongue. The fresh leaves are nearly odourless, but when dried they develop a sweet vanilla-like fragrance from coumarin crystals that form on the upper leaf surfaces. This oleoresin is not used in home aromatherapy due to coumarin toxicity.

Safety

Coumarin has toxic properties, including liver injury and haemorrhage risk. The oleoresin may also cause dermal irritation and phototoxicity due to lactones in the material. Do not use in home aromatherapy. Avoid during pregnancy and nursing. Internal use is not recommended.

Extraction & Characteristics

Oleoresin produced by solvent extraction from the dried leaves. The product is a dark green, heavy, viscous liquid with a rich, herbaceous, new-mown hay character. The principal constituent is coumarin at roughly 1.6 percent, with dihydrocoumarin and smaller amounts of terpenes, aldehydes, and ketones.

Where You Will Encounter It

Used as a fixative and fragrance component in soaps, detergents, and perfumery work. Employed in the flavouring of tobacco and as a starting material for the isolation of coumarin. The oleoresin blends well with Oakmoss Absolute, Rock Rose Essential Oil (labdanum), Lavandin Essential Oil, Frankincense Essential Oil, Clove Essential Oil, Patchouli Essential Oil, and oriental-type fragrances.

Magickal Use

Practitioners drawn to the vanilla-coumarin character of this material should work with the dried leaves of the plant rather than the oleoresin. See Deerstongue for traditional applications.
