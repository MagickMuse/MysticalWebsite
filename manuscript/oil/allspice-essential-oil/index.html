Both oils are dominated by eugenol, with the leaves carrying up to 96 per cent and the berries 60 to 80 per cent. Methyl eugenol, cineol, phellandrene, and caryophyllene appear in lesser amounts. Across both forms, the oil acts as anaesthetic, analgesic, anti-oxidant, antiseptic, carminative, muscle-relaxing, rubefacient, stimulant, and tonic.

Blending

Allspice berry oil blends well with ginger, geranium, lavender, opopanax, labdanum, ylang ylang, patchouli, neroli, and oriental or spicy base accords.

Aromatherapy Use

For circulation, muscles, and joints, allspice is reached for in cases of arthritis, fatigue, muscle cramp, rheumatism, and stiffness. In tiny amounts it appears in massage blends meant to clear severe muscle spasm and restore mobility quickly, or to warm a body running cold. On the respiratory side it eases chills, congested coughs, and bronchitis. In digestion it works for cramp, flatulence, indigestion, and nausea. For the nervous system it lifts depression and nervous exhaustion, softens neuralgia, and takes the edge off tension and stress.

Other Uses

Allspice oil shows up in aromatic carminative medicines and as a fragrance in soaps, aftershaves, and spicy or oriental perfumes. Both leaf and berry oils are widely used to flavour foods, especially savoury and frozen dishes, as well as alcoholic and soft drinks.
