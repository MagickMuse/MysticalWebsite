Notes/Warnings
Cold-pressed grapefruit oils are phototoxic. Wait 12-72 hours after topical application before sun exposure. Distilled grapefruit is not phototoxic but should be patch tested. Use distilled for topical blends; cold-pressed is safe for diffusion only.
Drug Interactions
Cold-pressed grapefruit oil contains furanocoumarins that inhibit intestinal CYP3A4 — same drug interactions as grapefruit juice. Avoid with statins, calcium channel blockers, immunosuppressants (cyclosporine, tacrolimus), some benzodiazepines, and many other CYP3A4 substrates. Phototoxic at high concentrations — dermal max 4%. Steam-distilled grapefruit oil does not have these interactions.

Botanical Information

Citrus paradisi from the Rutaceae family, extracted from the peels. A modern hybrid of sweet orange (Citrus sinensis) and pomelo (Citrus maxima), originating in the United States. Top note with fast absorption.

Scent Profile

Bright, sweet, and citrusy with mood-elevating properties.

Magickal Correspondences

Deity: Alectrona (Greek goddess of the sun and morning)

Crystal: Citrine for directing personal power and creativity

Herbal Lore

As a modern hybrid, grapefruit has little historical lore. Its magickal properties draw from both parent fruits, sweet orange and pomelo, creating a unique solar energy signature. This citrus carries the combined magickal legacy of both ancestors.

Botanical Source Detail

Citrus × paradisi is a cultivated tree of the Rutaceae family, often growing more than ten metres high, bearing glossy leaves and large yellow fruits. The species is believed to have derived from the shaddock (C. grandis) and is recognised as a recent hybrid of C. maxima and C. sinensis.

Family: Rutaceae

Synonyms: C. racemosa, C. maxima var. racemosa, shaddock (oil)

Distribution

Native to tropical Asia and the West Indies. Cultivated in California, Florida, Brazil, and Israel. The oil is mainly produced in California.

Cultivars

Many different cultivars exist; the 'Duncan' cultivar is standard in Florida.

Folk Tradition

Grapefruit shares the nutritional qualities of other citrus species, being high in Vitamin C and serving as a valuable protection against infectious illness.

Therapeutic Actions

Antiseptic, antitoxic, astringent, bactericidal, diuretic, depurative, lymphatic stimulant, digestive stimulant, and tonic.

Extraction & Characteristics

Method: Cold expression from the fresh peel. A lower-grade oil is also distilled from the peel and remains of the fruit after the juice has been utilised, but this is of inferior quality.

Appearance and scent: A yellow or greenish mobile liquid carrying a fresh, sweet citrus aroma.

Principal Constituents

Limonene at around 90 per cent, plus cadinene, paradisiol, neral, geraniol, citronellal, sinensal, esters, coumarins, and furocoumarins.

Safety

The original source classifies grapefruit oil as non-toxic, non-irritant, non-sensitising, and non-phototoxic, with a short shelf life that oxidises quickly. Note that other modern sources flag cold-pressed citrus oils containing furocoumarins as phototoxic; see the Notes/Warnings property and the existing safety guidance for this workspace's harm-reduction stance.

Aromatherapy Uses

  • Circulation, muscles, and joints: cellulitis, exercise preparation, muscle fatigue, obesity, stiffness, water retention.
  • Immune system: chills, colds, flu.
  • Nervous system: depression, headaches, nervous exhaustion, performance stress.

Blending Partners

Lemon Essential Oil, Palmarosa Essential Oil, Bergamot Essential Oil, Neroli Essential Oil, Rosemary Essential Oil, Cypress Essential Oil, Lavender Essential Oil, Geranium Essential Oil, Cardamom Essential Oil, and other spice oils.

Other Uses

Employed as a fragrance component in soaps, detergents, cosmetics, and perfumes. Extensively used in desserts, soft drinks, and alcoholic beverages.
