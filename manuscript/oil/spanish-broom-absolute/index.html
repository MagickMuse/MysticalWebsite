Spanish Broom

Spanish Broom Absolute is a solvent-extracted absolute drawn from the yellow pea-flowers of Spartium junceum.

Extraction & Characteristics

Source: Dried flowers of Spartium junceum

Method: Solvent extraction (the absolute is the only commercial form)

Note: Middle

Scent: A dark brown, viscous liquid with an intensely sweet, floral, hay-like aroma over a soft herbaceous undertone.

Constituents: Capryllic acid, phenols, aliphatics, terpenes, esters, scoparin, sparteine, plant wax, and trace alkaloids. The sparteine and scoparin together carry both the magick and the danger of the plant.

Blending

For perfumery use only, Spanish broom absolute layers beautifully with rose, tuberose, cassie, mimosa, violet, vetiver, and other herbaceous florals. A trace amount adds a sun-warmed hay note that can ground a heavy floral or lift a green chypre.

Safety

Do not use in home aromatherapy. The main active constituent, sparteine, is genuinely toxic. In meaningful doses it causes vomiting, kidney irritation, cardiac weakness, depression of the nervous system, lowered blood pressure, and in extreme cases death. The absolute should be handled only by trained perfumers and flavour technologists at industry-standard dilutions, never applied directly to skin or used in diffusion at home.

Where You Will Encounter It

  • A signature note in soaps, cosmetics, and high-class perfumery, especially in floral and chypre compositions.
  • A flavouring ingredient at very low levels in rich preserves, alcoholic beverages, and some soft drinks.
