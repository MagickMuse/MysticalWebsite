Dilution Guide: Standard topical dilution is 2–2.5% (12–15 drops per ounce of carrier oil). Many commercially sold frankincense oils are already pre-diluted to 10% in a carrier. If yours is labeled this way, use 1 drop per 2 teaspoons of carrier rather than measuring by drops alone. Frankincense Essential Oil is steam-distilled from the resin tears of the Boswellia carteri tree, creating a dark, viscous oil with warm, earthy, and slightly musky notes. This sacred oil has been used in religious, medicinal, and magickal applications for over five thousand years, making it one of the most precious substances in human history.

Herbal Lore

Frankincense has been used in religious, medicinal, and social applications for more than five thousand years. It is so often paired with myrrh (Commiphora myrrha) that people frequently mistake them for one resin.

Once the Boswellia carteri tree reaches twenty years of age, it is tapped in a way similar to how maple trees are tapped for syrup. Milky white tree sap begins to drip from the spigot, and when dried, the drops of resin that form are called tears. To create the signature frankincense oil that has crowned kings and queens, the resin is steam-distilled to produce a dark, viscous oil.

Pure frankincense is very expensive and therefore is one of the most adulterated oils on the market today. If the oil you are buying looks and feels like water, consider quality testing. If the oil is clear and thin, it is highly adulterated. Ethical companies will state plainly on the label the dilution (e.g., "10 percent frankincense in jojoba"). Pure frankincense essential oil should be dark and viscous.

Due to the popularity of frankincense in religious and spiritual rituals, frankincense is becoming harder to obtain and is in danger of becoming endangered. Though studies have been released on the danger to this beautifully scented tree, even if entire farms were devoted to growing frankincense they would not start producing frankincense tears for twenty years, leaving an obvious gap in the production schedule.

Medical research into the nature of frankincense has yielded information on its antidepressant qualities, especially its use in treating children with depression, as most antidepressants have the opposite effect on children eighteen and under. Frankincense is also being screened in cancer studies as an antitumor drug.

Social uses of frankincense go back over five thousand years:

  • Ground frankincense tears were worn as eyeliner
  • Chewing the tears was prescribed to treat halitosis and other ailments
  • Given at the birth of kings because it was believed to treat any ailment known to mankind
  • The Ebers Papyrus (1550 BC) mentions it by name among 1,877 healing prescriptions
  • A gift of frankincense was a display of wealth, as it was more valuable than gold at the time

The magickal uses of frankincense are as many as its religious and medicinal uses combined. It crosses religious barriers to invoke feelings of sanctity, comfort, safety, and security in churchgoers and ritualists the world over.

Frankincense is more effective than white sage for banishing and purifying people, places, and things. Frankincense does not cause the divisiveness that white sage does when used by non-native peoples. (White sage is wild-crafted, meaning it is harvested in the wild because it is difficult to farm, and it has become trendy in native plant medicine. This is not only insulting to native peoples but also makes it hard to find and drives up the price.)

Frankincense has antidepressant effects: it increases emotional strength and elevates mood while still being meditative.

Aromatherapy Profile

The essential oil is steam-distilled from selected oleo gum resin, yielding approximately 3 to 10 per cent oil from 60 to 70 per cent resin. An absolute is also produced, used mainly as a fixative. The oil is a pale yellow or greenish mobile liquid with a fresh, terpeney top note and a warm, rich, sweet-balsamic undertone. Principal constituents are mainly monoterpene hydrocarbons, notably pinene, dipentene, limonene, thujene, phellandrene, cymene, myrcene, and terpinene, along with octyl acetate, octanol, and incensole, among others.

Genito-urinary system: cystitis, dysmenorrhoea, leucorrhoea, metrorrhagia.

Immune system: colds, 'flu.

Nervous system: anxiety, nervous tension, and stress-related conditions. Frankincense has, among its physical properties, the ability to slow down and deepen the breath, which is very conducive to prayer and meditation.

Blending

Frankincense blends well with Sandalwood Essential Oil, Pine Essential Oil, Vetivert Essential Oil, Geranium Essential Oil, Lavender Essential Oil, mimosa, Neroli Essential Oil, Orange Essential Oil, Bergamot Essential Oil, Camphor Essential Oil, Basil Essential Oil, Black Pepper Essential Oil, Cinnamon Bark Essential Oil, and other spices. It modifies the sweetness of citrus blends in an intriguing way.

Other Uses

The gum and oil are used as fixatives and fragrance components in soaps, cosmetics, and perfumes, especially oriental, spice, and men's fragrances. They are employed in some pharmaceuticals such as liniments and throat pastilles, and extensively in the manufacture of incense. The oil is used in minute amounts in some foods such as meat products, as well as alcoholic and soft drinks.
