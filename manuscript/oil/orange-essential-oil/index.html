Notes/Warnings
Sweet orange is sensitizing; do not use in dilutions over 1 percent (1 drop per 5 ml carrier oil) for skin safety. In large doses its aroma can cause tiredness and lethargy.
Drug Interactions
Sweet orange (Citrus sinensis) is non-phototoxic and has no significant drug interactions. Bitter orange (Citrus aurantium) is phototoxic when expressed — if your oil is bitter orange, avoid intense UV exposure within 12 hours of dermal application; dermal max ~1.4%.

Botanical Information

Citrus sinensis from the Rutaceae family, cold-pressed from peels. Originates from China, South America, and Italy. Top note with fast absorption. Originated in China, made way to South Africa by fifteenth century through trade routes.

Extraction & Characteristics

Sweet Orange Essential Oil is produced primarily by cold expression, hand or machine, of the fresh ripe or almost-ripe outer peel of Orange. A second-grade oil is produced by steam distillation of the same peel, and an inferior version is sometimes distilled from the essences recovered as a byproduct of orange juice manufacture. Distilled sweet orange oil oxidizes very quickly, and antioxidant agents are often added at the place of production. An oil from the flowers is occasionally produced under the name neroli Portugal or neroli petalae, and a small quantity of leaf oil is also made.

The expressed oil is a yellowy-orange or dark orange mobile liquid with a sweet, fresh-fruity scent richer than the distilled oil. The distilled oil is a pale yellow or colourless mobile liquid with a sweet, light-fruity scent and little tenacity. Principal constituents are over 90 percent monoterpenes, mainly limonene; the cold-expressed oil additionally contains bergapten, auraptenol, and acids.

Therapeutic Uses

Skin Care: Dull and oily complexions, and mouth ulcers.

Circulation, Muscles, and Joints: Obesity, palpitations, and water retention.

Respiratory System: Bronchitis and chills.

Digestive System: Constipation, dyspepsia, and spasm.

Immune System: Colds and flu.

Nervous System: Nervous tension and stress-related conditions.

Recorded actions include antidepressant, anti-inflammatory, antiseptic, bactericidal, carminative, choleretic, digestive, fungicidal, hypotensive, sedative (nervous), stimulant (digestive and lymphatic), stomachic, and tonic.

Blending

Sweet Orange blends well with Lavender Essential Oil, Neroli Essential Oil, Lemon Essential Oil, Myrrh Oil, and spice oils such as Nutmeg Essential Oil.

Safety

Generally non-toxic, although ingestion of large amounts of orange peel has been known to be fatal to children. Non-irritant and non-sensitizing, although limonene has been found to cause dermatitis in a few individuals. Distilled sweet orange oil is phototoxic, and its use on the skin should be avoided if there is danger of exposure to direct sunlight; there is no evidence that expressed sweet orange oil is phototoxic, although it too contains coumarins.

Where You Will Encounter It

Sweet orange peel tincture is used to flavour pharmaceuticals. Sweet orange oil is extensively used as a fragrance component in soaps, detergents, cosmetics, and perfumes, especially eau-de-colognes, and across all areas of the food and drinks industry, more so than bitter orange oil. It is also used as the starting material for the isolation of natural limonene.
