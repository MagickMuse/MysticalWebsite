Vanilla Absolute is solvent-extracted from cured vanilla beans, producing a rich, warm, familiar scent treasured in love magick and energy work.

Other Names & Related Species

Known as common vanilla, Mexican vanilla, Bourbon vanilla, and Reunion vanilla, with the botanical synonym Vanilla fragrans. Several other vanilla species are also cultivated, including Tahiti vanilla (Tahiti Vanilla), a smaller bean, and the vanillons type (V. pompona), which yields an inferior-quality oil.

Origin & Distribution

Native to Central America and Mexico. Vanilla is cultivated mainly in Madagascar and Mexico, and also in Tahiti, the Comoro Islands, East Africa, and Indonesia, though the pods are often processed in Europe or the United States. The green capsules ripen on the vine for eight or nine months before they are picked, and the immature pod, fourteen to twenty-two centimetres long, must then be fermented and dried for up to six months to become the fragrant brown vanilla of commerce. During the drying process, vanillin sometimes accumulates as white crystals on the surface of the bean.

Folk Tradition

In cultivation, vanilla's deep trumpet-shaped flowers have to be hand-pollinated, except in Mexico, where native hummingbirds do most of the work.

Extraction & Characteristics

A resinoid, often called an oleoresin, is produced by solvent extraction from the cured beans. An absolute is occasionally produced by further extraction from the resinoid. The result is a viscous dark brown liquid with a rich, sweet, balsamic, vanilla-like odour. Principal constituents include vanillin at 1.3 to 2.9 percent, with over 150 other compounds, many of them traces, including hydroxybenzaldehyde, acetic acid, isobutyric acid, caproic acid, eugenol, and furfural. The single named action is balsamic.

Blending

Vanilla blends well with sandalwood, vetiver, opopanax, benzoin, balsams, and spice oils.

Safety

Non-toxic, but a common sensitizing agent. Vanilla absolute is also widely adulterated, and quality varies considerably between sources. Patch test before topical use, and avoid on broken or sensitive skin.

Where You Will Encounter It

Used as a flavouring agent in pharmaceutical products. Vanilla appears as a fragrance ingredient in perfumes, especially oriental types, and is widely used to flavour tobacco and as a food flavouring, mainly in ice cream, yoghurt, and chocolate.
