Notes/Warnings
The essential oil is non-toxic, non-irritant, and non-sensitizing, but should be avoided during pregnancy. Distilled from the dried rhizomes and crushed roots of Asarum canadense.
Drug Interactions
Possible skin sensitization — patch-test before broader use. Some Asarum species can contain aristolochic acid, which is nephrotoxic and carcinogenic — verify your source and species (Asarum canadense and Asarum europaeum are commonly traded and have lower or no aristolochic acid; avoid unidentified Asarum oils). Use sparingly at low dilution.

Wild Ginger Essential Oil is steam-distilled from the dried rhizomes and crushed roots of Wild Ginger (Asarum canadense), the small woodland herb of the Aristolochiaceae family also known as Snakeroot or Indian Ginger.

Extraction & Characteristics

The essential oil is produced by steam distillation from the dried rhizomes and crushed roots. The finished oil is a brownish-yellow or amber liquid with a warm, woody-spicy, rich, gingerlike odour. Principal constituents include pinene, linalol, borneol, terpineol, geraniol, eugenol, and methyl eugenol, among others.

Therapeutic Uses

Wild Ginger oil may possibly be used for its antispasmodic qualities, for example for period pains or indigestion. Recorded actions of the plant include anti-inflammatory, antispasmodic, carminative, diuretic, diaphoretic, emmenagogue, expectorant, febrifuge, stimulant, and stomachic, with traditional folk-medicine use covering chronic chest complaints, dropsy, rheumatism, painful bowel and stomach spasms, amenorrhoea, and colds.

Blending

The oil blends well with Bergamot Essential Oil, Costus Essential Oil, Oakmoss Absolute, Pine Essential Oil, Clary Sage Essential Oil, Mimosa Absolute, Cassie Absolute, and other florals.

Safety

Wild Ginger Essential Oil is non-toxic, non-irritant, and non-sensitizing. Avoid during pregnancy. Wild Ginger should not be confused with serpentaria oil from the Virginian snakeroot (Aristolochia serpentaria), which belongs to the same botanical family but contains asarone and is considered toxic.

Where You Will Encounter It

The oil is occasionally used in perfumery work. It is mainly used as a flavouring agent with other spicy materials, especially in confectionery.
