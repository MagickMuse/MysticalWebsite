Bitter Fennel Essential Oil is steam-distilled from the crushed seeds, or from the whole flowering herb (the wild "weed"), of Foeniculum vulgare var. amara, the bitter or common variety of fennel. The seed oil and the herb-distilled "weed" oil differ in colour and aroma. Although bitter fennel is considered medicinally superior to its sweet counterpart, it has no aromatherapy or home-use application: the oil carries a large fraction of harsh-noted fenchone and is classed as a skin sensitiser.

⚠️
Important safety notice: Bitter fennel oil should not be used on the skin at all. It may cause sensitisation in some individuals, is narcotic in large doses, and must not be used by epileptics or during pregnancy. There is no recognized aromatherapy or home-use application for this oil.

Botanical Information

  • Scientific name: Foeniculum vulgare var. amara
  • Family: Apiaceae (Umbelliferae)
  • Origin: Native to the Mediterranean. Found growing wild in France, Spain, Portugal, and North Africa, where it yields the "weed" oil. Cultivated extensively worldwide; the principal oil producers are Hungary, Bulgaria, Germany, France, Italy, and India.
  • Source of plant's oil: Crushed seeds, or the whole flowering herb in the case of the "weed" oil
  • Evaporation: Top to middle note

Extraction & Characteristics

Method: Steam distillation. Two distinct oils are produced:

  1. Seed oil: a pale yellow liquid carrying a sharp, warm, camphoraceous odour.
  2. Weed oil (distilled from the whole flowering herb): a pale orange-brown liquid carrying a sharp, peppery-camphoraceous odour.

Principal Constituents

Anethole at 50 to 60 per cent, plus limonene, phellandrene, pinene, anisic acid, anisic aldehyde, and camphene. The defining feature of bitter fennel is its 18 to 22 per cent fenchone content, the harsh-noted ketone almost absent in sweet fennel.

Safety

Non-irritant and relatively non-toxic, but narcotic in large doses and a potential dermal sensitiser. Bitter fennel oil should not be used on the skin at all. Avoid during pregnancy and do not use if epileptic.

Therapeutic Actions (Reference Only)

Bitter fennel is considered medicinally superior to sweet fennel in herbal preparations of the seed and herb. The plant's classical action profile covers: aperitif, anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, antiseptic, antispasmodic, carminative, depurative, diuretic, emmenagogue, expectorant, galactagogue, laxative, orexigenic, circulatory stimulant, splenic, stomachic, tonic, and vermifuge. These belong to herbal preparations of the seed or herb rather than to topical use of the essential oil.

Aromatherapy Uses

None. Bitter fennel oil has no recognized place in aromatherapy or home use; sweet fennel is preferred for all topical and aromatic applications.

Other Uses

Largely confined to industrial flavor and fragrance work. Bitter fennel oil serves as a masking agent for harsh notes in industrial products, room sprays, and insecticides, and appears as a flavouring in some alcoholic beverages and foodstuffs where its sharper profile suits certain liqueurs.
