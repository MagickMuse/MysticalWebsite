Lemon Verbena is a steam-distilled essential oil from the freshly harvested leaves of Lemon Verbena, prized for its sweet, lemony, fruity-floral fragrance and its long use as a calming digestive and nervine. True lemon verbena oil is rare in commerce; most so-called verbena oil on the market is something else entirely.

Other Names & Related Species

Known as verbena, lemon verbena, and herb Louisa, with botanical synonyms Aloysia citriodora, Verbena triphylla, Lippia citriodora, and L. triphylla. Botanically related to the oregano family. Not to be mistaken for so-called Spanish verbena or Spanish verbena oil (Thymus hiemalis), nor confused with the herb Vervain. The French name for lemon verbena is verveine (verveine citronelle, verveine odorante), which can deepen the confusion.

Origin & Distribution

Native to Chile and Argentina. Lemon verbena is cultivated, and found semi-wild, in the Mediterranean region, including France, Tunisia, and Algeria, as well as in Kenya and China. The oil is mainly produced in southern France and North Africa. The plant itself is a handsome deciduous perennial shrub up to five metres high, with a woody stem, very fragrant pale green lanceolate leaves arranged in threes, and small pale purple flowers, often grown as an ornamental bush in gardens.

Folk Tradition

The uses of lemon verbena are similar to those of mint, orange flowers, and melissa, and it is indicated especially for nervous conditions that manifest as digestive complaints. The dried leaves are still a popular household tea, especially on the Continent, taken both as a refreshing, uplifting pick-me-up and to help restore the liver after a hangover.

Extraction & Characteristics

The essential oil is steam distilled from the freshly harvested herb, yielding a pale olive or yellow mobile liquid with a sweet, fresh, lemony, fruity-floral fragrance. Principal constituents are citral at 30 to 35 percent, with nerol and geraniol among others. Reported actions include antiseptic, antispasmodic, carminative, detoxifying, digestive, febrifuge, hepatobiliary stimulant, sedative to the nerves, and stomachic.

Therapeutic Uses

  • Digestive system: cramps, indigestion, liver congestion.
  • Nervous system: anxiety, insomnia, nervous tension, and stress-related conditions.

Blending

Lemon verbena blends well with Neroli Essential Oil, Palmarosa Essential Oil, olibanum, Tolu Balsam Essential Oil, Elemi Essential Oil, Lemon Essential Oil, and other citrus oils.

Safety

Possible sensitization, and phototoxic due to its high citral content. Use in low dilutions, patch test before topical use, and avoid sun exposure or sunbeds on skin where the oil has been applied. True lemon verbena oil is virtually non-existent in commerce, and most so-called verbena oil is either from the inferior Spanish verbena or a blend of Lemongrass Essential Oil, lemon, citronella, and similar oils, so source quality matters.

Where You Will Encounter It

Used in perfumery and citrus colognes. Eau de verveine remains popular in France, the rest of Europe, and America.

Therapeutic Applications

  • Anxiety: Apply via massage, bath, or vaporization.
  • Nervous Tension and Stress: Apply via massage, bath, or vaporization.
  • Cramp & Gastric Spasm: Apply via massage or compress.
  • Indigestion & Flatulence: Apply via massage.
  • Liver Congestion: Apply via massage.
  • Insomnia: Apply via massage, bath, or vaporization.
