Cassia

Cassia Bark Essential Oil is the water-distilled oil of Cinnamomum cassia bark, twigs, and stalks: a deep, dark, resinous liquid that carries the full bone-warming fire of Chinese cinnamon. It is also one of the most hazardous oils in the aromatherapy trade and has no place on skin, in a diffuser, or in any home preparation.

Extraction & Characteristics

Source: Bark, leaves, twigs, and stalks of Cinnamomum cassia

Method: Water distillation (hydrodistillation)

Note: Base

Scent: A dark brown liquid with a strong, spicy-warm, resinous aroma. Significantly heavier and more aggressive than the leaf oil.

Constituents: Cinnamic aldehyde (75 to 90 percent), with smaller amounts of methyl eugenol, salicylaldehyde, and methyl salicylaldehyde.

Safety

Do not use cassia bark essential oil on the skin or in any home aromatherapy preparation. It is classed as a dermal toxin, dermal irritant, dermal sensitizer, and mucous membrane irritant, and counts among the most hazardous oils on the market. Avoid entirely during pregnancy and around children, and treat any contact with mucous membranes as a chemical exposure event.

For cassia's traditional digestive, warming, antimicrobial, and circulatory benefits, work with the dried bark (the spice itself), as a tea, decoction, or culinary infusion, under the guidance of a qualified herbalist. Never the essential oil.

Where You Will Encounter It

  • Pharmaceutical mouthwashes, toothpastes, and gargles, where its bactericidal properties are valued at very low concentrations.
  • Tonic and carminative preparations for digestive support.
  • A foundational flavour ingredient in food, alcoholic beverages (including bitters and liqueurs), and soft drinks (including some classic colas).
  • Rarely used in perfumes and cosmetics because the dark colour stains formulations.
