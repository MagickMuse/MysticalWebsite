Arnica

Arnica oil is the infused-oil form of the arnica flower, prepared by macerating the daisy-like blooms of Arnica montana in a carrier oil. It is one of the cornerstones of a topical home medicine cabinet, reached for after a knock or a long day on the trail.

Topical Use

Arnica is widely regarded as one of the best remedies for bruises and sprains. Applied to intact skin, it stimulates the peripheral blood supply, helping the body clear pooled blood from a bruise and ease swelling. It is also used for rheumatic pain, sore joints, muscle aches, and a range of painful or inflammatory skin conditions including minor boils and acne flare-ups. Reported actions are anti-inflammatory, stimulant, and vulnerary.

A Note on the Essential Oil

A true essential oil of arnica can be steam-distilled from the flowers and root, but it is highly toxic and is not used in aromatherapy or home care. The infused oil, tincture, and ointment are the safe and traditional preparations. The pharmaceutical industry uses the tincture in skin products, and the flower oil sees occasional use in herbaceous-style perfumes and as a flavouring in some liqueurs, but those are industrial applications carried out under strict controls.

Warnings

⚠️ For external use only. Never apply to broken skin or open wounds, and never take internally.

⚠️ Do not use the steam-distilled essential oil at home. Only the infused oil, tincture, or ointment is safe for topical use.

Therapeutic Applications

  • Bruises: Apply as a cream, infused oil, tincture, or ointment to the affected area on intact skin.
