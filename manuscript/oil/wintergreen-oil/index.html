DANGER. Wintergreen Essential Oil is toxic, irritant, and sensitizing. It is composed of up to 98 percent methyl salicylate, the same constituent that makes Sweet Birch oil dangerous. Wintergreen Essential Oil is steam distilled from the leaves of Wintergreen (Gaultheria procumbens), a small North American evergreen herb. The true essential oil is almost obsolete in commerce, having been replaced by synthetic methyl salicylate, and the natural oil itself is treated as a toxic and environmentally hazardous material.

Extraction & Characteristics

The oil is produced by steam or water distillation from the leaves, which are first macerated in warm water. The essential oil does not occur crudely in the plant; it is only produced during the process of decomposition in warm water. The finished oil is a pale yellow or pinkish liquid with an intense sweet-woody, almost fruity odour. Its principal constituents are almost exclusively methyl salicylate (up to 98 percent), with formaldehyde and gaultheriline. It blends well with Oregano Essential Oil, mints including Peppermint Essential Oil and Spearmint Essential Oil, Common Thyme Essential Oil, Ylang Ylang Essential Oil, Narcissus Oil, and Vanilla Absolute.

Safety

Do not use Wintergreen Essential Oil internally or externally. It is toxic, irritant, and sensitizing, and it is also classified as an environmental hazard and a marine pollutant. The methyl salicylate concentration approaches 98 percent, the same constituent that makes Sweet Birch Essential Oil dangerous, and the two oils have historically been used interchangeably. The true oil is almost obsolete and has been replaced by synthetic methyl salicylate in commercial preparations.

Where You Will Encounter It

Wintergreen survives in some pharmaceutical preparations, such as Olbas oil, and in a small number of perfumery applications, particularly forest-type fragrances. It is most extensively used as a flavouring agent in the United States, appearing in toothpaste, chewing gum, root beer, Coca Cola, and other soft drinks. Modern industrial use overwhelmingly relies on synthetic methyl salicylate rather than the distilled natural oil.

Magickal Use

For magickal work involving Wintergreen, use the dried leaves, stems, and berries of the Wintergreen rather than the essential oil.
