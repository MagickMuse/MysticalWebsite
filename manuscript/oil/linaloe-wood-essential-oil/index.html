A pale yellow liquid steam distilled from the wood of Linaloe (Bursera glabrifolia), produced mainly in Mexico. The oil carries a sweet-woody, floral scent similar to rosewood. An essential oil is also occasionally produced from the leaves and twigs.

Extraction & Characteristics

Steam distilled from the wood, which is only used for distillation when the tree reaches twenty or thirty years of age. Principal constituents are mainly linalol with some linalyl acetate. In Mexico the wood oil is used in a similar fashion to rosewood, which contains similar constituents.

Therapeutic Uses

For skin care, the oil is applied to acne, cuts, dermatitis, wounds, and is suitable for all skin types. For the nervous system, it is used for nervous tension and stress-related conditions.

Blending

Blends well with Rose Oil, Sandalwood Essential Oil, Cedar Essential Oil, Atlas Cedarwood Essential Oil, Texas Cedarwood Essential Oil, Virginian Cedarwood Essential Oil, Rosewood Essential Oil, Frankincense Essential Oil, and other floral and woody fragrances.

Safety

Non-toxic, non-irritant, and non-sensitizing.

Where You Will Encounter It

Used in soaps, toiletries, and perfumes. Also used for the production of natural linalol, although this is increasingly being replaced by synthetic linalol.

Therapeutic Applications

  • Nervous Tension and Stress: Apply via massage, bath, or vaporization.
  • Acne: Apply via massage, skin oil/lotion, flower water, bath, inhalation (steam), or neat application.
  • Cuts & Sores: Apply via skin oil/lotion or compress.
  • Dermatitis: Apply via massage, skin oil/lotion, compress, flower water, or bath.
  • Wounds: Apply via skin oil/lotion, compress, or bath.

Traditional Grimoire Notes

An enhancer of energetic vibrations. Also promotes good health and vitality.
