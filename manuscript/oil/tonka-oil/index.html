Tonka Oil is derived from tonka beans (Dipteryx odorata), obtained from Eastern Venezuela and Brazil. The beans have a rich, vanilla-like scent with warm, sweet undertones.

Botanical Information

Scientific name: Dipteryx odorata

Origin: Eastern Venezuela, Brazil

Source: Beans

Scent Profile

Warm, sweet, and balsamic with strong vanilla-like notes. The scent is comforting and inviting, with a depth that makes it useful as a fixative in perfumery.

Safety Concerns

Tonka beans contain coumarin, which was long used to create artificial vanilla widely sold in the United States until it was determined to be a health hazard. Exercise caution when using tonka products.

⚠️ TOXIC: Do not ingest tonka tincture.

Magickal Uses

Tonka's warm, inviting scent makes it a natural choice for love magick. Add to sachets and charm bags for attracting romantic partners.

Traditionally used in money-drawing work. The sweet, prosperous energy of tonka supports abundance and financial success.

Substitution

For a safer alternative, use the Tonka Bouquet recipe combining Benzoin Oil with a few drops of Vanilla tincture (extract).

Other Names & Related Species

Known as tonquin bean and Dutch tonka bean, with the botanical synonym Coumarouna odorata. Many species of Dipteryx produce beans suitable for extraction.

Origin & Distribution

Native to South America, especially Venezuela, Guyana, and Brazil. The tree is also cultivated in Nigeria and elsewhere in West Africa. Most beans come from South America after curing, then travel to Europe and the United States for processing.

Folk Tradition

The beans are known locally as rumara. They are collected and dried, then soaked in alcohol or rum for twelve to fifteen hours to make them swell. When removed, the beans dry and shrink, covered in a whitish powder of crystallized coumarin. The curing process is partly a sales-promotion convention rather than an indication of quality, since the frosted appearance has come to be expected of the product. In Holland, the fatty substance from the beans is sold as tarquin butter, which was once used as an insecticide against moth in linen cupboards. A fluid extract was historically used for whooping cough, though large doses were known to paralyse the heart.

Extraction & Characteristics

A concrete and an absolute are produced by solvent extraction from the cured beans. The absolute is a semi-solid yellow or amber mass with a very rich, warm, sweet, herbaceous-nutty odour. The principal constituent is coumarin at 20 to 40 percent in the absolute. Reported actions include insecticidal, narcotic, and cardiac tonic, but the absolute is not safe to apply for any of these.

Blending

Tonka blends well with lavender, lavandin, clary sage, styrax, bergamot, oakmoss, helichrysum, and citronella.

Where You Will Encounter It

Used to a limited extent as a pharmaceutical masking agent. The absolute serves as a fixative and fragrance component in oriental, new-mown hay, and chypre-type perfumes. It is no longer used as a flavouring due to the coumarin ban in many countries, though it is still used to flavour tobacco.
