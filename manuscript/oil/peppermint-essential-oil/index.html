⚠️
Dilution Guide, Handle with Care: Peppermint is a potent oil and requires conservative dilution. Maximum topical dilution is 1% (6 drops per ounce of carrier oil). Do not use on or near children under 5. The menthol content can cause fatal breathing lapses. Avoid bath applications entirely. For diffusing, 1 drop per 100 ml water is sufficient; large doses by inhalation can cause dizziness, nausea, and double vision. A little goes a long way.

Botanical Information

Mentha piperita from the Lamiaceae family, extracted from leaves. Originates from North America. Top note with fast absorption.

Extraction & Characteristics

Peppermint Essential Oil is steam-distilled from the flowering herb of Peppermint, with a yield of approximately 3 to 4 percent. The finished oil is a pale yellow or greenish liquid with a highly penetrating, grassy-minty, camphoraceous odour. Its principal constituents are menthol at 29 to 48 percent and menthone at 20 to 31 percent, alongside menthyl acetate, menthofuran, limonene, pulegone, cineol, and others.

Therapeutic Uses

For skin care, peppermint addresses acne, dermatitis, ringworm, scabies, and toothache. For circulation, muscles, and joints it eases neuralgia, muscular pain, and palpitations. For the respiratory system it is used for asthma, bronchitis, halitosis, sinusitis, and spasmodic cough; inhaled in steam, it checks catarrh temporarily and provides relief from head colds and bronchitis, and its antispasmodic action makes it a particularly useful inhalation for asthma. For the digestive system it addresses colic, cramp, dyspepsia, flatulence, and nausea. For the immune system it is useful for colds, flu, and fevers. For the nervous system it addresses fainting, headache, mental fatigue, migraine, nervous stress, and vertigo.

Recorded actions include analgesic, anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, antiphlogistic, antipruritic, antiseptic, antispasmodic, antiviral, astringent, carminative, cephalic, cholagogue, cordial, emmenagogue, expectorant, febrifuge, hepatic, nervine, stomachic, sudorific, vasoconstrictor, and vermifuge. Peppermint is current in the British Herbal Pharmacopoeia for intestinal colic, flatulence, common cold, vomiting in pregnancy, and dysmenorrhoea.

Blending

Peppermint blends well with Benzoin Absolute, Rosemary Essential Oil, Lavender Essential Oil, Marjoram Essential Oil, Lemon Essential Oil, Eucalyptus Essential Oil, and other mints.

Safety

Non-toxic and non-irritant except in concentration, with possible sensitization due to menthol. Use in moderation.

Where You Will Encounter It

Peppermint Essential Oil is used as a flavouring agent in pharmaceuticals and as an ingredient in cough, cold, and digestive remedies. It flavours many foods, especially chewing gum and sweets, alcoholic and soft drinks, and is widely used in tobacco. As a fragrance component it appears in soaps, toothpaste, detergents, cosmetics, colognes, and perfumes.

Herbal Lore

One of herbs reputed in Four Thieves Vinegar of Marseilles. Magickal and medicinal uses revolve around purification of mint family. Doctors told patients to rub fresh leaves on heads to relieve headaches. Modern headache rollers contain peppermint and lavender synergies.

Older name of mint family Labiatae refers to flower shape resembling female genitalia, therefore associated with love magick, like basil, hyssop, lavender, marjoram, oregano, rosemary, sage, savory, and thyme.
