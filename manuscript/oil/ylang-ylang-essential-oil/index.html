Ylang Ylang Essential Oil is steam or water distilled from the freshly picked flowers of Ylang Ylang (Cananga odorata var. genuina), the tall tropical Annonaceae tree of Indonesia and the Philippines whose flower has long been called the flower of flowers.

Extraction & Characteristics

The oil is produced by water or steam distillation from freshly picked flowers. The first distillate, about 40 percent of the total, is called ylang ylang extra and is the top grade. Three further successive distillates are called Grades 1, 2, and 3. A complete oil is also produced, representing the total or unfractionated oil, although it is sometimes constructed by blending ylang ylang 1 and 2, the two least popular grades. An absolute and a concrete are also produced by solvent extraction for their long-lasting floral-balsamic effect.

Ylang ylang extra is a pale yellow, oily liquid with an intensely sweet, soft, floral-balsamic, slightly spicy scent. A good oil has a creamy rich topnote, and the other grades lack the depth and richness of the extra. Principal constituents include methyl benzoate, methyl salicylate, methyl para-cretol, benzyl acetate, eugenol, geraniol, linalol, and terpenes such as pinene and cadinene.

Therapeutic Actions

Aphrodisiac, antidepressant, anti-infectious, antiseborrhoeic, antiseptic, euphoric, hypotensive, nervine, regulator, sedative for the nervous system, stimulant of the circulation, and tonic.

Therapeutic Uses

In skin care, ylang ylang addresses acne, hair growth, hair rinses, insect bites, irritated and oily skin, and general skin care. For circulation, muscles, and joints it is useful for high blood pressure, hyperpnoea (abnormally fast breathing), tachycardia, and palpitations. For the nervous system it addresses depression, frigidity, impotence, insomnia, nervous tension, and stress-related disorders. Ylang ylang is also reputed to soothe and inhibit anger born of frustration.

Blending

Ylang ylang is an excellent fixative and an intriguing perfume oil in its own right. It blends well with Rosewood Essential Oil, Jasmine Absolute, Vetivert Essential Oil, Untitled, Bergamot Essential Oil, Mimosa Absolute, Cassie Absolute, Peru Balsam Essential Oil, Rose Oil, Tuberose Oil, and Costus Essential Oil.

Safety

Non-toxic and non-irritant, with a few cases of sensitization reported. Use in moderation, since the heady scent can cause headaches or nausea.

Where You Will Encounter It

Ylang ylang is extensively used as a fragrance component and fixative in soaps, cosmetics, and perfumes, especially oriental and floral types. The extra grade tends to be reserved for high-class perfumes, while Grade 3 is found in soaps, detergents, and similar products. The oil is also used as a flavour ingredient, mainly in alcoholic and soft drinks, fruit flavours, and desserts.

Related Species

Very closely related to Cananga Essential Oil (Cananga odorata var. macrophylla), although the oil produced from the ylang ylang variety is considered of superior quality for perfumery work, having a more refined character.
