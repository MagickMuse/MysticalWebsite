⚠️
Dilution Guide — High Sensitizer: Cinnamon is one of the most skin-sensitizing essential oils available and must be heavily diluted. Maximum topical dilution is 0.5% (3 drops per ounce of carrier oil), do not exceed this. Do not add to baths under any circumstances; direct contact with mucous membranes causes painful burning. Never apply undiluted. Avoid during pregnancy. In blends, use no more than 1 drop total per recipe. When in doubt, diffuse rather than apply topically.

Cinnamon Bark Essential Oil is a warm, spicy oil extracted from the inner bark of Cinnamomum verum, known for its potent antimicrobial properties and powerful magickal applications in love, prosperity, and psychic work. It is also one of the most hazardous oils in the trade and should never be applied to skin or used in home aromatherapy. For therapeutic and aromatherapy use, see Cinnamon Leaf Essential Oil instead.

Botanical Information

Cinnamomum verum of the Lauraceae family. The essential oil is extracted from the inner bark; the evaporation note is middle. Origin: Sri Lanka, Madagascar, Comoro Islands, South India, Burma, Indochina, India, Jamaica, and Africa.

Scent Profile

Woody, spicy, and strong. Warming, energizing, and stimulating.

Application

Dilute before topical use, diffuse, direct inhalation (with caution)

Therapeutic Actions

Cinnamon oil is recognized as anthelmintic, antidiarrhoeal, antidote to poison, antimicrobial, antiseptic, antispasmodic, antiputrescent, aphrodisiac, astringent, carminative, digestive, emmenagogue, haemostatic, orexigenic, parasiticide, refrigerant, spasmolytic, stomachic, vermifuge, and a stimulant of the circulatory, cardiac, and respiratory systems.

Constituents

The bark oil is produced by water or steam distillation from the dried inner bark. It is a pale-to-dark yellow liquid carrying a sweet, warm-spicy, dry, tenacious odour. Cinnamaldehyde dominates at 40 to 50 percent, alongside eugenol at 4 to 10 percent, benzaldehyde, cuminaldehyde, pinene, cineol, phellandrene, furfurol, cymene, and linalol. See Cinnamon Leaf Essential Oil for the leaf oil's distinct composition.

Aromatherapy Uses

Cinnamon bark oil has no recognized aromatherapy or home use. It is classed as a dermal toxin, irritant, and sensitizer, and is also irritant to the mucous membranes. For all therapeutic skin care, circulation, digestive, genito-urinary, immune, and nervous-system applications, work with Cinnamon Leaf Essential Oil instead.

Blending Partners

Cinnamon blends well with Frankincense Essential Oil (olibanum), Ylang Ylang Essential Oil, Orange Essential Oil, mandarin, Benzoin Absolute, and Peru Balsam Essential Oil, and it suits oriental-type mixtures.

Other Uses

The bark oil contributes fragrance and therapeutic action to toothpastes, nasal sprays, mouthwashes, cough syrups, and other dental preparations under controlled formulation. It is also extensively employed as a food flavouring, particularly in alcoholic and soft drinks, including Coca Cola.

Magickal Uses

Cinnamon is a powerful ingredient in love magick, particularly when working with committed partners. Add 1 drop cinnamon oil with 1 drop basil oil in a diffuser to stoke lust between committed partners, or include in love sachets and oils to draw romantic energy and increase heat and passion.

Cinnamon is one of the most potent oils for money magick. Burn an incense of benzoin, cinnamon, and basil to attract customers to your place of business. Add to money oils and spells to attract wealth, or diffuse and anoint cash registers and doorways to invite prosperity.

Cinnamon enhances psychic abilities and spiritual connection. Use in divination work to enhance intuition, strengthen connection to higher realms, and bring power and authority to magickal workings. Cinnamon also provides access to astral and spiritual realms.

The warming nature of cinnamon makes it excellent for protection. It combats fatigue, increases vitality, imparts courage and fortitude, and cleanses spaces of negative energy.

Cinnamon sharpens mental faculties, improving concentration and focus. Diffuse while working to enhance productivity and stimulate creativity and inspiration.

Warnings

Avoid during pregnancy. Highly irritating to skin; always dilute heavily before any topical use. Can cause burns or sensitization; use with extreme caution. Not recommended for baths, as direct contact causes painful burning. Perform a patch test before use. A little goes a long way; no more than one drop in blends.
