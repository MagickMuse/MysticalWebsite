◇Notes/Warnings
Herbs high in 1,8 cineole should not be used on or around children under 5—can result in suffocation due to paralyzing autonomic functions. Do not use if you have high blood pressure or epilepsy. Avoid during pregnancy. Possible skin irritant; use in low dilutions.
◇Drug Interactions
Stimulant — caution with high blood pressure. Camphor chemotype contraindicated with epilepsy and seizure disorders. 1,8-cineole chemotype: do not use on or around infants and young children. Mild anticoagulant activity — caution with anticoagulant and antiplatelet drugs. Mild hypoglycemic — caution with diabetes medication.

Botanical Information

Rosmarinus officinalis from the Lamiaceae family, extracted from leaves. Originates from France and United States. Middle note with fast absorption.

Scent Profile

Herbal Lore

Shakespeare called it "herb of remembrance." Latin binomial tells origin story: Rosmarinus means "dew of the sea." Blue-flowered plant's origin story involves plant springing up where ocean water touched fertile soil.

Traditional saying: "Where rosemary flourishes, the woman rules."

Often mixed with juniper and burned over charcoal to cleanse temple spaces of evil spirits throughout Mediterranean.
