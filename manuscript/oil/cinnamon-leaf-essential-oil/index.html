Schinus Molle Essential Oil

Cinnamon Leaf Essential Oil is steam-distilled from the leaves and twigs of Cinnamomum verum. The resulting yellow to brownish liquid carries a sharp, slightly harsh spicy aroma driven by its high eugenol load. Compared to the bark oil, leaf oil sits at a far gentler position on the safety scale and is the cinnamon distillate suited to aromatherapy and skincare.

Botanical Information

Scientific name: Cinnamomum verum

Botanical family: Lauraceae

Origin: Sri Lanka, Madagascar, Comoro Islands, South India, Burma, Indochina, India, Jamaica, Africa

Source of plant's oil: Leaves and twigs

Evaporation: Middle note

Extraction & Characteristics

Method: Water or steam distillation

Scent: Yellow to brownish in colour, with a sharp, slightly harsh spicy aroma

Constituents: Eugenol dominates at 80 to 96 percent, alongside eugenol acetate, cinnamaldehyde (around 3 percent), benzyl benzoate, linalol, and safrol.

Safety

Leaf oil is broadly considered non-toxic, though cinnamaldehyde can irritate the skin and eugenol (the dominant constituent) can irritate the mucous membranes. Dilute heavily before any topical use, perform a patch test, keep doses moderate, and avoid during pregnancy.

Therapeutic Actions

Leaf oil shares cinnamon's broader profile: anthelmintic, antidiarrhoeal, antimicrobial, antiseptic, antispasmodic, antiputrescent, aphrodisiac, astringent, carminative, digestive, emmenagogue, parasiticide, refrigerant, spasmolytic, stomachic, vermifuge, and a stimulant of the circulatory, cardiac, and respiratory systems.

Aromatherapy Uses

  • Skin care: lice, scabies, tooth and gum care, warts, wasp stings
  • Circulation, muscles, and joints: poor circulation, rheumatism
  • Digestive system: anorexia, colitis, diarrhoea, dyspepsia, intestinal infection, sluggish digestion, spasm
  • Genito-urinary system: childbirth (stimulates contractions), frigidity, leucorrhoea, metrorrhagia, scanty periods
  • Immune system: chills, colds, flu, infectious diseases
  • Nervous system: debility, nervous exhaustion, and stress-related conditions

Other Uses

The leaf oil features in toothpastes, nasal sprays, mouthwashes, cough syrups, and dental preparations, as well as in soaps, cosmetics, toiletries, and perfumes. It is also extensively employed as a food flavouring, particularly in alcoholic and soft drinks.
