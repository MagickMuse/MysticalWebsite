Borneo Camphor

Borneol Essential Oil is steam-distilled from the wood of Dryobalanops aromatica.

Extraction & Characteristics

Source: Wood of Dryobalanops aromatica. The crude borneol itself is collected directly from the trunk as a crystalline exudation. Harvesters traditionally test each tree by making small incisions to detect its presence.

Method: Steam distillation of the wood

Note: Top to Middle

Scent: A watery white to viscous black oil, depending on its camphor content, with a distinctive sassafras-like, camphoraceous aroma.

Constituents: The crude resin is mostly d-borneol, an alcohol rather than a ketone (a key chemical difference from Japanese camphor). The oil contains roughly 35 percent terpenes (pinene, camphene, dipentene), 10 percent alcohols (d-borneol, terpineol), 20 percent sesquiterpenes, and 35 percent resin.

Therapeutic Uses

Skin Care: Cuts, bruises, and as an insect repellent.

Circulation, Muscles & Joints: Debility, poor circulation, rheumatism, and sprains.

Respiratory Support: Bronchitis and coughs.

Immune Support: Colds, fever, flu, and infectious illness.

Nervous System: Nervous exhaustion, stress-related conditions, and neuralgia.

Safety

Considered non-toxic and non-sensitizing, though it can be a dermal irritant in concentration. Always dilute well in a carrier and patch-test before broad use. Not interchangeable with Japanese or Formosa camphor oil, which is chemically different and considerably more toxic.
