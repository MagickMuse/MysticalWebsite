Onion Essential Oil

Garlic Essential Oil is produced by steam distillation from the fresh crushed bulbs of Garlic (Allium sativum), a strongly scented perennial herb up to 1.2 metres high with long, flat, firm leaves, whitish flowering stems, and a bulb made up of several cloves pressed together within a thin white skin.

Extraction & Characteristics

The oil is a colourless to pale yellow mobile liquid with a strong, unpleasant, familiar garlic-like odour. Major oil-producing countries include Egypt, Bulgaria, France, China, Germany, and Japan.

Principal Constituents

Allicin, allylpropyl disulphide, diallyl disulphide, diallyl trisulphide, citral, geraniol, linalol, and phellandrene, among others.

Documented Medicinal Tradition

Garlic has been used for thousands of years for its medicinal virtues: for respiratory and urinary tract infections; digestive disorders and infestations; skin eruptions; heart disease, high blood pressure, and arteriosclerosis; as well as epidemics and fever. It was used in the First World War to prevent gangrene and sepsis. In China it is applied to diarrhoea, dysentery, tuberculosis, diphtheria, hepatitis, ringworm, typhoid, and trachoma, among others. It is listed as specific in the British Herbal Pharmacopoeia for chronic bronchitis, and its properties have been attested by modern experimental and clinical research.

Aromatherapy & Home Use

Due to its unpleasant and pervasive smell, the oil is not often used externally. However, the capsules may be taken internally according to the instructions on the label for respiratory and gastro-intestinal infections, urinary tract infections such as cystitis, heart and circulatory problems, and to fight infectious diseases in general.

Other Uses

The oil is made into capsules and included in many health food products, mainly to help reduce high blood pressure and protect against heart disease. It is also extensively employed as a flavour ingredient in most major food categories, especially savouries.

Safety

Generally non-toxic and non-irritant, although it has been known to irritate the stomach. May also cause sensitization in some individuals.

Therapeutic Applications

  • High Blood Pressure: Generally taken internally as capsules per label instructions; not recommended for massage, bath, or vaporization due to strong odour and irritation.
