Silver Fir Templin Essential Oil is steam-distilled from the broken-up pieces of the cones of Silver Fir (Abies alba), a relatively small coniferous tree in the Pinaceae family native to north European mountainous regions.

Extraction & Characteristics

The oil is similar to the needle oil but with a more orange-like fragrance. Principal constituents include pinene, limonene, borneol, and bornyl acetate, among others.

Related Species

Oils distilled from the twigs and needles of various members of the coniferous families Abies, Larix, Picea, Spruce, Pinus, and Tsuga are all commonly called fir needle oil, so the specific botanical name matters. Other notable Abies species include Canadian Balsam (A. balsamifera), the Siberian fir (A. siberica) which is the most popular fir needle oil in Europe and the USA due to its fine fragrance, and the Japanese fir oils from A. mayriana and A. sachalinensis. See also Fir Needle Essential Oil, distilled from the needles and young twigs of the same tree.

Folk Tradition

Silver fir is highly esteemed on the Continent for its medicinal virtues and its fragrant scent. It is used mainly for respiratory complaints, fever, and muscular and rheumatic pain.

Therapeutic Uses

For circulation, muscles, and joints, templin oil addresses arthritis, muscular aches and pains, and rheumatism. In the respiratory system, it is used for bronchitis, coughs, and sinusitis, among other complaints. In the immune system, it supports the body through colds, fever, and 'flu.

Blending

Templin oil blends well with Galbanum Essential Oil, labdanum, Lavender Essential Oil, Rosemary Essential Oil, Lemon Essential Oil, Pine Essential Oil, and Marjoram Essential Oil.

Safety

Non-toxic, non-irritant (except in high concentration), non-sensitizing.

Where You Will Encounter It

Silver fir oils are employed as ingredients in some cough and cold remedies and rheumatic treatments, and as fragrance components in deodorants, room sprays, disinfectants, bath preparations, soaps, and perfumes.

Therapeutic Applications

  • Common Cold: Apply via massage, bath, vaporization, or steam inhalation.
  • Flu: Apply via massage, bath, vaporization, or steam inhalation.
  • Fever: Apply via compress or bath.
  • Coughs: Apply via massage, vaporizer/diffuser, or gentle inhalation.
  • Bronchitis: Apply via massage, vaporizer/diffuser, or gentle inhalation.
  • Rheumatism: Apply via massage, compress, or bath.
  • Aches and Pains: Apply via massage, compress, or bath.
  • Arthritis: Apply via massage, skin oil/lotion, compress, or bath.
  • Sinusitis: Apply via steam inhalation.
