Longleaf Pine Essential Oil is the steam-distilled aromatic of Longleaf Pine (Pinus palustris), the same southeastern American conifer that supplies most American turpentine.

Extraction & Characteristics

The crude oil is obtained by steam distillation from sawdust and wood chips taken from the heartwood and roots of the tree (wastage from the timber mills), then submitted to fractional distillation under atmospheric pressure to produce the essential oil. The finished oil is a water-white or pale yellow liquid with a sweet-balsamic, pinewood scent. Principal constituents are terpineol, estragole, fenchone, fenchyl alcohol, and borneol, among others.

Therapeutic Uses

Circulation, Muscles, and Joints: Arthritis, debility, lumbago, muscular aches and pains, poor circulation, rheumatism, and stiffness.

Respiratory System: Asthma, bronchitis, catarrh, and sinusitis.

Recorded actions include mild analgesic, antirheumatic, antiseptic, bactericidal, expectorant, insecticidal, and stimulant.

Blending

Longleaf Pine blends well with Rosemary Essential Oil, Pine Essential Oil, Atlas Cedarwood Essential Oil, Untitled, Rosewood Essential Oil, ho leaf, and Oakmoss Absolute.

Safety

Non-toxic and non-irritant except in concentration. Possible sensitization in some individuals.

Where You Will Encounter It

Longleaf pine oil is used extensively in medicine, particularly in veterinary antiseptic sprays, disinfectants, detergents, and insecticides (as a solvent carrier). It is employed as a fragrance component in soaps, toiletries, bath products, and perfumes, and is also used in paint manufacture, although it is increasingly being replaced by synthetic 'pine oil.'
