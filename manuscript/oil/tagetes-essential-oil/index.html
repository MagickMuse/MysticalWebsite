Tagetes is a strongly scented oil pressed from the orange, daisylike flowers of Tagetes minuta. It is potent enough that it is typically used in very small percentages, and even then with caution.

Other Names & Related Species

Known as tagette, taget, marigold, Mexican marigold, and wrongly called calendula oil, with the botanical synonym T. glandulifera. Several other tagetes species share similar characteristics and are also distilled, notably French marigold (Tagetes patula) and African or Aztec marigold (Tagetes erecta). Tagetes is not to be confused with the true marigold, Calendula, which has very different properties and constituents and is used extensively in herbal medicine.

Origin & Distribution

Native to South America and Mexico. Tagetes now grows wild in Africa, Europe, Asia, and North America. The essential oil is produced mainly in South Africa, France, Argentina, and Egypt; the absolute is produced in Nigeria and France.

Folk Tradition

In India, the locally grown flowering tops of French marigold are distilled into a receiver containing a solvent, often sandalwood oil, to produce attar genda, a popular Indian perfume material. In China, the flowers of African marigold are used for whooping cough, colds, colic, mumps, sore eyes, and mastitis, usually as a decoction.

Extraction & Characteristics

Two distinct products are made from the fresh flowering herb. The essential oil is produced by steam distillation and is a dark orange or yellow mobile liquid that slowly solidifies on exposure to air and light, with a bitter-green, herby odour. The absolute and concrete are produced by solvent extraction; the absolute is an orange, olive, or brown semi-liquid mass with an intense, sweet, green-fruity odour. Principal constituents are tagetones, with ocimene, myrcene, linalol, limonene, pinenes, carvone, citral, camphene, valeric acid, and salicylaldehyde among others. Reported actions include anthelmintic, antispasmodic, bactericidal, carminative, diaphoretic, emmenagogue, fungicidal, and stomachic.

Therapeutic Uses

  • Skin care: bunions, calluses, corns, fungal infections.

Blending

Tagetes blends well with clary sage, lavender, jasmine, bergamot, and other citrus oils, always in very small percentages.

Safety

Use with care and in moderation, in low dilutions only. Tagetone, the main constituent, may be harmful to the human organism, and dermatitis has been reported with tagetes species. Patch test before topical use, and avoid on broken or sensitive skin.

Where You Will Encounter It

Used in some pharmaceutical products. The absolute and oil appear to a limited extent in herbaceous and floral perfumes. Tagetes is also used to flavour tobacco and most major food categories, including alcoholic and soft drinks.

Therapeutic Applications

  • Verrucae: Apply via skin oil/lotion or neat application.
  • Warts & Corns: Apply via skin oil/lotion or neat application.
