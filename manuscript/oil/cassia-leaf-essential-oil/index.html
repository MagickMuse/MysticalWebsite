Cassia

Cassia Leaf Essential Oil is the steam-distilled oil of Cinnamomum cassia leaves: a brownish-yellow liquid carrying the same fierce cinnamic aldehyde profile as the bark, just slightly softer in the body. Despite the gentler scent, it shares the bark oil's serious safety profile and has no place in home aromatherapy or skincare.

Extraction & Characteristics

Source: Leaves of Cinnamomum cassia

Method: Steam distillation (rectified versions are pale yellow rather than brownish)

Note: Base

Scent: A brownish-yellow to pale yellow liquid with a sweet, woody-spicy, tenacious aroma. Less harsh than the bark oil, but still dominated by cinnamic aldehyde.

Constituents: Cinnamic aldehyde (75 to 90 percent), with smaller amounts of methyl eugenol, salicylaldehyde, and methyl salicylaldehyde.

Safety

Do not use cassia leaf essential oil on the skin or in any home aromatherapy preparation. It is classed as a dermal toxin, dermal irritant, dermal sensitizer, and mucous membrane irritant, and counts among the most hazardous oils on the market. Avoid entirely during pregnancy and around children, and treat any contact with mucous membranes as a chemical exposure event.

For cassia's traditional digestive, warming, and stimulant benefits, work with the dried bark (the spice you already cook with) under the guidance of a qualified herbalist, and never the essential oil.

Where You Will Encounter It

  • Pharmaceutical mouthwashes, toothpastes, and gargles, where its bactericidal properties are useful at very low concentrations.
  • Tonic and carminative preparations.
  • A heavy-duty flavour ingredient in food, alcoholic beverages, and soft drinks. The flavour you taste in cinnamon-flavoured candy and gum is often cassia.
  • Rarely used in fine perfumery because of its dark colour and aggressive scent profile.
