Cumin Essential Oil is steam-distilled from the ripe seeds of Cuminum cyminum, a small Apiaceae annual cultivated for millennia across the Mediterranean and the Indian subcontinent. The resulting pale yellow to greenish liquid carries a warm, soft, spicy aroma with a distinctly musky undertone that pairs naturally with oriental-style perfumery compositions. Cumin shares its botanical family (Apiaceae), and many of its therapeutic actions, with coriander.

Botanical Information

Scientific name: Cuminum cyminum

Botanical family: Apiaceae (Umbelliferae)

Origin: Native to upper Egypt; cultivated since ancient times throughout the Mediterranean basin (especially Spain, France, and Morocco), India, and the former USSR. The essential oil is produced principally in India, Spain, and France.

Source of plant's oil: Ripe seeds

Evaporation: Top to middle note

Extraction & Characteristics

Method: Steam distillation from the ripe seeds

Scent: Pale yellow or greenish liquid carrying a warm, soft, spicy aroma with a musky undertone

Constituents: Aldehydes dominate at up to 60 percent, with cuminaldehyde as the principal compound. Monoterpene hydrocarbons account for up to another 52 percent, including pinenes, terpinenes, cymene, phellandrene, myrcene, and limonene. Farnesene and caryophyllene are also present in smaller amounts.

Safety

Cumin oil is generally classed as non-toxic, non-irritant, and non-sensitizing in topical aromatic use. It is, however, phototoxic: skin treated with the oil should not be exposed to direct sunlight or UV light. The oil should also be avoided during pregnancy. Because it is rich in aldehydes, cumin oxidizes more quickly than many spice oils; storing cool, dark, and tightly sealed extends its useful shelf life.

Therapeutic Actions

Anti-oxidant, antiseptic, antispasmodic, antitoxic, aphrodisiac, bactericidal, carminative, depurative, digestive, diuretic, emmenagogue, larvicidal, nervine, stimulant, and tonic.

Aromatherapy Uses

  • Circulation, muscles, and joints: accumulation of fluids or toxins, poor circulation
  • Digestive system: colic, dyspepsia, flatulence, indigestion, spasm
  • Nervous system: debility, headaches, migraine, nervous exhaustion

Blending Partners

Cumin pairs especially well with Lavender Essential Oil, Lavandin Essential Oil, Rosemary Essential Oil, Galbanum Essential Oil, Rosewood Essential Oil, Cardamom Essential Oil, and oriental-style fragrance compositions.

Other Uses

Cumin oil features in veterinary digestive preparations and supplies a fragrance accent to cosmetics and perfumes. It also serves as a flavouring agent across many foods and drinks, with especially heavy use in meat products and condiments.

Therapeutic Applications

  • Headache: Apply via massage, compress, or vaporization.
  • Nervous Exhaustion: Apply via massage, bath, or vaporization.
  • Colic: Apply via massage.
  • Accumulation of Toxins: Apply via massage, skin oil/lotion, or bath.
  • Low Blood Pressure: Apply via massage or bath.
  • Poor Circulation: Apply via massage or bath.
  • Cramp & Gastric Spasm: Apply via massage or compress.
  • Indigestion & Flatulence: Apply via massage.
