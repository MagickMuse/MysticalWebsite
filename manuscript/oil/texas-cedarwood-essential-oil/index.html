Texas Cedarwood Essential Oil is produced by steam distillation from the heartwood and wood shavings of Juniperus ashei. Unlike the Virginian cedar, the tree is felled especially for its essential oil.

Characteristics

The crude oil is a dark orange to brownish viscous liquid with a smoky-woody, sweet, tar-like odour. The rectified oil is a colourless or pale yellow liquid with a sweet, balsamic, pencil-wood scent, similar to Virginian cedarwood but harsher. Blends well with patchouli, spruce, vetiver, pine, and leather-type scents.

Principal Constituents

Cedrene, cedrol (higher than the Virginian oil), thujopsene, and sabinene, among others. Otherwise similar to Virginian cedarwood.

Actions

Antiseptic, antispasmodic, astringent, diuretic, expectorant, sedative (nervous), stimulant (circulatory).

Folk Tradition

In New Mexico, the native Indians use cedarwood oil for skin rashes. It is also used for arthritis and rheumatism.

Therapeutic Applications

  • Nervous Tension and Stress: Apply via massage, bath, or vaporization.
  • Catarrh: Apply via massage, vaporizer/diffuser, or gentle inhalation.
  • Bronchitis: Apply via massage, vaporizer/diffuser, or gentle inhalation.
  • Rheumatism: Apply via massage, compress, or bath.
  • Arthritis: Apply via massage, skin oil/lotion, compress, or bath.
  • Baldness & Hair Care: Apply via skin oil/lotion or hair care.
  • Acne: Apply via massage, skin oil/lotion, flower water, bath, inhalation (steam), or neat application.
  • Dandruff: Apply via skin oil/lotion or hair care.
  • Dermatitis: Apply via massage, skin oil/lotion, compress, flower water, or bath.
  • Eczema: Apply via massage, skin oil/lotion, flower water, or bath.
  • Leucorrhoea: Apply via bath or douche.
  • Cystitis: Apply via compress, bath, or douche.

Traditional Grimoire Notes

An oil of Jupiter, promoting peace, contentment, wealth, and good fortune, in addition to driving away all evil and negativity. Believed to attract good spirits and repel bad ones.
