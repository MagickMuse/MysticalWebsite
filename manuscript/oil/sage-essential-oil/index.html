Sage Essential Oil is steam-distilled from the dried leaves of Sage (Salvia officinalis), the Mediterranean herb of the Lamiaceae family. The oil is dominated by the toxic ketone thujone and should be avoided in home aromatherapy.

Safety

Common Sage oil is an oral toxin due to its main constituent, thujone, which makes up around 42 percent of the oil. It is an abortifacient and must be avoided during pregnancy. It must also be avoided in epilepsy. Common Sage oil should be used with care or avoided in therapeutic work altogether. Spanish Sage (Salvia lavandulaefolia) and Clary Sage (Salvia sclarea) are safer alternatives.

Extraction & Characteristics

The essential oil is produced by steam distillation from the dried leaves; an oleoresin is also produced from the exhausted plant material. The finished oil is a pale yellow mobile liquid with a fresh, warm-spicy, herbaceous, somewhat camphoraceous odour. The oil blends well with Lavandin Essential Oil, Rosemary Essential Oil, Rosewood Essential Oil, Lavender Essential Oil, Hyssop Essential Oil, Lemon Essential Oil, and other citrus oils. Common sage oil is preferred in perfumery work to Spanish Sage oil, which is safer but has a less refined fragrance. Principal constituents are thujone (about 42 percent), cineol, borneol, caryophyllene, and other terpenes.

Where You Will Encounter It

Common Sage oil is used in some pharmaceutical preparations including mouthwashes, gargles, and toothpastes. It is employed as a fragrance component in soaps, shampoos, detergents, anti-perspirants, colognes, and perfumes, especially men's fragrances. The oil and oleoresin are extensively used for flavouring foods (mainly meat products), soft drinks, and alcoholic beverages, especially vermouth. It also serves as a source of natural antioxidants.

Spellwork Correspondences

Candle Dressing: Rub oil from center to either end to "dress" the candle, from top to bottom to draw energy toward you, or bottom to top to banish. Oils are flammable, use with care.
