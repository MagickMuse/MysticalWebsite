◇Notes/Warnings
Generally non-toxic, non-irritant, and non-sensitizing, but mace contains more myristicin than nutmeg. In large doses shows toxicity signs (nausea, stupor, tachycardia); large quantities are hallucinogenic. Mace is probably less safe than nutmeg, especially compared with West Indian nutmeg. High myristicin content — MAOI-like activity. Caution with antidepressants (especially MAOIs and SSRIs), CNS-active drugs, and tramadol. Psychoactive at high doses (toxic delirium possible). Tisserand & Young restrict to 0.075% dermal max.

Mace Essential Oil is steam-distilled from the dried orange-brown aril or husk of Mace, the bright red netlike covering surrounding the nutmeg seed inside the fruit of Myristica fragrans. A small quantity of mace oleoresin is also produced by solvent extraction from the same husk.

Extraction & Characteristics

Mace Essential Oil is a water-white or pale yellow mobile liquid with a sweet, warm-spicy scent. The mace oleoresin is an orange-brown viscous liquid with a fresh, spicy-warm, balsamic fragrance and good masking power. The oil's constituents are similar to those of nutmeg oil but contain more myristicin.

Therapeutic Uses

Circulation, Muscles, and Joints: Arthritis, gout, muscular aches and pains, poor circulation, and rheumatism.

Digestive System: Flatulence, indigestion, nausea, and sluggish digestion.

Immune System: Bacterial infection.

Nervous System: Frigidity, impotence, neuralgia, and nervous fatigue.

Recorded actions include analgesic, anti-emetic, anti-oxidant, antirheumatic, antiseptic, antispasmodic, aphrodisiac, carminative, digestive, emmenagogue, gastric secretory stimulant, larvicidal, orexigenic, prostaglandin inhibitor, stimulant, and tonic.

Blending

Mace blends well with Oakmoss Absolute, Lavandin Essential Oil, Bay Laurel Essential Oil, Peru Balsam Essential Oil, Orange Essential Oil, Geranium Essential Oil, Untitled, Rosemary Essential Oil, Lime Essential Oil, Petitgrain Essential Oil, Untitled, Coriander Seed Essential Oil, Untitled, and other spice oils.

Safety

Generally non-toxic, non-irritant, and non-sensitizing. Mace oil contains more myristicin than nutmeg oil, and in large doses both show signs of toxicity such as nausea, stupor, and tachycardia, with large quantities being hallucinogenic and excitant to the motor cortex. Mace is therefore probably less safe than nutmeg, especially the West Indian type. Use in moderation, and with care in pregnancy.

Where You Will Encounter It

Mace oil is used as a flavouring agent in pharmaceuticals, especially analgesic and tonic preparations, and in soaps, lotions, detergents, cosmetics, and perfumes. The mace oleoresin is used in colognes and perfumes, especially men's fragrances. Both the oil and the oleoresin appear in most major food categories, including alcoholic and soft drinks.
