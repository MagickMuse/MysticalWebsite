Yarrow Essential Oil (Achillea millefolium) is one of the true treasures of the Earth. This remarkable oil is naturally blue in color due to its azulene content and possesses an incredible scent.

Scent Profile

Complex, herbaceous, and sweet with green undertones. The scent is distinctive and highly prized among aromatherapists and magical practitioners alike.

Note on Color

Yarrow essential oil has a striking natural blue color, a result of the azulene compounds produced during steam distillation. This blue hue is not artificial but a genuine characteristic of properly distilled yarrow oil.

Note on Price

Due to the low oil yield from yarrow flowers, genuine essential oil is expensive. Use sparingly in blends, as a little goes a long way both aromatically and magically.

Magickal Uses

Yarrow's association with Aphrodite and Venus makes it powerful for love workings. Add small amounts to love blends.

Named for the hero Achilles who used it to heal his warriors, yarrow oil supports courage work. Include in blends designed to bolster bravery and resolve.

Yarrow enhances psychic abilities and intuition. Add to blends for divination, scrying, or developing psychic gifts.

Other Names & Related Species

Known as milfoil, common yarrow, nosebleed, and thousand leaf, with many other country names. The Achillea genus is very extensive. Other varieties include the Ligurian yarrow (A. ligustica) and the musk yarrow or iva (A. moschata), which also produces an essential oil, mainly cineol, used to make iva liquor, a medicinal aperitif. Cloth-of-Gold is a related ornamental species in the workspace.

Origin & Distribution

Native to Eurasia and naturalized in North America, yarrow now grows in most temperate zones of the world. The oil is mainly distilled in Germany, Hungary, France, and the former Yugoslavia, with smaller production in the United States and Africa. The plant is a perennial herb up to one metre high, with a simple stem and finely dissected leaves that give it a lacy appearance, bearing numerous dense pinky-white flowerheads.

Folk Tradition

An age-old herbal medicine used for a wide range of complaints, including fever, respiratory infections, digestive problems, nervous tension, and externally for sores, rashes, and wounds. The use of yarrow for wounds is said to go back to Achilles, who used it for injuries inflicted by iron weapons. In China it is used mainly for menstrual problems and haemorrhoids; in Norway it is also used for rheumatism. The stalks are traditionally used for divination in the I Ching, the Chinese classic. Yarrow is current in the British Herbal Pharmacopoeia as a specific for thrombotic conditions with hypertension.

Extraction & Characteristics

The essential oil is produced by steam distillation from the dried herb, yielding a dark blue or greenish-olive liquid with a fresh, green, sweet-herbaceous, slightly camphoraceous odour. Principal constituents are azulene at up to fifty-one percent, with pinenes, caryophyllene, borneol, terpineol, cineol, bornyl acetate, camphor, sabinene, and thujone among others. Constituents, especially azulene levels, vary according to source. Reported actions include anti-inflammatory, antipyretic, antirheumatic, antiseptic, antispasmodic, astringent, carminative, cicatrisant, diaphoretic, digestive, expectorant, haemostatic, hypotensive, stomachic, and tonic.

Therapeutic Uses

  • Skin care: acne, burns, cuts, eczema, hair rinse to promote hair growth, inflammations, rashes, scars, tones the skin, varicose veins, wounds.
  • Circulation, muscles, and joints: arteriosclerosis, high blood pressure, rheumatoid arthritis, thrombosis.
  • Digestive system: constipation, cramp, flatulence, haemorrhoids, indigestion.
  • Genito-urinary system: amenorrhoea, dysmenorrhoea, cystitis, and other infections.
  • Immune system: colds, fever, flu.
  • Nervous system: hypertension, insomnia, stress-related conditions.

Blending

Yarrow blends well with Atlas Cedarwood Essential Oil, Pine Essential Oil, Roman Chamomile Essential Oil, Valerian Essential Oil, Vetivert Essential Oil, and Oakmoss Absolute.

Safety

Non-toxic and non-irritant, with possible sensitization in some individuals. Patch test before topical use, and avoid on broken or sensitive skin.

Where You Will Encounter It

Occasionally used in pharmaceutical bath preparations for skin conditions, with limited use in perfumes and aftershaves. Also employed as a flavor ingredient in vermouths and bitters.
