Dill Weed Essential Oil is steam-distilled (sometimes water-distilled) from the fresh or partially dried herb of Dill (Anethum graveolens), an annual or biennial herb in the Apiaceae family. The weed oil is produced mainly in the USA.

Extraction & Characteristics

The oil is a colourless or pale yellow mobile liquid with a powerful sweet-spicy aroma. Principal constituents include carvone (much less than in the seed oil), limonene, pinene, and terpinene, among others.

Related Species

Indian or East Indian dill (A. sowa) is widely cultivated in India and Japan. A commercial oil is produced from its seed with a different chemical composition that contains 'dill apiol'.

Folk Tradition

Dill has been used since the earliest times as both a medicinal and culinary herb. In Germany and Scandinavia it is used with fish and cucumber, and the seeds baked in bread. In the west and east it serves as a soothing digestive aid for indigestion, wind, colic, and similar complaints, especially in children, a use still current in the British Herbal Pharmacopoeia.

Therapeutic Uses

In the digestive system, dill weed oil addresses colic, dyspepsia, flatulence, and indigestion. In the genito-urinary and endocrine systems, it is used for lack of periods and to promote milk flow in nursing mothers.

Blending

Dill weed blends well with elemi, Mint Essential Oil, Caraway Essential Oil, Nutmeg Essential Oil, and other spice and citrus oils.

Safety

Non-toxic, non-irritant, and non-sensitizing.

Where You Will Encounter It

Dill weed oil is used as a fragrance component in detergents, cosmetics, perfumes, and especially soaps. Both seed and weed oils are used extensively in alcoholic and soft drinks and foodstuffs, especially pickles and condiments.

Therapeutic Applications

  • Indigestion & Flatulence: Apply via massage.
  • Amenorrhoea: Apply via massage or bath.
