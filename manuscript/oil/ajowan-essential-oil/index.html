Notes/Warnings
Possible irritant to skin and mucous membranes. Best avoided during pregnancy due to its high thymol content. Not recommended for home aromatherapy use.
Drug Interactions
Anticoagulant and antiplatelet drugs (warfarin, aspirin, heparin); may inhibit CYP2D6 and CYP3A4 (high thymol/carvacrol). Caution with diabetes medication and before major surgery.

Plant1
Ajowan

Steam distillation of the greyish-brown seeds of Ajowan yields a yellow-orange to reddish liquid with a herbaceous-spicy, medicinal scent that reads very much like thyme. Its principal constituents include thymol, pinene, cymene, dipentene, terpinene, and carvacrol, among others. Its dominant actions are powerful antiseptic and germicide, with carminative qualities as well.

Traditional Use

In Indian medicine, the tincture, essential oil, and isolated thymol have all been put to use, with cholera among the conditions traditionally treated.

Other Uses

Historically, ajowan was a major source for the isolation of thymol, though that role has largely been taken over by synthetic thymol production.
