Botanical Information

Pogostemon cablin from the Lamiaceae family, extracted from fermented leaves. Originates from India. Base note with average absorption.

Extraction & Characteristics

Patchouli Essential Oil is steam-distilled from the dried leaves of Patchouli, which are usually subjected to fermentation prior to distillation. A resinoid is also produced, mainly as a fixative. The finished oil is an amber or dark orange viscous liquid with a sweet, rich, herbaceous-earthy odour, and it improves with age. Principal constituents are patchouli alcohol (approximately 40 percent), pogostol, bulnesol, nor patchoulenol, bulnese, and patchoulene, among others.

Therapeutic Uses

Skin Care: Acne, athlete's foot, cracked and chapped skin, dandruff, dermatitis, weeping eczema, fungal infections, hair care, impetigo, insect repellent, sores, oily hair and skin, open pores, wounds, and wrinkles.

Nervous System: Frigidity, nervous exhaustion, and stress-related complaints.

Recorded actions include antidepressant, anti-inflammatory, anti-emetic, antimicrobial, antiphlogistic, antiseptic, antitoxic, antiviral, aphrodisiac, astringent, bactericidal, carminative, cicatrisant, deodorant, digestive, diuretic, febrifuge, fungicidal, nervine, prophylactic, stimulant (nervous), stomachic, and tonic.

Blending

Patchouli blends well with labdanum, Vetivert Essential Oil, Sandalwood Essential Oil, Atlas Cedarwood Essential Oil, Oakmoss Absolute, Geranium Essential Oil, Lavender Essential Oil, Rose Otto Oil, Neroli Essential Oil, Bergamot Essential Oil, cassia, Myrrh Oil, Opopanax Essential Oil, and oriental-type bases.

Safety

Non-toxic, non-irritant, and non-sensitizing.

Where You Will Encounter It

Patchouli is extensively used in cosmetic preparations and as a fixative in soaps and perfumes, especially oriental types. It is also extensively used in the food industry, in alcoholic and soft drinks, and makes a good masking agent for unpleasant tastes and smells.

Herbal Lore

Historic scent reaching back hundreds of years before psychedelic era. When textiles shipped from India to England, lush fabrics layered with patchouli leaves to protect from insects during long months at sea. Developed association with luxury, money, and beauty. Antimicrobial properties kept material from molding or rotting, which leads to protection associations.

Associated with Indian ink export. Patchouli and camphor are principal materials giving Indian ink signature scent. Makes traditional Indian ink perfect for writing money spells.
