Calamus Essential Oil is steam-distilled from the rhizome of Acorus calamus.

Extraction & Characteristics

Source: Rhizomes (and sometimes the leaves) of Acorus calamus var. angustatus

Method: Steam distillation

Note: Base

Scent: A thick, pale yellow liquid with a strong, warm, woody-spicy fragrance. Lower-quality oils carry a camphoraceous edge. Blends with cananga, cinnamon, labdanum, olibanum, patchouli, cedarwood, amyris, and other woody, spicy, or oriental bases.

Constituents: Beta-asarone (the toxic and carcinogenic constituent, present at up to 80 percent in Indian oil and as little as 6 percent in Russian oil), with calamene, calamol, calamenene, eugenol, and shyobunones.

Safety

Do not use calamus essential oil in any therapeutic context. It is classed as an oral toxin and is reported to have carcinogenic properties. The oil should not be applied to skin, ingested, diffused medicinally, or used in any home aromatherapy preparation. Calamus and its derivatives (oil, extracts, etc.) are banned from food use in many countries.

For calamus's traditional digestive, nervous, and tonic benefits, work only with the dried rhizome (preferably from a low-asarone source) under the guidance of a qualified herbalist, and never the essential oil.

Where You Will Encounter It

  • A signature note in woody, oriental, and leather perfumery, where it is used at very low concentrations.
  • A scent ingredient in traditional hair powders and tooth powders, used in the same way as orris root.
  • A trace fragrance component in some cosmetics and high-end fine fragrance.

See the Calamus plant entry for the full botanical and folk-tradition context.
