Linden
⚠️
Adulteration warning: Most commercial linden products are adulterated or synthetic. No safety data is currently available for the genuine absolute. Verify sourcing carefully and treat with caution.

Botanical Source

Tilia vulgaris of the Tiliaceae family. Synonyms include T. europaea, lime tree, common lime, lyne, tillet, and tilea. The tree is a tall, graceful specimen reaching up to 30 metres, with smooth bark, spreading branches, and bright green heart-shaped leaves. Yellowy-white flowers gather in clusters that carry a remarkably powerful scent.

Distribution

Native to Europe and the northern hemisphere. Common across England, France, and Holland.

Other Species

Several related limes share the genus, notably the broad-leaved lime (T. platyphylla) and the small-leaved lime (T. cordata).

Folk and Historical Use

Linden tea, known as tilleul, is widely drunk on the Continent (especially in France) as a general relaxant. Beyond tea, the flowers serve folk traditions for indigestion, palpitations, nausea, hysteria, and catarrhal symptoms that linger after a cold. The honey gathered from linden flowers is highly prized for both medicinal preparations and liqueurs. Culpeper described the flowers as a "good cephalic and nervine, excellent for apoplexy, epilepsy, vertigo and palpitation of the heart." Lime flowers also appear in the British Herbal Pharmacopoeia, indicated for migraine, hysteria, arteriosclerotic hypertension, and feverish colds.

Therapeutic Actions

Mild astringent, antispasmodic, bechic, carminative, cephalic, diaphoretic, diuretic, emollient, nervine, sedative, and tonic.

Extraction & Characteristics

Both a concrete and an absolute are produced by solvent extraction from the dried flowers. The concrete is a hard, brittle, dark green mass with a herbaceous, dry, haylike odour and is very rich in waxes. The absolute presents as a yellow semi-solid mass with a green-herbaceous, dry, distinctive odour.

Principal Constituents

Predominantly farnesol, with the concrete carrying an especially heavy wax load.

Safety

No safety data is currently available. Because most commercial products are adulterated or synthetic, authentic linden absolute is rarely encountered on the open market. Treat as a use-with-caution material until further data emerges.

Aromatherapy Uses

Digestive system: Cramps, indigestion, liver pains.

Nervous system: Headaches, insomnia, migraine, nervous tension, and stress-related conditions.

Other Uses

Occasionally employed in high-class perfumery.
