Cananga Essential Oil

Birch Tar Oil is produced by slow destructive distillation of the bark of Betula alba, then steam-distilled to yield the rectified oil.

Extraction & Characteristics

Source: Bark of Betula alba

Method: Slow destructive distillation produces crude birch tar (an almost black, thick oily mass). The crude tar is then steam-distilled to yield rectified birch tar oil.

Note: Base

Scent: A brownish-yellow, clear oily liquid with a smoky, tar-like, leathery aroma. Blends beautifully with woody and balsamic oils.

Constituents: Phenol, cresol, xylenol, guaiacol, creosol, pyrocatechol, and pyrobetulin (which gives that famous leather note).

Therapeutic Uses

Birch tar has a long European tradition for chronic skin complaints, including psoriasis, eczema, and stubborn dermatitis. The crude tar still appears in some pharmaceutical ointments and lotions for dermatological care, while the rectified oil is the form used in dilute home preparations.

Perfumery & Other Uses

Rectified birch tar oil is a signature note in leather-style perfumes and aftershaves, and a traditional ingredient in soap and leather manufacture. A drop in a base accord lends animalic depth, smoke, and that unmistakable Russian leather edge.

Safety

Generally non-toxic and non-sensitizing in dilute use, but the phenol content means it must always be diluted heavily and patch-tested. Avoid during pregnancy. Keep away from broken skin and mucous membranes.
