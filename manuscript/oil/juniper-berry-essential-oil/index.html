A water-white or pale yellow mobile liquid steam distilled from the berries of Juniper (Juniperus communis). The oil carries a sweet, fresh, woody-balsamic odour. Berries are sometimes fermented first as a by-product of juniper-brandy manufacture, in which case the resulting oil is considered an inferior product. A resinoid, concrete, and absolute are also produced on a small scale.

Extraction & Characteristics

Steam distilled from the berries. Principal constituents are mainly monoterpenes: pinene, myrcene, and sabinene, with limonene, cymene, terpinene, thujene, and camphene among others. Source recommends using only the berry oil, in moderation, rather than the needle and wood oil (see Juniper Needle Essential Oil).

Therapeutic Uses

For skin care, the oil is applied to acne, dermatitis, eczema, hair loss, haemorrhoids, oily complexions, as a skin toner, and to wounds. For circulation, muscles, and joints, it addresses accumulation of toxins, arteriosclerosis, cellulitis, gout, obesity, and rheumatism. Immune-system uses cover colds, flu, and infections. Genito-urinary applications include amenorrhoea, cystitis, dysmenorrhoea, and leucorrhoea. For the nervous system, the oil is used for anxiety, nervous tension, and stress-related conditions.

Blending

Blends well with Vetivert Essential Oil, Sandalwood Essential Oil, Cedar Essential Oil, Atlas Cedarwood Essential Oil, Texas Cedarwood Essential Oil, Virginian Cedarwood Essential Oil, mastic, Oakmoss Absolute, Galbanum Essential Oil, Elemi Essential Oil, Cypress Essential Oil, Clary Sage Essential Oil, Pine Essential Oil, Lavender Essential Oil, Lavandin Essential Oil, Rock Rose Essential Oil (labdanum), Fir Needle Essential Oil, Rosemary Essential Oil, Benzoin Absolute, Tolu Balsam Essential Oil, Geranium Essential Oil, and citrus oils.

Safety

Non-sensitizing and may be slightly irritating; generally non-toxic. The oil stimulates the uterine muscle and must not be used during pregnancy. It must also be avoided by those with kidney disease due to its nephrotoxic effect. Use only in moderation.

Where You Will Encounter It

Berries and extracts are used in diuretic and laxative preparations and in veterinary preventatives of ticks and fleas. Employed as a fragrance component in soaps, detergents, cosmetics, and perfumes, especially spicy fragrances and aftershaves. Extensively used in food products and in alcoholic and soft drinks; the berries are used to flavour gin.
