Turpentine Essential Oil is the rectified steam-distillate of the crude oleoresin (gum turpentine) tapped from the trunks of several Pinus species, most notably the Longleaf Pine (Pinus palustris) of the American South. The oil is a familiar, fresh, balsamic liquid long valued for its analgesic, antiseptic, and rubefacient action on aching joints, congested lungs, and infected skin.

Extraction & Characteristics

Gum turpentine is the natural oleoresin formed as a physiological product in the trunks of various Pinus, Picea, and Abies species. The essential oil is produced by steam or water distillation of this crude oleoresin and then rectified, since the raw distillate is viscous, coloured, and acidic. The finished oil is a colourless, water-white, mobile liquid with a fresh, warm-balsamic, familiar odour. American oils contain mainly alphapinene (approximately 50 percent), betapinene (25 to 35 percent), and carene (20 to 60 percent), while European oils may run as high as 95 percent alphapinene, with the precise constituent profile varying by source species and region.

Source Species

Longleaf Pine

Folk Tradition

Galen and Hippocrates were both familiar with turpentine and recorded its many applications, especially for pulmonary and genito-urinary infections, digestive complaints, and external use against rheumatic or neuralgic pain and skin conditions. In Chinese practice the oleoresin has been used both internally and externally for centuries to address excess phlegm, bronchitis, rheumatism, stiff joints, toothache, boils, sores, ringworm, and dermatitis. The rectified essence or spirit of turpentine is reckoned to be roughly four times as active as the crude oleoresin.

Therapeutic Uses

  • Skin: Boils, cuts, fleas, lice, ringworm, scabies, wounds, and as an insect repellent.
  • Circulation, Muscles, and Joints: Arthritis, gout, muscular aches and pains, rheumatism, and sciatica.
  • Respiratory System: Bronchitis, catarrh, and whooping cough.
  • Genito-Urinary System: Cystitis, leucorrhoea, and urethritis.
  • Immune System: Colds.
  • Nervous System: Neuralgia.

Safety

Turpentine is an environmental hazard and a marine pollutant. The oil is relatively non-toxic and non-irritant, but possible sensitization in some individuals has been recorded. Therapeutic use should be avoided altogether or undertaken only in moderation and in low dilution.

Where You Will Encounter It

Turpentine is incorporated into many ointments and lotions for aches and pains, and into cough and cold remedies. Neither the oil nor the oleoresin is used in fine perfumery work, though resin derivatives serve as fixative agents and feature in pine-themed and industrial perfumes. The oil is more widely recognised as a paint and stain remover, a solvent, and an insecticide, and serves as a starting material for the production of terpineol and related synthetics.
