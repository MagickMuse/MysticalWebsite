Extraction & Characteristics

Produced by dry distillation from the needles. An inferior essential oil is also produced by dry distillation from the wood chippings, used in less refined applications. Gum turpentine, distinct from this needle oil, is produced by steam distillation from the oleoresin (see the forthcoming Turpentine entry). Principal constituents are 50 to 90 percent monoterpene hydrocarbons including pinenes, carene, dipentene, limonene, terpinenes, myrcene, ocimene, camphene, and sabinene, with bornyl acetate, cineol, citral, and chamazulene among others.

Therapeutic Uses

For skin care, the oil is applied to cuts, lice, excessive perspiration, scabies, and sores. For circulation, muscles, and joints, it addresses arthritis, gout, muscular aches and pains, poor circulation, and rheumatism. For the respiratory system, it is used for asthma, bronchitis, catarrh, coughs, sinusitis, and sore throat. Genito-urinary applications include cystitis and urinary infection. For the immune system, it supports treatment of colds and influenza. For the nervous system, it eases fatigue, nervous exhaustion, stress-related conditions, and neuralgia.

Blending

Blends well with Cedar Essential Oil, Atlas Cedarwood Essential Oil, Texas Cedarwood Essential Oil, Virginian Cedarwood Essential Oil, Rosemary Essential Oil, Tea Tree Essential Oil, Sage Essential Oil, Lavender Essential Oil, Juniper Berry Essential Oil, Juniper Needle Essential Oil, Lemon Essential Oil, Niaouli Essential Oil, Eucalyptus Essential Oil, and Marjoram Essential Oil.

Safety

Non-toxic and non-irritant except in concentration. Possible sensitization in some individuals. Avoid in allergic skin conditions.

Where You Will Encounter It

Used as a fragrance component in soaps, detergents, cosmetics, and toiletries, especially bath products, and to a limited extent in perfumes. Employed as a flavour ingredient in major food products and in alcoholic and soft drinks.
