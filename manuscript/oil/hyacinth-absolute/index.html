Hyacinth Absolute is solvent-extracted from the fragrant bell-shaped flowers of Hyacinthus orientalis. The product comes first as a concrete and then as an absolute; an essential oil can also be steam-distilled from the absolute. The Greeks described the fragrance as refreshing and invigorating to a tired mind, a quality that has carried hyacinth into modern self-hypnosis work and into oriental-floral perfumery.

⚠️
Important safety notice: No formal safety data exist for hyacinth absolute. Most commercial hyacinth on the market today is adulterated or fully synthetic. The oil contains methyl eugenol and eugenol; use sparingly, avoid during pregnancy as a precaution, and source carefully.

Botanical Information

  • Scientific name: Hyacinthus orientalis
  • Family: Liliaceae
  • Synonyms: Scilla nutans, bluebell
  • Plant: A much loved cultivated plant with fragrant, bell-shaped flowers in many colours, bright lance-shaped leaves, and a round bulb.
  • Source of oil: The flowers themselves, processed by solvent extraction.
  • Evaporation: Middle to base note (rich, sweet-green floral with soft floral undertone)

Distribution

Native to Asia Minor and said to be of Syrian origin. Cultivated mainly in Holland and southern France.

Other Species and Relations

Closely related to garlic (Allium sativum), onion (Allium cepa), and the wild bluebell (H. non scriptus). At one time bluebell essential oil was produced at Grasse in the south of France; that bluebell oil was fresher and more flowery in fragrance than the cultivated hyacinth.

Extraction & Characteristics

Method: Solvent extraction yields both a concrete and an absolute from the fresh flowers. An essential oil is also obtained by steam distillation from the absolute.

Appearance and scent: A reddish or greeny-brown viscous liquid carrying a sweet-green floral fragrance with a soft floral undertone.

Principal Constituents

Phenylethyl alcohol, benzaldehyde, cinnamaldehyde, benzyl alcohol, benzoic acid, benzyl acetate, benzyl benzoate, eugenol, methyl eugenol, and hydroquinone, among many others.

Safety

No safety data available at present. Most commercial hyacinth oil is adulterated or fully synthetic, so therapeutic use must be approached with caution. The natural absolute carries methyl eugenol and eugenol, which warrants restraint in dose and frequency. Avoid during pregnancy as a precaution. Not recommended for internal use.

Therapeutic Actions

Antiseptic, balsamic, hypnotic, sedative, and styptic.

Folk and Historical Use

The wild bluebell bulbs are themselves poisonous, yet the white juice of the bulb was once used as a substitute for starch or glue. The dried and powdered roots have been described as balsamic, with styptic properties that have not been fully investigated.

Aromatherapy Uses

  • Nervous system: stress-related conditions, mental fatigue, and tired-mind states. The Greeks recorded the fragrance as refreshing and invigorating to a weary mind. The oil has also been used in self-hypnosis techniques and in work intended to develop the creative right-hand side of the brain.

Source-Named Blending Partners

Narcissus Oil, Violet Leaf Absolute, Ylang Ylang Essential Oil, Levant Storax Essential Oil, Galbanum Essential Oil, Jasmine Absolute, Neroli Essential Oil, plus oriental-type perfume bases.

Other Uses

Used in high-class perfumery, especially oriental and floral compositions.

Therapeutic Applications

  • Nervous Exhaustion: Apply via massage, bath, or vaporization.
  • Nervous Tension and Stress: Apply via massage, bath, or vaporization.

Traditional Grimoire Notes

A very restful vibration. When worn, it gives peace and rest from anxiety. In a sachet bag under your pillow, it promotes sleep as well as psychic dreams.
