Guaiacwood Essential Oil

Amyris essential oil is steam-distilled from the broken-up wood and branches of Amyris. The wood is best seasoned before distillation and gives a very plentiful yield. The finished oil is pale yellow and slightly viscous, with a musty, faintly woody scent that fades quickly from the skin. It blends well with lavandin, citronella, oakmoss, sassafras, cedarwood, and other wood oils. Its principal constituents are caryophyllene, cadinene, and cadinol.

Aromatherapy Use

Amyris is used in aromatherapy as a perfume oil rather than for therapeutic indications. Its actions are described as antiseptic, balsamic, and sedative, but home and clinical applications are limited to fragrance work.

Other Uses

Amyris is most often employed as an inexpensive substitute for East Indian sandalwood in perfumery and cosmetics, though it lacks the same rich tenacity. Its chief application is as a fixative in soaps, with limited use in flavouring work, particularly liqueurs.
