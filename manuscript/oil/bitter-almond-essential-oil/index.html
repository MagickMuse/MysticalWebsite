Bitter Almond Essential Oil

Notes/Warnings
Contains prussic acid, also called hydrocyanic acid or cyanide, a well-known poison. Benzaldehyde is also moderately toxic. Not safe for aromatherapy or home use, internally or externally, even after rectification.

Do not use therapeutically — on Tisserand & Young's avoid list. Unrectified oil contains hydrogen cyanide; even rectified oil is contraindicated for therapeutic use.

Almond

Bitter almond essential oil is steam-distilled from the kernels of the bitter variety of Almond. The nuts are pressed and macerated in warm water for twelve to twenty-four hours before distillation, and prussic acid forms during that maceration. The raw seed contains none. Most commercial bitter almond oil is rectified to remove the acid and is labelled FFPA, free from prussic acid. The rectified product is a light, colourless liquid carrying the characteristic marzipan scent that gives the oil its commercial value.

Its principal constituents are benzaldehyde at around 95 per cent and prussic acid at around 3 per cent. Prussic acid, also called hydrocyanic acid, is cyanide. Benzaldehyde is itself moderately toxic. For these reasons, the oil is not suitable for therapeutic use in any form, internal or external, even after rectification.

Other Uses

Bitter almond oil is no longer used in internal medicine. The rectified version flavours foods, mostly confectionery, where almond essence and marzipan are the most common applications. In food flavouring it is increasingly being replaced by synthetic benzaldehyde.
