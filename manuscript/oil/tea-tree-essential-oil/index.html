Tea tree is a workhorse oil in any household kit, unusual in that it is active against bacteria, fungi, and viruses, and it acts as a powerful immuno-stimulant so the body responds more readily when threatened by any of these.

Other Names & Related Species

Known as narrow-leaved paperbark tea tree, ti-tree, ti-trol, and melasol. Tea tree is a general name for members of the Melaleuca family, which exists in many physiological forms including Cajuput, Niaouli, M. bracteata, and M. linariifolia, among others.

Origin & Distribution

Native to Australia. Other varieties have been cultivated elsewhere, but M. alternifolia is not produced outside Australia, mainly in New South Wales.

Folk Tradition

The name derives from local use of the leaves as a type of herbal tea. Present knowledge of the plant's properties rests on a very long history of use by Aboriginal Australians, who recognised and applied its medicinal qualities long before Western science took an interest. Modern research has since confirmed two key findings: the oil is active against bacteria, fungi, and viruses, and it is a powerful immuno-stimulant, increasing the body's ability to respond when threatened by any of these organisms.

Extraction & Characteristics

The essential oil is produced by steam or water distillation from the leaves and twigs. The liquid is pale yellowy-green or water-white and mobile, with a warm, fresh, spicy-camphoraceous odour. Principal constituents include terpinene-4-ol up to 30 percent, with cineol, pinene, terpinenes, cymene, sesquiterpenes, and sesquiterpene alcohols among others. Reported actions include anti-infectious, anti-inflammatory, antiseptic, antiviral, bactericidal, balsamic, cicatrisant, diaphoretic, expectorant, fungicidal, immuno-stimulant, parasiticide, and vulnerary.

Therapeutic Uses

  • Skin care: abscess, acne, athlete's foot, blisters, burns, cold sores, dandruff, herpes, insect bites, oily skin, rashes including nappy rash, spots, verrucae, warts, and infected wounds.
  • Respiratory system: asthma, bronchitis, catarrh, coughs, sinusitis, tuberculosis, whooping cough.
  • Genito-urinary system: thrush, vaginitis, cystitis, pruritis.
  • Immune system: colds, fever, flu, and infectious illnesses such as chickenpox.

Blending

Tea tree blends well with lavandin, lavender, clary sage, rosemary, oakmoss, pine, cananga, geranium, marjoram, and spice oils, especially clove and nutmeg.

Safety

Non-toxic and non-irritant, but possible sensitization in some individuals. Patch test before topical use, and dilute appropriately for skin application.

Where You Will Encounter It

Used in soaps, toothpastes, deodorants, disinfectants, gargles, and germicides, and increasingly in aftershaves and spicy colognes.
