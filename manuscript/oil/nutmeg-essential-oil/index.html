Botanical Information

Myristica fragrans from the Myristicaceae family, extracted from fruit and seeds. Originates from Indonesia. Middle note with fast absorption.

Extraction & Characteristics

Nutmeg Essential Oil is steam-distilled (or water-distilled) from the dried, worm-eaten nutmeg seed of Nutmeg; the worms eat away the starch and fat content, leaving the aromatic kernel. The finished oil is a water-white or pale yellow mobile liquid with a sweet, warm-spicy odour and a terpeney top-note. Its principal constituents are mainly monoterpene hydrocarbons (approximately 88 percent), including camphene, pinene, dipentene, sabinene, and cymene, with lesser amounts of geraniol, borneol, linalol, terpineol, myristicin (4 to 8 percent), safrol, and elemincin, among others.

Therapeutic Uses

Circulation, Muscles, and Joints: Arthritis, gout, muscular aches and pains, poor circulation, and rheumatism.

Digestive System: Flatulence, indigestion, nausea, and sluggish digestion.

Immune System: Bacterial infection.

Nervous System: Frigidity, impotence, neuralgia, and nervous fatigue.

Recorded actions include analgesic, anti-emetic, anti-oxidant, antirheumatic, antiseptic, antispasmodic, aphrodisiac, carminative, digestive, emmenagogue, gastric secretory stimulant, larvicidal, orexigenic, prostaglandin inhibitor, stimulant, and tonic. Nutmeg is current in the British Herbal Pharmacopoeia for flatulent dyspepsia, nausea, diarrhoea, dysentery, and topically for rheumatism.

Blending

Nutmeg blends well with Oakmoss Absolute, Lavandin Essential Oil, Bay Laurel Essential Oil, Peru Balsam Essential Oil, Orange Essential Oil, Geranium Essential Oil, Untitled, Rosemary Essential Oil, Lime Essential Oil, Petitgrain Essential Oil, Untitled, Coriander Seed Essential Oil, Untitled, and other spice oils.

Safety

Generally non-toxic, non-irritant, and non-sensitizing. In large doses, however, nutmeg shows signs of toxicity such as nausea, stupor, and tachycardia, believed to be due to the myristicin content. Large quantities are hallucinogenic and excitant to the motor cortex. Nutmeg, especially the West Indian type, is probably safer to use than mace. Use in moderation, and with care in pregnancy.

Where You Will Encounter It

Nutmeg Essential Oil is used as a flavouring agent in pharmaceuticals, especially analgesic and tonic preparations. It is used in soaps, lotions, detergents, cosmetics, and perfumes, and in most major food categories, including alcoholic and soft drinks.

Herbal Lore

Used far longer than recorded history. Earliest records state nutmeg already used for hundreds of years in embalming practices in ancient Egypt by time recipes were written down. During Middle Ages, mixed with fatty ointment as cure for diarrhea, nausea, muscle cramps, and digestive issues.

As with all warming herbs, conflated with magick of love, sex, and fidelity.
