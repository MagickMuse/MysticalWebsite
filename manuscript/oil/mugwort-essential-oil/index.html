⚠️
Strict safety warning. The essential oil of Mugwort (also sold as Armoise) is an oral toxin due to high thujone content and is abortifacient. The source explicitly states that it should not be used in therapy either internally or externally. This oil is restricted to industrial perfumery and fragrance applications. For magickal and herbal work with the plant, use dried herb, infused oil, or the flower essence remedy instead.

A colourless or pale yellow liquid steam distilled from the leaves and flowering tops of Mugwort (Artemisia vulgaris). The oil is also known commercially as Armoise. It carries a powerful camphoraceous, bitter-sweet, herbaceous odour and is produced in southern France, Morocco, Germany, Hungary, India, China, and Japan.

Extraction & Characteristics

Steam distilled from the leaves and flowering tops. Principal constituents are thujone, cineol, pinenes, and dihydromatricaria ester among others. Thujone is the dominant active and is responsible for the oil's neurotoxicity in concentrated form.

Therapeutic Uses

The source provides no aromatherapy or home-use indications for mugwort oil and explicitly forbids therapeutic use, internal or external. For the plant's traditional womb-tonic, dream-work, protective, and moxibustion applications, see the Mugwort plant entry, which describes safe forms (dried herb, infused oil, and flower essence remedy).

Blending

Despite its therapeutic restriction, the oil is widely used in perfumery and blends well with Oakmoss Absolute, Patchouli Essential Oil, Rosemary Essential Oil, Lavandin Essential Oil, Pine Essential Oil, Sage Essential Oil, Clary Sage Essential Oil, Cedar Essential Oil, Atlas Cedarwood Essential Oil, Texas Cedarwood Essential Oil, and Virginian Cedarwood Essential Oil. These blends are intended for fragrance use only.

Safety

Oral toxin due to high thujone content. Abortifacient. The source states explicitly that it should not be used in therapy either internally or externally. Avoid all skin contact and inhalation in concentrated form. Avoid completely during pregnancy and while nursing.

Where You Will Encounter It

Used as a fragrance component in soaps, colognes, and perfumes. Limited use in flavouring due to toxic levels of thujone.
