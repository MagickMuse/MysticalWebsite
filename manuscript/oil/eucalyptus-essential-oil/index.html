Eucalyptus Essential Oil is steam-distilled from the leaves of various eucalyptus species, creating a camphoraceous, cool oil with powerful cleansing and healing properties. Known as "fever tree" by aboriginal cultures of Australia, eucalyptus has been used for respiratory support, spiritual purification, and energetic protection for generations.

Botanical Information

Eucalyptus globulus (most common, plus over 450 varieties) of the Myrtaceae family. Origin: Australia. Source of oil: leaves. Top note.

  • Eucalyptus globulus (Blue Gum): Most common in aromatherapy, best for respiratory ailments. Often rectified (double-distilled) for increased eucalyptol content.
  • Eucalyptus radiata (Narrow-leaved Peppermint): Lower 1,8 cineole content makes it safest for children. Supports lymph function.
  • Eucalyptus citriodora (Lemon Eucalyptus): Aldehyde-rich, excellent bug spray and anti-inflammatory when blended with helichrysum.
  • Eucalyptus staigeriana (Lemon Ironbark): Citrus-scented disinfectant, kills airborne bacteria.
  • Eucalyptus dives (Peppermint Eucalyptus): High eucalyptol content, warming, beneficial for respiratory system.
  • Eucalyptus polybractea (Blue-leaved Mallee): Similar to E. globulus but stronger; less common but worth the higher cost.

Scent Profile

Camphoraceous, cool, herbal, minty. Promotes wellness and purification.

Application

Diffuse (1 drop per 100ml water), direct inhalation from tissue

Physical Uses

Medicinal applications: Respiratory ailments, cold and flu prevention, asthma support (adults only), anxiety relief, mental clarity, fatigue reduction.

Cautions: Do not use on children under six years old; oils rich in 1,8 cineole can cause respiratory failure in young children. Use with caution on children under twelve. Relaxes muscles vital for breath but can cause suffocation. Common allergen and sensitizing essential oil. Not recommended for aroma necklaces as it can sensitize delicate skin or overwhelm nasal passages.

Professional supervision recommended for both bay laurel and eucalyptus as potentially sensitizing oils. Blends well with grapefruit oils, bay laurel (Laurus nobilis), and helichrysum.

Herbal Lore

Eucalyptus is known as "fever tree" by the aboriginal cultures of Australia. This varied member of the myrtle family has over 450 plants in the genera Eucalyptus, though only seven to ten are commonly used in aromatherapy. Each eucalyptus species performs slightly different functions, and different scents can affect their magickal properties.

Eucalyptus radiata helps with lymph function and can facilitate lymph drainage with proper use by a trained massage therapist. Eucalyptus globulus is the most affordable and commonly suggested for respiratory synergies. Eucalyptus citriodora makes an excellent bug spray and anti-inflammatory blend. Eucalyptus staigeriana works as a powerful disinfectant for killing airborne bacteria during cold and flu season.

Blue Gum (Eucalyptus globulus var. globulus): Source Detail

The Blue Gum is the most widely cultivated eucalyptus and the primary commercial source of "eucalyptus oil" worldwide.

Gum Tree, Southern Blue Gum, Tasmanian Blue Gum, Fever Tree, Stringy Bark.

Native to Tasmania and Australia. Most commercial production has shifted to Spain and Portugal, with additional cultivation in Brazil, California, Russia, and China. Very little oil now comes from its native range.

Steam-distilled from fresh or partially dried leaves and young twigs. The oil is a colourless mobile liquid (yellows on ageing) with a somewhat harsh camphoraceous odour and a woody-sweet undertone.

💡
On variety choice: The narrow-leaved eucalyptus (E. radiata var. australiana) is often preferred over Blue Gum in aromatherapy work, being similarly rich in cineol but with a sweeter and less harsh odour.

Cineol (70 to 85 per cent), pinene, limonene, cymene, phellandrene, terpinene, aromadendrene, and others.

Externally classed as non-toxic, non-irritant in dilution, and non-sensitising. Internally, eucalyptus oil is toxic; as little as 3.5 ml has been reported as fatal. Never ingest. Other sources classify eucalyptus as a common allergen and potentially sensitising, so patch-test before topical use.

Analgesic, antineuralgic, antirheumatic, antiseptic, antispasmodic, antiviral, balsamic, cicatrisant, decongestant, deodorant, depurative, diuretic, expectorant, febrifuge, hypoglycaemic, parasiticide, prophylactic, rubefacient, stimulant, vermifuge, vulnerary.

  • Skin care: burns, blisters, cuts, herpes, insect bites, insect repellent, lice, skin infections, wounds.
  • Circulation, muscles, and joints: muscular aches and pains, poor circulation, rheumatoid arthritis, sprains.
  • Respiratory system: asthma, bronchitis, catarrh, coughs, sinusitis, throat infections.
  • Genito-urinary system: cystitis, leucorrhoea.
  • Immune system: chickenpox, colds, epidemics, flu, measles.
  • Nervous system: debility, headaches, neuralgia.

Common Thyme Essential Oil, Rosemary Essential Oil, Lavender Essential Oil, Marjoram Essential Oil, Pine Essential Oil, Lemon Essential Oil, and the cedarwoods (Atlas Cedarwood Essential Oil, Texas Cedarwood Essential Oil, and Virginian Cedarwood Essential Oil).

The source groups the 500-plus essential-oil-producing eucalyptus species into three categories:

  • Medicinal (cineol-rich): Blue Gum (E. globulus), Blue Mallee (E. polybractea), Narrow-Leaved Peppermint (E. radiata var. australiana), Gully Gum (E. smithii).
  • Industrial (piperitone- and phellandrene-rich): Peppermint Eucalyptus (E. piperita), Grey Peppermint (E. radiata var. phellandra), Broad-Leaved Peppermint (E. dives var. Type).
  • Perfumery (citronellal-rich): Lemon-Scented Eucalyptus (E. citriodora).

The oil and isolated cineol are widely used in liniments, inhalants, cough syrups, ointments, toothpastes, and pharmaceutical flavourings, and feature in veterinary practice and dentistry. The oil also serves as a fragrance component in soaps, detergents, and toiletries (little used in fine perfumery), as a source for cineol isolation, and as a flavour ingredient across most major food categories.
