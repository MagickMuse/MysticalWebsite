Hemlock spruce yields a fresh, sweet-fruity coniferous oil long used for respiratory care, muscular ease, and grounding work in meditation and yoga.

Other Names & Related Species

Known as eastern hemlock, common hemlock, hemlock spruce, fir needle oil, and simply spruce oil, with botanical synonyms Pinus canadensis and Abies canadensis. This is the coniferous hemlock spruce (Tsuga canadensis, Pinaceae). It is not the poisonous hemlock (Conium maculatum, Apiaceae) used to execute Socrates; the two share a common name but are entirely unrelated. Numerous cultivars of Tsuga canadensis exist, and the oil is often produced from a mixture of types. Similar oils sold simply as spruce oil come from black spruce (Picea nigra or P. mariana), Norway spruce (Spruce), and white or Canadian spruce (P. glauca). The oil from western hemlock (Tsuga heterophylla) has quite different constituents. Hemlock spruce is also closely related to Douglas fir (Pseudotsuga taxifolia), which yields its own essential oil and balsam.

Origin & Distribution

Native to the west coast of the United States. The oil is produced in Vermont, New York, New Hampshire, Virginia, and Wisconsin.

Folk Tradition

The bark contains tannins, resin, and volatile oil. It is listed in the British Herbal Pharmacopoeia for diarrhoea, cystitis, mucous colitis, leucorrhoea, uterine prolapse, pharyngitis, stomatitis, and gingivitis. An extract of the bark is also used in the tanning industry.

Extraction & Characteristics

The essential oil is produced by steam distillation from the needles and twigs. The liquid is colourless or pale yellow with a pleasing, fresh-balsamic, sweet-fruity odour. Principal constituents include pinenes, limonene, bornyl acetate, tricyclene, phellandrene, myrcene, thujone, dipentene, and cadinene. Composition varies with source and exact botanical species, which are sometimes mixed. Reported actions include antimicrobial, antiseptic, antitussive, astringent, diaphoretic, diuretic, expectorant, nervine, rubefacient, and tonic.

Therapeutic Uses

  • Circulation, muscles, and joints: muscular aches and pains, poor circulation, rheumatism.
  • Respiratory system: asthma, bronchitis, coughs, respiratory weakness.
  • Immune system: colds, flu, infections.
  • Nervous system: anxiety and stress-related conditions; described as opening and elevating though grounding, and excellent for yoga and meditation.

Blending

Hemlock spruce blends well with pine, oakmoss, cedarwood, galbanum, benzoin, lavender, lavandin, and rosemary.

Safety

Non-toxic, non-irritant, and non-sensitizing.

Where You Will Encounter It

Used in veterinary liniments and extensively in room spray perfumes, household detergents, soaps, bath preparations, and toiletries, especially in the United States.
