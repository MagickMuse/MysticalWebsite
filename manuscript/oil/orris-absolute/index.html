Orris Absolute is the high-perfumery aromatic of Orris (Iris pallida), the eastern Mediterranean iris whose rhizomes must be aged for at least three years before they yield any scent. The aromatic has no aromatherapy or home use; it is reserved for fine perfumery and as a dry powder for cosmetics and linens.

Safety

No aromatherapy or home use is recorded for orris oil or absolute. The fresh root causes nausea and vomiting in large doses. The oil and absolute are much adulterated or synthetic, and true orris absolute is three times the price of jasmine.

Extraction & Characteristics

Orris is rendered in three aromatic forms, all from rhizomes that have been peeled, washed, dried, pulverized, and stored for a minimum of three years (without this aging the rhizomes have virtually no scent). The essential oil, often called a concrete, is produced by steam distillation and solidifies at room temperature to a cream-coloured mass with a woody, violetlike scent and a soft, floral-fruity undertone. The absolute is produced by alkali washing in ethyl ether solution to remove the myristic acid from the concrete; it is a water-white or pale yellow oily liquid with a delicate, sweet, floral-woody odour. The resin or resinoid is produced by alcohol extraction from the peeled rhizomes and is a brown or dark orange viscous mass with a deep, woody-sweet, tobacco-like, very tenacious scent. Principal constituents are myristic acid (the odourless substance, 85 to 90 percent, that makes the oil solid), alpha-irone, and oleic acid.

Blending

Orris blends well with Atlas Cedarwood Essential Oil, Sandalwood Essential Oil, Vetivert Essential Oil, Cypress Essential Oil, mimosa, labdanum, Bergamot Essential Oil, Untitled, Rose Otto Oil, Violet Leaf Absolute, and other florals.

Where You Will Encounter It

The absolute and concrete oil are reserved for high-class perfumery work, and the resin is used in soaps, colognes, and perfumes. Powdered orris is a common article and may be used as a dry shampoo, a body powder, a fixative for pot pourris, and to scent linen. The powder is also used to scent dentifrices and toothpowders. Orris is occasionally used on the Continent in confectionery and fruit flavourings.

Traditional Grimoire Notes

Aids concentration and creative endeavors, and is said to strengthen determination and desire. Also commonly referred to as iris.
