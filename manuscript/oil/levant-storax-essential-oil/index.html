Levant Storax Essential Oil, also called Styrax, is steam-distilled from the balsamic exudate of Storax (Liquidambar orientalis), the oriental sweetgum tree of Asia Minor. The oil shares similar qualities with Benzoin Absolute, which is produced from the related Styrax benzoin.

Extraction & Characteristics

The essential oil is produced by steam distillation from the crude balsam, which is itself a pathological secretion induced by pounding the bark of the tree. A resinoid and an absolute are also produced from the crude by solvent extraction. The finished oil is a water-white or pale yellow liquid with a sweet-balsamic, rich, tenacious odour. Principal constituents are mainly styrene, with vanillin, phenylpropyl alcohol, cinnamic alcohol, benzyl alcohol, and ethyl alcohol, among others.

Therapeutic Uses

Storax is recorded as anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, antiseptic, antitussive, bactericidal, balsamic, expectorant, nervine, and stimulant. In aromatherapy and home use, the oil is applied to skin care for cuts, ringworm, scabies, and wounds; to the respiratory system for bronchitis, catarrh, and coughs; and to the nervous system for anxiety and stress-related conditions.

Blending

The oil blends well with Ylang Ylang Essential Oil, Jasmine Absolute, Mimosa Absolute, Rose Oil, Lavender Essential Oil, Carnation Oil, Violet Leaf Absolute, Cassie Absolute, and spice oils.

Safety

Storax is non-toxic and non-irritant, with possible sensitization in some individuals. The oil is frequently adulterated; low-priced storax oil is usually an imitation rather than the genuine product.

Where You Will Encounter It

Storax is used in compound benzoin tincture, mainly for respiratory conditions. The oil and resinoid are used as fixatives and fragrance components mainly in soaps, floral and oriental perfumes. The resinoid and absolute are used in most major food categories, including alcoholic and soft drinks.

Magickal Uses

Storax has a long history in sacred perfumery. Its resinous depth adds gravitas to ritual blends.

Traditionally used in magickal and religious incenses for its grounding, centering properties.

Substitution

Substitute Benzoin essential oil for storax. While this does not duplicate the fragrance exactly, it can be used in magickal formulas calling for storax. Benzoin Siam is related to storax and makes the closest substitute.

Therapeutic Applications

  • Anxiety: Apply via massage, bath, or vaporization.
  • Nervous Tension and Stress: Apply via massage, bath, or vaporization.
  • Chronic Coughs: Apply via massage, vaporizer/diffuser, or steam inhalation.
  • Catarrh: Apply via massage, vaporizer/diffuser, or gentle inhalation.
  • Bronchitis: Apply via massage, vaporizer/diffuser, or gentle inhalation.
  • Wounds: Apply via skin oil/lotion, compress, or bath.
  • Ringworm: Apply via skin oil/lotion or hair care.
  • Scabies: Apply via skin oil/lotion.

Traditional Grimoire Notes

Storax gum (also known as stacte) has a cloyingly sweet, balsamic scent. One of the oldest sacred incense ingredients, long used in Catholic and Orthodox church incenses. Substitute benzoin siam when storax is unavailable, as they are botanically related. Best fumed on charcoal to release its full aromatic richness.
