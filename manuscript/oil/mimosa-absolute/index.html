Botanical Source

Mimosa (Acacia dealbata) of the Mimosaceae family, also classified as A. decurrens var. dealbata and known as Sydney black wattle. The plant is an attractive small tree reaching about 12 metres, bearing greyish-brown bark with irregular longitudinal ridges, delicate foliage, and clusters of ball-shaped fragrant yellow flowers.

Distribution

Native to Australia and naturalized across North and Central Africa. Brought to Europe as an ornamental plant in the early nineteenth century, it now grows wild on the continent. Concrete and absolute production centres on southern France, with additional output from Italy.

Other Species

The genus Acacia contains many close relatives. The East African type (A. arabica) is very similar in character. The florist-shop "mimosa" is A. floribunda. Brazilian mimosa or sensitive plant (Mimosa humilis) yields a homoeopathic tincture used for swelling of the ankles. Mimosa is also closely related to cassie.

Folk and Historical Use

Mimosa bark, called "wattle bark," carries a leather-like odour and astringent taste, with up to 42 percent tannins along with gallic acid. The tanning industry relies on it heavily. Medicinally it is used in ways parallel to oak bark, particularly as a specific for diarrhoea and as an astringent gargle and ointment. Black catechu (A. catechu), a related extract, appears in the British Herbal Pharmacopoeia as a specific for chronic diarrhoea with colitis.

Therapeutic Actions

Antiseptic and astringent.

Extraction & Characteristics

A concrete and an absolute are produced by solvent extraction from the flowers and twig ends. The concrete is a hard wax-like yellow mass with a sweet-woody, deep floral fragrance. The absolute is an amber-coloured viscous liquid carrying a slightly green, woody-floral scent.

Principal Constituents

Predominantly hydrocarbons, with palmic aldehyde, enanthic acid, anisic acid, acetic acid, and phenols among the named contributors.

Safety

Non-toxic, non-irritant, and non-sensitizing.

Aromatherapy Uses

Skin care: Oily, sensitive, and general skin care.

Nervous system: Anxiety, nervous tension, over-sensitivity, and stress.

Source-Named Blending Partners

Blends well with floral and spice oils generally. Specifically named partners include Lavandin Essential Oil, Lavender Essential Oil, Ylang Ylang Essential Oil, Violet Leaf Absolute, Levant Storax Essential Oil, Citronella Essential Oil, Peru Balsam Essential Oil, and Cassie Absolute.

Other Uses

Used extensively in soaps for its excellent fixative properties. Also features in high-class perfumery, particularly colognes and floral and oriental compositions.
