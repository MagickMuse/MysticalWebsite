Petitgrain Essential Oil

A dark yellow or brownish-yellow mobile liquid produced by cold expression (hand or machine pressing) from the outer peel of the almost ripe fruit of Bitter Orange (Citrus aurantium var. amara). The oil carries a fresh, dry, almost floral odour with a rich, sweet undertone. Trade-named bigarade.

Extraction & Characteristics

Cold expressed from the outer peel of the almost ripe fruit. A terpeneless oil version is also produced for industrial use. Principal constituents are over 90 percent monoterpenes, mainly limonene, myrcene, camphene, pinene, ocimene, and cymene, with small amounts of alcohols, aldehydes, and ketones. The leaves of this same tree yield Petitgrain Essential Oil; the blossoms yield Neroli Essential Oil.

Therapeutic Uses

The source defers full aromatherapy and home-use applications to Orange Essential Oil (sweet orange), which shares the broad citrus profile of mood support, antianxiety, and digestive comfort. Bitter orange's own actions are anti-inflammatory, antiseptic, astringent, bactericidal, carminative, choleretic, fungicidal, mildly sedative, stomachic, and tonic, making it especially suited to digestive and skincare blends.

Folk tradition records strengthening of the heart, support during palpitation, scurvy, jaundice, bleedings, heartburn, and relaxed throat (the herb is described as powerfully anti-scorbutic). The dried bitter orange peel is used as a tonic and carminative for dyspepsia. Chinese medicine uses the dried fruit and peel for prolapse of the uterus and anus, diarrhoea, and blood in the faeces.

Safety

Phototoxic; do not apply before sun exposure. Otherwise generally non-toxic, non-irritant, and non-sensitizing. Limonene has been reported to cause contact dermatitis in some individuals. Avoid internal use by children.

Where You Will Encounter It

Used in certain stomachic, laxative, and carminative preparations. Employed as a fragrance component in soaps, detergents, cosmetics, colognes, and perfumes. Extensively used as a flavouring material, especially in liqueurs and soft drinks. Also used as a starting material for the isolation of natural limonene.
