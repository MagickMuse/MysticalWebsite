Botanical Source

Common oregano (Origanum vulgare) of the Lamiaceae (Labiatae) family. Also called European oregano, wild marjoram, common marjoram, grove marjoram, joy of the mountain, and origanum (as the oil). The plant is a hardy, bushy perennial herb growing up to 90 centimetres high, with an erect hairy stem, dark green ovate leaves, and pinky-purple flowers. A common garden plant releasing a strong aroma when its leaves are bruised.

Distribution

Native to Europe and now cultivated worldwide, including the USA, India, and South America. Oil production centres on the former USSR, Bulgaria, and Italy.

Other Species

The botanical classification of the marjoram and oregano species is famously tangled, with over thirty varieties recognised, several of which yield essential oils:

  • Winter or Greek marjoram (O. heracleoticum)
  • African oregano (O. glandulosum)
  • Moroccan oregano (O. virens)
  • Mexican oregano (Lippia graveolens or L. palmeri)
  • Syrian oregano (O. maru)
  • Spanish oregano (Thymus capitatus), the source of most commercial "oregano oil"

See also entries on Spanish oregano and sweet marjoram.

Folk and Historical Use

The "true" oregano of the herb garden carries a very ancient medical reputation. Traditional uses include digestive upsets, respiratory complaints (asthma, bronchitis, coughs), colds and flu, and inflammations of the mouth and throat. In China it is also used for fever, vomiting, diarrhoea, jaundice, and itchy skin conditions. The diluted oil has been applied externally in herbal medicine for headaches, rheumatism, general aches and pains, and to stings and bites.

Therapeutic Actions

Analgesic, anthelmintic, antirheumatic, antiseptic, antispasmodic, antitoxic, antiviral, bactericidal, carminative, choleretic, cytophylactic, diaphoretic, diuretic, emmenagogue, expectorant, febrifuge, fungicidal, parasiticide, rubefacient, stimulant, and tonic.

Extraction & Characteristics

The essential oil is steam-distilled from the dried flowering herb. It presents as a pale yellow liquid that browns with age, carrying a warm, spicy-herbaceous, camphoraceous odour.

Principal Constituents

Carvacrol, thymol, cymene, caryophyllene, pinene, bisabolene, linalol, borneol, geranyl acetate, linalyl acetate, and terpinene. Constituents vary widely with source; oils classified as "oregano" or "oreganum" share thymol and/or carvacrol as their major components.

Safety

⚠️
Dermal toxin, skin irritant, and mucous membrane irritant. Should not be used on the skin at all. Avoid during pregnancy.

Aromatherapy Uses

None. The oil is not employed in aromatherapy or home use.

Blending Partners

Noted as a fragrance component compatible with Lavandin Essential Oil, Oakmoss Absolute, Pine Essential Oil, spike lavender, Citronella Essential Oil, Rosemary Essential Oil, camphor, and cedarwood.

Other Uses

A fragrance component in soaps, colognes, and perfumes, especially men's fragrances. Used to a moderate extent as a flavouring agent, mainly in meat products and pizzas.
