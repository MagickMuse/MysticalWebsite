◇Notes/Warnings
Moderately toxic and irritant. Myristicin (the dominant constituent of the herb oil, up to 85 percent) has been shown to have toxic properties, and apiol has been shown to have irritant properties. The herb oil's high myristicin content makes it the more toxic of the two parsley oils. Otherwise non-sensitizing. Use in moderation and well diluted. Avoid during pregnancy.

A pale yellow or greenish mobile liquid steam distilled from the leaves and stalks of Parsley (Petroselinum sativum). The oil carries a heavy, warm, spicy-sweet odour reminiscent of the fresh herb itself.

Extraction & Characteristics

Steam distilled from the herb (fresh leaves and stalks). Principal constituent is myristicin, present at up to 85 percent, with phellandrene, myrcene, apiol, terpinolene, menthatriene, pinene, and carotel among others. The very high myristicin content makes the herb oil more chemically potent and more toxic than the seed oil.

Therapeutic Uses

For circulation, muscles, and joints, the oil is applied to accumulation of toxins, arthritis, broken blood vessels, cellulitis, rheumatism, and sciatica. For the digestive system, it addresses colic, flatulence, indigestion, and haemorrhoids. Genito-urinary applications include amenorrhoea, dysmenorrhoea, aid in labour, cystitis, and urinary infection. Folk tradition also records the plant's use for stimulating hair growth and helping to eliminate head lice.

Blending

Blends well with Rose Oil, Neroli Essential Oil (orange blossom), Cananga Essential Oil, Tea Tree Essential Oil, Oakmoss Absolute, Clary Sage Essential Oil, and spice oils.

Safety

Moderately toxic and irritant. Myristicin (the dominant constituent) is toxic in concentrated form; apiol is irritant. Non-sensitizing. Use in moderation and well diluted. Avoid during pregnancy. The herb oil is the more toxic of the two parsley essential oils.

Where You Will Encounter It

Used extensively as a flavouring material in meats, pickles, sauces, and beverages, often alongside the herb itself and the seed oleoresin. Less commonly used in perfumery than the seed oil.

Therapeutic Applications

  • Hemorrhoids: Apply via skin oil/lotion, compress, or bath.
  • Lice: Apply via skin oil/lotion or hair care.
