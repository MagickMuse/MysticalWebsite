Turmeric oil is a yellowy-orange liquid distilled from the cured rhizome of Curcuma longa, the same root that gives curry its colour. It is potent enough to require care, in low dilutions and over a limited period, but useful for inflammation, sluggish digestion, and aching joints.

Other Names & Related Species

Known as curcuma, Indian saffron, Indian yellow root, and curmuma oil, with botanical synonyms Curcuma domestica and Amomum curcuma. Closely related to common Ginger. Not to be confused with the Indian turmeric or American yellow root (Hydrastis canadensis), which is a wholly unrelated plant.

Origin & Distribution

Native to southern Asia. Turmeric is extensively cultivated in India, China, Indonesia, Jamaica, and Haiti. The oil is mainly distilled in India, China, and Japan, and some roots are imported to Europe and the United States for distillation there.

Folk Tradition

A common household spice, especially in curry powder, turmeric is high in minerals and vitamins, especially vitamin C, and is widely used as a local home medicine. In Chinese herbalism, it is used for bruises, sores, ringworm, toothache, chest pains, colic, and menstrual problems, usually in combination with other remedies. It was once used as a cure for jaundice.

Extraction & Characteristics

The essential oil is produced by steam distillation from the cured rhizome, which is first boiled, cleaned, and sun-dried. An oleoresin, absolute, and concrete are also produced by solvent extraction. The liquid is yellowy-orange with a faint blue fluorescence and a fresh, spicy-woody odour. Principal constituents are tumerone at around 60 percent, with ar-tumerone, atlantones, zingiberene, cineol, borneol, sabinene, and phellandrene among others. Reported actions include analgesic, anti-arthritic, anti-inflammatory, anti-oxidant, bactericidal, cholagogue, digestive, diuretic, hypotensive, insecticidal, laxative, rubefacient, and stimulant.

Therapeutic Uses

  • Circulation, muscles, and joints: arthritis, muscular aches and pains, rheumatism.
  • Digestive system: anorexia, sluggish digestion, liver congestion.

Blending

Turmeric blends well with cananga, labdanum, elecampane, ginger, orris, cassie, clary sage, and mimosa.

Safety

Tumerone, the main constituent, is moderately toxic and irritant in high concentration, and sensitization is possible. Use the essential oil in moderation, in low dilutions, and only for a limited period. Patch test before topical use, and avoid on broken or sensitive skin.

Where You Will Encounter It

Used in perfumery for oriental and fantasy-type fragrances. The oleoresin appears as a flavour ingredient in some foods, mainly curries, meat products, and condiments.

Therapeutic Applications

  • Constipation & Sluggish Digestion: Apply via massage or bath.
  • Rheumatism: Apply via massage, compress, or bath.
  • Aches and Pains: Apply via massage, compress, or bath.
  • Arthritis: Apply via massage, skin oil/lotion, compress, or bath.
  • Sprains & Strains: Apply via compress.
  • Liver Congestion: Apply via massage.
