Spanish Oregano
⚠️
Strict safety warning. The essential oil of Spanish Oregano (Thymus capitatus) is a dermal toxin, skin irritant, and mucous membrane irritant. The source states explicitly that it should not be used on the skin at all. Most commercial bottles labelled "oregano oil" are this product, despite being botanically a thyme. Restricted to flavouring and industrial applications.

A dark brownish-red or purple essential oil produced by steam distillation from the dried flowering tops of Spanish Oregano (Thymus capitatus). The oil carries a strong, tarlike, herbaceous, refreshing odour. Despite the trade name, this is botanically a thyme and serves as the source for most so-called "oregano oil" on the market.

Extraction & Characteristics

Steam distilled from the dried flowering tops. Principal constituents are carvacrol, thymol, cymene, caryophyllene, pinene, limonene, linalol, borneol, myrcene, thujone, and terpinene. The high carvacrol and thymol content is responsible for the oil's potency and skin-irritant character.

Therapeutic Uses

The source provides no aromatherapy or home-use indications and explicitly forbids skin application. For comparable folk applications, see Oregano Essential Oil (common oregano, Origanum vulgare) and Common Thyme Essential Oil (T. vulgaris), both of which the source defers to for actions and uses.

Safety

Dermal toxin, skin irritant, and mucous membrane irritant. The source states explicitly that it should not be used on the skin at all. Avoid all topical and therapeutic use. Avoid completely during pregnancy.

Where You Will Encounter It

The source defers other uses to common oregano. In practice, this oil is found in food flavouring and certain industrial fragrance applications. Most retail "oregano oil" labels point to this product.
