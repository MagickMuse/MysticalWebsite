Lavender Essential Oil

Mastic Essential Oil is distilled from the oleoresin of Mastic (Pistacia lentiscus), the small Mediterranean shrub whose bark exudes brittle pea-sized resin lumps after incision.

Extraction & Characteristics

Two aromatic products are produced from mastic. A resinoid is produced by solvent extraction from the oleoresin and is a pale amber or greenish viscous mass with a faint balsamic turpentine-like odour. The essential oil is produced by steam distillation from the oleoresin, or occasionally directly from the leaves and branches, and is a pale yellow mobile liquid with a fresh balsamic turpentine-like odour. The oil's principal constituents are monoterpene hydrocarbons, mostly pinenes.

Therapeutic Uses

In the West, mastic oil was historically used in a similar way to turpentine, sharing many of the properties of coniferous turpentines and once widely employed in medicine. Its recorded actions include antimicrobial, antiseptic, antispasmodic, astringent, diuretic, expectorant, and stimulant.

Blending

Mastic blends well with Lavender Essential Oil, mimosa, citrus, and floral oils.

Safety

Non-toxic and non-irritant. Possible sensitization in some individuals.

Where You Will Encounter It

Mastic is used in dentistry and in the production of varnish. Both the resinoid and the essential oil are employed in high-class colognes and perfumes, and as a flavouring agent, especially in liqueurs.

Traditional Grimoire Notes

Mastic gum has a sharp, light aroma and must be combined with an oil before use in perfumery or ritual blends. Used in high-class colognes and as a fixative, it lends a bright, resinous clarity to incense and sachets alike.
