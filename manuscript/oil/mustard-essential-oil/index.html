Mustard
⚠️
Strictest safety warning. The essential oil of Mustard (Brassica nigra) is considered one of the most toxic of all essential oils. It is an oral toxin, a dermal toxin, and a mucous membrane irritant. The source states explicitly that it should not be used in therapy either externally or internally. This page exists for catalog completeness only. Do not confuse with Mustard Seed Oil, the cold-pressed culinary carrier oil, which is a different product entirely.

A colourless or pale yellow liquid steam distilled from the seeds of Mustard (Brassica nigra), after the seeds have been macerated in warm water. The oil carries a sharp, penetrating, acrid odour.

Extraction & Characteristics

Produced by steam (or water) distillation from the black mustard seeds, which have first been macerated in warm water. Principal constituent is allyl isothiocyanate (99 percent). Notably, black mustard seed or powder does not contain this constituent; allyl isothiocyanate is only formed by contact with water during the production of the essential oil.

Therapeutic Uses

The source provides no aromatherapy or home-use indications and explicitly forbids therapeutic use, internal or external. For the seeds' traditional digestive, warming, and rubefacient applications in herbal medicine, see the Mustard plant entry. For culinary and carrier applications, see Mustard Seed Oil.

Safety

Oral toxin, dermal toxin, and mucous membrane irritant. Considered one of the most toxic of all essential oils. The source states explicitly that it should not be used in therapy either externally or internally. Avoid all skin contact and inhalation. Not suitable for any use during pregnancy.

Where You Will Encounter It

Used in certain rubefacient or counter-irritant liniments. Used extensively by the food industry, especially in pickles, seasonings, and sauces. Little used as a fragrance component except in cat and dog repellents.
