Botanical Information

Scientific name: Pelargonium graveolens

Botanical family: Geraniaceae

Origin: South Africa, Egypt, Réunion

Source of plant's oil: Leaves and stems

Evaporation: Middle note

Scent Profile

Description: Floral, green, rosy, slightly minty

Impact: Balancing, uplifting, comforting

Application

Topical (diluted), diffuse, direct inhalation

Magickal Correspondences

Magickal uses: Love, healing, protection, fertility, hex breaking, freedom, emotional release, spiritual connection

Associated with: Emotional healing, relationships, letting go, independence

Physical Uses

Medicinal applications: Hemorrhaging, intestinal parasites, body odor, skin health, hormone balance

Emotional aromatherapy applications: Mood enhancement, stress relief, emotional balance, comfort during transitions

Cautions: Generally safe for most users. Dilute before topical use.

Blending

Geranium blends well with Basil Essential Oil, Mountain Pepper Essential Oil, and Ravensara Essential Oil.

Herbal Lore

Geranium's associations with Venus and hex breaking make this essential oil the perfect ally for work involving freedom, healing relationships, and emotional release. The plant's protective and fertility-enhancing properties extend to its essential oil, making it valuable for both physical and emotional healing work.

Magickal Uses

To grant a romantic partner freedom and healing after a breakup:

  1. Dilute geranium oil: 1 drop of geranium essential oil in 2 ml of carrier oil
  2. Anoint two candles (identical tapers or different colors to represent each partner)
  3. Place the candles in candleholders and light them
  4. As the candles burn, gradually move them further and further apart
  5. By the time they have finished burning, they should be in separate rooms if possible

This ritual honors the relationship while allowing both parties to move forward with grace.

For healing relationships that have reached the point of no return:

  1. Write the other person a letter. Pour out all those feelings
  2. Whether it's one page or fifty pages, do not censor yourself
  3. Get out every hurtful thing that has been carried around
  4. Take the letter outside
  5. Sprinkle it with a few drops of geranium essential oil
  6. Burn it in a fire-safe container outside (backyard fire pits, grills, and cauldrons all work)
  7. Make sure to obey burning ordinances, especially in drought-ridden areas

This ritual allows for emotional catharsis and symbolic release of pain.

Geranium oil's spiritual properties make it excellent for daily devotional work, prayer, meditation, and spellwork. Its calming nature helps center the practitioner and opens channels for connection with divine energies.

Use geranium oil in spells for freedom and to protect against ill health, hexes, and people. The oil helps dissolve threads of entanglement, supporting freedom, independence, inner strength, and the determination to be in control of one's own life.

Extraction & Characteristics

Steam distilled from the leaves, stalks, and flowers of Geranium (Pelargonium graveolens). An absolute and concrete are also produced in Morocco. The Bourbon oil (from Reunion) is a greenish-olive liquid with a green, rosy-sweet, minty scent and is generally preferred in perfumery. Principal constituents include citronellol, geraniol, linalol, isomenthone, menthone, phellandrene, sabinene, and limonene. Constituents vary according to type and source.

Therapeutic Uses

For skin care, the oil is used for acne, bruises, broken capillaries, burns, congested skin, cuts, dermatitis, eczema, haemorrhoids, lice, oily and mature complexions, mosquito repellent, ringworm, ulcers, and wounds. For circulation, muscles, and joints, it is applied to cellulitis, breast engorgement, oedema, and poor circulation. Respiratory uses include sore throat and tonsillitis. For the genito-urinary and endocrine systems, it supports the adrenocortical glands, menopausal problems, and PMT. For the nervous system, it is used for nervous tension, neuralgia, and stress-related conditions.

Blending

Blends well with Lavender Essential Oil, Patchouli Essential Oil, Clove Essential Oil, Rose Oil, Sandalwood Essential Oil, Jasmine Absolute, Juniper Berry Essential Oil, Neroli Essential Oil, Bergamot Essential Oil, and other citrus oils.

Safety

Non-toxic, non-irritant, and generally non-sensitizing. Hypersensitive individuals may experience contact dermatitis, especially with the Bourbon type.

Where You Will Encounter It

Used as a fragrance component in soaps, creams, perfumes, and cosmetic products of all kinds. Extensively employed as a flavouring agent in most major food categories and in alcoholic and soft drinks.
