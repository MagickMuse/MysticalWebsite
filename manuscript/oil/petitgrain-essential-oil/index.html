Petitgrain Essential Oil is extracted from the leaves and twigs of the bitter orange tree (Citrus aurantium). This protective, bitter orange scent makes the oil useful in protective blends.

Botanical Information

Scientific name: Citrus aurantium

Botanical family: Rutaceae

Source of plant's oil: Leaves and twigs

Evaporation: Top to middle note

Extraction & Characteristics

Petitgrain Essential Oil is steam-distilled from the leaves and twigs of Petitgrain (Citrus aurantium var. amara), the same tree that produces Bitter Orange Essential Oil and Neroli Essential Oil. An orange leaf-and-flower water absolute called petitgrain sur fleurs is also produced. The finished oil is a pale yellow to amber liquid with a fresh-floral citrus scent and a woody-herbaceous undertone. Principal constituents are 40 to 80 percent esters, mainly linalyl acetate and geranyl acetate, alongside linalol, nerol, terpineol, geraniol, nerolidol, farnesol, and limonene, among others.

Therapeutic Uses

Skin Care: Acne, excessive perspiration, greasy skin and hair, and toning.

Digestive System: Dyspepsia and flatulence.

Nervous System: Convalescence, insomnia, nervous exhaustion, and stress-related conditions.

Recorded actions include antiseptic, antispasmodic, deodorant, digestive, nervine, stimulant (digestive and nervous), stomachic, and tonic.

Blending

Petitgrain blends well with Rosemary Essential Oil, Lavender Essential Oil, Geranium Essential Oil, Bergamot Essential Oil, Bitter Orange Essential Oil, Neroli Essential Oil (orange blossom), labdanum, Oakmoss Absolute, Jasmine Absolute, Benzoin Absolute, Palmarosa Essential Oil, and balsams.

Safety

Non-toxic, non-irritant, non-sensitizing, and non-phototoxic.

Where You Will Encounter It

Petitgrain is extensively used as a fragrance component in soaps, detergents, cosmetics, and perfumes, especially colognes, where it sometimes replaces orange blossom. It is employed as a flavour component in many foods, especially confectionery, as well as alcoholic and soft drinks. At one time the oil used to be extracted from the green unripe oranges when they were still the size of a cherry, hence the name petitgrains or 'little grains.' Petitgrain remains one of the classic ingredients of eau-de-cologne.

Scent Profile

Description: Fresh, green, citrusy with floral undertones and a slightly woody, herbaceous quality

Impact: Calming, uplifting, balancing

Application

Dilute before topical use, direct inhalation, diffuse

Magickal Correspondences

Magickal uses: Emotional balance, anger transformation, mental clarity, peace, renewal, transformation

Relationship to Other Citrus Oils

Petitgrain, neroli, and bitter orange essential oils all come from the same tree (Citrus aurantium):

  • Petitgrain: from leaves and twigs
  • Neroli: from blossoms
  • Bitter Orange: from fruit peel

Each offers a different aromatic profile and energetic signature while sharing citrus family properties.

Uses

Petitgrain excels at transforming difficult emotions, particularly anger, frustration, and irritation, into productive energy. Its balancing nature helps ground intense feelings while maintaining clarity and focus.

The fresh, green quality of petitgrain clears mental fog and supports concentration during challenging tasks or emotional processing.

Diffuse or apply diluted petitgrain when seeking to restore emotional equilibrium after periods of stress or conflict.

Therapeutic Applications

  • Nervous Exhaustion: Apply via massage, bath, or vaporization.
  • Nervous Tension and Stress: Apply via massage, bath, or vaporization.
  • Acne: Apply via massage, skin oil/lotion, flower water, bath, inhalation (steam), or neat application.
  • Excessive Perspiration: Apply via skin oil/lotion or bath.
  • Slack Tissue: Apply via massage, skin oil/lotion, or bath.
  • Indigestion & Flatulence: Apply via massage.
  • Insomnia: Apply via massage, bath, or vaporization.
