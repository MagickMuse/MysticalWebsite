Sassafras

Sassafras essential oil is highly toxic and is not for home aromatherapy, internal use, or external use. It is included here for botanical and historical reference only.

Other Names & Related Species

Known as common sassafras, North American sassafras, and sassafrax, with botanical synonyms Sassafras officinale, Laurus sassafras, and S. variifolium. Several other species exist, notably Brazilian sassafras (Ocotea pretiosa), which also yields an essential oil and is also highly toxic.

Origin & Distribution

Native to the eastern United States. The oil is produced mainly from Florida to Canada and in Mexico.

Folk Tradition

The plant has been used for treating high blood pressure, rheumatism, arthritis, gout, menstrual and kidney problems, and skin complaints. Sassafras pith was used as a demulcent, especially for inflammation of the eyes, and as a soothing drink in catarrhal affection. The wood and bark yield a bright yellow dye.

Extraction & Characteristics

The essential oil is produced by steam distillation from dried root bark chips. The liquid is yellowy-brown and oily, with a fresh, sweet-spicy, woody-camphoraceous odour. A safrol-free sassafras oil is produced by alcohol extraction. The oil is roughly 80 to 90 percent safrole, with pinenes, phellandrenes, asarone, camphor, thujone, myristicin, and menthone among other constituents. Reported actions include antiviral, diaphoretic, diuretic, carminative, pediculicide, and stimulant, but the oil is not safe to apply for any of these.

Safety

Do not use sassafras essential oil in home aromatherapy, internally or externally. The oil is highly toxic; ingestion of even small amounts has caused death. It is a carcinogen, an irritant, and an abortifacient. Pregnant and nursing people, children, and anyone with sensitive skin or compromised health must avoid it entirely. The crude oil is banned from food use.

Where You Will Encounter It

Sassafras oil and crude are banned from food use. The safrol-free extract is used to a limited extent in flavouring work. Safrole itself serves as a starting material for the fragrance ingredient heliotropin.
