Botanical Source

Dwarf pine (Pinus mugo var. pumilio) of the Pinaceae family. Also classified as P. mugo, P. montana, and P. pumilio, and known as mountain pine or Swiss mountain pine; the oil is sometimes traded as "pine needle." The plant is a pyramidal shrub or small tree up to 12 metres high, with black bark, stiff and twisted needles borne in clusters, and brown cones that begin bluish in hue.

Distribution

Native to the mountainous regions of central and southern Europe. Oil production centres on Austria (Tirol), the former Yugoslavia, Denmark, and Italy.

Other Species

There are very many species of pine yielding essential oil from their needles and wood, or used in turpentine production. The so-called huon pine (Dacrydium franklinii), whose essential oil is also a skin irritant, belongs to a different family altogether (Podocarpaceae) and is not a true pine. See also Scotch pine.

Folk and Historical Use

A preparation made from the needles has been used internally for bladder, kidney, and rheumatic complaints, as a liniment for rheumatism and muscular pain, and as an inhalant for bronchitis, catarrh, colds, and similar respiratory conditions.

Therapeutic Actions

Analgesic, antimicrobial, antiseptic, antitussive, antiviral, balsamic, diuretic, expectorant, and rubefacient.

Extraction & Characteristics

The essential oil is steam-distilled from the needles and twigs. It presents as a water-white liquid carrying a very pleasant, balsamic-sweet, spicy-woody scent of good tenacity. This is the favoured pine fragrance for perfumery work, prized for its unique delicate odour.

Principal Constituents

Mainly monoterpene hydrocarbons: limonene, pinenes, phellandrene, dipentene, camphene, myrcene, and bornyl acetate, among others. The unusual scent is attributed to the oil's aldehyde content.

Safety

⚠️
Dermal irritant and common sensitizing agent; otherwise non-toxic. Best avoided therapeutically due to irritant hazards.

Aromatherapy Uses

None. The oil is not employed in aromatherapy or home use due to its irritant and sensitizing profile.

Blending Partners

Favoured perfumery partners include cedarwood, Lavandin Essential Oil, Rosemary Essential Oil, Sage Essential Oil, Cananga Essential Oil, Rock Rose Essential Oil, Juniper Berry Essential Oil, and other coniferous oils such as Fir Needle Essential Oil and Silver Fir Templin Essential Oil.

Other Uses

A fragrance and flavour component in pharmaceutical preparations for coughs, colds, and nasal congestion, and externally in analgesic ointments and liniments. Extensively employed in soaps, bath preparations, toiletries, cosmetics, and perfumes, especially "leather" and "woody" type fragrances. Also used across major food categories, alcoholic drinks, and soft drinks.
