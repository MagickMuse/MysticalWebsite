Sweet Birch

Sweet Birch Essential Oil is the wintergreen-scented oil of Betula lenta: a steam-distilled bark oil so dominated by methyl salicylate that it is essentially a chemical twin of true wintergreen. Almost never used in modern aromatherapy due to serious toxicity concerns. Most often recognised today through its historical role and as a trace ingredient in commercial products.

Extraction & Characteristics

Source: Bark of Betula lenta, macerated in warm water before distillation

Method: Steam distillation

Note: Top to Middle

Scent: A colourless, pale yellow, or reddish-tinted liquid with an intense, sweet-woody, wintergreen-like aroma.

Constituents: Almost entirely methyl salicylate (around 98 percent), generated during the maceration process. Chemically near-identical to wintergreen oil.

Safety

Do not use in home aromatherapy. Methyl salicylate is highly absorbable through the skin, and fatal poisoning by topical application has been documented. The oil is also classified as an environmental hazard and marine pollutant.

Avoid entirely if you are:

  • Pregnant or nursing
  • On blood-thinning medication
  • Sensitive to aspirin or other salicylates
  • Caring for children or pets in the same space

Where You Will Encounter It

Despite its toxicity, sweet birch oil shows up in trace amounts as:

  • A counter-irritant in commercial anti-arthritic ointments and analgesic balms.
  • A fragrance note in some cosmetics and perfumes.
  • A flavouring agent, especially in root beer, chewing gum, and toothpaste, always at very low levels.
