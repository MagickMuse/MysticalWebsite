Rosewood Essential Oil is steam-distilled from the wood chippings of Rosewood (Aniba rosaeodora), the medium-sized tropical evergreen of the Lauraceae family native to the Amazon. Aniba rosaeodora is one of the trees being extensively felled in the clearing of the South American rainforests, so continual production of rosewood oil is environmentally damaging.

Extraction & Characteristics

The essential oil is produced by steam distillation of the wood chippings. It is a colourless to pale yellow liquid with a very sweet, woody-floral fragrance and a spicy hint. The oil blends well with most oils, especially citrus, woods, and florals, and helps give body and round off sharp edges in a blend. Principal constituents are linalol, comprising 90 to 97 percent in cayenne rosewood and 80 to 90 percent in the Brazilian oil, alongside cineol, terpineol, geraniol, citronellal, limonene, pinene, and others.

Therapeutic Uses

Skin Care: Acne, dermatitis, scars, wounds, wrinkles, and general skin care for sensitive, dry, dull, or combination oily and dry complexions. Rosewood does not have dramatic curative power, but is mild, safe, and especially useful in skin care.

Immune System: Colds, coughs, fever, infections, and immune-system stimulation.

Nervous System: Frigidity, headaches, nausea, nervous tension, and stress-related conditions.

Recorded actions include mild analgesic, anticonvulsant, antidepressant, antimicrobial, antiseptic, aphrodisiac, bactericidal, cellular stimulant, cephalic, deodorant, immune-system stimulant, tissue regenerator, and tonic.

Blending

Rosewood blends well with most oils, especially citrus, woods, and florals. It is a useful body and rounding agent for blends with sharp edges.

Safety

Non-toxic, non-irritant, and non-sensitizing.

Sustainability

Aniba rosaeodora is among the species being extensively felled in the clearing of the South American rainforests, and continual production of rosewood oil is consequently environmentally damaging. Once extensively used as a source of natural linalol, rosewood oil is increasingly being replaced by the synthetic form.

Where You Will Encounter It

Acetylated rosewood oil is used extensively in perfumery work, including soaps, toiletries, cosmetics, and perfumes. The oil is employed in most major food categories, as well as alcoholic and soft drinks. Once extensively used as a source of natural linalol, rosewood is increasingly being replaced by synthetic linalol.
