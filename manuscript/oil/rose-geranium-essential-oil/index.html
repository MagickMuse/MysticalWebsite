Warnings

Potentially toxic if ingested. Never use essential oils internally without the supervision of a trained professional. Commonly adulterated. Use a reputable dealer to find this oil.

Herbal Lore

Seventeen species of the Pelargonium family are commonly used to create this essential oil, and P. graveolens is the one most often used. The medicinally used geranium species is not a Pelargonium at all. It is a true geranium, Geranium maculatum, or cranesbill. It is important not to confuse the two. The Pelargoniums used in aromatherapy have different magickal uses from their medicinal counterpart.

Pelargonium graveolens is also called rose geranium because it smells similar to traditional English roses and this common garden annual. Of the seventeen species used to create scent, there are many wildly different versions, from spicy fragrances reminiscent of mint, nutmeg, or clove to citrusy and light ones. Because all of these scents can be derived from geraniums, they blend well with each other.

The energy of geranium, especially rose geranium, reminds us that we are whole, strong, and protected. The magick of the geraniums is not offense; it is defense. The scent of rose is uplifting and strengthening. The oil reminds us that depression, illness, and hexes are opportunistic parasites that strike when we are at our weakest and cannot adequately protect ourselves.

Think of rose geranium like echinacea: It is not used to cure illness; it is supposed to prevent illness by boosting the immune system before sickness can occur.

Rose absolute is rarely 100 percent rose. Rose geranium is a common adulterant to rose oil, and as such the price of Pelargonium graveolens has gone up as well. This leads to a chain reaction where the adulteration of geranium oils themselves has increased. Make sure to check quality testing when purchasing this oil.

Uses

Harmony is the hallmark of geranium. Plant a border of geranium plants around the home to stabilize those living within. Alternate with another member of the geranium family, Pelargonium citrosum, for a pleasant citrus and rose blend.

Diffuse 1 drop of geranium essential oil in water to calm tense situations. Whether it's having a tough discussion with a partner or mediating a dispute between coven members, peers, or children, it will calm the emotions of those present and help the conversation move forward.

Geranium can facilitate inner growth as well. Diffuse 1 drop of geranium essential oil in water during religious counseling sessions to help release old thought patterns.

Geranium has a reputation for healing work, both physically and emotionally. It inspires compassion as well, making it a good long-term care essential oil. It not only supports the healing work of the patient but also helps to stave off compassion fatigue for the caregiver.

Geranium is emotionally uplifting. Diffuse its essential oil regularly when seasonal affective disorder (SAD) is likely to creep in.

Geranium is a hearty ally for those battling emotional disturbances. It inspires courage and confidence, and a calm emotional state. It is balancing and therefore helps to alleviate depression. It can act as a temporary mood stabilizer while professional mental health advocates are arranged. Remember: magick is no substitute for professional medical services.

If things start to feel routine or stagnant at home, geranium can renew those feelings of joy.

Recipe for Uplift: Diffuse geranium with bergamot for an uplifting treat. Just 1 drop of geranium and 1 drop of Citrus bergamia each will have occupants feeling a spring in their step in no time.

Geranium's scent and Venusian associations have been used in place of rose from time immemorial. Thus, it is used for love spells, fertility rites, and relationship healing.

Geranium is an easy ally to work with for hex breaking. It stimulates awareness internally and externally. It also inspires focused action in response to threats. The hex-breaking properties of geranium also make it a powerful ally for protection work.

Protection Recipe: Place 1 drop of geranium essential oil in 2 ml of carrier oil and shake. Apply to doors, windows, and the backs of mirrors.

Geranium is not only helpful when it comes to hex breaking. It can avert the hex before it can even be cast. If you suspect someone may set a curse or hex in motion, be proactive and diffuse geranium oil in the home (and office).

Geraniums have been used in magick dealing with the fae. Diffuse the essential oil in ritual spaces where meditations dealing with the hidden folk are performed. Consider having potted geraniums in the space as well.

Devotion is another badge of geranium oil. It can be used to anoint deity statues, sacred space, and devotional candles.

Want to bring on change in a hurry? Add geranium to the spell! Growing geranium at home means a steady supply of leaves that can be dried and added to workings. Use geranium anointing oil to dress charms, mojo bags, and candles for magickal fuel. Simply place 1 drop of geranium in 2 ml of carrier oil and shake.

Geranium can be used to banish nightmares. Diffuse it with another nightmare-banishing oil, like lavender, thirty minutes before bed to relax the mind before sleep.

Extraction & Characteristics

Steam distilled from the leaves, stalks, and flowers of Untitled. An absolute and concrete are also produced in Morocco. The Bourbon oil (from Reunion) is a greenish-olive liquid with a green, rosy-sweet, minty scent and is generally preferred in perfumery. Principal constituents include citronellol, geraniol, linalol, isomenthone, menthone, phellandrene, sabinene, and limonene. Constituents vary according to type and source.

Therapeutic Uses

For skin care, the oil is used for acne, bruises, broken capillaries, burns, congested skin, cuts, dermatitis, eczema, haemorrhoids, lice, oily and mature complexions, mosquito repellent, ringworm, ulcers, and wounds. For circulation, muscles, and joints, it is applied to cellulitis, breast engorgement, oedema, and poor circulation. Respiratory uses include sore throat and tonsillitis. For the genito-urinary and endocrine systems, it supports the adrenocortical glands, menopausal problems, and PMT. For the nervous system, it is used for nervous tension, neuralgia, and stress-related conditions.

Blending

Blends well with Lavender Essential Oil, Patchouli Essential Oil, Clove Essential Oil, Rose Oil, Sandalwood Essential Oil, Jasmine Absolute, Juniper Berry Essential Oil, Neroli Essential Oil, Bergamot Essential Oil, and other citrus oils.

Safety

Non-toxic, non-irritant, and generally non-sensitizing. Hypersensitive individuals may experience contact dermatitis, especially with the Bourbon type.

Where You Will Encounter It

Used as a fragrance component in soaps, creams, perfumes, and cosmetic products of all kinds. Extensively employed as a flavouring agent in most major food categories and in alcoholic and soft drinks.

Traditional Grimoire Notes

Provides psychic protection and is traditionally used to exorcise and protect a new home by anointing the doors and windowsills. Also said to promote good health, dispel fear, and inspire courage.
