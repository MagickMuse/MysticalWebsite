Fir Needle Essential Oil is steam-distilled from the needles and young twigs of Silver Fir (Abies alba), a relatively small coniferous tree in the Pinaceae family native to north European mountainous regions and cultivated mainly in Switzerland, Poland, Germany, France, Austria, and especially the former Yugoslavia.

Extraction & Characteristics

The oil is a colourless or pale yellow liquid with a pleasing, rich, sweet-balsamic odour. Principal constituents are santene, pinene, limonene, bornyl acetate, and lauraldehyde, among others.

Related Species

Oils distilled from the twigs and needles of various members of the coniferous families Abies, Larix, Picea, Spruce, Pinus, and Tsuga are all commonly called fir needle oil, so the specific botanical name matters. Other notable Abies species include Canadian Balsam (A. balsamifera), the Siberian fir (A. siberica) which is the most popular fir needle oil in Europe and the USA due to its fine fragrance, and the Japanese fir oils from A. mayriana and A. sachalinensis. A second oil from the same tree, Silver Fir Templin Essential Oil, is distilled from the broken-up pieces of the fir cones.

Folk Tradition

Silver fir is highly esteemed on the Continent for its medicinal virtues and its fragrant scent. It is used mainly for respiratory complaints, fever, and muscular and rheumatic pain.

Therapeutic Uses

For circulation, muscles, and joints, fir needle oil addresses arthritis, muscular aches and pains, and rheumatism. In the respiratory system, it is used for bronchitis, coughs, and sinusitis, among other complaints. In the immune system, it supports the body through colds, fever, and 'flu.

Blending

Fir needle blends well with Galbanum Essential Oil, labdanum, Lavender Essential Oil, Rosemary Essential Oil, Lemon Essential Oil, Pine Essential Oil, and Marjoram Essential Oil.

Safety

Non-toxic, non-irritant (except in high concentration), non-sensitizing.

Where You Will Encounter It

Fir needle is employed as an ingredient in some cough and cold remedies and rheumatic treatments, and as a fragrance component in deodorants, room sprays, disinfectants, bath preparations, soaps, and perfumes.

Traditional Grimoire Notes

Promotes peace and contentment.
