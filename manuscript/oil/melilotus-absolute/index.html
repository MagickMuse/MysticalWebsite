Melilotus

Melilotus Absolute is the solvent-extracted aromatic of Melilotus (Melilotus officinalis), the sweet clover whose dried flowers carry the unmistakable scent of new-mown hay. The aromatic has no aromatherapy or home use because of its high coumarin content.

Safety

Do not use Melilotus Absolute in home aromatherapy. It is rich in coumarins, including melilotic acid and orthocoumaric acid. Coumarin was banned from flavourings in 1953 in some countries, including the USA, because of toxicity, and some coumarins are also known to be phototoxic. Avoid during pregnancy.

Extraction & Characteristics

A concrete, usually called a resinoid or oleoresin, is produced by solvent extraction from the dry flowers. The finished aromatic is a viscous dark green liquid with a rich, sweet-herbaceous, new-mown hay scent. Its principal constituents are coumarins, mainly melilotic acid and orthocoumaric acid.

Where You Will Encounter It

The oleoresin is used in high-class perfumery work. It is also used extensively for flavouring tobacco in countries without the coumarin ban.

Traditional Grimoire Notes

Traditionally employed to combat depression — historically referred to as melancholy.
