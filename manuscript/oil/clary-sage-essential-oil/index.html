Botanical Information

Salvia sclarea of the Lamiaceae family. Origin: France and United States. Source of oil: flowers and leaves. Middle note.

Scent Profile

Soft, herbal, musty. Relieves stress and calms in emergencies while allowing clear thinking and triage prioritization.

Application

Diffuse, direct inhalation, diluted on the skin.

Warnings

Contact a certified aromatherapist before using clary sage essential oil during pregnancy. Current data suggests increases in estrogen and progesterone levels with use. Use caution if you are male or are taking synthetic hormones for menopause. Avoid with a history of epilepsy or other seizure disorders.

Though clary sage can induce euphoria when used in large doses or without proper ventilation, it can cause headaches. Do not combine clary sage with alcohol or sedatives. Anticoagulant effects have been reported; use caution under the care of a doctor, trained herbalist, or certified aromatherapist. Not suggested for long-term use with panic disorders, as hyperventilation or strong inhalations of clary sage or use with sedatives can cause headaches and other medical issues.

Herbal Lore

Clary sage is a good ally in an emergency. It calms in a way that allows the subject to deal with the situation at hand in a triage protocol. The most important problem gets handled first and allows smooth flow of information without panic.

Clary sage is used largely because the common garden sage (Salvia officinalis) can be safely used only in small amounts. Clary sage is commonly used to help hormone levels in women. It is commonly added to aromatherapeutic blends to ease premenstrual symptoms. This does not mean that men cannot see the magickal benefit of this incredible ally.

The maternal-like energy of clary sage is the perfect base for floral perfumes and aromatic therapies.

Other Names & Related Species

Clary is also known as clary wort, muscatel sage, clear eye, see bright, common clary, clarry, and eye bright. It is closely related to garden sage (Salvia officinalis) and Spanish sage (S. lavendulaefolia), both of which yield essential oils. Other types of sage include meadow clary (S. pratensis) and vervain sage (S. verbenaca). Clary sage should not be confused with the common wayside herb eyebright (Euphrasia).

Origin & Distribution

Clary is native to southern Europe and is cultivated worldwide, especially in the Mediterranean region, Russia, the USA, England, Morocco, and central Europe. The French, Moroccan, and English clary are considered of superior quality for perfumery work.

Folk Tradition

This herb was highly esteemed in the Middle Ages and has now largely fallen out of use. It was used for digestive disorders, kidney disease, uterine and menstrual complaints, for cleansing ulcers, and as a general nerve tonic. The mucilage from the seeds was used for treating tumours and for removing dust particles from the eyes. Like garden sage, it cools inflammation and is especially useful for throat and respiratory infections.

Extraction & Characteristics

The essential oil is produced by steam distillation from the flowering tops and leaves. A concrete and absolute are also produced in small quantities by solvent extraction. The liquid is colourless or pale yellowy-green with a sweet, nutty-herbaceous scent. Linalyl acetate makes up as much as 75 percent of the oil, with linalol, pinene, myrcene, and phellandrene also present. Constituents vary by geographical origin, and several chemotypes exist.

Therapeutic Uses

Skin care: acne, boils, dandruff, hair loss, inflamed conditions, oily skin and hair, ophthalmia, ulcers, wrinkles.

Circulation, muscles, and joints: high blood pressure, muscular aches and pains.

Respiratory system: asthma, throat infections, whooping cough.

Digestive system: colic, cramp, dyspepsia, flatulence.

Genito-urinary system: amenorrhoea, labour pain, dysmenorrhoea, leucorrhoea.

Nervous system: depression, frigidity, impotence, migraine, nervous tension, and stress-related disorders.

Blending

Clary sage blends well with juniper, lavender, coriander, cardamom, geranium, sandalwood, cedarwood, pine, labdanum, jasmine, frankincense, bergamot, and other citrus oils.

Safety

Non-toxic, non-irritant, non-sensitizing. Avoid during pregnancy. Do not use clary sage oil while drinking alcohol, as it can induce a narcotic effect and exaggerate drunkenness. Clary sage is generally preferred over garden sage in aromatherapy due to its lower toxicity level.

Where You Will Encounter It

The oil and absolute serve as fragrance components and fixatives in soaps, detergents, cosmetics, and perfumes. The oil is used extensively by the food and drink industry, especially in the production of wines with a muscatel flavour.
