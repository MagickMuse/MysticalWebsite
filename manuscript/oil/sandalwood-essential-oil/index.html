Sandalwood Essential Oil is steam-distilled from the powdered, dried roots and heartwood of Sandalwood (Santalum album), the small evergreen parasitic tree of the Santalaceae family native to tropical Asia. The tree must be over thirty years old before it is ready for oil production.

Extraction & Characteristics

The essential oil is produced by water or steam distillation from the powdered and dried roots and heartwood. The finished oil is a pale yellow, greenish, or brownish viscous liquid with a deep, soft, sweet-woody balsamic scent of excellent tenacity. Principal constituents are about 90 percent santalols and 6 percent sesquiterpene hydrocarbons including santene, teresantol, borneol, santalone, and tri-cyclo-ekasantalal, among others.

Therapeutic Uses

Skin Care: Acne, dry, cracked, and chapped skin, aftershave and barber's rash, greasy skin, and as a moisturizer.

Respiratory System: Bronchitis, catarrh, dry persistent coughs, laryngitis, and sore throat.

Digestive System: Diarrhoea and nausea.

Genito-Urinary System: Cystitis.

Nervous System: Depression, insomnia, nervous tension, and stress-related complaints.

Recorded actions include antidepressant, antiphlogistic, antiseptic (urinary and pulmonary), antispasmodic, aphrodisiac, astringent, bactericidal, carminative, cicatrisant, diuretic, expectorant, fungicidal, insecticidal, sedative, and tonic.

Blending

Sandalwood blends well with Rose Oil, Violet Leaf Absolute, Tuberose Oil, Untitled, Lavender Essential Oil, Black Pepper Essential Oil, Bergamot Essential Oil, Rosewood Essential Oil, Geranium Essential Oil, labdanum, Oakmoss Absolute, Benzoin Absolute, Vetivert Essential Oil, Untitled, mimosa, cassie, Costus Essential Oil, Untitled, and Jasmine Absolute. In India, sandalwood is often combined with rose in the famous scent aytar.

Safety

Non-toxic, non-irritant, and non-sensitizing.

Where You Will Encounter It

Sandalwood was once used as a pharmaceutical disinfectant, an application now largely abandoned. It is extensively employed as a fragrance component and fixative in soaps, detergents, cosmetics, and perfumes, especially oriental, woody, aftershave, and chypre compositions, and is extensively used in the production of incense. It is also employed as a flavour ingredient in most major food categories, including soft and alcoholic drinks.

Botanical Information

White Sandalwood: Santalum album (Santalaceae family)

Red Sandalwood: Pterocarpus santalinus

Extracted from bark. Originates from India. Base note with average absorption.

Heartwood of sandalwood used for distilling yields high-quality oil with yellowish tinge. As tree grows, red of wood bleached by sun, leaving only heartwood ruddy red-brown. Expensive and precious.

Ethical Sourcing: Due to poaching and strict export laws, Australian sandalwood (Santalum spicatum) makes up lion's share of market. Largest sustainable farm in Australia roughly 3x size of France, heavily regulated by Australian government. Consider Australian sandalwood as an aromatic and ethical replacement; this closely related species carries the same correspondences without moral or ethical dilemmas.

Substitution

Cedar can be substituted for sandalwood if the true wood cannot be found. While cedar does not duplicate the fragrance, it shares protective and purification associations that make it suitable for magickal formulas calling for sandalwood.

Herbal Lore

Medicinal use history as long as magickal one. Ayurvedic medicine uses for fevers, melancholy, peptic discomfort, and septic skin conditions. As countries crack down on export of precious oil, getting more common to find adulterated, traded, and false oils. Quality test from multiple companies, work with them, see which produces results.
