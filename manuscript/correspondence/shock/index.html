Shock Therapeutic Index

Definition

Shock is a sudden disturbance of the nervous system in response to physical injury, emotional trauma, frightening news, accident, bereavement, or any abrupt overload that the body and psyche cannot immediately process. Symptoms range from pallor, cold sweat, trembling, shallow breathing, racing or thready pulse, dizziness, and nausea, through to faintness, dissociation, weeping, and collapse. Aromatic intervention does not replace medical care for clinical shock, but it does steady the nervous system, anchor the breath, and ease the rolling waves of disorientation that follow a shock event.

Indicated Oils

  • Lemon Balm Essential Oil
  • Lavandin Essential Oil
  • Spike Lavender Essential Oil
  • Lavender Essential Oil
  • Neroli Essential Oil

Application Methods

  • Massage: Dilute 4 to 6 drops of essential oil per ounce of carrier oil and apply with slow, grounding strokes along the back, shoulders, abdomen, and feet. Massage at this dilution settles the autonomic response, draws the breath out of the chest, and gives the body a steady, predictable rhythm to follow back into safety.
  • Bath: Add 4 to 6 drops of essential oil to a tablespoon of carrier oil or unscented bath base, then disperse in warm (not hot) water and soak for 15 to 20 minutes. The combined warmth, weight of the water, and aroma soothe the trembling that follows shock and help signal safety to the nervous system.
  • Vaporizer/Diffuser: Add 4 to 6 drops to an electric diffuser or aroma vaporizer to perfume the room with a steady, anchoring scent. Useful in the immediate aftermath of bad news or a frightening event, when the person needs a continuous aromatic anchor without having to do anything themselves.

Safety

Clinical shock (massive blood loss, severe burns, sepsis, anaphylaxis, cardiac event) is a medical emergency and requires immediate professional care. Aromatherapy is supportive only and must not delay calling for help. For emotional or vasovagal shock, ensure the person is warm, lying down with feet slightly elevated, and breathing slowly before introducing oils. Spike lavender and lavandin should be avoided during pregnancy and in epilepsy. Lemon balm essential oil can sensitize the skin and is most safely used by diffusion or in a tablespoon of carrier oil for the bath rather than neat. Neroli is gentle and pregnancy-friendly at low dilution, making it the safest first choice when uncertain. Avoid offering oils orally or applying anything strongly stimulating in the first minutes after a shock event; the goal is to slow and steady the system, not to rouse it further.
