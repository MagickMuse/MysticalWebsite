The practice of creating images to represent desired outcomes, with the intention that the image produces the wanted effect. Pictograph magick operates as a form of sympathetic magick based on the concept that like attracts like, following the adage "As above, so below," meaning whatever is imprinted in the astral realms will manifest on the physical planes.

Ancient Origins

Pictographs are among the oldest forms of human communication and magickal expression. The earliest known examples date back tens of thousands of years:

  • Blombos Cave, South Africa: A rock fragment decorated with cross-hatching in red ochre, approximately 77,000 years old
  • Western Iran: Animal and human pictographs estimated at 40,000 years old, bearing striking resemblance to North American examples
  • Global cave art: Depictions of pregnancy, dances, successful hunts, sacred plants, and ceremonial figures suggest ritualistic and magickal purposes

Before written languages developed, simple images represented words, phrases, ideas, and objects. These visual symbols served both practical communication and magickal intention.

How Pictographs Work

A pictograph is a visual representation that encapsulates and expresses the power, energy, and essence of thoughts and wishes. When a hunter draws an image of their party surrounded by herds of deer, they send a message to the universe that more deer are desired.

The simpler the image, the more powerful it can be. Think of your magickal pictograph as a logo: something that instantly conveys the identity and "brand" of your magickal wish.

Common Pictographs

Cosmic Symbols

Moon (circle or crescent): Increases psychic sensitivity, boosts magickal power, invokes lunar energies and the lunar goddess.

Sun (plain circle, rayed circle, circle with center dot, equal-armed cross): Brings energy and life to magick, invokes success, spurs growth, brings victory, increases fertility, boosts strength and courage, manifests resources.

Earth (cross, cross within circle): Confers strength, power, and stability. Represents foundation-building, environment, plants, and animals. Brings balance, stability, prosperity, and luck to travel spells. The cross form is especially useful for travel or protection over long distances, invoking the four cardinal directions.

Spiral: Represents the galaxy, cycles, or the cosmos as a whole.

  • Clockwise spiral (outer to center): Draws things toward you, speeds up processes
  • Counterclockwise spiral (center outward): Repels, pushes things away

Animal Imagery

One of the most common pictograph motifs in ancient rock art. Animal images can:

  • Attract a particular animal
  • Express desire for abundance
  • Invoke the powers and characteristics of the animal represented

Contemporary Symbols

Happy face: Brings joy, improves mood, attracts friends and good times.

Peace sign: Brings calm to volatile situations, ends conflicts, invokes cooperation.

Heart: Attracts love, strengthens bonds, magnifies affection and desire.

Geometric Patterns

Lines, dots, and cup marks appear globally in ancient pictographs. These same patterns are sometimes seen during altered states of consciousness. Such pictographs aid meditation and facilitate mental shifts that enable and empower magick.

Creating Custom Pictographs

  1. Think about the essence of your intention
  2. Choose simple, straightforward imagery or symbols
  3. You do not need to be an artist, just express yourself
  4. Go with gut instincts, do not overthink

Examples:

  • Money: Dollar sign, euro symbol
  • New love: Heart with two eyes in the middle
  • Home protection: House surrounded by an impenetrable wall, or guarded by protective dogs or dragons

Placement Options

Portable Charms

  • Small rocks, wood chips, nuts, or other pocket-size items
  • Ordinary writing paper to carry, display, give, or keep on an altar
  • Homemade beads or charms (flour and salt dough baked at low temperature)

Body and Clothing

  • Eyeliner, lip liner, lipstick, eye shadow, foundation, or body paints
  • Fabric paints or markers on T-shirts, jeans, hats, headbands, gloves, belts
  • Bottom of shoes (passes power into the ground with every step)
  • Jewelry pieces

Outdoor Pictographs

Use nontoxic, biodegradable, temporary materials:

  • Different shades of soil sprinkled in image shape
  • Petroglyphs engraved in bare ground with a pointed stick
  • Mud, ashes, or mashed berry pigments on rocks or shells
  • Chalk, flour, turmeric, or cinnamon powder (use sparingly for animal safety)

Ritual Tools and Home

  • Wands, cups, cauldrons, athames, candles, cloths, incense holders, candlesticks
  • Permanent or washable markers, paint, tape, or stickers
  • Glow-in-the-dark paints for added dimension
  • Large canvas paintings for display
  • Paper-mache creations (bracelets, ceremonial masks, piñatas)

Timing

For increasing, strengthening, attracting, or manifesting:

  • Waxing moon
  • Sun rising prior to midpoint

For diminishing, repelling, or weakening:

  • Waning moon
  • Sun going down

Preparation

Surface Preparation

  1. Remove debris and clean the surface
  2. Envision the area as a blank canvas
  3. Hold your hand over it and direct energy from your palm, driving out negativity and adding desired vibrations

For body pictographs: Shower or bathe beforehand, scrub with salt or add sea salt to bathwater. Alternatively, rub a sachet of rose petals or sage over your body.

Tool Charging

  1. Hold the pen, marker, or makeup in your hand (or hold your hand over paint containers)
  2. Think of the purpose of your pictograph
  3. Call in helpful energies: Jupiter for luck, Moon for psychic power, Sun or fertility goddesses for growth
  4. Envision those forces entering your tools and imparting new vibrations

Creating the Pictograph

  1. Envision the aim of your magick being realized
  2. Imagine energies flowing through you and into the pictograph as you draw or paint
  3. Hold the completed image firmly in your mind's eye
  4. Feel energy building, connecting your thoughts and emotions with the physical pictograph
  5. At the height of the energy, direct it in a final blast into the image
  6. Envision the pictograph glowing and pulsing with magickal power
  7. Ground yourself: rub hands with salt or water, or place palms on bare earth

Applications

Pictographs function as:

  • Lucky charms to attract
  • Protective symbols to repel or defend
  • Focal points for concentration
  • Magnifiers for magickal energies

Their magick can be conferred upon place, person, and object alike. Pictographs are a symbolic and magickal language that translates across dimensions.
