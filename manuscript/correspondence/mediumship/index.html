Mediumship is contact with a spirit or spirits that occurs through dream, trance, or altered states of consciousness. These altered states can be quite subtle, we slip in and out of them naturally throughout the day while falling asleep, driving, or washing the dishes.

Mediumship is for Everyone

The popular narrative that mediumship requires a special "gift" is a myth, often perpetuated by people trying to establish authority. Everyone is capable of achieving basic mediumistic contact with their ancestors. They are a part of us and we are a part of them. Mediumistic contact with your ancestors is as natural as your brain communicating with your hands.

Mediumship stories, like fish stories, tend to be embellished. Don't let anyone else's narrative override your own experience.

You May Already Be Receiving Messages

Many people receive ancestral guidance without a framework for recognizing it: an intuition to take a different route, an irrational pull toward a particular color, person, or food, a vague but insistent sense that something must be done a certain way. These are not accidents. They are how the ancestors communicate with those who have no ritual training, through a gradual series of small preferences that shape the direction life moves in over time.

A Weekly Practice

A mediumship session once a week is a sustainable practice. A natural time is Sunday evening, giving thanks for the week that has just passed and preparing for the one to come. This practice supports emotional balance and helps the practitioner navigate life with ancestral guidance.

What Ancestors Advise On

Ancestors tailor their advice to where you are right now. They empathize after having faced many of the same human struggles. Unlike other sources of advice, they understand there is no one right way to be a person. The lessons they have for each practitioner are different.

Common areas of ancestral opinion:

  • The home. Ancestors are like roommates. Keep the space clean and tended.
  • Etiquette and how you treat others. Social mores take on new dimension in ancestral practice. Write thank-you notes. Bring gifts to hosts. Tip well.
  • Decisions that affect the family tree. Marriages, serious relationships, raising children, and handling inheritances. Consider how your decisions uplift or undermine your family name, and what you will leave behind for the generations that follow.

You Are in the Driver's Seat

Listen to your ancestors and honor their opinions, but remember: you are the one who is alive today. After everyone has spoken their piece, you make the final call.

If ancestors become overbearing, gently but firmly remind them that you hold the sacred office of being incarnate.

Integration: The Other Half of Mediumship

Discussions of mediumship often focus on receiving more messages by increasing psychic sensitivity. But perception is only half the battle.

Consider communication with the living: we can now video chat in ultra HD with someone on the other side of the world for free. We perceive more voices than ever before, and yet we're no better at connecting. If anything, 24/7 contact through social media makes us feel more alone.

As mediumistic perception increases, you need a way to process what you receive. The goal of divination isn't to perceive with the most detail or accuracy, but to use information to change your life for the better. What's the point of knowing the future if you're powerless to change it?

Borrowing a term from the psychedelic therapy community: this process is called integration. Fully integrated messages from the ancestors serve as medicine. They help us live better lives full of meaning and beauty, dispel illusion, and break out of destructive patterns.

How to Integrate a Message

Create something inspired by the message. Paint, sing, write, move, cook, whatever modality fits. Don't focus on recording exact details. Instead, try to give concrete form to what you felt. Your creation will:

  • Help you remember the big lessons when you need them
  • Allow you to return to the experience upon reflection
  • Reveal new insights over time, the way a dream yields more details after waking
  • Gain new relevance as your circumstances change

Integrating Messages from Other Mediums

If you are involved in a spiritist community, don't take every message shared by everyone who claims to be a medium seriously. If a medium you have reason to trust shares a message with you, pray to your ancestors and ask them to independently confirm whether it's correct. Otherwise you risk being distracted from the messages that truly matter, or, worse, taken advantage of by a medium or spirit who doesn't have your best interests at heart.

On Synchronicity

The most important messages from the ancestors will find their way to you from multiple unrelated sources, as if to underline themselves. Really important messages arrive through different channels, reinforced by synchronicity. You don't need to immediately turn your life upside-down when you receive a new message, whether directly or through a medium.

Dispelling Myths

Go into mediumship with no expectations so you can be fully present for whatever happens.

Mediumship is not always visual. It's just as likely that you will perceive something with the same inner eye that sees dreams as you will see something clear as day. Others experience it through hearing, feeling, smelling, or tasting. Others through senses beyond the classic five, such as proprioception. And still others simply know things without knowing why, processing spiritual information without it resembling a physical sense at all.

Faint or unclear impressions are still valid. Your conscious awareness of what you receive may be more or less clear. Messages that seem faint or don't immediately make sense are still useful. Mediumship is a lot like looking at art: it's just that the art is inside of you instead of outside of you.

You will not accidentally open a portal to hell. Mediumship is not like horror movies. It carries risks, but so does crossing the street. The biggest dangers lie in how you respond to mediumship, not in the nature of the spirits themselves.

Don't glamorize phenomena. Assuming mediumship experiences must be extreme in order to be real is one of the most dangerous mistakes a practitioner can make. Bragging about dramatic spirit contact usually comes from insecurity. If spirits are keeping you up at night or disrupting your home, that's a bad sign, not a badge of honor. Treat it like a conflict with a loved one: keep it private, figure out what's wrong, and fix it.

General Advice

Mediumship is like divination, but your body is the tool instead of cards. Care for your tool and it will serve you well.

  • Diet. Pay attention to what you eat. Excessive salt, meat, and dairy are commonly reported to reduce psychic sensitivity.
  • Exercise. Physical disciplines that require body awareness, such as dance, martial arts, yoga (asana), or Pilates, prepare the body to be more receptive. Many practitioners find their best mediumship and divination work comes after physical practice.
  • Hydration and breath. Water and air are traditionally associated with spirit contact. Stay hydrated and breathe deeply. Calling in the elements while casting a circle won't help if you stop breathing.
  • Herbal allies. Many herbs have a reputation for enhancing receptivity to mediumship. If you go this route: start with teas or liqueurs prepared by professionals before making your own. Know the risks and contraindications for anything you ingest. Mugwort, for example, is a famous dream herb that can be smoked or brewed as tea, but it is also an abortifacient and should not be ingested by pregnant people.
  • Experiment and track results. There are no universal rules for what a body responds to. Work with a personal trainer or nutritionist if you need guidance, and pay attention to your own patterns.

On Tension and Release

Mediumship often comes in waves of tension and release. Working through what feels like a conflict, facing something you never wanted to face, creates space. Afterward, things flow freely. Have faith that when you are in a tense moment, the flow will return if you stick to your practice.

Exercises

Developing as a medium requires regular effort, but that doesn't mean it has to be ascetic. It can be sensual. It can even be fun.

Sensations: A 5-Day Practice

Sharpening your physical senses will help you process information received through mediumship. Each day for five days, pick a new sense to focus on. Go freeform or use the prompts below as a jumping-off point, but let it lead you.

Day 1: Sight

  • Watch the trees move in the wind. Each part moves differently: the trunk informs the branch, which informs the leaf.
  • Go to an art museum. Find a painting that is all black, then one that is all white. Stay with each. How does each make you feel? Which would you rather stand in front of all day?
  • Draw something using blind contour drawing: never look down at the paper or lift your pen. Set a timer for 5 minutes, then 10, then 20. The point isn't a good result, it's to look at something.
  • Pick a visual cue (a red car, a butterfly) as an alarm clock for conscious awareness. Every time you see it, amp up your awareness.

Day 2: Hearing

  • Listen to your favorite song as though you have never heard it before. What happens inside you at each line?
  • Listen to your favorite song from ten years ago. What do you remember about that period in your life? How was it different from now?
  • Listen to silence. How does it make you feel? Do you keep sound playing all day to avoid it?

Day 3: Smell

  • Make a list of your favorite scents. Why are you fond of them? What memories are associated with them?
  • Visit a botanical garden and smell as many varieties of rose as you can. Does one speak to you?
  • Smell a well-crafted perfume and track how the scent unfolds over time:
    • Top notes shape the first impression. They evaporate quickly (10–15 min) and are often citrus: bergamot, orange, grapefruit, lemon.
    • Heart notes bloom next. They are the core of the perfume and tend to be mellow and floral: lavender, rose.
    • Base notes emerge as the heart fades. Heavy molecules that act as fixatives: woods like cedarwood and sandalwood, or musks.

    Record how each stage affects your emotional state, either in notes or a drawing.

Day 4: Taste

  • Pour yourself a complex drink like wine or whiskey. How many distinct flavors can you find? How do they change as the ice melts?
  • Don't shy away from bitter things. Many medicinal herbs are bitter, and herbalists say the bitter taste is part of the medicine. Try digestive bitters dropped directly on your tongue.

Day 5: Touch & Proprioception

  • Find a soft, fuzzy fabric and a rough, scratchy one. Eyes closed, drag each slowly along the inside of your wrist. Note how each makes you feel.
  • Hold something warm (a cup of tea) against your belly, then replace it with something cool (a glass of ice water). Pay attention to your whole body, not just where you're being touched.
  • Explore proprioception: stand with feet together, hands at your sides. Then gently lie down in the same position. These are the same posture, only your orientation to the earth has changed. Can you feel how gravity is pulling on you differently?

Dérive

The dérive is a practice developed by the Situationists as a remedy against the monotony of everyday life. It is an unplanned journey through a landscape, usually urban, in which participants drop their everyday relationship to space and "let themselves be drawn by the attractions of the terrain and the encounters they find there."

Guy Debord described the phenomenon the dérive is designed to reveal:

The sudden change of ambiance in a street within the space of a few meters; the evident division of a city into zones of distinct psychic atmospheres; the path of least resistance that is automatically followed in aimless strolls... the appealing or repelling character of certain places, these phenomena all seem to be neglected.

In short, the dérive is a tour guided by the unconscious mind. Because it follows the path of least resistance, the direction it takes you reflects something about the psychic landscape. This is useful data for anyone working with spirits of place (genius loci).

It is recommended to undertake a dérive in a small group so you can cross-check your experience with others.

Adapting the dérive for home: Spend an afternoon communing with your living space. Notice where you end up sitting, standing, pacing. Are there places you naturally gravitate toward? Places that seem like they should be comfortable but that you cannot force yourself to inhabit? How do these observations align with other members of your household, or with your pets? Once you understand your natural patterns of occupation, notice when your habits change suddenly. This could be a sign of spirit intrusion and should be brought to your ancestors.

Opening a Fist

Butoh is an avant-garde dance form created by Tatsumi Hijikata in late 1950s Japan. Unlike most dance forms, which force the body into predefined external shapes, Butoh performances come from within, guided by the unconscious through visceral imagery. This exercise is drawn from Butoh training.

Directions:

  1. Make a fist with your right hand.
  2. Open it over the course of three minutes.

Movement that seems simple is the result of complex biomechanical cooperation. Your skeleton provides form. Skeletal muscles work in pairs, one contracting while the other expands. Most movements require many such pairs working together. By slowing down, we become aware of these dynamics, noticing how parts of the body swell, tense, and adjust during what seemed like a smooth motion.

There is no right or wrong observation. What you find is entirely your own.

Discernment of Spirits

St. Ignatius of Loyola, a Spanish Basque priest and theologian, wrote guidance on the discernment of spirits that translates well to mediumship practice.

Good Spirits vs. Evil Spirits

According to St. Ignatius:

  • Good spirits promote the desire to connect with and help others, along with feelings of gratitude, trust, and love. They strengthen, encourage, console, remove obstacles, and give peace to those trying to live in alignment with their purpose.
  • Evil spirits make us feel cut off from others. They stir up anxiety, false sadness, needless confusion, and frustration. In modern psychological terms, their effects resemble depression or anxiety.

Importantly, this is not a simple equation of good = happy and evil = sad. The behavior flips depending on where a person is spiritually:

  • For someone in alignment with their soul's purpose, good spirits are reassuring and evil spirits are annoying.
  • For someone who has drifted away from their soul's purpose, good spirits will disturb and challenge them, trying to shake them out of complacency, while evil spirits will try to keep them distracted and comfortable in their confusion.

Pronoia

The good spirit's work can be reframed as pronoia, the opposite of paranoia: the sense that there is a grand conspiracy working for you rather than against you, that the world is secretly set up to benefit people. A mediumistic message that leaves you feeling loved, supported, and part of something larger than yourself that benefits both you and others is, by St. Ignatius's standard, the work of a good spirit.

Evaluating Messages

When assessing a message received through mediumship, ask:

  • Does this make you feel wrong in your body?
  • Is it painful, but does it make you feel excited to be alive?
  • Does it erode or restore your faith in the world?
  • Does it make you feel alienated, or does it move you toward connecting with your friends and family?

After integration, discernment of spirits is the most fundamental skill for getting the most out of communion with your ancestors.

Backing Up Mediumship with Divination

Professional mediums, diviners, and magical practitioners may wish to use divination to validate and add concrete specificity to messages received. Some religious and magical traditions have specific divination tools used to clarify messages from the dead.

Obi divination (used in Ocha, also called Lukumí and Santería) is one example. In Yorubaland, obi is performed using kola nuts. In Latin America and the Caribbean, it uses four pieces of coconut, thrown and interpreted based on how many land face-up or face-down. The result is a binary yes-or-no response with qualifications about how much work must be done to achieve the desired result. When a bad omen appears, the diviner must take immediate action to remedy the situation.

Journal Prompts

  1. If you know people who work with spirits or identify as mediums, how have their stories influenced your beliefs or practices? Are there any attitudes you adopted from them that you might want to let go of now that you have a rooted ancestor veneration practice?
  2. Have you ever received a message from your ancestors or spirit guides that changed your life?
  3. Are there any messages that you have received from your ancestors or spirit guides that you feel like you haven't fully integrated into your life? Why do you feel that way? What's one small step that you could take today to integrate that medicine?
  4. How do you practice spiritual discernment? How do you work through messages you receive from your unconscious?
  5. Do you practice any forms of divination? Do you ever feel as though divination is a channel for talking to your ancestors or spirit guides? If you haven't felt this in the past, is it something you would like to explore?
  6. Do you pay attention to your dreams? If not, is this something you would like to explore?

The Broken China Boundary Technique

Some practitioners of Afro-Caribbean traditions put out food offerings to their ancestors on broken china to reaffirm the divide between the living and the dead. This is a physical, symbolic assertion of the boundary, a way of making clear who is alive today. A similar technique can be adapted to suit your practice.
