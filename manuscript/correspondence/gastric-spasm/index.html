Cramp and gastric spasm describe involuntary, painful contractions of smooth or skeletal muscle, most often felt in the digestive tract, the abdomen, or the limbs. Aromatic oils with antispasmodic, carminative, and warming properties relax the contracted muscle, soothe the nerves that drive the spasm, and ease the discomfort that follows.

Massage and compress are the two recommended routes. Always dilute essential oils in a carrier before applying to skin. Use 1 to 3 percent dilution for adult abdominal or limb massage, working with slow, warm, sustained strokes over the area. For compresses, add 5 to 8 drops of essential oil to a basin of warm water, agitate, soak a cloth, wring out, and lay over the affected area until it cools. Avoid tarragon and nutmeg in pregnancy, keep cumin off skin exposed to sunlight, take care with cinnamon leaf and black pepper at low dilution to avoid irritation, and consult a qualified practitioner during pregnancy, while nursing, or with chronic spasmodic conditions.

Suggested Application of Oils

  • Massage: Dilute the chosen oil to 1 to 3 percent in a vegetable carrier oil and massage with slow, warm, sustained strokes across the abdomen or affected muscle group. For digestive spasm, follow the natural clockwise path of the colon. For limb cramp, work from the distal end of the muscle toward the heart to support circulation and relax the tissue.
  • Compress: Add 5 to 8 drops of essential oil to a basin of warm water, agitate to disperse, soak a clean cloth, wring out, and lay over the area of cramp or gastric tension. Cover with a dry towel to retain heat, and replace when the compress cools. Repeat as needed until the spasm settles.
