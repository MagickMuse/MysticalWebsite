What Is Spiritual Hygiene?

Just as we tend to our physical bodies, showering, brushing our teeth, wearing perfume, we must also tend to ourselves on the spiritual level. The line between the physical and spiritual is often blurry, and where one ends, the other begins is not always clear.

In modern Hellenic Reconstructionist Paganism (Hellenismos), this type of spiritual pollution is called miasma. Miasma is believed to be especially strong wherever life is strong, near bodily fluids and strong emotions. Spiritual hygiene refers to any practice aimed at restoring the body and spirit to a state of tranquility by removing miasma.

Spiritual hygiene is most effective when applied to:

  • Yourself
  • Your family
  • Your home

Like Attracts Like

In magically operative communities throughout the world, the principle of like attracting like is foundational, and it is especially true when it comes to scent, perhaps because smell is the sense most closely tied to memory.

  • Evil spirits are often believed to be attracted to foul odors, and may be employed in cursing.
  • Elevated spirits are said to be attracted to sweet perfumes, especially floral ones.

As you develop your ancestor veneration practice and become more sensitive to spiritual forces, you will start to notice this principle at work in your daily life. There will be days when you feel in a funk with no obvious cause, on those days, focused spiritual hygiene practice can help lift the weight.

Spiritual Hygiene as Devotion

Beyond its practical benefits, spiritual hygiene is an act of devotion. Your ancestors dwell with you in your home. Keeping it clean and comfortable is one way to honor them.

If you neglect spiritual hygiene, there's a good chance your ancestors will call you out on it in dreams and divination.

Tend to what you already know needs tending, so your ancestors can spend more time advising you on the things you don't know yet.

The Home as Spiritual Ecosystem

Whether it's a house, a mansion, or a studio apartment, your home is a spiritual ecosystem. You are part of that ecosystem. For any individual entity within it to thrive, the whole must be healthy.

This means fostering an environment where the following are healthy, balanced, and in right relationship with one another:

  • You and your living family
  • Your pets and plants
  • Your ancestors
  • The house spirits that dwell in your home

Every home is different and has its own unique physical and spiritual features. Trust your own knowledge of your home above all else.

House Cleansings

Just as a spiritual bath won't help if you immediately step back into a miasma-filled home, spiritual hygiene must also be practiced at the household level. A little daily cleansing prevents spiritual buildup that might eventually require professional intervention.

Mundane Cleaning Matters

Good old-fashioned physical cleaning is the foundation:

  • Sweep the floor
  • Dust the shelves
  • Take out the trash frequently
  • Don't let dirty dishes sit in the sink

The last two are especially important. Food that hasn't been formally offered to your ancestors, and isn't being stored or served to living people, will attract ghosts who aren't receiving offerings elsewhere. Keep the kitchen clear.

Floor Washes

Floor washes serve both physical and metaphysical purposes: they physically clean the floor and clear miasma to invite in blessings. Commercially prepared floor washes are available in botánicas and metaphysical shops.

DIY floor wash method:

  1. Place cleansing herbs (such as lemongrass, which also repels insects) in a muslin bag
  2. Boil for a few minutes
  3. Let cool, then combine with a standard floor cleaning product in a pine or lemon scent (both are commonly used in cleansings)
  4. Work room by room from the back of your home to the front

Windowsill Washes

A personal practice developed with friend Asc Helvetius, especially useful if your home faces a busy street and windowsills accumulate ash and dust quickly.

Recipe: Cinnamon, mint, and bay leaves, soaked (if fresh) or boiled (if dried) in water, combined with a pinch of baking soda. These herbs are associated with money-drawing and success, and their volatile oils also repel insects.

Smoke Cleansings

Miasma exists on a continuum from the purely material (dirt and grime) to the immaterial. Washes address the material end. After washing, burn herbs to clear the subtler manifestations of miasma. Because smoke rises, praying over burning herbs can help deliver prayers directly to the gods.

Boiling Herbs (Smoke-Free Alternative)

If burning herbs isn't possible (rental restrictions, asthma, etc.), boil aromatic herbs such as camphor on your stove. Many spiritists do this before séances, as the scent is believed to call in good spirits and chase out bad ones.

Winter alternative: A radiator humidifier (ceramic or cast iron) placed on a radiator creates a slow, steady output of aromatic steam that requires no monitoring, safer than a boiling pot.

Holy Waters

Holy water (also called lustral water) appears in spiritual traditions worldwide. It can be:

  • Blessed by a religious figure in your tradition or the tradition of your ancestors
  • Made yourself by praying over water
  • Gathered from a sacred natural source

Making Your Own Holy Water

The simplest method: gather water in a bowl and pray over it, to your ancestors or a form of the divine.

Waxing Moon method (popular among witches and Pagans):

  1. Drop a piece of silver (such as a silver ring) into water on the night of the new moon
  2. Pray over it
  3. Leave it out to gather the light of the waxing moon
  4. Collect the water on the night of the full moon

Water made this way carries the energy of the waxing moon: abundance, growth, and prosperity.

Khernips, Greek Holy Water

The ancient Greeks called holy water khernips (χέρνιψ). Traditionally made by extinguishing a torch from a shrine in water. A modern adaptation:

  • Add a small amount of seawater to a bowl of spring water
  • Light a bay leaf or sprig of vervain
  • Drop it into the water to extinguish it, saying: "Xerniptosai!" (zer-NIP-to-sei-ee; "Be purified!")

Catholic Holy Water

Freely available at most Catholic churches in large fonts. Used for blessing and protection. Saint Teresa of Avila wrote: "I know by frequent experience that there is nothing which puts the devils to flight like Holy water." Saint Martha was said to tame the dragon-like Tarrasque using holy water.

Sacred Waters from Nature

Water gathered from healing springs, rivers, and wells carries its own power. Many are dedicated to feminine divinities:

  • Lourdes, France
  • The Ọṣun River, Nigeria
  • The Ganges, India

In European folklore, wells and springs are associated with ancestors and their power to heal, bless, and bestow fertility. Notable example: the Chalice Well in Glastonbury, England, considered a gateway to the spirit world in Welsh and Irish folklore, where one might encounter ancestors, faeries, and other spirits.

Using Holy Water

Regardless of source or method, holy water may be sprinkled on yourself, around your home, or on objects to bless and protect them.

Spiritual Baths

When a stronger remedy is needed than daily practice, a spiritual bath can help. Methods vary by tradition:

  • Soaking bath with full submersion
  • Pouring over the head (common in African-American conjure traditions)
  • Air-drying afterward so ingredients settle on the skin (a gentle pat-down also works)

The right method depends on available space, ambient temperature, and the tradition being drawn from. There is no single correct approach.

Related Concepts

  • Miasma, the Greek Reconstructionist term for spiritual pollution; the substance spiritual hygiene seeks to remove
  • Cleansing, active ritual removal of psychic and spiritual debris
  • Purification, deeper ritual restoration of spiritual purity
  • Hearth Magick, the spiritual work centered in the home
