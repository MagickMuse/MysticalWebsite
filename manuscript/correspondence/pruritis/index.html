Pruritis Therapeutic Index

Definition

Pruritis is the medical term for itching. The Therapeutic Index entry concerns genital and perineal pruritis specifically, the persistent itching of the vulvar, vaginal, or perineal tissues that often accompanies thrush, urethritis, eczema, dermatitis, allergic reactions, or simple irritation from soaps, fabrics, and hygiene products. Aromatherapy supports this condition with antiseptic, anti-fungal, and soothing oils delivered through warm-water douche, which reaches inflamed mucous membranes directly and calms the itch at its source.

Persistent or recurrent genital pruritis warrants medical evaluation, since the symptom can signal infection, allergy, hormonal change, lichen sclerosus, or other dermatological conditions that benefit from direct treatment.
Frequent douching can disrupt the natural flora of the genito-urinary tract, so this method should be reserved for short therapeutic courses, and avoided during pregnancy. Bergamot is phototoxic on skin exposed to UV light, though this matters less for douche applications. Myrrh and juniper must be avoided during pregnancy; juniper must also be avoided in those with kidney disease. Always dilute essential oils thoroughly in warm water before contact with the genital region, and never apply undiluted oil to mucous membranes.

Suggested Application of Oils

  • Douche: Add 2 to 3 drops of essential oil to a cup of warm distilled water with a teaspoon of cider vinegar or fine sea salt, shake well to emulsify, and use as a vaginal douche. Apply once daily for no more than a week. The antiseptic, anti-fungal, and anti-inflammatory action of the oils reaches inflamed mucous membranes directly, easing itch and supporting the body's response to the underlying cause.
