The head and the heart work together as the logical and the emotional aspects of the body as a sacred map. The head stands for the intellect, the mind, wisdom, and reasoned thought, and for ruling power or the "top" of something, as in the Head of State. Because of its spherical shape it is also likened to the Universe. To bow the head before someone is to submit to them, and to nod the head signals assent, at least in the West.

The head and face are the most easily recognized parts of the body, and so were prized as a great trophy in more warlike times. The head removed from the body meant instant annihilation, and for a warrior to return with the head of an enemy was to take on that enemy's potency. The head was a status symbol of war, sometimes preserved in oil so that there could be no doubt of its owner's identity or of the certainty of death. In the same way the head of an animal is held to be the hunter's most valued trophy, a display of human dominion over the animal world, the more savage the beast the greater the kudos of its killer.

In myth, though, not every severed head was lifeless. This reflects the ancient belief that the head holds the true seat of the soul, the essence of the person and of life itself, so that disembodied heads carried great wisdom and could serve as oracles. In the Celtic tale of Bran the Blessed, Bran is beheaded yet his head goes on speaking lucidly, telling his people to bury it at the White Hill in London and promising that so long as it remains there Britain will be safe from invasion; the White Hill is now called Tower Hill. The Norse God Odin is said to have drawn much of his wisdom from the oracular head of an earlier deity, Mimir.

To sever the head from the body was to separate the physical from the spiritual, and the touching of a sword to each shoulder in the ceremony of knighthood recalls this idea.

Religious Associations

The Hindu Goddess Kali is easily known by her necklace of severed heads, said to be the source of her wisdom. They also stand for the letters of the alphabet and form the "beads" of her sacred rosary, the japamala.
