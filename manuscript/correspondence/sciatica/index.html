Sciatica is pain that radiates down the back of the leg along the path of the sciatic nerve, the largest nerve in the body. It is most often caused by compression or irritation of the nerve roots in the lower spine from a herniated disc, spinal stenosis, piriformis muscle tension, or pregnancy-related pelvic shift. The pain may be sharp and electric, dull and aching, or accompanied by numbness, tingling, and weakness in the leg or foot. Aromatherapy supports sciatica through warming, analgesic, and antispasmodic oils applied externally to ease nerve pain and the muscular tension that often compresses the nerve.

Patch-test before broad use. Do not apply to broken or acutely inflamed skin. Spike lavender, sweet marjoram, rosemary, hyssop, nutmeg, and turpentine should be avoided during pregnancy. Those with epilepsy or seizure disorders should avoid rosemary and hyssop. Severe sciatica with loss of bladder or bowel control, or sudden leg weakness, requires immediate medical attention. Dilute essential oils to 1 to 2.5 percent in a carrier oil for massage, and use 5 to 10 drops dispersed in carrier for the bath.

Suggested Application of Oils

  • Massage: Dilute 6 to 15 drops of essential oil per ounce of carrier oil (1 to 2.5 percent) and massage in long, slow strokes from the lower back down the affected leg, following the nerve path. Use lighter pressure over the lumbar spine and avoid direct firm pressure on the most painful point.
  • Bath: Add 5 to 10 drops of essential oil to a tablespoon of carrier oil or unscented bath base, then disperse into a warm bath. Soak for 15 to 20 minutes to ease nerve irritation and relax the muscles of the lower back, hips, and legs.
