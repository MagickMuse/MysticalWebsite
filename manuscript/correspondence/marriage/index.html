In the broadest sense, and especially in alchemy, marriage represents the union of two opposing principles, above all the male and the female.

The idea of a "sacred marriage" runs deep in the ancient world. In Greece, India, and the East, temples were dedicated to sacred sexuality, where ritual intercourse was performed for a religious purpose as part of an old fertility rite; in Hebrew, the word later rendered as "harlot" originally meant a shrine priestess, in effect a holy woman. Times have changed.

A wedding is packed with symbolism, some of it sacred and some more frankly secular. The veil worn over the face, lifted by the partner, symbolizes both the removal of virginity and an introduction to a new state of being. The shared cutting of the cake, the knife held by both partners together, is another phallic allusion, and the wedding ring is a symbol of eternity. In neo-pagan ceremonies such as the handfasting, the symbolism of binding the hands together speaks for itself.

Religious Associations

For Christians, the marriage of two people also symbolizes the union of the believer with the Church, and a Catholic nun is called a "Bride of Christ." The idea that a marriage can signify the union of a person with their God appears in many different cultures.
