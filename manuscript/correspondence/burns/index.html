A burn is an injury to the skin caused by heat, friction, electricity, chemicals, or radiation. Aromatherapy supports the body's recovery from minor first-degree burns and small superficial second-degree burns by cooling the skin, reducing inflammation, calming pain, and helping new tissue regenerate.

Aromatherapy is appropriate only for minor burns. Seek immediate medical care for serious or extensive burns, electrical burns, chemical burns, burns to the face or hands, or any burn that breaks deeply through the skin. Cool a fresh burn under cool (not icy) running water for at least ten minutes before applying anything else. Always patch-test before broader use, and avoid neat application on sensitive skin or for children.

Suggested Application of Oils

  • Compress: After cooling the burn under running water, add 4 to 6 drops of essential oil to a bowl of cool water, swirl, soak a clean soft cloth, wring out, and lay over the burn. Refresh as the cloth warms. Suited to tender or larger areas where neat application would be too intense.
  • Neat application: A small number of essential oils, notably true lavender and tea tree, are gentle enough to apply undiluted to a single minor burn. Place one drop directly on the affected spot. For all other oils, dilute to roughly 2 percent (12 drops per ounce of carrier oil) and gently smooth over the area.
  • Calendula infused oil: Calendula is used as the infused oil rather than a steam-distilled essential oil. Apply directly to the cooled, intact burn following the product's guidance.
