Vertigo Therapeutic Index

Definition

Vertigo is the false sensation of movement, typically a feeling that the self or the surrounding environment is spinning, tilting, or swaying when no actual motion is occurring. It originates in disturbances of the vestibular system of the inner ear, the vestibular nerve, or the central balance pathways of the brainstem and cerebellum. Common triggers include benign paroxysmal positional vertigo, vestibular migraine, labyrinthitis, Meniere's disease, motion sickness, low blood pressure, anxiety states, and exhaustion. Episodes are often accompanied by nausea, sweating, pallor, and the urge to lie still until the spinning settles. Aromatherapy supports vertigo by steadying the nervous system, easing the accompanying nausea, and offering aromatic anchors that the brain can use to reorient itself.

Application Methods

  • Vaporizer/Diffuser: Add 4 to 6 drops to an electric diffuser or aroma vaporizer to perfume the room with steadying, balance-supportive vapor. Useful in the home or workspace where episodes recur, providing a continuous aromatic field that the vestibular system can lock onto.
  • Steam Inhalation: Add 2 to 3 drops of essential oil to a bowl of hot (not boiling) water, drape a towel over the head, close the eyes, and inhale gently for 5 to 10 minutes. The combination of warmth, deep breathing, and concentrated aroma calms the nervous system and eases the nausea that often accompanies vertigo.
  • Tissue or Pillow Drop: Place 1 to 2 drops on a tissue, handkerchief, or aromatic inhaler stick and inhale gently as needed during an acute episode. Keeping the inhaler within reach allows immediate relief at the first hint of dizziness, before the episode escalates.

Safety

Persistent or recurrent vertigo, vertigo accompanied by hearing loss, severe headache, weakness, slurred speech, or neurological symptoms warrants prompt medical evaluation, as it may indicate stroke, vestibular neuritis, Meniere's disease, or a central nervous system cause that requires diagnosis. Aromatherapy supports comfort and nausea relief but does not treat the underlying vestibular condition. Spike lavender, lavandin, and high-camphor oils should be avoided during pregnancy and in epilepsy. Peppermint and high-menthol oils should not be used on or near the faces of children under five. Lemon balm essential oil can sensitize the skin and should not be used neat; for vertigo it is most safely employed via diffusion or tissue inhalation rather than topical application. Steam inhalation should be used cautiously in asthmatics, who may prefer gentle vaporization or a tissue drop. Avoid driving, operating machinery, or any task requiring balance during an active episode regardless of which oil is used.
