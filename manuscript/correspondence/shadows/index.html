The dark shape cast in bright light, the shadow has come to mean far more than a simple physical phenomenon. Since Carl Jung, psychologists have used the word for the unconscious mind, the "dark side," the part of the personality that may be difficult or unwelcome yet remains an inherent part of the psyche.

The shadow comes and goes, a fleeting, insubstantial, ghostly thing, not quite real, a reminder of the illusory nature of the world. Plato amplified this idea in his allegory of the Cave of Shadows, describing what he saw as humanity's lot: a prisoner chained in a cave so that he can look in only one direction, with a fire burning behind and above, watching only the shadows of unseen objects dancing on the wall.

In many parts of the world the shadow is equated with the soul. Traditionally, creatures without a soul have no reflection and no shadow, and to lack a shadow is the mark of one who has sold their soul to the Devil; vampires have neither reflection nor shadow.

The shadow is also a symbol of influence, as when someone is said to cast a long shadow. In witchcraft, the Book of Shadows is the name given to a book of spells and rituals belonging to the craft.

Religious Associations

For followers of the Dharmic religions, the shadow is a symbol of the illusory nature of the world we live in.

In Taoism, the absence of a shadow signals not evil but its opposite: the Immortals cast no shadow, being so transcendent as to be barely physical, and they stood directly beneath the Sun, the source of the light that would have cast it. Here the lack of a shadow is a symbol of peace and harmony, the person placed firmly at the center of their own Universe.
