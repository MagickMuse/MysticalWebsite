Philter and Chalice Tradition

In traditional witchcraft, romantic love workings are governed by the chalice and by philters and potions compounded within it. The chalice is dedicated to the subtler powers of the unseen considered feminine in nature: receptive and enchanting, in polarity to the more aggressive force of the athame or wand. No protective circle is necessary for love operations because the force invoked is benevolent rather than hostile, requiring no sharp magickal boundary. The place of working needs only to be purified and sealed from intrusion.

The witch may invoke the Lady of Delight before these workings by any of her names: Habondia, Hulda, Herodias, Aradia (also Ariadne or Arianne), Diana, Hecate, or Rhiannon. She may be visualized clad in silver, mantled in starred darkness, long-haired, crowned with flowers and corn, with the lunar disk above her brow flanked by two rearing serpents and the white dove on her right hand.

The altar table carries its triangle cover with one angle pointing east, the chalice at the center flanked by lamps, a thurible with love incense, and the compounded philter materials with pestle and mortar. Fresh sweet-smelling flowers, especially roses, are strewn in a circle around the altar triangle.

All philters and love charms make use of either an object link (something belonging to the person being worked on) or a power object: something manufactured and charged by the witch and physically conveyed into the presence of the intended person. Completing the circuit by using both together gives the working its greatest chance of success.

Timing for Love Workings

Friday is the preferred day, at 8am, 3pm, or 10pm, during a waxing moon. For the most traditionally precise timing: the moon should be passing through the first ten degrees of Taurus or Virgo, well aspected to Venus and Saturn. Observing the correct moon phase, day of the week, and time of day, with firmly resolved intention, is the minimum necessary.
