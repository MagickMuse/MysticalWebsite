Fire governs intuition and spiritual pursuits, embodying a choleric temperament characterized by enthusiasm and transformative energy. Those drawn to Fire are typically passionate and driven, finding power in action and creative expression.

The Element of Clarity

For millennia, our ancestors gathered around fires to cook, tell stories, and celebrate life. Because of its warmth, fire represents our passions, enthusiasm, and kinship. Fire also allowed early people to see in the darkness, therefore magicians connect it with clarity, vision, and enlightenment.

Transformation and the Phoenix

When humans discovered fire and learned how to use it, their lives were transformed. Thus the fire element is associated with transformation. Think of the mythical phoenix rising from the flames, a symbol of fiery transformation. If you seek to transform something in your life, whether a relationship, career path, or health condition, tap into fire's dynamic power.

Fire's Volatile Nature

Fire energy moves rapidly and is volatile and unpredictable. Think of how a wildfire can rage out of control or how lightning bursts in the sky. Draw on fire when you want to kick your spell's power up a notch or see rapid results.

Fire Applications:

  • Banish fear
  • See the future
  • Purification spells and rituals
  • Amplify spell power
  • Rapid transformation

Fire Personalities

People who have a lot of fire energy in their makeup tend to be self-confident, passionate, impulsive, outgoing, vigorous, and courageous individuals. Fire conveys inspiration, enthusiasm, vitality, and daring.

Fire in Spell-casting

Fire is the most self-willed of the elements, answering to no one and resisting every attempt at containment. It can heal, charge, scrub clean, and refine. It is the great transformer, and it demands respect above all else. While any element can turn dangerous (floods, mudslides, tornadoes), fire is typically the most hazardous. No amount of spiritual focus replaces common sense fire safety. Never assume that ritual context makes open flame risk free. Stay alert.

Fire is a masculine, yang force. Candle magick is its most popular expression, but magickal bonfires and oil lamps serve the same purpose. Magick mirrors sometimes harness the raw, shape shifting force of flame, creative and destructive in equal measure.

Capnomancy

Capnomancy receives its oracle from images perceived in the smoke of large ritual fires and bonfires. Enhance its divinatory power by burning herbs associated with psychic vision: mugwort, wormwood, Syrian rue, and diviner's sage.

Tephramancy

Tephramancy reads the ashes, cinders, and soot left behind after a fire has gone out. The more sacred the fire, the greater weight the information carries. Once hearth fires or ritual bonfires have cooled, examine the ash for letters, words, or shapes emerging from the remains. No formulated question is necessary; the practice is one of open reception rather than directed inquiry.

Fire in Myth and Landscape

"Just as a candle cannot burn without fire, men cannot live without a spiritual life."
The Buddha

The Shape of Fire

More than any of the other three elements, fire holds both the power to create and the power to destroy. Its symbol is the upright triangle, and the plainest thing humans have built in that shape is the pyramid, whose very name comes from the Greek word for fire. In the natural world its clearest form is the Holy Mountain, the mound of earth that rose up out of the primal waters.

Spirit and Eternal Life

Fire also stands for the spirit and for life itself. We talk of the spark of life as though it were a kind of flame. The eternal flames kept in temples and churches of every denomination, like the Olympic torch, stand for life that never ends. In the Acts of the Apostles the Holy Spirit descends among Christ's disciples as "tongues like flames of fire," and the phoenix, burning itself to nothing, is born again out of the very same fire.

The Purifying Flame

As a cleansing force, fire is the favored way of laying the body to rest in many religions. The flames and smoke carry the spirit upward toward the Heavens while purifying the body through destruction, much as they do for the phoenix. For the same reason fire served as a means of sacrifice, a fitting way to make an offering to the gods. The ashes left once the flames have finished their work carry their own meaning, marking our return to the dust from which the material body is made. As vibhuti, the ash of cow dung burned in the sacred fire, or homa, it is used to mark tilaka, sacred signs of devotion, onto the foreheads and bodies of Hindu ascetics, showing that they have turned away from the lures of the material world toward the spiritual. The Celts put the purifying power of fire to work at Beltane on the first of May, when cattle were driven between two great bonfires in the hope of warding off disease. Later the Great Fire of London, even as it razed much of the city, was held to have burned away the horrors of the plague. We may be baptized in water, yet we also speak of a baptism of fire, a swift and testing ordeal of initiation that leads to enlightenment once its purification is done. In Sanskrit the words for pure and for fire are one and the same.

Fire and Water

A Fulani saying captures the bond between the two elements neatly:

"Fire comes from Heaven, because it goes up. Water comes from Earth, because it comes down as rain."

Where water can lie as still and clear as a mirror, the nature of fire is ceaseless movement, never pausing for a moment until its fuel is spent.

Passion and the Spark

Fire is bound up closely with passion and sexual energy. When people first learned to kindle it, they did so by rubbing two things together with an up-and-down motion whose friction struck the first spark, and the sexual symbolism of the act is hard to miss. Flames raised this way were seen as either demonic or divine, and an old belief held that fire was somehow gestated within the genitals of a sorceress. The erect phallus, too, stands as a symbol of fire.
