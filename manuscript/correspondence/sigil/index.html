Sigils in Magick

Sigils are symbolic designs used to represent and invoke specific spiritual forces. They serve as visual keys, each encoding the essence of a particular entity, and function as tools for summoning, honoring, and meditating upon spirits.

Angelic Sigils

Each angel possesses a specific sigil that can be used to summon them. These sigils are written on paper or engraved onto metal, serving as direct channels of communication with angelic forces.

Veve

The veve designs of Haitian Vodou serve a similar purpose to angelic sigils. Each lwa or spirit has a unique veve that expresses its essence and is worthy of meditation in its own right. Beyond contemplation, veves are also used to summon and honor spirits. While they may be drawn on paper, veves are most frequently rendered on the ground.

Other Spirit Sigils

Candomble and Romany spirits possess their own sigils, as do entities across many other traditions. These designs function in much the same way, encoding spiritual identity and serving as invocational tools.

Rangoli

Rangoli is a women's spiritual art from India that uses rice flour along with brightly colored flowers and spices to create intricate patterns on the ground. As small creatures consume the rice flour, they are believed to carry the embedded prayers and petitions down into the Earth's womb.

Pemba

In Brazil, pemba is a type of chalk that may contain pulverized herbs, used to create invocational markings on the Earth. This practice originates in Africa, and the finest pemba is still believed to come from that continent, sometimes imported and purchased at significant cost. Pemba markings serve as ground-level sigils for summoning and communicating with spirits.

Ground Drawings

Pen and ink represent only one form of magickal writing. Many traditions involve drawing designs directly on the ground, particularly to invite, invoke, and honor spirits. Materials used for these ground drawings include flowers, flour, cornmeal, and special rangoli powder.
