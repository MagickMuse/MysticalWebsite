What Is the Shadow Self?

Swiss psychiatrist Carl Jung developed the idea of the shadow self in his writings from the early to mid-twentieth century. He describes it as the part of the unconscious that contains all of the emotions and facets of ourselves that we repress. These can be faults like dishonesty, selfishness, cruelty, and intolerance, but they can also be more universal traits like laziness, arrogance, childishness, bitterness, desire, and ignorance. According to Jung, everyone has a shadow self and everyone struggles with this part of their identity throughout their entire lives.

The main goal of shadow work is to be better than our shadow while at the same time accepting that we will always be flawed and loving ourselves anyway.

Not All Shadows Are Dark

Life is certainly not all love and light, and this isn't a bad thing. Nature is all about duality: day and night, summer and winter, predator and prey, life and death. Human nature is also dualistic. We experience happiness and sadness, fulfillment and want, abundance and scarcity, growth and stagnation, peace and war, love and hate, and also life and death.

You might feel grief at letting something or someone go even if it was the right choice, or you might feel guilt for hiding a major part of your identity because it was the only safe thing for you to do. Shadow work can help you confront hard truths and fears to pave the way for a more harmonious tomorrow, but you should always handle your shadow self with love and care.

Sometimes there are good reasons why our shadows embody even the good parts of ourselves. You may be shunned for showing compassion toward the black sheep, get called difficult for being assertive, or face criticism for insisting that your needs are met.

Signs Your Shadow Needs Attention

  • Disturbing recurring dreams and nightmares
  • Suddenly developing an irrational fear or phobia
  • Repetition of challenging tarot cards (Tower, Death) in readings
  • Spells going awry for no apparent reason
  • A shadow deity appearing in your life unexpectedly

When to Seek Professional Help

You cannot "magick away" legitimate medical issues such as physical ailments or mental illness. Seek professional help when:

  • Your shadow is interfering with daily life
  • You suspect an untreated medical or mental health condition
  • You're dealing with abuse, addiction, domestic violence, sexual assault, PTSD, unresolved trauma
  • You experience suicidal thoughts or desire to hurt yourself or others

Learned vs. Innate Emotions

Neurobiologists suggest humans are hardwired to experience only four emotions: happiness, sadness, anger, and fear. This means negative emotions such as embarrassment, shame, guilt, and jealousy are all learned behavior. If you feel any of these emotions, they have been taught to you or picked up from your family, culture, or society. That means they can be unlearned.

Another goal of shadow work is to separate your inner feelings and experiences from those imposed on you from outside yourself.

Shadow Allies

Many plants, animals, gods, goddesses, and symbols can be called upon when working with your shadow side. Most are related to shadow energies such as rot, decay, disease, disintegration, destruction, conflict, loss, chaos, fear, night, darkness, and death. You can "flip" their associations into constructive ones like resourcefulness, integration, transformation, regeneration, adaptability, protection, and new beginnings.

Timing

Traditionally, the waning or new moon is the time to work with shadow energy, but it should be done whenever the need arises.

28 Journaling Prompts:

  1. How would you describe yourself?
  2. How would other people describe you? How do you feel about that?
  3. What are your best and worst character traits?
  4. What do you like and dislike about yourself?
  5. What part of you doesn’t feel heard?
  6. What would you like to say?
  7. What makes you happy?
  8. Who do you trust the most? Why?
  9. Do you have issues with trust?
  10. What things trigger you the most? Why are those things triggering for you?
  11. What things are you judgmental about?
  12. How do you pass judgement on yourself?
  13. When is the last time you felt let down? Why?
  14. Who has the most influence over you in your life? Is it a healthy influence?
  15. How often do you lie to yourself and others?
  16. Is there any part of you in denial?
  17. What is your biggest fear? Why?
  18. What irrational fears do you have? Why?
  19. What grudges do you hold? Why? Are they rational?
  20. Do you hold on to any resentment? Towards whom? Why?
  21. What am I afraid of? What shadow allies can I call on?
  22. When did I ignore my intuition that something was "wrong," and what happened?
  23. What disturbing dreams have I had, and what do I think they mean?
  24. What ill omens have I seen recently? How do they make me feel?
  25. If I could go back in time, what is one thing that I would do differently?
  26. When have I used toxic positivity to ignore my shadow?
  27. When did I let go of something that was no longer serving me but then ended up getting no closure?
  28. How else can I use Witchcraft to connect with my shadow self?

Final Thoughts

Remember to love yourself and treat all of your identities, your shadow self included, with gentleness, kindness, and forgiveness. Work with your shadow, but don't drown yourself diving into it. The last goal of shadow work is to integrate all of your selves together. You don't have to be perfect to be beautiful, you don't have to be flawless to be whole, and you don't have to be validated to feel valid. Embrace your shadow side and let it teach you the lessons you need to learn.
