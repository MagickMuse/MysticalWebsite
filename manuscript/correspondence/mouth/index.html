The mouth speaks, eats, and breathes, three vital functions of an essential organ. Yet the mouth is also a cave-like hollow resembling the vagina, and because of this likeness it often carries sexual meaning, with vulva and mouth treated as synonyms the world over. The most fearsome of these images is the "vagina dentata," the vagina imagined with teeth like a mouth, able to bite off the penis; like a mouth, the vagina has lips, the labia.

As the cave holds hidden secrets, so does the mouth, here in the form of words. Words carry great power, and the mouth shares that power as the home of the sacred sounds and syllables it shapes. Because of the magickal power of sound and speech, gagging the mouth as an initiatory rite is a symbol of secrecy, of that which must not be spoken.

The mouth can be shown as a gentle pair of lips that kiss and whisper secrets and sweet nothings, or as a greedy, gaping maw that devours and destroys whatever enters it.

In ancient Egypt a ceremony called the Opening of the Mouth was performed on every corpse before burial. The priest opened the mouth with two implements, one a hook and the other a small golden tool shaped like a finger, so that the dead could speak the truth before the deities who would question them in the next world.
