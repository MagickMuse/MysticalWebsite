Whooping cough (pertussis) is a highly contagious bacterial infection of the respiratory tract caused by Bordetella pertussis. It is marked by paroxysms of intense, uncontrollable coughing that often end in a long, high-pitched "whoop" as the person gasps for breath, followed by exhaustion and sometimes vomiting. Early symptoms resemble a common cold (runny nose, mild cough, low fever), but within one to two weeks the cough becomes severe and persistent and can last for weeks or months. The illness is most dangerous in infants and young children, in whom it can cause pneumonia, apnea, and other serious complications.

Safety: Whooping cough is a serious, notifiable disease. Seek medical evaluation; treatment is with antibiotics, and vaccination remains the primary preventive measure. Aromatherapy is supportive only and does not replace medical care, especially for infants, young children, the elderly, or anyone with a compromised respiratory system.

Several of the oils below carry their own cautions. Hyssop is moderately toxic (pinocamphone content) and must be avoided in pregnancy and epilepsy and used only in moderation. Clary sage and Spanish sage should be avoided during pregnancy. Asafetida should be avoided during pregnancy and may interact with anticoagulants. Mastic can cause sensitization in some individuals. Rosemary should be avoided in pregnancy, with high blood pressure, or with epilepsy, and not used on or around children under five. Turpentine should be used only in low dilution and avoided altogether by anyone with sensitive skin or kidney issues. Always patch test, dilute appropriately, and consult a qualified aromatherapist.

Suggested Application of Oils

Massage: Dilute 2–3% in a carrier oil and massage gently into the chest, upper back, and throat using slow upward strokes. Supports drainage, calms spasm, and helps loosen the cough between paroxysms. Best applied between coughing fits rather than during one.

Steam Inhalation: Add 1–2 drops of a chosen oil (or a small blend, such as lavender with niaouli) to a bowl of hot, not boiling, water. Drape a towel over the head and inhale the steam for 5–10 minutes. Useful for opening airways and thinning mucus. Skip steam inhalation for infants, young children, and anyone with asthmatic tendency or active wheeze.
