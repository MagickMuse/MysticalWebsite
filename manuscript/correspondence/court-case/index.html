Closed Practice Notice: Court Case magick is rooted in Hoodoo, an African American folk magick tradition with deep roots in West African, Indigenous, and European spiritual practices. Hoodoo is a closed practice. It belongs to the communities who developed it through generations of survival, resistance, and spiritual ingenuity. If you are not part of those communities, approach with respect: study its history, support its practitioners, and do not appropriate its methods as your own.

What Is a Court Case Spell?

"Court Case" is Hoodoo terminology for any spell with a legal or justice-oriented aim, regardless of whether the working involves actual courtroom proceedings. The courthouse itself functions as a metaphor in this tradition: a crossroads where fortunes are won or lost, shifted by forces both seen and unseen. Court Case spells are ultimately about seizing that turning point and directing it toward a favorable outcome.

Beyond literal legal situations, these workings can be understood as attempts to maintain personal autonomy against unwanted authority or outside interference with one's plans.

What Falls Under This Category

Court Case spells are among the most varied in magical tradition, covering a wide range of situations:

  • Spells to avoid jail or secure a release from prison
  • Spells to keep someone imprisoned
  • Workings to influence the sympathies of judges, juries, attorneys, or expert witnesses
  • Spells to strengthen one's own testimony
  • Spells to hinder an opponent's ability to speak freely or tell the full truth
  • Justice spells petitioning the universe or spirits to ensure that right prevails

Cultural Context

Court Case spells appear most densely in traditions from cultures where legal justice is an accessible concept: where there is at least the theoretical possibility that a person can appeal to a formal system for redress. Practitioners from traditions that lack access to legal recourse have less reason to develop this category of working.

The Inherent Challenge

Court Case magick operates under a notable tension: in any contested legal proceeding, it is entirely possible that all parties present are working magick simultaneously. Whether or not this cancels out depends on the tradition's view. One perspective holds that the most skilled practitioner, or the one with the most potent materials and strongest spiritual backing, will prevail. Another perspective sidesteps the contest entirely by turning to justice spells, which ask spirit forces to ensure that the outcome reflects what is genuinely right. For those certain they are in the right, this can be a meaningful starting point.

Protection Against Magical Influence

Those who sit in courtrooms in official capacities, such as judges, attorneys, and jurors, are considered natural targets for magical influence in this tradition. A sprig of fresh rue worn on the body, or a cimaruta (the Italian silver charm shaped after rue), is a traditional ward against such influence.
