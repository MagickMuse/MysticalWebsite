♂️Foreskin

Type
Body

Removing the foreskin, known as circumcision, might be seen as a form of mutilation, yet evidence of the practice reaches back to the Stone Age, and for many it is regarded as correct and in the best interests of the man or boy concerned.

Several explanations are given. Apart from religious or spiritual reasons, the removal of the foreskin is believed to be hygienic and is sometimes said to help prevent sexually transmitted infections and genital cancers, though the evidence for these claims is not conclusive. On a more symbolic level, for some the cutting away of the foreskin is a statement of detachment from the sexual and material self, a severing of the bond between God and matter, much as a baby's umbilical cord is cut. There is also a sacrificial element, propitiating the Gods through the removal of a part of the body essential to the survival of the species.

Circumcision is perhaps best known as a Jewish practice, but it is also carried out by other peoples, such as the Dogon and the Bambara of Africa. They hold that the foreskin embodies the material form of the female soul within the man, and that removing it restores his full masculinity, making circumcision a symbol of initiation into manhood; the foreskins are said to be transformed into "sun lizards."

In the Jewish faith, the foreskin is removed as a sign of God's covenant with the people of Israel. Religious law makes it obligatory for Jewish males to be circumcised, unless it would put life at risk, and it is carried out on the eighth day after birth. In the Roman Catholic calendar, January 1 is known as the Feast of the Circumcision, as the day on which Christ would have undergone the rite. As a holy relic the foreskin of Christ became a potent symbol, and as many as eighteen "Holy Prepuces" appeared around Europe in the Middle Ages.
