👁️Open Sesame Third Eye

Dance is a form of wordless communication, carrying with it great freedom and spontaneity. To dance is a joyous act, celebrating the union of body and soul and an expression of the spirit.

Dancing in a repetitive, rhythmic way allows the dancer to forget the self and become one with the rhythm and the music; in this way dance can carry a person into a state of ecstasy or trance.

Every gesture, foot position, and movement of the eyes can be charged with meaning. Human beings carry within them a rhythm, sometimes called the nada or "sound of power," echoing the beat of the heart.

Dance may look like an act of pure spontaneity, yet effortless movement comes only with discipline and practice, as anyone who has tried even a simple dance such as the waltz will know. It is a way of playing the body as an instrument.

Used to send participants into trance and to invite spirit possession, dance has a great part to play in Vodou ceremonies.

Religious Associations

Although many places of worship might seem to have no connection with dancing, sacred dance runs through many traditions. In Christian tradition, Christ has been called the "Lord of the Dance." The Hindu God Shiva, in his form as Nataraja the Cosmic Dancer, performs a dance that encompasses the whole of creation and the primordial sound, aum. The Tantric Buddhist deity Buddha Amoghasiddhi is likewise called the "Lord of the Dance." In India, dance is woven into the yogic discipline that seeks to unite body, mind, and soul with the harmony of the Universe, a practice rich in symbolism in which every gesture, or mudra, is deeply meaningful. The Whirling Dervishes of the Sufi tradition perform a turning dance, or sema, whose name speaks of listening; it expresses the connection between the movements of the dance and the universal sound, and does not always involve the distinctive whirling.
