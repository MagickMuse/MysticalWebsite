Correspondence Grimoire

💪Arm

Type
Body

Arms convey strength and protection, and they also stand for safety and justice, as in the phrase "the strong arm of the law."

Some deities are shown with more than the usual two arms. Brahma is depicted with four arms to express omnipotence, and Shiva with many arms as a sign of action, energy, and accomplishment. The Egyptians shared this idea that the arm signifies activity, and used it to convey the concept in their hieroglyphs.

In Celtic legend, the priest-king Nuada lost an arm in battle. Because he could no longer rule, he was replaced by Bres, whose reign proved a disaster. Bres was forced to surrender power, and Nuada returned to the throne once a working arm of silver had been fashioned for him. The restored arm symbolized the restoration of his leadership and authority.

For the Bambara of Africa, the forearm symbolizes the communication between the divine and humankind. It is this part of the arm that carries food to the mouth, through the pivotal action of the elbow, itself a symbol of the divine nature. The forearm therefore also represents the cubit, the distance between humanity and God. That measurement is reckoned at twenty-two fingerbreadths, matching the Bambara belief in twenty-two categories of created beings, twenty-two also being the number of the Universe. The same number appears in the Tarot, as the count of cards in the Major Arcana.

Raising the arms to the sky suggests a passive state of acceptance and is often used as a spiritual gesture, opening the whole body to heavenly influences and setting aside the will normally exerted through the arms and hands. In a similar way, prisoners raise their arms to show they hold no weapons. More than a practical act of submission, this is also a symbol of spiritual and physical defenselessness, and of surrender.
