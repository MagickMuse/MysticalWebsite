Wounds encompass cuts, sores, and other breaks in the skin that benefit from antiseptic, vulnerary, and tissue-regenerating essential oils.

Wounds

⚠️
Deep, infected, or non-healing wounds need medical evaluation. Several of these oils carry safety precautions: avoid hyssop, eucalyptus (blue gum), juniper, clove bud, lavandin, frankincense, and myrrh during pregnancy. Hyssop should also be avoided by those with epilepsy, and juniper by those with kidney disease. Do not use eucalyptus on children under six. Use clove bud and turpentine in low dilutions only. Bergamot is phototoxic, so avoid sun exposure on treated skin. Patch test peru balsam, tolu balsam, mastic, and vetivert, which can cause sensitization in some individuals.

Suggested Application of Oils

  • Skin oil or lotion: Dilute the chosen oil in a carrier such as jojoba, sweet almond, or calendula-infused oil and apply to and around the cleaned wound to support tissue repair and prevent infection.
  • Compress: Add a few drops to a bowl of warm or cool water, soak a clean cloth, wring it out, and apply gently to the wound area.
  • Bath: For wounds across larger areas of the body, dilute drops of essential oil into a small carrier (such as a tablespoon of sweet almond oil or whole milk) before adding to a warm bath.

Cuts & Sores

Cuts and sores are minor breaks in the skin that benefit from antiseptic and vulnerary oils to clean the area and support tissue repair.

⚠️ Avoid hyssop, sweet fennel, lavandin, common thyme (red type), Spanish sage, eucalyptus, clary sage, and myrrh during pregnancy. Do not apply eucalyptus to children under six. Use red thyme and clove at low dilutions only.

Suggested Application of Oils

  • Skin oil or lotion
  • Compress
