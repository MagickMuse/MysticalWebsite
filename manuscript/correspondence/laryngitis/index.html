Laryngitis Therapeutic Index

Definition

Laryngitis is inflammation of the larynx (voice box), producing hoarseness, voice loss, throat soreness, and a raw, dry, or tickly cough. Acute laryngitis usually follows a viral upper respiratory infection or vocal strain; chronic laryngitis is tied to ongoing irritation from smoke, reflux, allergens, or sustained vocal overuse. The voice may drop to a whisper or disappear entirely until the inflammation resolves.

Indicated Oils

Application Methods

Steam Inhalation (primary): Add 2 to 3 drops of essential oil to a bowl of hot (not boiling) water, drape a towel over the head, close the eyes, and inhale gently for 5 to 10 minutes. The warm vapor soothes inflamed mucous membranes, thins secretions, and delivers anti-inflammatory and antiseptic compounds directly to the larynx. Repeat 2 to 3 times per day during acute episodes.

Vaporizer/Diffuser: Add 4 to 6 drops to a diffuser to perfume the room with anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial vapor. Especially useful overnight when steam inhalation is not practical.

Tissue Inhalation: Place 1 to 2 drops on a tissue or handkerchief and inhale gently throughout the day. A gentler option for those who find direct steam too intense.

Throat Compress (supportive): A warm compress with 1 to 2 drops of a soothing oil (lavender, sandalwood, frankincense) in a basin of warm water, wrung out and laid across the throat, can ease inflammation alongside steam work.

Safety

Laryngitis lasting more than two weeks, or accompanied by high fever, difficulty breathing, difficulty swallowing, blood-streaked sputum, or a lump in the neck, warrants medical evaluation; persistent hoarseness can signal more serious throat or vocal-cord conditions. Aromatherapy supports recovery but does not replace medical care.

Avoid steam inhalation entirely in young children, asthmatics, and anyone prone to bronchospasm; switch to gentle diffusion or tissue inhalation. Thyme (especially red thyme and the thymol/carvacrol chemotypes), Spanish sage, and high-camphor oils (clary sage, lavandin, spike lavender) should be used in low dilution and avoided during pregnancy and with epilepsy or seizure disorders. Cubeb internal use is contraindicated in nephritis. Caraway and jasmine should be avoided during pregnancy. Frankincense and benzoin may sensitize cross-reactively; patch test before topical use.

Rest the voice. Whispering can strain the larynx as much as speaking; quiet breathing and silence are kinder to inflamed cords than forced whispering. Hydrate generously. Avoid smoke, alcohol, and excessive caffeine, which all dry the throat further.
