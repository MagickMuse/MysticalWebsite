The word "mystery" comes from the Greek musterion or the Latin mysterium and means, quite literally, "secret doctrine" or "secret rite." The only ones who could take part in such rites were the mystes, the "initiated." In this sense Freemasonry is a mystery religion, and for many so is Wicca.

In the ancient world the mystery cults were those whose secrets were closely guarded and forbidden to the public. Those admitted to their inner workings formed an elite, an inner sanctum, and the secretive nature of these groups generally meant that a new member could be accepted only if vouched for by existing initiates. Because of this secrecy there was little or no written material describing their workings; their secrets were passed on orally, held to be the more fitting way to preserve the Arcanum, or secret wisdom.

The lack of definitive information about the ancient Mysteries has kept them a continuing source of intrigue. The worship of the Goddesses Isis and Cybele took the form of mystery cults, as did the Orphic, the Mithraic, and the Eleusinian mysteries.

Religious Associations

Most major religions keep an inner sanctum of arcane knowledge. In Judaism this is the Kabbalah, and the inner sanctum of the Ka'aba at Mecca is reserved for the mysteries of Islam.
