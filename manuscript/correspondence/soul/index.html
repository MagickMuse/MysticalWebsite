Mind, body, and soul: the essential trinity that describes what we are. Yet the soul is hard to pin down. Does it exist outside the physical body, and if so, in what form? The belief in a "ghost" that leaves the body at the moment of death is found across religions and cultures and is closely tied to the breath as the essence of life.

The Latin animus, the Greek anemos, and the Sanskrit aniti all mean "breath" or "air" and refer to the soul as an animating force. The Celts called the soul the anamon, a word closely linked to their word for harmony and to the Mother Goddess Ana.

The immortality of the soul is a deeply rooted idea, though beliefs about it vary widely. Some hold that the disembodied soul lingers to haunt the places it knew in the body, occupying a kind of parallel world, while some cultures account for the soul's different aspects by holding that each person has several spirits. The Buryats, an ethnic Mongolian people, believe that one soul goes to heaven or hell, one remains on earth as a mischievous spirit, and a third is reborn in another body. Birds and winged creatures such as moths and butterflies are believed to carry the soul of the dead, their wings symbolizing transcendence.

Throughout history people have tried to locate the seat of the soul in the body. It was once thought to lie in the heart; the seventeenth-century philosopher Rene Descartes placed it firmly in the pineal gland, the small gland in the brain that, among other things, produces melatonin. In yogic practice and metaphysical belief the pineal gland is linked with the third eye, a mysterious inner eye that, once awakened, is said to allow telepathic communication.

The idea that the soul can be bought or sold is so old that its origin cannot be traced. One who sells their soul to the Devil is said to lose both shadow and reflection, each tied to the idea of the soul or spirit. To be called soulless is to be thought less than human, without passion, emotion, or conscience.

Religious Associations

Across organized religions, beliefs about the soul's fate vary: it may be recycled through reincarnation, or absorbed into a collective "oneness" that is part of the Godhead, while others hold that it is sent to a heaven or a hell according to its actions in life.

Catholic missionaries once spoke of gathering collections of souls that had been "saved."
