As the pillars that support the rest of the body, the legs are symbols of strength and stability. Because they carry us from place to place and remove barriers, they are also symbols of communication and movement. To say that something "has legs" is to say it is full of potential and will endure.

The emblem of both the Isle of Man and Sicily, the Triskele of three conjoined legs, also called the Trie Cassyn, is of ancient origin. It appears on coins from Asia Minor in the fifth century BCE, and in connection with the Isle of Man from the thirteenth century onward. It also appears some three hundred years earlier on coins minted in honor of the Norse king Anlaf, who ruled both the Isle of Man and Dublin. Like other triskele forms, these three Manx legs are a solar symbol, and they must always appear to "run" clockwise, since the reverse direction is held to be malevolent.
