Invisible yet essential, breath represents the soul or spirit and the animating life-force. In the biblical story, God brings Adam to life by breathing into Adam's nostrils, sharing the divine breath. In Taoist belief, nine original "breaths" entwined together to create physical space and so the prerequisite for everything that exists in the Universe. Hindu belief similarly sees breath, or atman, as the factor that unifies everything. Like the soul, the breath is held to be immortal and unchanging, yet when it is taken away the physical body perishes. Yogis believe that control of the breath, or pranayama, is the key to both health and enlightenment, an idea shared by Chinese mystics.

Taking in breath is quite literally equated with inspiration, when an idea comes to the willing recipient as though from a divine source. Breathing out is equated with the idea of death.

Breath is also equated with sound, the two being inseparably linked in the act of Creation. For the Druids, the breath carried magickal properties, and breathing or blowing onto something forms part of magickal practice in mystical traditions all over the world. In Persia, amulets were given their power when the sorcerer blew onto them, the final and most vital part of the spell.
