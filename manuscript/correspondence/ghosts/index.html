Death Spells: Death, Ghosts, Necromancy & Vampires

Ghosts are souls of the dead who remain on the earthly plane, haunting the living because they have unfinished personal business. Something or someone keeps them tied to the realm of the living. Some ghosts reputedly linger because they are unaware they are dead. Something in their transition from life to death was botched. Beyond their presence, which may be inadvertently frightening, these ghosts do not intentionally impact the living.

Some ghosts seek justice or vengeance, but not all are innately harmful. They may remain to protect loved ones or simply linger in their presence. Others feel protective or attached to a specific location or object. Once a ghost's unfinished business is complete, it usually leaves and may never be heard from again, though some are very persistent.

Soul Beliefs and Ghost Formation

Many metaphysical traditions reject the idea of a singular soul, believing instead that each person carries multiple souls, each with a distinct function. One may travel during dreams, another guards the body, and another is destined for reincarnation. Ghosts may represent one of these soul aspects that fails to complete its intended transition. In traditional Hmong belief, the soul is understood as a trinity: one part stands guard at the grave, one journeys to the realm of the dead, and a third undergoes reincarnation.

In ancestor-venerating traditions, death opens a crossroads: will the soul transform into a loving, guiding ancestral spirit, or will it become a ghost? If it becomes a ghost, will it be a peaceful presence content to linger near the living, or will it turn mischievous, resentful, or actively troublesome? The road taken at that crossroads may depend directly on the actions of the living: whether proper funeral rites were given, whether the body was treated with respect, and whether necessary spiritual precautions were observed.

Those who die violently far from home, or whose funerals or graves are neglected, have the greatest potential to become wandering or semi-malevolent ghosts. It is never too late to lay a ghost, however, and many rituals exist worldwide for propitiating souls who were not given proper rites.

Honoring the Forgotten Dead

Many cultures set aside specific times to care for wandering ghosts alongside their own ancestors. During the Chinese Festival of Hungry Ghosts, held in the seventh month of the lunar calendar, paper offerings are burned for one's personal ancestors, with extra offerings set aside to appease wandering ghosts who have no family to remember them. In Mexico, the second of November, the Day of the Dead, is a national holiday during which families visit cemeteries, tend graves, and make offerings. Many add candles and gifts specifically for forgotten souls, those who have no one to welcome or care for them. These may be placed on a family altar or left by the roadside for passing ghosts.

Detecting Ghost Presence

Ghosts signal their presence in characteristic ways. Candles that burn dim, low, or with a blue flame may indicate a ghost nearby. An unexpected and significant drop in temperature, a cold spot, is a common signal. Ghosts may also announce themselves through a specific fragrance, a kind of aromatic calling card personal to them in life.

Peaceful coexistence with a ghost requires no spells. Many homes feature a resident ghost who wants nothing more than to linger in the presence of loved ones. If a ghost is not causing harm, there is generally no need to exorcise it. Attempting to do so may antagonize a previously peaceful spirit and create problems where none existed.

Magickal Work with Ghosts

There are two broad categories of ghost spells. The first provides protection from troublesome or hostile ghosts and keeps them at a distance. The second seeks to access ghosts and their particular powers or to ask them to perform specific tasks on behalf of the living.

Negotiating with Ghosts

Laying a ghost can be a cooperative process. Rather than presuming the needs, intentions, or desires of a ghost, it is possible to negotiate directly. Seek to understand why the ghost is present, what its cultural post-death expectations might be, and what steps could transform a troublesome ghost into a protective ally.

Summoning Ghosts and Seeking Cooperation

Not all ghostly visitations are unwelcome. Some ghosts visit out of care and concern for the living, and their help can be genuinely useful. Before automatically repelling a ghost that has not yet proved hostile, it may be worth determining who it is and why it has appeared. The dead may also be summoned deliberately: for protection, to perform tasks impossible for the living, or for divination and communication. Spells for summoning the dead for informational purposes fall under Necromancy.

Ghosts and Saints

Discussion of traditional beliefs about ghosts sheds light on the sacred power of saints. Many saints are praised specifically for guarding against ghosts and other restless dead. Horror movies to the contrary, baneful ghosts are not the norm. Most lingering souls of the dead are not dangerous.

Mesopotamian Ghost Practices

The ancient Mesopotamians had a sophisticated tradition of both repelling and appeasing ghosts depending on the circumstances. Ghosts most likely to cause trouble were those given irregular funeral rites, those no longer receiving post-funerary offerings, those who died violently or by execution, and those left unburied entirely.

To banish a ghost through symbolic confrontation: burn an image representing the ghost. This serves as a direct warning that stronger action will follow if the haunting continues.

For peaceful coexistence with a ghost that cannot or should not be expelled: pour regular offerings of flour and water on a set schedule. Traditionally, water was poured from an ox horn or hoof, and horn-shaped beakers are considered an appropriate modern substitute.

Negotiating With Ghosts

Laying a ghost to rest does not always require banishment. Rather than assuming a ghost's needs or intentions, a practitioner may negotiate directly: asking why the ghost is present, what its post-death cultural expectations are, and whether it can be transformed from a troublesome presence into a protective ally. If direct communication is difficult, necromantic techniques may be used to open a channel across realms.

Dumb Supper Traditions

A Dumb Supper is a ritual meal shared between the living and the dead. "Dumb" refers to silence, not intelligence: the entire meal and any communication are conducted without speaking. The living prepare and serve the food; the dead are formally invited to attend. While Dumb Suppers may be performed at any time, they are traditionally scheduled on Halloween, when the boundary between realms is at its thinnest. The host may extend specific invitations to named individuals or leave the guest list open.

Contrast with Other Active Dead

Unlike vampires, ghosts do not prey upon the living or drain life energy. Unlike ancestors, ghosts are not necessarily related to those they haunt. Unlike spirit guides, ghosts typically have their own unfinished business rather than dedicating themselves to helping the living. Unlike saints, ghosts do not produce miracles or respond to petitions.
