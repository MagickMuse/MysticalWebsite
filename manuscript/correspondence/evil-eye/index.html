What Is the Evil Eye?

The Evil Eye is not a spell. A malevolent spell is targeted deliberately and intentionally; the spell must actually be cast. The Evil Eye is something different: an ill-defined, somewhat nebulous, but entirely malefic force naturally present in the universe. Although there is general agreement on what constitutes the Evil Eye, who is vulnerable, and the nature of its destructive effects, exactly where the Eye emanates from is subject to heated metaphysical debate.

Two major perspectives exist, and they are not mutually exclusive:

  • As abstract force: The Evil Eye is an omnipresent destructive force like an infectious cloud, that one must attempt to avoid, ward off, repel, minimize, or remove. Try as you may to hide happiness and good fortune, the Evil Eye is attracted, sees all, and spoils it.
  • As human force: The Evil Eye is a destructive power that emanates from certain, or potentially all, people. It is the human ability to scorch with a glance, the force of envy, jealousy, and resentment that shoots out of the human soul like a heat-seeking missile, sometimes knowingly and sometimes completely involuntarily.

Avoiding the Evil Eye

Two primary methods exist:

  1. Don't boast, brag, or call undue attention to your good fortune.
  2. Protection through magickally empowered amulets and spells.

Because the secrets of other hearts are unknown, discretion is counseled regarding personal matters. What you might never construe as bragging may still invoke the Eye from another. The bereft or heartbroken person, overhearing someone complain about abundance they themselves desperately lack, may involuntarily cast the Eye. It just shoots out.

The Jettatura

In southern Italian tradition, the jettatura is a person who knows they cast the Eye because they do it constantly and cannot stop. It is simply a force that emanates from them: everything they look at turns to disaster. Glance at a building: within 24 hours it burns down. Glance at a champion cow: by nightfall it's sick and dying.

They are not necessarily bad people. They may even be very good people. Even a pope was once branded a jettatura; people fled his benevolent gaze anyway. There are magickal solutions for the jettatura and for those who fear them.

If you are afraid that you are a jettatura, allegedly focusing your vision onto your left nostril incapacitates the Eye. You may look odd but no one gets hurt.

Who Is Most Vulnerable?

Although anyone may be struck by the Evil Eye, not everyone is equally at risk. Those considered exceptionally vulnerable include:

  • Brides
  • Babies and young children
  • Pregnant women
  • Horses

Men are not inherently vulnerable, but their fertility, genitals, and reproductive capacity are.

Names Across Cultures

The Evil Eye is recognized across many cultures and languages:

  • Italian: mal d'occhio
  • Hebrew: ayin hara ("evil eye")
  • Arabic: ayin ("the eye")

Anti-Evil Eye amulets invariably take the form of eyes. In Mediterranean lands, they are almost always blue eyes, reflecting the historical suspicion of blue eyes in those regions. In Northern Europe, dark skin was suspect. Redheads, frequently a minority, are often targeted as possessing the Eye.

This historical use of the Evil Eye concept to persecute minorities reflects little true magickal knowledge. It represents either a fundamental misunderstanding of the Eye as a phenomenon or the complete degeneration of a magickal system. Real magick makes discrimination unnecessary. The Evil Eye either emanates from the universe and not from any individual, or every individual potentially casts it. There is also some belief that the Tenth Commandment's prohibition against coveting refers to deliberate casting of the Evil Eye.

Signs of Attack

  • Sudden disaster: Everything was going well and then, without warning, a major problem or cascade of problems hits like a bolt of lightning. A normal pregnancy suddenly isn't. A healthy child suddenly has a health crisis.
  • Malaise: Consistent lack of energy and interest; wasting illnesses that resist conventional diagnosis or treatment.
  • Stubborn, persistent head or body lice.
  • Sudden, excessive biting of the lips, especially during sleep.
  • Blocked fertility, though this is only one of many possible metaphysical causes for difficulty conceiving or carrying a child.

Dealing with the Evil Eye

There are three distinct concerns when addressing the Evil Eye: prevention (so the Eye cannot be cast upon you), repelling (so that even if cast it bounces off without causing harm), and removal (of the Eye and its effects once it has taken hold).

Key principles:

  • The most common preventive method is carrying magickally charged charms and amulets.
  • Many Evil Eye amulets also ward off other dangers: malevolent magick, spiritual attack, and similar threats, since each type of vulnerability tends to open the door to others.
  • Because the Evil Eye is typically inflicted during personal contact, most frequently outside the home, most protective charms are mobile: amulets and conjure bags that travel with the wearer.
  • Because one can never be sure when or from what direction the Eye will be cast, many anti-Evil Eye spells take the form of amulets that, once charged, will repel the Eye independently, without requiring further action from the spell-caster.
  • Anyone or anything perceived as exceptionally vulnerable (babies, brides, new cars, new homes) may be protected before the Eye falls upon them.
  • Evil Eye removal spells frequently require the participation of another person to perform the working on behalf of the afflicted. Always select ritual assistants carefully. The target of the spell is the person afflicted with the Eye, not whoever cast it.

Body Defiance

Just as certain people and objects are inherently vulnerable to the Eye, certain images, objects, and botanicals are perceived as the Evil Eye's natural enemies, repelling, removing, defying, and destroying the Eye by power of their very nature, none more so than images depicting parts of the human anatomy.

It takes an eye to defeat an eye. Eye-shaped amulets match the Evil Eye's glance, distract it, and overpower it. Natural stones or crystals that vaguely or literally resemble eyes are valued but rare. Because the Evil Eye is so omnipresent, people developed more accessible talismans: glass beads that look like tiny, perpetually open eyes, favored worldwide, especially in blue.

Blue is an important color indicating spiritual protection. Doors, ceilings, and amulets are customarily painted blue for the same reason. That blue eye beads protect from the Evil Eye does not indicate that blue-eyed people are any more likely to cast it. The most famous and accessible blue eye bead is the Turkish nazar boncuk. These round blue beads may be used independently or strung together to form shapes, especially horseshoes.

Blue eye beads' power is enhanced by cleansing, charging, and consecrating them before use and whenever you perceive that they've worked for you. Methods of use: wear a single bead around the neck; pin individual beads using safety pins; sew them onto clothing or anything else needing protection; hang from a rear-view mirror. If a bead cracks, it is understood to have intercepted a direct hit from the Evil Eye. Bury it with honor and replace it.

Formula Waters

Several formula waters carry a natural power to repel the Evil Eye: Holy Water, Indigo Water, Marie Laveau Water, and Notre Dame Water. A substantial quantity of one or a blend of several can be added to a bath for full-body protection. For spatial cleansing, a wand made from rue may be dipped into the water and used to asperge a room, dispersing the Eye's influence throughout the space.

Feigning Aggression

Many Evil Eye workings operate not by brute force but by threatening the Eye's own sense of self-preservation. The Eye recoils from the prospect of being poked or punctured, just as any creature flinches from a perceived threat. This is why sharp objects, horns, pointed fingers, and gestures that mime striking at an eye all function as effective amulets and defences. The target is always the abstract malevolent force, never any person's actual sight or ability to see.

Conjure Bags

Conjure bags assembled for Evil Eye protection offer a general layer of security and work primarily as a preventive or repelling measure. A bag put together after the Eye has already taken hold will not necessarily lift it, removal requires a different working.

Anatomical Amulets

Because the Evil Eye can be understood as the anti-life force, a withering force of sterility and destruction, powerful and assertive signs of life repel and prevent it. The defiantly life-affirming energy implicit in the human genitals is among the most important elements in Evil Eye amulets. Amulets in the form of genitals are among the most ancient and prevalent of all charms. They are not prurient but affirm life, energy, and vitality: the very antithesis of the Evil Eye.

For most Evil Eye amulets to be effective, they must be visible. If hidden away, their capacity to search out and deflect the Eye is limited.

Symbolic representations include:

  • Downward-facing triangle: represents the vulva; considered among the most potent anti-Evil Eye amulets of all, ubiquitous in beads, tribal tattoos, and tribal ornamentation
  • Upward-facing triangle: represents the penis
  • Diamond and lozenge shapes: can stand in for the vagina; sometimes represent eyes as well
  • Seashells: metaphors for the vulva
  • Horseshoe: the most subtle rendering of female genitalia
  • A pair of horns: represents women's internal reproductive organs
  • A single horn (or anything vaguely similar): represents a penis

Hand Amulets and Gestures

The human hand is one of the most universal protective symbols across cultures. The hand may be rendered realistically or reduced to near-abstraction; in some traditions, five dots or a pentagon are sufficient to invoke the power of five fingers against the Eye.

The fig hand (Italian: fica) is among the oldest Italian Evil Eye amulets. It shows a hand with the thumb pushed between the first two fingers, a gesture that mirrors the reproductive act. The word fig itself is ancient Italian slang for the vulva, making the amulet a direct invocation of generative power against the sterility of the Eye. Wear or carry as a physical charm, or make the gesture directly.

The horned hand (mano cornuto) is another traditional Italian charm, showing a closed fist with the index and little fingers extended. Wear as an amulet or make the gesture discreetly.

Winged phalluses, associated with the Roman spirit Mutinus and found among the ruins of Pompeii, also serve as prophylactic charms. Wear or display as needed.

Hand gestures function as on-the-spot wards when no physical amulet is available. The gesture is typically made behind the suspect's back as they leave, out of their sight. The intent is to stop the Eye quietly, not to confront.

Mirror Charms

Mirrors are among the most powerful passive defenses against the Evil Eye. Rather than absorbing or repelling the Eye directly, a mirror reflects it back to its source. In folk tradition, this is understood to be potentially fatal to whoever cast it, much like the fabled gaze of the basilisk turned against itself.

Small mirrors may be sewn onto clothing or incorporated into amulets to carry this reflective protection on the body. Larger mirrors can be positioned in or around the home to intercept and redirect the Eye before it reaches its target.

In Witchcraft: The Art of Fascination

Practitioners distinguish between the Evil Eye as a passive, ambient force and the deliberate projection of personal power through the gaze. The second form is called fascination, and is understood in this tradition as the direct, intentional use of witch power.

Witch power, known historically as od, odyle, magnetism, or telergy, is understood to radiate from the human body in an egg-shaped field often identified with the aura. This energy is produced through the nervous system and can be consciously projected. Red-haired individuals are traditionally said to carry this force in a particularly free-floating, projectable form, which accounts for the long-held awe of red-headed witches from ancient Egypt onward.

Three channels are considered most effective for projecting witch power directly:

  1. The gaze: Fix the target intently with both eyes. When eyes are mutually locked and beams joined, spirits are said to commingle and a strong ligature forms. Sudden, intense eye contact is considered the most potent form of fascination and is called the basilisk gaze.
  2. Touch: Establish manual contact with the target, ideally while passing them an object to examine. The projection flows through the hands into the recipient.
  3. Breath: Contrive to breathe on the target, preferably near the face or into the mouth, pushing the full radiance of witch power through the breath.

Rhythmic breathing is used to intensify the power before projection: oxygenation quickens the blood and ties breath to the pulse, accelerating the metabolism and increasing the available output of nervous energy.

Identifying the Caster

Not all traditions agree on whether the Evil Eye is cast unwittingly. Some hold that it is a fully deliberate act, no different from throwing a targeted curse. When deliberate casting is suspected, divination methods can be used to identify who is responsible. The two approaches below come from Hungarian and Turkish folk traditions respectively.

The Licking Cure

To diagnose whether a child's illness stems from bewitchment, lick the child's forehead upward, then across, then up again, and spit behind the child's back. If you taste the distinct flavor of salt, the diagnosis of enchantment is confirmed, further, stronger action may then be beneficial.

Evil Eye Removal

Removing an established Evil Eye requires a different approach from prevention or repelling. The Eye has already taken hold, and the working must actively extract it.

Most removal spells share a common structure: a curer holds a substance in their hand and passes it around the victim's head three times. The substance must be both magnetic (to attract and pull the Eye out) and absorbent (to take it in). Once the Eye has been drawn into the substance, that substance is typically thrown immediately into fire, destroying the Eye along with it.

This ritual can be performed in response to an active attack, out of suspicion, or as a nightly precautionary measure. The person performing the removal focuses their attention on drawing the Eye away from the victim and replacing it with blessing and protection. The working may be accompanied by murmured prayers, sacred texts, or defiant spoken words aimed at the Eye. A spell done mechanically will have some effect, but a consciously empowered one carries far greater potency.

Verbal Curse Delivery

The evil eye and the spoken curse can be combined into a direct, personal method of delivering baneful intent. This technique draws on the practitioner's accumulated witch power and channels it through the body in a controlled, focused burst.

Stance: Stand on the right foot only, with the left foot slightly raised. This posture concentrates the body's energy and signals the shift into an offensive mode.

Eye: Close the right eye. The left eye alone delivers the glance, directed with full intensity at the target. A single eye creates a more focused, penetrating beam than both eyes open.

Hand of power: Extend the left arm fully toward the target. Hold the fist clenched, then point the first two fingers (index and middle) directly at them. This gesture concentrates and aims the witch's projective force.

Delivery: Speak the curse or ill-wish directly toward the target, pushing the words with breath and will simultaneously. The breath, gaze, and hand-of-power all fire together as a single combined projection.

This method is for use at close range when direct contact is possible. The combination of physical stance, gaze, hand-of-power, and spoken word creates a layered delivery that engages multiple channels of the practitioner's projective force at once.
