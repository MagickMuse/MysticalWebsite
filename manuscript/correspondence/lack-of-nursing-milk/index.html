📿Sulis Lactation Votive Charm

Lack of Nursing Milk, also known as insufficient milk supply or hypogalactia, describes a slower than expected let-down or low production of milk in the early days and weeks of breastfeeding. Causes include fatigue, dehydration, stress, hormonal shifts, infrequent feeds, retained placental tissue, or thyroid dysfunction. Aromatherapy supports galactagogue (milk-promoting) action through gentle massage with oils that have historically been used in nursing traditions to encourage flow, ease the let-down reflex, and steady the nervous system around feeding.

Galactagogue oils are powerful and must be diluted carefully. Apply only to the upper back, shoulders, and around (never directly on) the breasts. Wash the application area thoroughly before nursing so the infant does not ingest essential oil. Stop immediately if the infant shows any aversion at the breast, fussiness, or rash. Persistent low supply, weight loss in the infant, or breast pain warrants medical and lactation-consultant evaluation. Aromatherapy supports nursing. It does not replace lactation care.

Suggested Application

  • Massage: Dilute 4 to 6 drops of essential oil per ounce of carrier oil. With clean hands, apply with slow, sweeping strokes across the upper back, shoulders, and the chest above and around the breasts (avoiding the nipple and areola). Best done in the warm steam of a shower or just after a warm compress, when the milk ducts are already softened and the nervous system has begun to settle. Wipe the chest clean before the next feed.

Oils to Use

  • Celery Seed Essential Oil
  • Dill Seed Essential Oil
  • Sweet Fennel Essential Oil
  • Hops Essential Oil
