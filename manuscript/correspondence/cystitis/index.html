🫙Cystitis (UTI) Remedy Tea

Cystitis is an inflammation of the bladder, usually characterized by burning pain on urinating and a frequent urge to pass water. Aromatherapy supports the body through bladder irritation and infection by drawing on oils that act as urinary antiseptics, diuretics, and antispasmodics to calm the bladder wall and ease the discomfort that accompanies the condition.

Use only with intact skin. Always dilute essential oils in a carrier base, in full-fat milk, or in a tablespoon of bath dispersant before adding to bathwater. Many of the recommended oils are contraindicated during pregnancy, including juniper berry, spike lavender, lavandin, lovage, parsley seed, common thyme, yarrow, turpentine, atlas cedarwood, texas cedarwood, and virginian cedarwood. Juniper berry, lovage, and thyme can stress the kidneys at high dose, so reduce dilution or substitute when kidney disease is present. Patch test before topical use, and discontinue at the first sign of irritation. Persistent or recurrent cystitis must be reviewed by a clinician to rule out kidney involvement.

Suggested Application of Oils

  • Compress: Add 4 to 6 drops of essential oil to a bowl of warm water, soak a clean cloth, wring it out, and lay across the lower abdomen for 15 to 20 minutes. Reapply as the cloth cools.
  • Bath: Add 6 to 8 drops of essential oil, dispersed in a tablespoon of carrier oil, full-fat milk, or bath salts, to a warm (not hot) bath. Soak for 15 to 20 minutes once or twice daily during a flare.
  • Douche: Add 2 to 3 drops of essential oil to a teaspoon of cider vinegar, then mix into 500 ml of warm boiled water. Use only with practitioner guidance, and never during pregnancy.
