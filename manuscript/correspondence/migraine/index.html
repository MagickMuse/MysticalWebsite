Migraine is a recurring, severe headache often confined to one side of the head, accompanied by throbbing or pulsating pain, sensitivity to light and sound, nausea, and sometimes visual or sensory aura. Triggers include hormonal shifts, missed meals, sleep disruption, stress, certain foods, scents, or weather changes. Aromatherapy supports migraine through cool, calming compresses applied to the brow, temples, and back of the neck to ease vascular constriction, quiet the nervous system, and shorten the attack.

Patch test before broader use. Strong scents can trigger migraine in sensitive people; introduce one oil at a time. Clary sage and sweet marjoram should be avoided during pregnancy. Those with epilepsy or seizure disorders should use these oils with caution. Valerian is sedative; do not use before driving. Persistent or atypical migraine, sudden severe headache, or headache with neurological deficit requires medical evaluation.

Suggested Application of Oils

  • Compress: Add 4 to 6 drops of essential oil to a basin of cool water, agitate to disperse, lay a folded cloth on the surface to soak up the oil-bearing layer, wring out lightly, and lay across the forehead, temples, or back of the neck. Refresh as it warms. A cool compress narrows dilated blood vessels and quiets the throbbing; some practitioners alternate cool over the forehead with a warm compress at the nape to draw blood away from the head.
