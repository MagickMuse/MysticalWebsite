Protect Against Spirits

  • Goddesses: Bastet, Hekate, Kupala

One of the most fundamental and frequently practiced intentions in all of magick. Protection encompasses any working designed to shield, guard, or defend a person, place, object, or situation from harm, whether physical, emotional, psychic, or spiritual.

The Nature of Protective Magick

Protective workings operate on the principle that energy can be directed to form barriers, deflect negativity, and create zones of safety. Unlike banishing, which removes something already present, protection is primarily preventative. It establishes defenses before threats arrive.

There are several distinct approaches to protective magick, each suited to different situations:

  • Shielding creates an energetic barrier around the target, like an invisible wall that deflects unwanted energy
  • Warding anchors protective energy to a specific place such as a home, a room, or a doorway, creating a standing guard
  • Binding restricts a known source of harm from causing further damage
  • Deflection redirects negative energy back to its source or safely into the ground
  • Concealment hides the target from hostile attention entirely

When to Work with Protection

Protection is often the first type of magick a new practitioner learns, and for good reason. Before opening yourself to new energies through spellwork, divination, or spirit contact, establishing strong protections is essential. Think of it as locking the doors before you settle in for the night.

Common situations that call for protective workings include:

  • Beginning a new spiritual practice or opening psychic channels
  • Entering environments with heavy or chaotic energy
  • Dealing with difficult people or toxic situations
  • Protecting a home, vehicle, or personal belongings
  • Shielding during vulnerable times such as illness, grief, or major life transitions
  • Before and after ritual work, especially when working with unfamiliar forces

Building a Protective Working

As the Correspondence Guide describes, start with your intention and follow the links. Protection connects to a wide web of allies across every grimoire, from minerals like Black Tourmaline and Obsidian, to plants like Rosemary and Basil, to entities like Archangel Michael and Hekate, to timing like Saturday and the influence of Mars.

You do not need every linked element. One protective plant combined with one protective stone and the right timing is more than sufficient. Choose the materials you have access to and that resonate with your practice.

Layering Protections

Experienced practitioners often layer multiple forms of protection rather than relying on a single working. A protected home might include a warded threshold, a protective sachet by the door, a charged crystal on the windowsill, and a spoken charm renewed regularly. Each layer reinforces the others, creating a stronger whole.

Maintaining Protective Workings

Protection is not a one and done act. Wards fade, shields thin, and charged objects gradually lose their potency. Regular renewal, whether through recharging, recasting, or simply refreshing your intention, keeps your protections effective. Many practitioners tie their protective maintenance to lunar cycles, renewing wards during the waning moon when banishing energy is strongest.

In Counter Magick and Protection

Magickal attack works by binding the victim's soul and deep mind, creating an unconscious blind spot that the attacker then exploits. This renders the victim accident-prone and unable to perceive or resist the influence directed against them.

A key distinction in protective workings is that between amulets and talismans. Amulets are charged with the intention of inducing harmony, generosity, expansiveness, security, and optimism in the deep minds of those nearby. Talismans are charged for a specific, defined purpose. Earth power and earth-based amulets operate through direct contact with the earth itself, drawing on both mineral and vegetable forces.

Holystones

A holystone must be naturally formed by the erosion of wind and water. It cannot be purchased; it must be found. When discovered, it is picked up in Hertha's name and hung near the hearth or in the most-used room of the home. No purification is needed. The holystone is used au naturel, exactly as found.

Witch Balls

Witch balls take two traditional forms. The first is a hollow silvered glass globe, hung from the ceiling or placed in the front hall. The second is the traditional fisherman's glass net-float of green bottle-glass, hung in Hertha's name. Both serve as protective presences within the home.

Portable Amulets

When carrying protective amulets, restrict to one at a time, excluding the witch jewel. As one's powers develop, the aim is to rely less on these portable batteries and more on one's own cultivated inner strength.

Cross-Stone (Staurolite)

The cross-stone, also called staurotides, is a crystal of ferrous aluminium silicate (staurolite) that occurs naturally in orthorhombic form, with three planes set at right angles to each other, forming the shape of a three-dimensional Greek cross or solid cross of nature. Symbolically, this shape encodes the seven mystical dimensions of space: north, south, east, west, the abyss above, the abyss below, and the holy centre, laying the groundwork for the complex Cabalistic Ararita formula. As a protective amulet, the cross-stone is suspended in a red cloth bag tied with red thread from the neck of the bearer, placing them under the protection of Hertha.

Sign of the Fig

An image of the hand making the phallic sign of the fig (fist clenched, thumb thrust between first and second fingers) carved in nephrite (jade), amber, coral, jet, carnelian, or any other fascination gem, suspended in red from the bearer's neck in Hertha's name, is considered a potent earthly amulet. An old iron key serves the same protective purpose, sharing similar sexual implications.

Elf-Shot

Ancient flint or stone arrowheads of pre-Celtic Pictish origin, always considered highly potent by witches in matters of protection. Employed in the same manner as the preceding amulets.

Siderite (Lodestone) Amulet Consecration

The siderite, a stone of magnetic iron oxide (lodestone or magnetite), is among the most prized protective amulets in witchcraft. By the light of the full moon, with the window open if working indoors, place the lodestone on a piece of talisman paper laid over the workbook. Consecrate it with as many drops of Sabbat oil as there are years in the bearer's life, plus one extra drop for the present year, chanting with each touch: May Hertha bless thee [N] with all her might and main. Visualize the drops of oil glowing as they touch the stone, infusing it with witch power. Tie the stone loosely in a red flannel or chamois leather bag bound with red ribbon. Seal with So mote it be.

Every full moon, remove the siderite, dip it in a glass of water, dry it, and sprinkle on a small pinch of iron pyrites dust or iron filings in Hertha's name. This is called feeding the stone. The siderite must be worn around the neck or carried in the pocket. Male practitioners have traditionally carried it near the genital organs to increase sexual vigour and potency; in this case, the Horned One is invoked alongside Hertha in the initial consecration.

Magickal Defence

Signs that a definite occult attack may be in operation include recurrent nightmares, sustained runs of unbelievably bad luck, psychosomatic disturbances and allergies, poltergeist manifestations, and in extreme cases, acute mental disturbance. All of these symptoms except poltergeist activity can have non-occult causes, and it is always best to consult a doctor or psychiatrist before assuming magickal origin. However, if a practitioner has been directly informed that they are being targeted, the pattern becomes harder to ignore.

Nightmares are generally not the attack itself, but the deep mind's cry for help to the conscious self. They will often recur with a specific repeated motif and, if magickally induced, will tend to correspond to lunar phases, reaching their peak at the new moon rather than the full. As an attack worsens, the deep mind's pleas may escalate to waking hallucinations: visual, aural, or olfactory sensory disturbances including disembodied voices, music, a high-pitched tone (historically called the astral belt by occultists), or imaginary odours, usually fetid in the case of attack.

Runs of misfortune are often taken as signs of a lodged curse but are more frequently self-inflicted by the victim's own deep mind. Skilled attackers diagnose the victim's psychological weak spots in advance, then aim the attack precisely at those built-in deep-mind blind spots. The most deadly attack is the least suspected.

The chief danger to avoid is surrendering judgment to fear. Paranoia is a known pitfall for otherwise competent practitioners. When attack is suspected, a trusted fellow practitioner should handle the divination, exercising scepticism. The pendulum, Vassago, and rune sticks are the most reliable methods. Tarot and the I Ching may also be used. The aim of divination is to establish the nature and, where possible, the source of the hostility. Motivations are generally either lust or hate.

The Fetch (Projected Attack)

A skilled magickal antagonist may project witch power into a definite shape and transfer consciousness into it, sending it forth to accomplish the witch's will. This projected shape or fetch is often animal in form, corresponding either to the ensouling emotion of the projector or to the coven totem. This process is identical to modern astral or etheric projection. The fetch may be perceived by the victim through inner vision or, very rarely, with physical sight if either party possesses materializing medium abilities.

The legends of the werewolf and the vampire both derive from this practice. The werewolf represents materialized witch power ensouled by destructive rage; the vampire, incubus, and succubus by lust. The artificially produced fetch differs from the magistellus or Watcher in that the witch transfers her own consciousness to the fetch and not to the magistellus, and that the fetch relies solely on witch power and deep mind, while the magistellus involves some exterior demonic or elemental agency.

Tradition holds that when a habitual fetch-sender dies, the vampire or werewolf personality can persist as a self-perpetuating psychic fragment, feeding on the magnetism of the sleeping living. This practice gave rise to the lore of lycanthropy, including its traditional methods of dispatching such entities. Should a projected fetch achieve any degree of materialization, wounds inflicted upon it may be transferred back to the projector's sleeping body through the process of repercussion.

Passive Defence

Passive defence is the most effective form of retaliation in occult warfare. When a magical attack misfires or meets adequate protection, the curse returns to its sender. As the craft saying goes: An the witch bide the issue! Skilled practitioners avoid this recoil by delegating the actual cursing to less advanced associates; regardless of who ultimately bears the consequence, the defended practitioner should proceed with all defensive preparations as surely as one would board up a house before a hurricane.

Attacks generally begin after sundown; consult an ephemeris or almanac for the exact sunset time. Primary defences are the grand magickal circle and the pentacle of protection, supplemented by the magickal power of iron and protective herbs, plus all active amulets and magistelli.

Herb Planting by Moon Phase

For herbs whose end product is the root, plant during the dark of the moon (waning). For herbs to be used for their leaves or flowers, plant during the waxing moon. When harvesting, pick in the lunar quarter corresponding to the type of magick intended: the bright quarter for constructive workings, the dark quarter for baneful or destructive ones.

Trituration (Sachet and Incense Preparation)

Sachet powders have two components: agents (flower essences, perfumes, gum resins) and a base (orris root, patchouli, rose petals, sandalwood, agarwood, cedarwood shavings, or similar). Method:

  1. Assemble all ingredients.
  2. Grind the base herbs well in a pestle and mortar.
  3. Grind the flower essences or perfumes into a portion of the herb base.
  4. Add any gum resins (frankincense, myrrh, benzoin) to the remaining herb base and powder as finely as possible.
  5. Combine the two powdered portions.
  6. Add oils or liquids last (honey, wine, blood). If the oil quantity is very small, dissolve it first in a small amount of pure alcohol, then blend in for greater mixing power.
  7. Allow to steep at least overnight. Add extra base if the result remains too liquid.
  8. Store in well-stoppered nonporous bottles, labeled, protected from bright light at a moderate temperature.

Protective Potion

To calm those who suspect bewitchment, steep powdered angelica, marjoram, bay, and fennel seed in hot wine. Chant a few words of benediction and reassurance and charge strongly with witch power.

Iron in Occult Defence

Iron has a definite jamming or scrambling effect on any magickal signal when used with intention. For this reason, iron and all metals are rigorously excluded from the dress of a candidate in magickal initiation rituals where a definite transmission of power is required.
