A witch's animal or spirit helper in magickal work.

Overview

Familiars are spirits or animals that assist practitioners in their magickal workings. They may manifest as physical creatures (cats, toads, rabbits, dogs, birds) or as purely spiritual entities that accompany and aid the witch.

Types of Familiars

Animal Familiars, living creatures with whom the witch develops a deep psychic bond. The animal serves as both companion and magickal ally, lending its energy and instincts to the practitioner's work.

Spirit Familiars, non-corporeal entities that attach themselves to a practitioner. These may be ancestral spirits, nature spirits, or other intelligences that choose to work alongside the witch.

Historical Context

During the witch trials, the presence of any animal, particularly cats, toads, or insects, was often cited as "evidence" of witchcraft. Accused witches were said to suckle their familiars through witch's marks on their bodies. In reality, familiars represent the deep connection between practitioners and the natural and spirit worlds.

Working with Familiars

Familiars are not summoned or commanded, they choose their practitioners. The relationship is one of mutual respect and benefit. A familiar may:

  • Lend energy to spells and rituals
  • Serve as a messenger between realms
  • Offer protection during magickal work
  • Scout out information for the practitioner
  • Warn of spiritual danger

In Counter Magick and Protection

The Latin term for familiar is magistellus (plural: magistelli), meaning little master. The word familiar itself was coined by ecclesiastical authorities during the persecutions, implying merely an intimate friend or servant. There are three principal types of magistellus.

The Coven Totem: The first is the coven totem animal, agreed upon by mutual decision among coven members at the coven's founding, following introspection and divination. The chosen totemic animal form, and there may be more than one, is ritually assumed by members during the chain dance at the Sabbat, and also during the practice of sending the fetch (astral projection). Omens and auguries may be drawn from the appearance of one's totem animal in daily life. This witchlore may be the origin of popular beliefs about lucky or unlucky black cats crossing one's path.

The Pet Familiar: The second is the pet animal with whom the practitioner enters into emotional rapport. If the animal is of the same species as a coven totem, it takes on additional witchly significance. A pet may be used as a power object by charging it with a curse or blessing directed at someone; that person, on coming into contact with the animal, will discharge the spell and receive its full impact.

The Elemental Guardian (Watcher): The third, and for the individual witch most important, is the protective spirit or Watcher, a magickal guardian bound to the home. This may be a demonic entity such as Vassago bound to a show stone or mirror, permanently or temporarily. It may also be an elemental creature formed from the combination of witch power and some natural phenomenon. Unlike general Earth protection spells that guard against ambient bad vibrations, the magistellus possesses a definite will of its own, the entire aim of which is to protect the house and its inhabitants from out-and-out magickal attack. Two simpler types of elemental magistelli are made from plants.

The Mandragore

The mandragore is fashioned from a mandrake root (Mandragora officinalis) or, preferably for safety reasons, from English mandrake (white bryony, Bryonia dioica), which is equally traditional. The root is carved into the likeness of a human figure of the sex opposite to the practitioner's own, while the phrase Guard this home in Hertha's name is chanted throughout. It is then buried for a full lunar month, watered regularly with diluted blood or milk, then uprooted, cleaned, and dried in vervain smoke before being enshrined near the hearth. Once installed, the mandragore serves as a living elemental guardian of the home, possessed of its own protective will.

The Alraun

The alraun serves the same purpose as the mandragore but is fashioned from a branch of rowan (mountain ash, quickbeam), traditionally a warlock's device. Rowan is among the most powerful countermagick woods in European witch lore and is by some authorities identified with Yggdrasil, the Teutonic world tree, connecting it to the Cabalistic Tree of Life. After circling the chosen rowan tree and watering it for a full lunar month, the selected branch is severed with the Athame and carved into a small female figure between 5 and 12 inches in length. The image is cured in vervain smoke. At the full moon, the practitioner sleeps with the alraun, and it is then enshrined near the hearth to begin its protective work.
