Blood and the color red are inseparably linked, each a symbol of the other. Red stands for life, energy, vitality, and the element of fire, and by association the Sun, all qualities shared with blood itself.

For early peoples, the bond between the color and the life-giving power of blood was so strong that some burial rites involved daubing the corpse with red powders and ointments, an act of sympathetic magick in the hope that the red would restore the soul to life. One example is the ritual burial of a young tribal chieftain at the Paviland Caves in South Wales.

Blood also symbolizes kinship: to speak of a "blood line" is to speak of generations of one family. The term "blue blood" for the aristocracy arose because the veins of the nobility showed through pale skin unaccustomed to manual labor or the elements.

There is also an ancient tradition of mixing blood to seal allegiance. This sign of kinship is still observed at some Romany weddings, where a swift cut to the hand or arm and the mingling of blood is enough to swear lifelong loyalty.

Menstrual blood, coming from that most sacred of places, the womb, is credited with particular magickal power and stands for feminine energy and the Moon.

Blood spilled in ritual, whether to propitiate the Gods or offered deliberately to the Earth in harvest rites, was believed to bring fertility. In the Christian tradition, communion wine represents the blood of Christ.
