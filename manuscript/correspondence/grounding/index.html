The term "ground" has two meanings in the Craft.

To ground yourself means to break a magickal mood and return to ordinary consciousness. In a nonmagickal context, to be grounded means to be firmly in touch with reality, to climb down from cloud nine and get real.

To ground power means to return the energy that you have raised for a spell, to send it back to its source. One simple way to do this is to shake your hands, palms down, over the floor or earth. Power should always be returned after a spell. Keeping power is never a good idea. The least of its dangers is that it can cause unpleasant things such as angry spirits or malevolent entities to enter your life.

Earth Connection for Grounding

Nature is our home. Spending time in a tranquil outdoor space is a wonderful way to reboot your mindset and gain perspective. In the past, people were more naturally connected to the earth while growing their own food, foraging in the woodlands, and hunting. In our busy modern world, making a conscious effort to connect with nature is more important than ever.

Grounding in Magickal Practice

Ground and center before all magickal work to avoid depleting personal energy levels by augmenting internal energies. Ground again after all magickal work to avoid overload of personal energy levels by draining out the excess energies.

Solo Time in Nature

Poet Mary Oliver wrote about the benefits of visiting a forest alone. In her poem "How I Go into the Woods," she describes enjoying the forest without a companion. Alone in the woods, she can connect with the birds and trees and pray in her own way without the distraction of human conversation. By "becoming invisible," she can witness the animals in the woodlands run by her without concern.

Spending solo time outside can help you connect with your innermost thoughts and the wisdom stored within. Quiet time in nature is like a form of meditation. It is a delight to sit in silence and listen to the sounds of nature. There is no need to verbalize anything, unless you want to talk with your favorite tree or encourage the newest flower bud that is beginning to bloom nearby.

Alone time in nature helps you focus on the present moment and nothing else. Consider stopping long enough to view the winged visitors near a flower garden. Take time to observe a butterfly or bee as it dances around the petals of a flower. Those beautiful moments in nature tend to make time feel like it is standing still. It also seems easier to get your mind in order when you are outside by yourself. Spending time in a natural green space is a great way to recharge yourself.
