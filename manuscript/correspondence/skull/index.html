🦴Skeletal System

The skull is perhaps the most important bone in the body, instantly recognizable for what it is, unlike most other bones, and was believed to hold the life-force of both body and spirit. To sever the skull from the body brings instant death, which is the root of the power held within the skull or head.

The skull is, above all, a symbol of death, and also of the vanity of worldly things and the fleeting nature of time. Skulls often appear on old gravestones and in tombs.

There is an ancient tradition of talking skulls with oracular powers. The skull of Orpheus, kept on the Greek island of Lesbos, was said to possess such gifts.

The head or skull was also a prized trophy of war, and some warriors used the skull of a slain enemy as a drinking vessel, a grisly but effective show of supremacy.

In Mexico, during the festival of the Day of the Dead, the skull takes on a festive face, and colorful models of skeletons and skulls made of candy serve as a reminder of the nearness of death and the need to celebrate life.

In alchemy the skull, together with the grave and the raven, is a symbol of a stage of the work called "mortification," itself a symbol of dying to the world, casting off earthly things, and finally returning to the earth. In Freemasonry too the skull stands for a cycle of initiation, a symbolic death before a new life.

Religious Associations

In Christianity the skull was meditated upon as a reminder of the nearness of death and of divine Judgment.
