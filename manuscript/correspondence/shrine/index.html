What is a Shrine?

A shrine is a place of veneration and a place to honor something or someone and to leave offerings. It serves as a physical point of contact where you can connect to a god or goddess, your guardian angel, a spirit animal, or an ancestor.

Shrines may be elaborate or simple, planned or spontaneous. Some shrines are spontaneously erected at the site of tragedies as people bring candles, flowers, notes, and gifts to mark the spot. The most common shrines are gravesites or, if there is no grave, the place where a death occurred. According to legend, part of a person's soul remains there, commanded by God to do favors for the living if asked, thus bypassing the Church and the beatification process.

A shrine is sacred because of associations with a saint or holy being, but its sacred quality is also enhanced by the presence of those who strive and struggle to visit.

Unlike an altar, a shrine is not a workspace for doing magick. Shrines are easier to set up and maintain because they aren't as complex. A shrine can consist of a postcard, a specially charged crystal, a plant, or a small statue.

Discreet Practice

If you don't want your family or roommate to know what you're doing, keep your shrine simple. You know why those particular things are together; anyone else looking at them will think it's just a decorative arrangement.

Using a shrine as a focus for specific activities can also help unclutter your main altar area.

Famous Shrines

Since ancient times, all cultures have created shrines to honor deities and mark sacred space:

  • Stonehenge, Giant megalithic circle in Wiltshire, England; may have been a sacred ritual site or shrine to deities
  • Sekhmet Sanctuary at Luxor, Stunning shrine to the lion-headed goddess in Egypt
  • Hanging Gardens of Haifa, Sacred site in Israel
  • Machu Picchu, Ancient Incan sacred site in Peru
  • Grand Mosque in Mecca, Islam's holiest shrine

Elemental Shrines

An element shrine is a place where you can connect with one element or all four. Think about what each element means to you and gather items that represent your feelings and intentions regarding it.

Water Shrine

A goblet of water, a small fountain, shells, river stones, or pictures of a waterfall or calm lake.

Fire Shrine

A collection of candles in reds and golds set on a crimson cloth, perhaps with a small brass figurine of a lion.

Earth Shrine

Stones, crystals, potted plants, pottery, or images of mountains and forests.

Air Shrine

Feathers, wind chimes, incense holders, or images of sky and clouds.

Shrines for Harmony in the Home

Experiment with creating four separate shrines in different places in your home:

  • North, Earth shrine
  • East, Air shrine
  • South, Fire shrine
  • West, Water shrine

Make sure to have one shrine for each element so your home remains balanced.

Balancing Excess Energy

Does your home have a room where people tend to lose their tempers or energy runs too high? It may have an excess of fire energy. Try setting up an earth shrine or water shrine in that room to ease tension and bring harmony. Choose a peaceful picture, a pretty blue or green stone, a shell, or a plant.

If an area of your home or workplace seems sluggish or heavy, consider building a fire shrine there, a photo of a sunny beach, a red stone, or a vase of red or orange flowers. You can do this in your office too. Nobody has to know!

Memorial Shrines

Around the world, people create shrines to honor those who have died in battles or catastrophes. These memorials serve as focal points for love, respect, prayers, gratitude, and hope.

Not only do memorials remind us of victims and circumstances, they also hold the intentions of visitors and invoke peaceful energies to help neutralize traumas that took place there.

When you visit the grave of a beloved friend or relative and lay flowers or mementos there, you are showing reverence at a shrine created to honor that person.

Roadside Shrines

Mexican roadside shrines are known as descansos. Once upon a time, coffins were carried by pallbearers to the camposanto, or cemetery. They were heavy and, periodically, the coffin was laid on the ground giving the pallbearers an opportunity to rest (descansar). It became traditional to mark these resting spots with a flower, a cross, or some other marking.

Animita

In Chilean parlance, an animita is a roadside shrine marking the death of a loved one. Candles and flowers are customarily brought to the animita in remembrance, and also to beseech favors from the dead.
