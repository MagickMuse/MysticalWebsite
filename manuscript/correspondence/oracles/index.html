Oracles were historically inseparable from sacred shrines and spiritual centers. One of the primary functions of these sites was to serve as a source of divination, understood to operate under the authority of the presiding deity or spirit. Questioners would travel to the shrine, make offerings, and receive a response believed to come directly from the divine.

The Oracle of Delphi

Delphi was the most celebrated oracle of the ancient world. Originally dedicated to Gaia, it was served by snake priestesses who delivered prophecies directly. Apollo, arriving from the north, forcibly took over the shrine. The pythonesses were retained, but their prophecies were subsequently filtered through male priests before reaching the questioner.

Apollo and Aleuromancy

Apollo was not only the god of medicine, music, and prophecy but also a guardian of grain, operating in the guise of a mouse as his familiar. He provided a grain-based oracle at his shrine, a system known as aleuromancy, in which questions and answers were baked inside balls of dough. A donation to the shrine was the traditional price of a reading. This practice is considered an ancestor of the modern fortune cookie and can be reproduced at home.

Other Divine Oracles

Oracular shrines were not exclusive to Apollo. Zeus answered questions through the whispering oaks at his shrine in Dodona. The oracle of Ammon operated in the Libyan desert. Hermes, the trickster and master of the crossroads, offered the simplest oracular method of all: the first words overheard after a period of deliberate silence.
