Catarrh: inflammation of mucous membranes, usually associated with an increase in secretion of mucus.

Definition

Catarrh is the inflammation of mucous membranes, particularly in the nose, throat, and sinuses, accompanied by excess mucus production. It commonly follows colds, flu, allergies, or sinus infections, and may be either acute or chronic.

Application Methods

  • Massage: Dilute 2 to 3 drops in 5 ml of carrier oil and massage into the chest, throat, upper back, and around the sinuses (avoiding the eyes).
  • Vaporizer/Diffuser: Add 4 to 6 drops to a diffuser or vaporizer to thin mucus and ease congestion throughout a room.
  • Inhalation: Add 2 to 3 drops to a bowl of hot (not boiling) water, drape a towel over the head, and inhale steam for 5 to 10 minutes. A drop on a tissue or pillow also works for gentle, ongoing exposure.

Suggested Blends

  • Decongestant Steam: Eucalyptus (2 drops) + Peppermint (1 drop) + Tea Tree (1 drop) in a bowl of steaming water.
  • Chest Rub: Frankincense (3 drops) + Lavender (3 drops) + Hyssop (2 drops) in 30 ml carrier oil.
  • Sinus Diffuser Blend: Cajeput (3 drops) + Niaouli (2 drops) + Lemon (2 drops).
  • Night Pillow Drop: Sandalwood (1 drop) + Myrrh (1 drop) on a tissue tucked beside the pillow.

Safety

  • Steam inhalation should be used cautiously in young children and asthmatics; use gentle vaporization instead.
  • Hyssop, sage, and high-camphor oils (camphor, spike lavender, lavandin) should be avoided in pregnancy and epilepsy.
  • Cold-pressed citruses (lemon, lime) are phototoxic; avoid sun exposure on treated skin for 12 to 18 hours.
  • Peppermint, eucalyptus, and rosemary should not be used on or near the faces of children under 5.
  • Elecampane is a known sensitizer; test on a small skin patch before broader use, and avoid prolonged contact.
