The breast is a symbol of motherhood, the female principle, comfort, nourishment, and abundance, as well as of beauty and fertility. It is the newborn's first point of contact as it suckles its first food, milk, which gives the breast its primal nature as a symbol. The Egyptians believed that the stars of the Milky Way were milk spilling from the breasts of the Moon Goddess, who was the source of all the other stars too. The right breast is said to represent the Sun, and the left the Moon.

In Hebrew, the word for "breast" is the same as the word for "girl" and for "liquid measure." This connects the breast to the idea of restriction, since any measurement is necessarily finite.

To return to the bosom is to be enfolded in safety like a child, and dying is sometimes spoken of as returning to the "bosom of the Earth."

The breast is also a potent sexual symbol, reflected in the popularity of breast augmentation and in the prominence given to the breast in depictions of sex symbols; in less liberal times, images showing a great deal of cleavage were banned.

Mountains are seen as symbols of breasts, and many bear female names. Annapurna in India means "Great Breast Full of Nourishment," and Mount Olympus in Greece is named after the Goddess Gaia Olympia, also known as the Deep Breasted One.

Some Goddess depictions appear to show many breasts. These may express the ultra-maternal nature of the Goddess, though in the statue of Artemis at Ephesus they may instead represent bulls' testicles as symbols of male virility.
