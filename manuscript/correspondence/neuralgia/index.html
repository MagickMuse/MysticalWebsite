Neuralgia is a stabbing or shooting pain that follows the path of a nerve, often felt as sudden, sharp, electric-shock-like sensations along the face, back, ribs, or limbs. Common causes include nerve compression, viral inflammation (such as post-herpetic neuralgia after shingles), trauma, or chronic conditions like trigeminal neuralgia. Aromatherapy supports neuralgia through warming, analgesic, and antispasmodic oils applied externally to soothe the inflamed nerve and ease the pain that travels with it.

Patch-test before broad use. Do not apply to broken or acutely inflamed skin. Spike lavender, sweet marjoram, rosemary, hyssop, nutmeg, and turpentine should be avoided during pregnancy. Those with epilepsy or seizure disorders should avoid rosemary and hyssop. Dilute essential oils to 1 to 2.5 percent in a carrier oil for massage, and use 5 to 10 drops dispersed in carrier for the bath.

Suggested Application of Oils

  • Massage: Dilute 6 to 15 drops of essential oil per ounce of carrier oil (1 to 2.5 percent) and massage gently along the affected nerve pathway in slow, smooth strokes. Avoid direct pressure on the most tender point during an acute flare.
  • Bath: Add 5 to 10 drops of essential oil to a tablespoon of carrier oil or unscented bath base, then disperse into a warm bath. Soak for 15 to 20 minutes to ease nerve pain and the muscular tension that usually accompanies neuralgia.
