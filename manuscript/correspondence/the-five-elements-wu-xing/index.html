🧭The Five Elements (Wu Xing)

◇Type
Concept
≡Also called
Wu Xing, Hong Fan
↗Other Correspondences1
🏠Feng Shui

The Chinese reckon the natural world not by four elements but by five: Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, and Water. The oldest source for this scheme is thought to be the Hong Fan, a piece of Chinese philosophy reaching back to the second millennium BC, which sets out the correspondences each element carries.

Directions and Seasons

Each of the five elements governs a direction and a season, and several are also fixed to a position in the cosmos.

  • Water holds the nadir, winter, and the north, sitting at the bottom of the map.
  • Fire holds the zenith, summer, and the south.
  • Wood belongs to spring and the east.
  • Metal belongs to autumn and the west.
  • Earth stands at the central point from which all the others emanate, and so it answers to every season and every direction.

Detailed Correspondences

The Hong Fan and later sources assign each of the five elements a wide web of correspondences, from taste and color to the bodily organs and the emotions.
