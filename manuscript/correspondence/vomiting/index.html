Nausea is the queasy, unsettled sensation that often precedes vomiting, and vomiting is the forceful expulsion of stomach contents. Both arise from triggers as varied as motion, anxiety, hormonal shifts, viral and food-borne illness, migraine, medication, pregnancy, vertigo, and digestive overload. Aromatherapy supports the queasy stomach by calming spasm in the gut wall, settling the brain's emetic centre, and offering aromatic anchors that the senses can latch onto when the system feels disordered. Inhalation tends to act faster than topical application during an acute episode, while gentle abdominal massage between episodes helps prevent recurrence and ease residual cramping.

Use only well diluted, on intact skin, and well away from the eyes and mucous membranes. Avoid Allspice, Clove, Nutmeg, Sweet Fennel, Spike Lavender, Lavandin, Cabbage Rose Absolute, and Peppermint during pregnancy unless cleared by a qualified practitioner. Keep Peppermint, Spike Lavender, and Camphoraceous oils away from the faces of children under five, since menthol and 1,8-cineole can cause respiratory distress in young children. Patch test before broader use, and consult a doctor for persistent vomiting, vomiting with blood, severe abdominal pain, or signs of dehydration. Vomiting in pregnancy beyond mild morning sickness should always be reviewed clinically.

Suggested Application of Oils

  • Massage: Blend 1 to 2 drops of carminative oil per teaspoon of carrier and stroke gently in clockwise circles over the upper abdomen and lower ribs between episodes, or massage diluted oil over the temples, nape, and wrists when nausea is the dominant symptom.
  • Vaporization: Add 4 to 6 drops to an electric diffuser or aroma vaporizer, or place 1 to 2 drops on a tissue, handkerchief, or aromatic inhaler stick and inhale gently as queasiness rises. Steady aromatic exposure helps settle the brain's emetic centre and gives the senses something stable to anchor on during an episode.
