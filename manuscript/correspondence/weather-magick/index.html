Weather magick has been a mainstay of witchcraft probably as far back as humans have been doing magick. Weather affects everyone, from the need to avoid violent weather to the need for calm weather while hunting, fishing, or harvesting edible plants. So there have always been charms to bring rain or send it away, quiet storms, and the like.

Climate Change Considerations

Climate change has complicated these ancient practices. Relationships between humanity and nature have become fraught. Nature spirits often can't or won't distinguish between responsible nature lovers and irresponsible industrialists. Some weather magick techniques that used to work well are now less reliable. If sky spirits in your area have become resistant or hostile, try working through intermediaries instead.

Techniques

Chants & Songs

Around the world, people use music for weather magick. Drum rhythms, chants, and songs can summon or banish rain, snow, sun, or wind. Sea chanteys redirect wind. You can adapt traditional chants like changing "Rain, rain, go away" to "Rain, rain, come and stay."

Whistling

"Whistle up a wind" and "whistle up a storm" come from the old belief that whistling can cause wind to blow harder. This is sympathetic magick because wind itself makes a whistling sound. Works with mouth alone, hands, or instruments like tin whistles.

Musical Instruments

  • Woodwinds: For wind
  • Strings: For rain
  • Percussion: For thunder

Probability Manipulation

Folk magick principle: it is much easier to influence probabilities in motion than to summon something from scratch. Tilt "might" toward "will" or "won't" when weather has "a chance" of something.

Sympathetic Magick

"Like attracts like" or "like represents like." Washing your car or watering plants to bring rain. Leaving your jacket home to bring cold weather. Placing a fork in the ground or turning a glass upside down to contain rain.

Intermediary Spirits

If sky spirits have become uncooperative, work through intermediaries:

Land Spirits: Calmer and more stable than sky spirits. Ask them to intercede for rain to water plants or for wind to pass by leaving trees intact. Good for quelling dust storms and sand storms.

Water Spirits: Spirits of lakes, rivers, and seas are related to atmospheric water. Ask them to influence celestial aspects.

Deities: Most pantheons have sky, weather, storm, rain, wind, or snow deities. Develop working relationships and ask for help with weather needs.

Magickal Artifacts

Broom: Sweeping motion "sweeps away" undesired weather or "sweeps together" moisture for rain.

Cauldron: Contains a tempest (especially with a lid) or brews a storm when needed.

Classic Rain Spell: Fill a cauldron with stored rainwater, dip a broom in, and fling droplets around to symbolize and summon rain.

Brass Bell: Charge with sunlight and chime away clouds when sun is needed.

Children's Weather Folklore

Methods for summoning snow days:

  • Placing spoons under your pillow
  • Flushing ice cubes down the toilet
  • Placing white crayons on the windowsill
  • Begging the "snow gods" for a storm
