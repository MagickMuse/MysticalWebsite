The care with which the ancient Egyptian embalmers removed the intestines reflects the significance they gave this organ, which they believed held magickal powers. The bowels were preserved in a special canopic jar associated with Qebehsenuef, whose lid took the shape of a falcon's head. The three other internal organs removed and kept in their own canopic jars were the liver, the lungs, and the stomach. These organs had to be protected from demons and other entities, and the jars were believed to provide that protection.
