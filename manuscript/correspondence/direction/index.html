The four cardinal points follow a clear seasonal and daily logic. North answers to winter and to night, south to summer and to day, east to spring and to dawn, and west to Fall and to twilight. Beyond the four quarters lie two further directions of great importance, "above" and "below," which correspond to Heaven and Earth.

Each direction carries its own symbolism. Some of the most lyrical images come from the Druid tradition, which assigns one of four sacred animals to every quarter. Many Druid ceremonies open the ceremonial circle to the elements and the directions, honoring and respecting their spirits in the hope that they will bless the proceedings. In this tradition the north is the "great starry bear of the Heavens," the south is the "great stag in the heat of the chase," the east is the "hawk of the dawn," and the west is the "salmon of wisdom."
