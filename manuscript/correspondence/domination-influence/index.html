Domination spells are workings intended to encourage compliance, cooperation, or obedience in another person without causing harm. The goal is not injury but influence: ideally all parties continue to thrive, simply according to a different arrangement than would have occurred naturally. They are distinct from hexes.

The Ethical Debate

Modern Wicca and much Wicca-influenced practice treats domination magick with suspicion or outright rejection, viewing any attempt to influence another's will as a violation of personal sovereignty. This position reflects a genuine and admirable ethical concern. It also reflects a relatively recent historical moment. Pre-modern magickal texts across every culture show little interest in this question. The ethics of influence magick are a product of the Aquarian Age, not of antiquity.

Other traditions argue differently: that the entire purpose of spellcasting is to bend circumstances toward a desired outcome, and that one cannot meaningfully separate influence over the material world from influence over the people within it. This remains an active philosophical debate within the occult community.

Historical Context: Magick of the Disempowered

Domination spells did not originate with the powerful. Societies in which people control their own bodies, families, and futures have no use for them. These workings developed under conditions of severe power imbalance.

Many were created by women whose survival depended on the goodwill of husbands, fathers, brothers, or sons. A failure to produce a child, a husband's infatuation with another woman, or a shift in household loyalty could mean loss of home, status, or safety. Under such conditions, influencing another becomes a survival skill.

Others were developed by enslaved people, whose lives, bodies, and families were entirely at the disposal of others. For those with no legal recourse and no material power, magick was sometimes the only available tool for self-preservation.

Dominant people have no need of domination spells. Those with wealth and institutional power have faster and more effective means available. The magickal record shows that domination workings are consistently the province of the disempowered attempting to level an unequal situation.

Socio-political history is embedded in the spell record. The prevalence of workings in which women seek influence over men, with almost no corresponding tradition in the reverse, is not evidence of female malevolence. It reflects who historically needed these tools and who did not.

Ethical Considerations

Personal motivation must be examined before casting any spell of this type. If the goal is control for its own sake or for selfish advantage, these workings should not be performed, both on ethical grounds and because magick cast with malicious intent tends to return to its sender. Examine the need honestly before proceeding.

These spells are appropriate when options are genuinely limited, when the power differential is real and serious, and when the alternative is harm to oneself or one's dependents. They are the recourse of last resort, not the first tool reached for.

Do As I Say Oil

Do As I Say Oil, also marketed as As You Please or As I Please Oil, is among the milder commanding condition oils. Where most commanding formulas focus on direct authority and compliance, Do As I Say Oil works through a more seductive mechanism: its intent is to make others genuinely wish to please you. This dual quality reflects the botanical character of its primary ingredient, calamus (sweet flag), which carries both commanding properties and aphrodisiac associations.

Do As I Say Oil is used in both romantic and business contexts. Many spells calling for it carry an erotic dimension, functioning as a hybrid between a commanding oil and a seduction oil. It can also be used to soften resistant professional relationships or encourage cooperation without confrontation.

Commanding Oils

Commandin oils are the most widely used tools in modern commanding and compelling magick, rooted in the Hoodoo and New Orleans Voodoo traditions. Despite carrying names like Commanding Oil, Compelling Oil, All High Conquering Oil, and Essence of Bend Over, these formulas share a common foundation that traces back at least as far as ancient Egypt.

The two core botanical ingredients in all commanding oils are calamus (sweet flag) and licorice root. Each can be used independently for commanding purposes, but together their effectiveness is considered to multiply dramatically. Their pairing appears in the Alexandrian Magical Papyri, where they are used for identical purposes.

Key oil types and their distinctions:

  • Oils named with variations of Command or Compel are roughly equivalent in potency
  • Oils using the term Conquering, such as All High Conquering Oil, suggest the presence of High John the Conqueror root, though this varies by formulation
  • Essence of Bend Over (also sold as Bend Over Oil) is the most concentrated and direct of the commanding oils

All commanding oils also carry a protective quality: they shield against hexes, harmful intentions, and attempts by others to dominate the practitioner.

Because commercially sold versions frequently contain little or no actual plant material, sourcing from reputable suppliers or blending your own is strongly recommended. Recipes are in the Formulary.

⚠️ Hoodoo and New Orleans Voodoo are closed practices rooted in African American folk magick traditions. Use these formulas with awareness and respect for their cultural origins.

Women's Domination Spells

A distinct sub-genre of domination workings evolved specifically to serve women whose survival or wellbeing depended on a partner's cooperation. The traditional target was a husband or other man with significant authority over the caster's life. The goal was not harm but improved conditions.

Certain botanicals are understood in this tradition to confer personal power differently across genders. Angelica and rosemary, the two botanical cornerstones of women's domination workings, may be used by anyone for protection and healing but are said to activate commanding personal power only when used by women. Comparable plants for men include High John the Conqueror, Master root, and Sampson snake root.

Men's domination spells targeting women almost exclusively operate through sexuality. Because their motivation is desire rather than survival, those workings are catalogued in the Love, Sex, and Seduction section rather than here.

Underfoot Spells

Some domination workings keep the target symbolically underfoot rather than directing a formula at them. In ancient Egypt, an enemy's name or image was painted on the soles of shoes so that every step ground them further into submission. A modern, more discreet version simply places a photograph of the target inside the shoe. A third variation, suitable when neither a photograph nor artistic skill is available, involves writing the target's name and mother's name on brown paper in a commanding ink and wearing it underfoot.

Spell-Casting Tips

  • The primary botanicals for domination workings are calamus (sweet flag), licorice root, bergamot, High John the Conqueror, and vetiver. Their inherent qualities carry much of the working's force.
  • The intensity of the spell-caster's own desire and urgency is the other essential ingredient. These workings draw their power from real need.
  • Purple is the color of authority and dominance. Incorporate it into candle workings or wherever color is relevant to the spell.
