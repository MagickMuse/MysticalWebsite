PMS Therapeutic Index

Definition

Premenstrual syndrome, also called premenstrual tension or PMT, names the cluster of physical, emotional, and behavioural symptoms that arise in the luteal phase of the menstrual cycle and resolve at the onset of menses. Common features include irritability, mood swings, low mood, anxiety, breast tenderness, bloating, fluid retention, abdominal cramping, headache, fatigue, food cravings, and disturbed sleep. Aromatherapy supports this condition with hormone-balancing, sedative, antispasmodic, and uplifting oils delivered through massage, bath, and vaporization in the week or two before the period.

Begin oils five to seven days before the period is expected and continue until bleeding starts.
Hormonal aromatherapy is gentlest when introduced gradually, so test small dilutions first and adjust according to how the cycle responds. Carrot seed, German chamomile, geranium, marjoram, and tarragon must be avoided during pregnancy. Tarragon is moderately toxic due to its estragole content and should be used sparingly even outside of pregnancy. Patch test on the inner forearm before broader skin work, and dilute to 2 to 2.5 percent in a carrier oil for body application and 1 percent for facial or sensitive-skin work.

Suggested Application of Oils

  • Massage: Dilute 12 to 15 drops of essential oil per ounce of carrier oil and massage gently over the lower abdomen, lower back, breasts, and shoulders. Clockwise circles across the lower belly ease cramping, while long sweeping strokes along the spine and shoulders release tension. Begin in the second half of the cycle and continue daily until the period begins.
  • Bath: Add 6 to 8 drops of essential oil to a tablespoon of carrier oil or unscented bath base, disperse in warm water, and soak for 15 to 20 minutes. Warm aromatic baths relax the abdominal wall, ease breast tenderness, lower-back discomfort, and water retention, and quiet emotional volatility.
  • Vaporization: Add 4 to 6 drops of essential oil to a diffuser with 100 ml of water and run it through the home or workspace, especially in the late afternoon and evening. Diffusion eases mood, lifts low spirits, calms irritability, and supports steadier sleep during the premenstrual window.
