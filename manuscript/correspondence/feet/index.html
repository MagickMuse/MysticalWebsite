Symbolism

The foot is a symbol of strength, stability, and resolve, since it supports the rest of the body, and of our connection with the earth. To say that someone has their feet firmly on the ground is to call them sensible and practical.

Feet have always served as a measure: the imperial "foot" was based on its average length of twelve inches. Pacing out a distance is a way of measuring it, and in this way both the Buddha and Vishnu measured out the Universe, the Buddha by taking seven steps in each direction and Vishnu by three great strides across Earth and the Heavens. In China the foot measured not only space but time, and the sign of a footprint says "I was here and continue to be here." Pilgrims walk in the footsteps of those they revere, making the same journey to see the same sights and feel the same things; to walk for such a purpose is itself a meditation, and the unhurried pace of walking is said to let a person recognize their true place in the world.

Bare feet are a sign of humility, and to walk on holy ground unshod is an ancient ritual still as powerful as ever. At the same time, to go barefoot is to walk in power, since the feet can absorb the energy of the Earth directly; modern Druids and witches often work their ceremonies barefoot for this reason. Natural footprint shapes in rock are believed to be the marks of holy people, and bare feet are thought to pick up vibrations from the Earth and from those who walked there before.

In India shoes are removed not only in temples and ashrams but in the home, since footwear is believed to gather negative influences from the outside world, and removing them shows respect for the physical and spiritual cleanliness of a place. Shoes are likewise removed on entering a mosque, and Moses approached the Burning Bush barefoot.

The Footprint of the Buddha, or Buddhapada, is a popular symbol showing the soles of the Buddha's feet marked with other emblems such as the eight auspicious objects, used to indicate the places visited during his life on Earth. In the yogic tradition it is considered deeply rude to point the soles of the feet toward the guru, or even toward an image of the guru.

To wash another's feet is a symbol of humility, since the feet touch the ground, while having one's feet washed is a sign of purification.

In China the practice of foot binding endured for a thousand years. At the age of six or younger, girls' feet were bound tightly so that the bones broke and the muscles wasted, keeping the feet tiny; a three-inch foot was considered perfect and called the "golden lotus." Its origins are unclear, perhaps an attempt to imitate a concubine who danced in silk-wrapped feet. Bound feet became a symbol of wealth and power, since only the rich could keep a woman unable to walk, and the custom died out after it was banned in 1911.

One sign of a demonic or less-than-human being is extraordinary feet, such as the cloven hooves of the Devil in old images, or the reputed six toes of the witch.
