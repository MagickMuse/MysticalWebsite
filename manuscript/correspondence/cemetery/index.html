The cemetery is the threshold between the realms of the living and the dead, a place of transformation where the boundary between worlds grows thin. Like the broader crossroads, the cemetery swirls with energy, though of a more specific kind: ghosts, souls of the departed, abstract life-and-death forces, spiritual entities, protective guardians, and malevolent beings drawn to grief or decay all inhabit this space.

Magickal Uses

Many spells require either casting in the graveyard or burying spell remnants there, as though conducting a funeral. These include protection, banishing, love spells, and hexes. Notably, many necromantic spells for communicating with those who have passed on do not actually require a trip to the cemetery.

Cultural Perceptions

Whether the cemetery registers as benevolent or threatening depends largely on cultural attitudes toward what happens to the soul after death. Cultures that rely on protective ancestral spirits rarely fear the graveyard. Cultures that believe human memory and emotion truly die, leaving behind only a hungry, destructive ghost, tend to avoid the cemetery except for purposes of malevolent magick.

The Cemetery as Neutral Power

The term "necropolis," city of the dead, is Greek in origin but stems from ancient Egyptian practice. Historically, the desperately poor sometimes made their homes among the graves, a situation that persists in various places today.

Cemeteries are sites of great neutral power. The swirling radiant energy present there can be harnessed for good or ill, depending on the practitioner's intent.

The Cemetery Gates

Even within the cemetery, certain areas concentrate more power than others. The threshold of the threshold is at the cemetery gates. Older cemeteries traditionally feature iron gates to mark this boundary. Iron, with the exception of menstrual blood, is the single most protective substance on Earth and will repel and contain malevolent spirits and ghosts.

Many spells instruct practitioners to leave items at the cemetery gates. This is not because people feared entering the graveyard itself, but because that threshold point carries exponentially greater power.

Resident Spirits

Many powerful spirits reside in or are associated with the cemetery. India's Kali and Shiva, Matron and Patron of Tantra, dwell among the graves. Ancient Egypt's road-openers, Anubis, the jackal-headed inventor of embalming, and Wepwawet, a wolf deity whose name sounds like a wolf's cry when spoken quickly, also make the cemetery their domain.

Accessing the power of crossroads and cemeteries is common to most magickal traditions, though the extent of engagement varies.
