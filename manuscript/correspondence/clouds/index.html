🌦️Weather

The ever-changing nature of clouds, formed from countless particles of water, makes them a fitting symbol of ambiguity. Their undefined state, as full of promise as a seed, led the Sufis to liken the unknowable nature of the cloud to that of Allah before his physical manifestation. Because their shapes shift from one moment to the next, clouds were long used for divination, a practice carried out extensively by the Celts under the curiously named neladoracht.

The image of the cloud is sometimes used in the same way as the veil, hiding from sight whatever is too glorious to be looked upon all at once. To have one's head in the clouds suggests a dreamy, impractical temperament, cut off from the cares of the world by simple obliviousness to them. Saints and sages are often shown drifting up toward the Heavens, riding the clouds like a celestial vehicle. In Greek myth the clouds were counted among the daughters of Ocean.
