Vampires are among the most enduring figures in death lore, often described as the un-dead: entities occupying a liminal space between the living and the dead. All vampires share one defining quality: they drain vitality from others, whether through blood, life force, or psychic energy.

Origins of Vampirism

Different traditions attribute the creation of vampires to different causes. Improper or inadequate funeral rites are frequently cited as a source, as the soul fails to properly transition. Vampirism also carries a contagious quality: an attack from a blood-drinking vampire may transform the victim into a vampire in turn, or simply kill them. In Transylvanian folklore specifically, children born outside of Church-sanctioned marriages were said to be fated for vampirism. Ukrainian tradition understood vampires as the offspring of relations between witches and werewolves. Some legends identified those born on Christmas Day as future vampires. In other belief systems, certain spirits are considered innately vampiric by nature, with no prior cause required.

Traditional Repellents and Antidotes

Widely recognized defenses against vampires include bells, bright light, sunlight, garlic, onions, and silver bullets or bullets cast from melted bell metal. The effectiveness of crosses and Church-blessed holy water is subject to debate, with some traditions asserting it is protective and others treating it as later Christian influence overlaid on older folk belief.

Psychic Vampires

Psychic vampires are a distinct category of vampire with no connection to blood. These are living people who absorb vitality and energy from those around them, often entirely without intention. The effect is consistent: no matter how much you enjoy the person's company, time spent with them leaves you exhausted and drained. A weak or damaged aura makes a person particularly susceptible, as leaked psychic energy functions as an unconscious signal of vulnerability. Protection against psychic vampires focuses on shielding and fortifying one's own energy field rather than avoiding the person entirely.

Accessing Healing Energy: Unintentional Vampirism

A related but distinct phenomenon involves those who hold abundant life-force and energy, including healers, counselors, psychic practitioners, and caregivers, who find themselves drained because others unconsciously draw from their energy reserves. This form of vampirism carries no malevolence; in some traditions it is understood as one of the ways that magickal power is transmitted between people. However, those who experience it must establish clear internal boundaries determining when they are available to give and when they are not. Black tourmaline and physical barriers such as sunglasses or headphones can help demarcate personal space.

Vampire Sorcerers: Soul Stealers

In Chinese magickal tradition, practitioners who extend their own power by consuming the life-force of another are known as soul stealers. The most common method involves taking strands of the victim's hair and combining them with specific incantations to extract the soul. A victim whose hair has been taken may be saved if the hair is recovered and a shaman intervenes. Some methods are sexually oriented. A separate technique targets newborns specifically: peach wood is secretly primed near a pregnant woman until the baby is born, then carved into a doll resembling the child. Incantations invite a spirit into the doll, gradually drawing the infant's soul out of the body and into the sorcerer's control. The child wastes away as its spirit migrates. Should the infant die, the spirit passes entirely to the sorcerer, who can use it for divination and other services.

Magickal Repellents

Beyond traditional antidotes, more specialized occult workings exist to banish, repel, and destroy vampires. These include protective charms worn on the body, infused oils applied to the skin, and workings performed directly at the vampire's gravesite.
