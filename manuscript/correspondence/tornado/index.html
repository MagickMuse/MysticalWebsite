In Frank L. Baum's The Wizard of Oz, the tornado sweeps Dorothy off the plains of Kansas and sets her down, by pure chance, in a wholly different land. With its ordered, spiraling form, the tornado or whirlwind stands for a higher power steering the course of mortal lives. That supernatural force is not necessarily kind or cruel; more often it is pictured as wholly indifferent.
