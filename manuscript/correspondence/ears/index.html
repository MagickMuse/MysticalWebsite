👂Earring Line of Defense

Before reading and writing were widespread, information was received by ear, and so the ear is a symbol of knowledge and of memory.

The shape of the ear hints at further meaning. Resembling a spiral or a whorled shell, a form not unlike that of the vulva, the ear is also a symbol of birth. In a Hindu story, Karna, son of the Sun God Surya, was born from his mother's ear. The same idea appears in depictions of the Virgin Mary receiving the message of the Holy Spirit, in the form of a dove, through her ear, expressing the belief that she conceived the Spirit just as she might hear a sound, the idea of the Virgin Birth.

In China, large ears and long earlobes are linked with both longevity and wisdom; Lao Tzu, the founder of Taoism, was nicknamed "long ears." The Buddha too is shown with extended earlobes as a sign of wisdom and royalty. Pointed ears belong to the God Pan, and to fauns, satyrs, pixies, and similar otherworldly creatures.

In ancient Egypt the ear was associated with the cycle of life, the right ear receiving the "air of life" and the left the "air of death."

Piercing the ears is an ancient practice still carried out all over the world, and the piercings have often carried a coded meaning. A pierced ear could be a sign of servitude or subjugation, yet wealthy Romans pierced their ears so that earrings could display their wealth. Sailors pierced both ears in the belief that it improved their eyesight, and members of the Bektashi order of the Sufi faith pierce one ear to show their celibate status.

An ancient Irish druidic charm also concerns the ears. The "Briamon Smethraige" is said to be a spell in which the druid rubbed the ear of a person and so caused their "death." This was most likely a symbolic death rather than a physical one, the rubbing of the ears a gesture equivalent to shunning someone, cutting them off from all communication.
