Common Cold Therapeutic Index

Definition

The common cold is a viral upper respiratory infection that produces nasal congestion, sneezing, sore throat, low-grade fever, fatigue, and a runny or stuffy nose. Symptoms usually peak within two to three days and resolve within a week to ten days. Aromatherapy supports the body by easing congestion, soothing irritated mucous membranes, lifting fatigue, and reinforcing immune response while the cold runs its course.

Safety: Dilute essential oils before topical application (1 to 2 percent for adult skin). Avoid steam inhalation with very young children, asthmatics, and those prone to bronchospasm; use gentle diffusion instead. Eucalyptus, peppermint, and rosemary should not be used on or near the faces of children under five. Hyssop, sage, and high-camphor oils (camphor, spike lavender, lavandin) should be avoided in pregnancy and epilepsy. Cold-pressed citruses are phototoxic; avoid sun exposure on treated skin for 12 to 18 hours.

Suggested Application of Oils

  • Massage: Dilute 2 to 3 drops in 5 ml of carrier oil and massage gently into the chest, throat, upper back, and shoulders. Slow upward strokes toward the heart support drainage and ease tightness.
  • Bath: Disperse 6 to 8 drops in a tablespoon of carrier oil or whole milk and add to a warm bath. Soak for 15 to 20 minutes to ease aches, encourage sweating, and support rest.
  • Vaporizer/Diffuser: Add 4 to 6 drops to an electric diffuser or aroma vaporizer to perfume the room with antiseptic and decongestant vapor. Especially useful overnight and during shared sick-room time to reduce ambient pathogen load.
  • Steam Inhalation: Add 2 to 3 drops to a bowl of hot (not boiling) water, drape a towel over the head, close the eyes, and inhale gently for 5 to 10 minutes. The warm vapor thins mucus, opens the nasal passages, and delivers antimicrobial constituents directly to the upper respiratory tract. Repeat 2 to 3 times per day during the acute phase.
