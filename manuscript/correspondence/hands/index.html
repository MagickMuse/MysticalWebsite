Hands in Magickal Practice

The hands are tools for beseeching, invoking, blessing, unification, drawing energy inward, and extending inner energies outward to share. The palms both absorb and release energy. During spellwork, the hands gather the energies called upon and direct them into the spell materials.

Channeling with the Hands

The right hand draws in Elemental Fire for energizing and empowering. The left hand draws in Elemental Water to soothe, protect, and urge matters to completion. Both hands together invoke, unite, receive, and bless.

Directing Energy

Both hands can focus raised energy into a palpable ball, then push that energy into the spell object. Both hands can also push (send) energy in the general direction needed for the spell to work, or upward into the Universe for action. The hands move energy on their own or with a wand or athame in hand.

Raising Energy through Movement

Dancing around the Circle with arms raised and palms open gathers in energy. In coven practice, dancing with hands held or opened raises a cone of energy at the center of the group, which the leader then directs.

Symbolism

Word origins often hint at the nature of things. The Latin word for hand is manus, which shares its root with words such as "manifestation," a clear sign that to be manifest is to be held in or made by the hand.

The hand is among the most accessible and expressive parts of the body. We shake hands in greeting, and use our hands to make signs and symbols, to gesture, and to communicate. We can quite literally talk with our hands, and the hand as an instrument of language is used the world over, letting people understand one another even when they share no spoken tongue, for the meaning of gesture transcends nationality and culture.

To give one's hand is to imply trust, as when we speak of giving someone's hand in marriage. Shaking the right hand on meeting is a sign of friendship and trust, showing that neither person holds a weapon.

This right and left symbolism recurs often. The right hand is linked with cleanliness and the left with dirt, and in some countries to offer something with the left hand is seen as an insult. As an extension of the will and the intention, the hands carry great power, and in the practice of "laying on of hands" they serve as agents of healing energy.

Hand Gestures

Hand gestures can speak volumes in silence. The "V for Victory" sign, palm forward with index and middle fingers extended, is recognized everywhere, as is its rude version with the palm turned inward. An apocryphal story traces this signal to the Hundred Years War, when the bow was a major weapon. The English were renowned with the longbow, and captured archers are said to have had the index and middle fingers used to draw the string cut off by the French, so the gesture was born as a sign of taunting defiance.

The meaning of a gesture can change from place to place. The mano fico, or "sign of the fig," made by thrusting the thumb between the middle and index fingers of a curled hand, may have been a good-luck charm for the ancient Romans and for modern Brazilians, yet elsewhere it is insulting and even threatening. The mano cornuta, or "horned hand," also has a double meaning: the index and little fingers point straight while the thumb curls over the middle two. Also called the "goat's horns," it may be an ancient sign to ward off the evil eye by mimicking the horns of the Devil, though others read it as a mark of allegiance with evil forces. Made secretly behind a person's head, it signals that their partner is unfaithful, a reference to the horns of the goat, an animal of lascivious reputation.

The Japanese beckoning cat, or Maneki Neko, uses a welcoming gesture understood everywhere, the palm raised to shoulder height and facing outward. Another beckoning sign hooks the index finger repeatedly, as if reeling something in, asking a person to come closer.

The sign of benediction or blessing is also universal, the index and middle fingers extended while the others curl into the palm. First recorded among the Romans, who used it to gain attention or to signal that they were about to speak, a more elaborate version of the raised hand of a schoolchild wanting to answer, it is used to bless holy water, wine, and bread, and it crosses religious boundaries, used by the Pope as well as by druids and Wiccans.

The clenched fist is a symbol of power and unity, of victory and defiance, with power held tightly in the hand.

Crossed fingers are a universal sign of hope or good luck, usually made as a wish is spoken aloud. The gesture carries one of two meanings: the cross is a protective shape that averts the evil eye, or any bad luck is trapped within it. Someone telling a lie might also cross their fingers in secret to avert the misfortune the lie could bring.

The "thumbs up" has come to mean approval and "thumbs down" the opposite. Though filmmakers use them to signal a gladiator's life or death, their true origins are uncertain and may reach back to a time when the thumbprint was used to seal documents.

Mudras

This Sanskrit word comes from the verb mud, "to please," the sense being that it is the Gods who are pleased, and it also means "seal," "sign," or "mark." Mudras are hand signals of a more sacred nature than the secular gestures above. They carry spiritual meaning not only through intention but because each part of the hand and each finger is dedicated to a deity. Mudras are used in yoga and dance as well as in religious images and statues; the Buddha, for instance, is usually shown with his hands in these eloquent gestures.

In yogic practice, mudras help focus the mind on abstract ideas and on the intention behind a pose, and practitioners say the movements connect directly to the nervous system and can aid breath control. Each finger carries several meanings and is dedicated to one of the five elements: the thumb is space, the index finger air, the middle finger fire, the fourth finger water, and the little finger earth.

Some widely known gestures grow from these sacred signs. The Chin Mudra is effectively the same as the "OK" sign, a symbol of approval or that all is well, the tips of the index finger and thumb closing in a circle while the other three fingers stay straight. Because in Hindu belief the thumb represents the universal spirit and the index finger the individual spirit, the circle they form symbolizes the self meeting the universe, a circle of completion and wholeness.

Another universally known gesture is the Anjali Mudra, or Namaskara Mudra, which many in the West recognize as the gesture of prayer, both hands together at chest level. The touching palms represent the union of spirit and matter, and the gesture seals and contains energy. Often accompanied by a bow, the word namaskara, or namaste, means "I bow to you."

Religious Associations

In the Kabbalah, the left hand of God signifies justice and the right hand mercy, and blessings and benedictions are given with the right hand.
