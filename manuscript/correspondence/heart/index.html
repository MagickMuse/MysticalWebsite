Physically the heart keeps blood flowing around the body at a steady pace, yet symbolically it has come to mean far more than this simple pump. Just as the head holds the wisdom born of knowledge and learning, the heart holds the wisdom of feeling and empathy, and it is a symbol of compassion, love, and charity.

The heart stands for the very center of being, physical and spiritual, and has been twinned with the soul since the earliest times, even before the Egyptian "heart-soul" was weighed by Ma'at, the Goddess of Truth. As the one organ left in the mummy, the ideal heart was meant to be as light as a feather; Ma'at wore the ostrich feather, with its evenly balanced fronds, as a symbol of justice, and the heart should not be weighed down by misdeeds or untruths.

The shape of the heart resembles an inverted triangle, the basis of the simplified heart symbol. The heart is also shown as a chalice, or in ancient Egyptian hieroglyphs as a vase, in each case a receptacle. The heart shape also recalls the female pubic mound, or yoni, and the cuneiform sign for woman is heart shaped, likely based on the same part of the body.

To put your heart and soul into something is to give it all the energy and commitment you can muster, and to place a hand on the heart while making a vow shows sincerity in keeping it.

The heart is believed to be the first part of the body formed in the womb and the first organ to die.

The heart became linked with love relatively recently, in the Middle Ages, and today the stylized heart is synonymous with both the word and the idea of love, most of all around St. Valentine's Day on February 14.

Religious Associations

In many religions the heart symbolizes God. In the Hindu faith it is the Brahmapura, the House of Brahma; in Christianity the Kingdom of God; in China the Home of the Spirit. The idea of the heart as the body's center and the seat of the divine is carried outward into the world too, so that in the Jewish tradition the Holy of Holies is the heart of the temple in Jerusalem, which is itself the heart of the world. This sense of the heart as the home of God is echoed in the Kabbalistic image of the inverted heart containing the letters of the tetragrammaton, the secret name of God.

In Islam the heart represents the inner life of a person, of meditation and contemplation. Called the Qalb, in the Sufi tradition it stands not only for God's mercy but is also believed to hold the essence of God, governing the physical organs of the body as well as the thoughts.

The heart of Joan of Arc was said to have survived the flames that killed her at the stake, taken as a sign of her truthfulness and that she was indeed chosen by God.

The Sacred Heart is popular in Catholic imagery, showing Christ with a flaming heart ringed by a crown of thorns and topped by the cross; here the heart refers to the manner of his death, the fire standing for the fire of eternal love.
