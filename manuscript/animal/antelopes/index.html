Symbolism

Perhaps the most striking and beautiful feature of the antelope is its eyes, and among the highest compliments paid to a woman in the East is to say that she has the eyes of a gazelle.

Those large eyes are not only lovely but keen, able to catch movement as far as four miles away. This sharp sight made the creature sacred to the goddess of wisdom, Athena, also known as Minerva, who shares the animal's clear perception. The Egyptians noticed the same faculty and credited the antelope with powers of prophecy. The yearly flooding of the Nile was a critical season for them, and they believed the animal gave warning of it by sneezing.

Its speed also shaped its meaning. Bursts of up to seventy miles an hour have been recorded within fifteen seconds of a standing start, and it can hold thirty to forty five miles an hour with ease. For this reason the Rig Veda describes the gazelle as carrying the wind upon its back.

⚠️ The lore that follows belongs to Native American tradition, a closed practice. It is recorded here for understanding only and should be practiced solely by those within the originating cultures.

For Native American peoples the animal's swiftness, vision, and peaceable nature made it sacred. Its bones were kept safely for a year before being laid to rest in a rite that called upon the spirit of the antelope to aid the hunt. The creature was regarded as a teacher of humankind endowed with magickal power, and as one of the spirit animals into which a skilled shaman could transform.
