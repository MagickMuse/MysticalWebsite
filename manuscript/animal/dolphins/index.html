The dolphin ranks among the most beloved animal symbols, embraced widely in modern spiritual culture for its gentle smiling face, its keen intelligence, its ability to communicate vocally with others of its kind, and its nature as a mammal wholly at ease in a watery world otherwise reserved for fish.

Name and the Oracle

The word traces to the Greek delphinos, meaning womb. The Delphic Oracle, where the priestesses delivered their prophecies, carries the same root. That temple belonged to Apollo, who was said to arrive at Delphi in the form of a dolphin.

Guide to the Otherworld

For thousands of years the dolphin has been regarded as a helpful friend to humankind. It also serves as a psychopomp, a guide to the other world, a role well suited to a creature able to move through a dimension foreign to people and long seen rescuing those who might otherwise drown. In Greek art it appears on vases and friezes bearing the souls of the dead upon its back toward the Isles of the Blest. Racing through the water alongside ships, dolphins act as vanguards of the sea gods such as Poseidon and Neptune, who could shield humans from the ravages of the oceans when they chose.

Metamorphosis and Rescue

The dolphin is also a symbol of metamorphosis. A Greek legend tells of pirates who seized Dionysus and bound him, then fell overboard and were transformed into dolphins. Its habit of aiding those in peril at sea became the subject of fable. One tale describes a beautiful youth and poet who dived into the water to escape sailors bent on killing him; dolphins lifted him from the sea and carried him to safety on their backs.

Savior of Souls

In its role as the saver of souls, the dolphin has been compared to Christ and to the Roman sun god Mithras, whose worship was eventually displaced by that of Christianity.
