Amulet Uses

A ladybug carried as an amulet attracts good luck, wealth, and success.

Dedicated to the Goddess

A member of the beetle family, the ladybug's bright shell and spots have long made it a favorite of children, and it appears in countless poems and nursery rhymes. It has always been dedicated to the Goddess, and once the pagan religions gave way to Christianity it was said to be named for the Virgin Mary.

Luck, Prosperity, and Fertility

Wherever the ladybug appears it is a sign of good luck, and in Turkey they are known as lucky bugs. As a prolific insect it also stands for prosperity and fertility, and folk belief holds that if a ladybug crawls across a girl's hand she will be married within the year.

Protection and Little Things

The ladybug is a symbol of protection. The story goes that in the Middle Ages, when aphids and other pests were ruining the crops, desperate farmers prayed to the Virgin for help. Soon afterward thousands of ladybugs appeared and cleared the aphids away. For this reason the ladybug is a reminder of the power held in little things, and of the truth that the whole is greater than the sum of its parts.

Self-Reliance

The ladybug defends itself from larger predators by giving off a scent that repels would-be attackers, which makes this small insect a symbol of self-reliance.

The Meaning of the Spots

The number of spots upon its back is significant too, said to mark the time it will take for something good to arrive, six hours or six days, for example.
