Symbolism

Among some superstitious people in Britain, especially those with ties to the sea, it is taboo to speak the rat's name aloud, so the animal is called the "longtail" instead. The custom grows from the old belief that rats abandon a sinking ship, an idea that runs so deep that the rat is still regarded as a harbinger of doom. To name a creature is to call upon the power of its owner, and the very reluctance to do so is a measure of the rat's power.

In the West most of the rat's symbolism carries a negative charge. To call a person a rat is to brand them immoral, despicable, dishonest, and greedy, and the animal is thought of as an unclean scavenger, bound up with the bubonic plague and other diseases. Yet the rat is also remarkably resilient, and its intelligence and its capacity to survive almost anything are widely acknowledged. Even these strengths are given a darker twist and dismissed as mere cunning, a self-serving and somehow immoral sort of cleverness.

In the Hindu tradition the rat is seen very differently, as a creature of foresight and prudence. In China it is one of the rulers of the years in the Zodiac, where it stands for imagination, creativity, adaptability, and ambition.
