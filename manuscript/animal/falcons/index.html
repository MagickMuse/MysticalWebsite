Falcons belong to the genus Falco and are found on every continent except Antarctica, making their home in mountainous areas, open woodland, and urban environments where tall buildings serve in place of cliffs. The peregrine falcon averages 17 inches in length with a wingspan of approximately 42 inches and a weight of around 2.5 pounds. A distinctive hooked curve at the tip of the beak is one of the falcon's identifying features. Male peregrine falcons are notably smaller than females.

Interesting Facts

Falcon refers to any species within the genus Falco. These birds are renowned for a hunting dive called a stoop, during which the falcon pauses in the air before arrowing downward with extraordinary speed to seize prey. Peregrine falcons have been recorded reaching speeds of approximately 200 miles per hour in these stoops, though regular flight averages around 30 mph and pursuit speed reaches approximately 65 mph. Like eagles, falcons possess remarkably precise eyesight. One of the older names for falcons was tiercel, meaning "third," based on the belief that only one egg in three hatched a male bird.

Mythology and Folklore

The falcon was one of the sacred forms associated with the Egyptian god Horus, deity of war and hunting. The Eye of Horus talisman, correctly known as the Wedjat, represents the all-seeing eye of both bird and god. The hieroglyph of a falcon refers directly to Horus and carries the meaning "that which is above."

Freyja, the Norse goddess of love and sexuality, possessed a falcon-feather cloak with which she shapeshifted and took flight. Falconry, the practice of hunting game with trained birds of prey, was a widely practiced and prestigious social pursuit in medieval Europe. The falcon is sometimes used as a symbol of freedom from bondage, evoking the image of a trained bird that escapes the keeper's hand and flies free, never to return. In a similar vein, the falcon can represent unfettered or passionate love, drawing on the image of a tamed bird that, once unhooded and untied, bursts forth in full pursuit of its desire.

Omens and Divinatory Meaning

The falcon is power in a small package. These birds move efficiently in more than one sense, wasting no energy or motion. A falcon sighting may prompt reflection on your own efficiency: are you making the most of your resources? You may benefit from sharpening your split-second decision making.

"Peregrine" means wanderer. A peregrine falcon sighting may be a reminder that movement matters. This does not necessarily mean a literal relocation, though it might. More often, it suggests that clinging too tightly to the familiar can become limiting. Being overly comfortable and failing to seek new horizons can diminish potential. The invitation is to push outside your comfort zone and expose yourself to new ideas and perspectives.

The falcon is a diurnal symbol, broadly associated with the sun. However, Horus had two eyes: one representing the sun and the other the moon. The falcon can therefore also be read as a symbol of balance and completion, holding both light and dark, day and night, within its nature.
