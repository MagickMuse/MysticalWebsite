Symbolism

The mole was once held to be a magical animal with the power to see into the future, largely because it moves through a dimension alien to humankind, living in the bowels of the earth in a state of blindness yet still able to reach hidden knowledge. This is a quality it shares with other creatures that operate under cover of darkness, such as the owl. Elizabethans believed that eating the heart of a mole would grant them its divinatory powers, provided they could stomach the method, for the heart had to be torn from the animal and eaten while it was still pulsating.

In a thirteenth-century bestiary the mole appears as an emblem of the earth element, and as a symbol of healing it was an attribute of the Greek God Asclepius and of the Indian God Rudra. The secrets the mole reaches in its underground domain include knowledge of the mysteries of death, and once these were mastered it followed naturally that the mole could heal the sick and even bring the dead back to life.

It is possible that the network of tunnels the creature builds beneath the ground influenced and inspired the ancient mazes and labyrinths.
