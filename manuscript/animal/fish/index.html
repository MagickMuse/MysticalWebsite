Symbolism

The fish is the foremost symbol of the element of water, and from that root its meanings branch out in many directions. It dwells in the depths that stand for the Underworld and reaches secret places closed to humankind. Since water is bound up with the womb and with birth and rebirth, the fish carries these meanings too. Its access to hidden depths also makes it a keeper of secret and sacred knowledge. The Vedas, said to hold all arcane wisdom, were delivered by a fish that served as an avatar of Vishnu, and for the Celts the salmon was a fish of wisdom and hidden knowledge. Christ chose fishermen as his disciples, and early Christians, forced to keep their faith hidden, recognized one another by a fish shaped symbol called the Ichthys.

The fish is linked with the feminine as well. The sacred shape known as the Vesica Piscis is fishlike in form, and the scent of the female body further tied the fish to the eternal feminine and to the element of water and the sea. In Syria the goddess Qedeshet is commonly shown holding a fish.

Because fish lay vast quantities of eggs, they stand for fertility and life. In the East a pair of fish is used as a lucky charm in wedding ceremonies, and in China the fish is paired with the crane as an emblem of longevity. The zodiac sign of two fish swimming in opposite directions is Pisces, and in Buddhism a pair of golden fish is one of the Eight Auspicious Symbols.
