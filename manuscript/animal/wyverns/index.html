Wyverns

◇Type
Mythical

🐉
Wyverns function as archetypal symbols of primal, untamed draconic energy. Distinct from the more sovereign Dragon, Wyvern energy encompasses raw force, fierce territorial instinct, and the wilder, more dangerous aspects of elemental power.

Related to but distinct from Dragons, Wyverns appear in European heraldry and folklore as two-legged winged serpents, frequently associated with pestilence, war, and territorial aggression. Where the Dragon is often depicted as wise and sovereign, the Wyvern is wilder and less governed by intellect, pure elemental ferocity.

In magickal practice, Wyvern energy is invoked for fierce, fast-moving protection, the aggressive defense of boundaries, and workings where a practitioner needs to project an aura of dangerous credibility rather than mere authority.

Form and Name

The word wyvern derives from the Latin vipera, meaning viper or serpent. The creature is assembled from the parts of several different beasts, and its look shifts from one artist to the next; in the main, though, it bears the head of a dragon, with the same fire-breathing power, and the tail of a serpent, sometimes ending in a barbed tip. It has wings and the talons of a bird of prey, and, unlike other dragon-like creatures, it stands on only two legs.

The Serpent Woman

French myth gives the wyvern the head and torso of a voluptuous woman, a further instance of the old link between the female and the serpent. This figure carries an added, exotic allure in the ruby set at the point of her third eye, said to help her find her way through the Underworld.
