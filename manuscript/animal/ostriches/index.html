The ostrich is best known, unfairly, as a symbol of avoidance and willful ignorance, owing to the belief that it hides its head in the sand. The habit is a myth, and a useful reminder that a symbol can grow from error rather than truth: in fact the ostrich buries its eggs in the sand to protect them and will sometimes lie over the nest to disguise it, a nest that may hold as many as thirty eggs from several different birds. It is likewise thought stupid for swallowing sharp objects and stones, though these abrasives merely aid its digestion.

Symbol of Truth

It is a fitting irony that the two best-known ostrich symbols rest on misunderstandings, for as an emblem of truth the ostrich feather is unrivalled. The feather is the attribute of Ma'at, the Egyptian goddess of truth, who weighs it upon her scales of reckoning against the heart, the seat of the conscience. It carries this meaning because, unlike the feathers of flying birds, which are heavier on one side, the ostrich feather is perfectly symmetrical.

The World Egg

In Mali, ostrich eggs are set upon the pinnacles of Muslim temples, for the egg stands for the World Egg and, through the ostrich's long gestation, carries a reminder of faith and patience. There is also a tale that Barak Baba, the twelfth-century Dervish leader, induced the ostrich to fly, a story understood to be symbolic.

Feather Magick

Use ostrich feathers in charms and spells for truth.
