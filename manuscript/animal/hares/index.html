Lunar Creature of the Goddess

As a nocturnal animal, the hare carries the full symbolism of the Moon: light within the darkness, concealed wisdom, arcane knowledge, intuition, and the Goddess herself. The Moon stands for rebirth and resurrection through its visible phases, and the hare shares these meanings. In some parts of the world the shape of a hare can be seen in the face of the Moon, which deepens the bond between the two.

Fertility and the Easter Bunny

The hare is famed for its fertility, and in this guise it has been simplified into the Easter Bunny. The springtime goddess Eostre governed the cycles of fertility, and her emblem was the egg, which survives in the chocolate eggs given to children at Easter, a remnant of a pagan custom that its Christian inheritors have never fully explained away.

Sacred and Uneaten

An animal that is not eaten usually holds sacred status. Certain Muslim sects refuse the flesh of the hare, believing it to be the reincarnation of Ali, the Prophet's son in law, who intercedes between the faithful and Allah. Pre-Christian Celtic peoples likewise considered the hare too sacred to eat and kept them as pets.

A Savior in Animal Form

⚠️ The lore that follows belongs to Native American tradition, a closed practice. It is recorded here for understanding only and should be practiced solely by those within the originating cultures.

Some Native American peoples who later adopted Christianity came to link the hare with Jesus Christ, for an earlier savior of theirs was a Great Spirit Hare named Menebuch who held Christ-like qualities. Menebuch was said to have descended from the heavens to teach humankind and to guard them from monsters and evil spirits.

The Three Hares

Three hares joined at the ears and running in a circle in the form of a Triskele is a mysterious ancient symbol that has crossed cultural and geographical borders. At the center of the ring the ears form an equilateral triangle. The motif appears in Buddhist caves 2,500 years old, in Muslim art and design, in synagogues, and in several Christian places of worship across Europe. No single interpretation is settled, though it may express the attributes of the Triple Goddess, an old concept that reaches beyond religious dogma. As a lunar creature, the hare belongs to the Goddess.
