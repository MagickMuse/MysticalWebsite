Arachne's Myth

Arachne was a master weaver of fabrics and tapestries at Colophon in Lydia, the daughter of a man involved with the trade in the rare purple dye then reserved for royalty and the spiritual elite. There are various versions of Arachne's story but somehow she ended up in a tapestry-making contest with the goddess Athena, credited by the Greeks as the inventor of weaving.

Both wove tapestries; the general population was permitted to choose the winner. Arachne won, with a cynical tapestry mocking the lifestyles of the gods, especially Athena's father Zeus' prodigious love life. Daddy's girl was enraged. Exactly what happened next depends on the version of the story:

  • Athena transformed Arachne into a spider
  • Athena hanged Arachne and then changed her into a spider
  • Arachne hung herself but Athena, out of pity, changed the rope into a web and Arachne into a spider, the ultimate weaver

Spiders are now classified as belonging to the Arachnid family, as are scorpions, emblems of Egyptian goddesses.

Etymology and Spinning

The word "spider" derives from the Old English spinan, "to spin." It is thus closely related to "spinster," which although given the colloquial meaning "old maid" with the added implication of being dowdy and undesirable, technically refers to an unwed, independent woman. Spinning was once not only an occupation and art associated with women but a spiritual and magical tradition. Spiders are sponsors of spinning and emblems of witchcraft.

Species and Biology

There are perhaps 100,000 species of spiders on Earth. They are a unique species; only spiders create webs from within their bodies. The web is the spider's home and the manner in which she captures her prey. On the outside of her body, spiders possess four or six (depending upon species) spinnerets. Liquid spurts from these teat-like organs, which solidifies almost immediately on contact with air, forming spider silk. Spiders can employ one or more spinnerets as desired. Seven different types of spider silk exist; all spiders can produce three while some can produce more. The tensile strength of spider thread is second only to fused quartz.

Spider Webs and Artistry

Spider webs can be beautiful. Dew shining on spider webs in the sun resembles sparkling diamonds. Complex, artistic webs are spun by female spiders. Designs are mazelike and may have inspired labyrinths and mandalas. Spiders inspired the art of spinning; magical theory says spiders themselves taught women how to spin.

True artists, spiders spin webs out of their own bodies, in similar fashion to the way women birth babies and produce milk.

Predatory Nature

"'Come into my parlor,' said the spider to the fly..." All spiders are predatory. They suck their victims empty of fluids, leaving nothing but dead husks behind, in the manner of vampires or succubi. Various species, not only the black widow, cannibalize their mates and children.

Arachnophobia

Spiders terrify many people, disproportionately to their ability to harm. Arachnophobia is the scientific name for fear of spiders (and scorpions, too, which are also arachnids).

This primal, irrational fear is evoked in the gigantic threatening spiders in J. R. R. Tolkien's Return of the King and J. K. Rowling's Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets. Shelob, the female spider in the Tolkien book, particularly evokes some kind of terrible, primal, chthonic goddess. Her hunger and ferocity transform her into a veritable guardian spirit, albeit not for Frodo.

Black Widows

Black Widows truly are dangerous. Once you know what they look like, they're hard to mistake: shiny, glossy black spiders, the females wear a red hour-glass shape on their underside. Their color scheme and venomous potential, combined with their conjugal habits, make Black Widows the spiders most identified with witchcraft, their name synonymous with femmes fatales.

Mating Habits

Mating habits of spiders are pretty unique, too. Their mating terrifies and fascinates people, often especially men. Having consummated the relationship, the female spider, usually the larger of the two, often attempts to consume the male, quite frequently succeeding. (There's one male who won't kiss and tell!)

Spiders have been used as metaphors for the dangers of sex, both literally and also in terms of sex being the trap that leads to the death of men's immortal souls.

Fertility and Female Generative Power

Because spiders give birth to huge quantities of young at one time they are also ancient emblems of fertility and female generative power. Spiders are associated with birth, death, sex, immortality, destiny, and the acquisition of wealth, power, and magickal knowledge. Because spiders (and spinners) are understood to spin and cut the threads of life, many deities take the form of spiders or are allied with them.

Spider Goddesses

Spider goddesses include:

Askhe-tanne-mat, the Ainu spider goddess, manifests as a long-fingered woman who guides babies through the birth canal.

Female spider deities are heroines of Native North American spirituality; variously known by names like Spider Woman or Old Spider Grandmother they rescue people from disaster, sponsor culture heroes and perform miraculous actions like providing people with fire.

Morticia of the Addams Family, whose tight black spider gown is intended to evoke the magical Black Widow (although Morticia verges on the saintly!)

Folk Traditions

In Hungarian tradition, spider webs are the gossamer thread spun by fairies. In Germanic areas, spider webs are considered threads from Mother Holle's spindle.

Chinese mythology looks at both sides of the spider controversy. Spiders are sacred to the saintly Weaving Maiden, the romantic guardian spirit of young women. On the other hand, female spiders, bored with male spiders and seeking to up the ante, transform into the shape of beautiful women. Men, Chinese folklore warns, should you meet a mysterious, seductive maiden, be on your guard! She may be a spider transformed into a girl, out to ensnare you.

Halloween and Witchcraft Iconography

Spiders show up in classic Halloween iconography; fake spider webs decorate haunted Halloween houses. Halloween witch costumes frequently may as well be spider costumes. (Several years ago a witch dress named Spiderella was popular.) A spider-witch plays the role of the Weird Sisters in Akira Kurosawa's film interpretation of Macbeth, Throne of Blood.

Perhaps in remembrance of their former sacred status, it's believed unlucky to kill spiders. To do so is to risk losing wealth and spiritual protection.

Zigzag Spider: Reading the Web

Argiope aurantia

Spider crouches on her web, sensing creation's strands. Everything connects, she teaches, and understanding those connections proves essential. While seemingly waiting for prey, Spider actually reads the world's temperature and listens for relationship patterns. Breeze ruffling threads communicates force and direction. Thick morning dew signals autumn's arrival and warns of winter's scarce flies ahead.

Your life forms a web too. Pause to feel different strands' interplay and their effects on the overall weaving. Spider reminds you: be still and take time to comprehend interconnections.

Consider Your Web

Spider knows that pulling one strand affects the whole web's shape. Intentions and their resulting actions create strong pulls on the web you inhabit. Before manifesting intentions, carefully examine cause and effect they'll set in motion. Nothing occurs in isolation.

Create a mindmap to explore your web of effect: Place your intention in a center circle. Around it, add circles for everything affected by enacting this intention, such as partner, children, job, and yoga teacher. Consider relationships to family, friends, finances, career, home, spiritual life, and other world areas. Investigate thoroughly, examining both benefits and shadows. Understanding these ripples prepares you for conscious manifestation.

Amulet Uses

A spider carried as an amulet attracts good luck, protects from enemies, and promotes wisdom, work success, and money.

Weaver of the Universe

The image of the spider as creator and creatrix recurs across the world, and in many creation stories it is the spider who spins the very fabric of the universe. Yet the apparent frailty of the cobweb led some to suppose that what the spider weaves is only the illusory veil of reality, the Maya of Vedic teaching. The act of spinning and weaving belongs likewise to the Fates, both in Greek myth and in Islamic tradition. Some Native American peoples called the spider Thinking Woman, who holds the power to make the world and to unmake it should it fail to please her. In parts of Africa the spider was said to have furnished the materials from which the first man and woman were made, and to have woven the web of the Heavens. Able to travel in any direction and to drift through the air on its own thread, the spider was seen as a go-between linking humankind and the Gods.

Divination

That same power to move between dimensions made the spider an instrument of divination. The African bird-eating spider is held to be especially gifted in this art: tokens are set at the mouth of its burrow so that the creature disturbs them as it comes and goes, and the resulting patterns are read as omens.

The Disembodied Soul

Many peoples regard the spider as a soul set loose from the body. In some Vietnamese belief the spirit slips free during sleep and takes the shape of a spider, which makes it gravely unlucky to kill one. It is the spider, too, who weaves the boat that ferries the souls of the dead to the Afterlife.
