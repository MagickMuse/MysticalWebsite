The Scarab

The scarab is one of the most important symbols in ancient Egyptian belief, revered above all as the sacred animal of Khepera, the God of the Rising Sun. The Egyptians watched the scarab beetle roll its ball of dung across the ground just as Khepera was thought to roll the Sun across the sky, and because they believed the beetle hatched itself from that ball of dung, it became an emblem of death and rebirth. In its divine form the scarab was depicted with the wings, legs, and tail of a falcon.

During mummification a scarab was laid upon the heart of the corpse. Since the heart stood for the conscience, the scarab was meant to keep it from speaking, so that the soul could say nothing to hinder its passage into the Afterlife. So great was the creature's power that its image appears throughout Egyptian art and artifacts, on seals, amulets, and pieces of jewelry. Modern Egyptians still hold that dried and powdered scarab will help them conceive.

Although the scarab is most closely tied to Egypt, it had already served as an important symbol since the Paleolithic, between ten and twenty thousand years ago.

Amulet Uses

A scarab beetle carried as an amulet wards off evil and symbolizes eternal life.
