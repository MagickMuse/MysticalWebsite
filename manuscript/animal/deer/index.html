Symbolism & Medicine

Deer embodies graceful decisiveness and intuitive wisdom. She teaches the balance between careful observation and confident action, moving through life with both caution and certainty.

Behavioral Wisdom

Deer's medicine is never impulsive. She pauses first, feeling into each situation and reaching out with all her senses to assess the environment. Only after getting the lay of the land does she step gracefully and surely into action.

Magickal Applications

Deer offers support during life's cyclical changes and transitions, especially when one season ends and another begins. She gifts self-confidence and helps you make definitive decisions, then step into them with grace and trust in your intuition.

The Power of the Pivot

While Deer is a prey animal, swift and wily, she is far from helpless. Deer is known for duplicity, deceiving hunters into a false chase. She enters dense underbrush then gracefully and quickly pivots, running off in a different direction.

Reflection

Deer's tactical redirection illustrates how apparent vulnerability can be transformed through intelligent strategy. The pivot represents a sudden change of course that alters the balance of power, turning disadvantage into advantage through cunning rather than force.

Amulet Uses

A deer antler carried as an amulet promotes fertility, encourages quick gains, and attracts success.

The Celtic Otherworld

In the Celtic pantheon the deer is an otherworldly creature, belonging to the realm of the fairies and possessing magickal powers. Counted among the oldest of creatures, its home in the darkness of the forest lets it share in hidden secrets. Flidais, the Celtic Goddess of Wild Things, rode in a chariot drawn by deer.

Universal Symbolism

Some of the deer's meanings are found everywhere: meekness, gentleness, and swiftness. Despite the masculine energy of the stag, the deer is a feminine animal, linked to the Goddess in her aspect as a huntress. The antlers carry their own significance, standing for the tree of life and for fertility and vitality.

Psychopomp and Soul-Guide

The deer appears prominently in Paleolithic cave paintings, a reminder of how long it has been held significant. Shamans connected with the spirit of the deer by wearing its antlers and skins, believing the animal could speak with the Gods. For this reason the deer was also seen as a psychopomp, a sacred creature that guided the soul on its journey to the Afterlife. The Scythians reflected this by placing the deer motif on funerary equipment, hoping it would speed the soul to its final destination.

Buddhist and Hindu Lore

In Buddhism the deer recalls the teachings of the Buddha, who began his calling in a deer park. In Hindu stories the Goddess of Learning, Sarasvati, takes the form of the deer, and her followers hope that sitting on deerskin mats will help them absorb her knowledge.

The Hind

The hind, an old name for the doe, is essentially a female symbol, gentle and shy, at once a mother and a virgin. Several goddesses are bound to this lovely forest creature, among them Diana and Artemis, the goddess of the hunt, and the Celtic goddess of the woodland, Flidais. Genghis Khan was said to be the offspring of a hind and a blue wolf, an almost alchemical image in which the herbivore mates with the carnivore, the marriage of spirit and matter. The hind is also the shape taken by the Valkyries, who bear the spirits of dead warriors into the Afterworld.

The Ceryneian Hind of Greek myth is the origin of the Golden Hind, a creature with antlers of gold and hooves of brass that could run like the wind and was sacred to Artemis, the chaste goddess of the hunt, as the hind herself was chaste. Its capture was one of the labors of Hercules. For the Celts, the hunt for the hind was a metaphor for the search for wisdom.

The Stag and Masculine Power

The antlers of the stag mark it as a magickal and sacred animal and a potent masculine symbol, as horned creatures so often are. Through those antlers the stag becomes an emblem of fertility and male sexuality, and like the goat it lies behind the word "horny" for one who is sexually charged. Amulets shaped as the phallus were carved from stag horn, and some traditions of Chinese medicine grind the horn into an aphrodisiac.

Rebirth and the Sun

Because the antlers are shed and grown anew each year, they evoke the World Tree, and so the stag itself comes to stand for rebirth and, by extension, for the Sun. It is no accident that the Hopi cut their image of the Sun God from a piece of deerskin.

Pan, Cernunnos, and the Horned God

The pre-Christian God Pan wears the cloven hooves and horned headdress of the stag. Seeking to make the old Gods fearsome to those who had once worshipped them, and so to drive them toward the One God of the new faith, early Christians recast Pan as the Devil and gave him these same stag-borrowed features. The Celtic Horned God Cernunnos, Ruler of the Beasts and a God of Plenty, bears a striking likeness to Pan. So sacred was the stag to the early peoples of Switzerland that it was sometimes buried with full ceremony beside people and horses.

The Watchful Fighter

The stag's behavior shapes its meaning. It is a sensitive animal, catching scents on the air from far off, swift to act and able to cover great ground in little time. It is a courageous fighter as well, defending its territory with determination and clashing antlers against any rival that intrudes.

The White Stag

Wherever a white stag appears, in myth, in folktale, or in dreams, it signifies the world of spirit. In the Arthurian legends its ethereal presence sends the Knights off on spiritual quests. In China the white stag is the emblem of Shou-hsien, the God of Immortality.

Healer and Emblem of Christ

The stag is also credited with healing powers, and is sometimes shown with an arrow in its side and herbs in its mouth, for it was said to know the use of every medicinal plant in the forest and to eat them to mend its wound. The stag was believed to kill serpents as well, and where it is depicted trampling a snake underfoot it signifies the victory of good over evil. In this guise, and because it was hunted for sacrifice, the stag becomes an emblem of Christ, appearing then with a cross set between its antlers.
