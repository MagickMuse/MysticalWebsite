The Sheep and the Flock

It may seem strange that the grown form of the lamb, the sheep, carries such negative symbolism, standing for a creature that blindly follows the flock and cannot think for itself. The word sheep is generally used as an insult.

Innocence and Purity

The lamb, by contrast, is a far more positive symbol. It stands for innocence and purity, for the spiritual, the compliant, and the gentle. It is also a symbol of spring, of new hope, and of triumph over adversity. The first lamb of the season, as the most potent embodiment of these qualities, was usually sacrificed to the gods.

The Sacrificial Lamb

The sacrificial nature of the lamb resonates through the Christian, Jewish, and Muslim faiths, giving rise to the image of the Christ as the Lamb of God, sacrificed for all humankind yet resurrected by a beneficent God. One particular symbol, the Paschal Lamb, embodies this perfectly, the lamb shown with a halo and a banner to signify both sanctity and victory.

Peace and Concord

The lamb is also a symbol of peace, an aspect deepened when it appears alongside the dove. At times the lamb and the lion are shown together, a universal emblem of concord and harmony and the balancing of opposites.
