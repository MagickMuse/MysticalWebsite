Griffins function as archetypal symbols of divine guardianship and sovereign power. Their energy (valor, the protection of what is precious, and mastery of both sky and earth) is fully accessible through the creature's correspondences and image.

Combining the eagle's aerial dominion with the lion's terrestrial authority, the Griffin represents the union of two apex predators into a single guardian force. Across Greek, Persian, and medieval European traditions, Griffins protected treasure, sacred sites, and divine authority. They pulled the chariot of the sun and guarded the gold of the Hyperboreans.

Connection between life and death, and between heaven and earth.

In magickal practice, Griffin energy is invoked for the protection of valuable things (material or spiritual), for courage in facing powerful adversaries, and in workings that require the integration of intellectual clarity (eagle) with instinctual strength (lion).

Form and Heraldry

The griffin has the body of a lion and the head of an eagle, sometimes with prominent tufted ears, and usually, though not always, a pair of wings. It is sometimes confused with the wyvern, but the griffin has four legs to the wyvern's two, and those legs end in an eagle's talons. Aeschylus, in his play Prometheus Bound, called griffins the sharp-toothed hounds of Zeus that do not bark. As a creature that economically unites the eagle and the lion, the griffin makes an admirable heraldic emblem: in medieval symbolism it first stood for knowledge and later for the guardian, its ears marking attentiveness, its lion's body courage, and its beak tenacity.

Guardian Across Civilizations

The griffin appears in many civilizations, among them the Greek and the Egyptian, and its chief role everywhere is to protect what is precious. In Crete it was the guardian of throne rooms; in central Asia it watched over deposits of gold and gems, falling upon any who came as a threat and tearing them apart with its talons.

The Hippogriff

The hippogriff, lately brought out of mythological obscurity by its appearance in J.K. Rowling's Harry Potter series, is the offspring of a griffin and a horse.
