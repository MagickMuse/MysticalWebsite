Temperance

Symbolism

The camel wears a disdainful expression that some take for bad temper. Despite its truculent manner and stubborn reputation, it is invaluable to people, the only creature able to carry them across a desert. A symbol of sobriety and temperance, and commonly known as the ship of the desert, the camel can survive long periods without drinking and has been used for thousands of years to reach otherwise inaccessible places.

The camel has become a pictorial metaphor for the Middle East, and its image appears on all sorts of products that come from the region.

Because the camel protects travelers on their perilous journey across parched land, its role as a guardian is implied in the Persian holy scriptures. In the Avesta, winged camels watch over the earthly paradise.

Saint John the Baptist, among other ascetics, drew on another aspect of the camel as inspiration for his unworldly lifestyle. The creature's austerity meant that the coarse cloth made from its hair was a suitable material for robes.
