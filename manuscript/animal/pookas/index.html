A Shape-Changer of Celtic Folklore

The pooka of Celtic folklore, known as the púca in Ireland and the pwca in Wales, is a changeling creature that may take the shape of a goat or an eagle but most often appears as a spirit horse. Its appearance is usually a warning of death.

The Fatal Ride

Like the kelpie, the pooka can tempt a person to climb onto its back, but the journey that follows always ends in the rider's death. It speaks with a human voice and will sometimes lure people out of their homes.

Samhain and the Last of the Harvest

The pooka is bound up with the old pre-Christian festival of Samhain, which became Halloween. Any crops still standing in the fields after that night were left ungathered, given over to appease the Pooka.

The Horse as Psychopomp

The horse is among the creatures regarded as a psychopomp, able to guide the souls of the dead on their journey to the Afterlife, and the pooka most likely grew out of this older horse symbolism.
