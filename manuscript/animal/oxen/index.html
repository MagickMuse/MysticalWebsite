Symbol of Patient Strength

The ox stands for patience, strength, and hardworking amiability. As a beast of burden it is an invaluable asset to humankind, above all in agricultural work, and the plowing of the first furrows of the season is treated as an act of magical significance, accompanied by much ceremony.

Worthy of Sacrifice

Only valuable animals were deemed worthy of sacrifice to the Gods, and the esteem in which the Ancient Greeks held the ox is shown by the hecatomb, an offering of as many as one hundred oxen at once. The danger of violating such sacred animals runs through the old stories: when the starving crew of Odysseus slaughtered and ate the sacred oxen on one of the islands where they landed, defying his orders, every one of them perished except Odysseus himself.

A Sacred Animal

The ox is regarded as holy well beyond India, where the cow holds its famous sacred status. It is sacred too across many parts of North Africa. As a religious symbol the ox serves as the emblem of St Luke the Evangelist, and because it is an animal of sacrifice, parallels are drawn between the ox and the sacrifice of Christ.
