Lice are tiny, wingless ectoparasites that feed on the blood of mammals and birds. Three species infest humans: head lice (Pediculus humanus capitis), body lice (Pediculus humanus humanus), and pubic lice (Pthirus pubis). Infestations spread by close contact and shared brushes, hats, bedding, or clothing, and tend to flourish wherever bodies congregate, regardless of cleanliness. The aromatic oils gathered below have a long folk and clinical history of dispatching adult lice and discouraging their return.

Always dilute essential oils for scalp use. Patch-test on the inner forearm before applying to a child's head, and avoid the eyes. Pregnant or nursing people should avoid parsley, rosemary, and thyme oils. Children under five should not be treated with rosemary or thyme oils.

Suggested Application of Oils

The following oils may be applied via skin oil/lotion or hair care. A common approach is a 1 to 2 percent dilution in a carrier oil (such as olive, coconut, or sweet almond), massaged thoroughly into the scalp and hair, covered with a shower cap, and left for 30 to 60 minutes before combing through with a fine-toothed nit comb and washing out with shampoo. Repeat every three to four days for at least two weeks to break the louse life cycle.

Alternatively, add 5 to 10 drops of essential oil to 100 ml of unscented shampoo or vodka and use as a scalp rinse.
