Unicorns function as archetypal symbols of untamed spiritual purity. Their energy of healing, luck, and the protection of innocence is fully accessible through the creature's correspondences and image, whether or not unicorns exist in a physical sense.

The Unicorn appears across European, Persian, and Chinese traditions as a creature of singular purity and grace that cannot be caught by force, but only by the approach of someone genuine in spirit. Its horn (the alicorn) was believed in medieval Europe to neutralize poison and purify water, making the creature synonymous with healing and discernment of truth.

In magickal practice, Unicorn energy is invoked for healing workings, the protection of the vulnerable, and situations where authenticity and integrity are the keys to success.

Amulet Uses

A unicorn image or figurine carried as an amulet promotes fertility, pureness, and sexuality.

Purity and the Phallic Horn

In tapestries the unicorn is shown sparkling white against a ground of dark green foliage. It carries a double meaning: on one hand it stands for purity and chastity, while on the other its single horn is plainly a phallic symbol. Set in the center of the head, the symbolic seat of the mind, the horn comes to represent the sublimation of the sexual urge rather than its indulgence.

The Virgin and the Church

Popular legend holds that only a virgin pure in mind as well as body may touch the unicorn, and medieval tapestries pairing the creature with the Virgin Mary served as reminders of the Virgin Birth. Even this innocent imagery was forbidden by the Church in the late Middle Ages, judged too erotic.

Lion and Unicorn

The lion and the unicorn often appear together, most famously on heraldic devices, where the lion stands for the Sun and the unicorn for the Moon.
