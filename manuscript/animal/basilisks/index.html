From Lizard to Monster

Many creatures taken for myth began as ordinary animals. The living basilisk is a hooded lizard with an inflatable crest upon its head. The basilisk that haunts the human imagination, however, is something else: a hybrid of bird and snake whose glance or breath brings death. It was said to hatch from a misshapen egg laid on a dung heap by an aged hen and brooded by a toad, details that all mark it as a creature of evil and ungodliness, fitting for a serpent hybrid.

The Venom and the Mirror

The basilisk was said to rampage without restraint, smashing all in its way or burning obstacles with its fiery breath. The Roman writer Pliny the Elder told of a horseman who thought he had slain one by driving a spear through it, only for the venom to race back up the shaft and kill both horse and rider. The one sure way to destroy the beast was to make it look into a mirror, whereupon its own reflection would undo it.

The Weasel and the Herb of Grace

Curiously, the single natural creature able to face the basilisk unharmed was the weasel, so long as it had first eaten rue, the herb of grace. A carving in Worcester Cathedral shows a basilisk flanked by two weasels with foliage in their mouths, where the monster stands for Satan overcome by the Old and New Testaments.

The Unconscious Confronted

In psychological terms the basilisk is read as the unconscious mind, a force that must be faced and understood rather than fled.
