The hoopoe is a crested bird with a long and storied association with magic, prophecy, and the bridging of the worlds of spirit and matter. An indifferent housekeeper that rarely clears the debris from its nest, and a notoriously foul-smelling creature, it was named an unclean bird in Hebrew scripture and forbidden as food. Despite this, it was honoured across the ancient world, its image appearing on the tomb walls of Crete and Egypt.

Mythology and Folklore

Few birds are reckoned better at carrying messages between the worlds of spirit and matter. According to the Qu'ran, it was the hoopoe that brought King Solomon word of the Queen of Sheba, and it alone could tell the King where to find the hidden underground springs. The Egyptians made the hoopoe a symbol of gratitude, for the young were believed to care for their aging parents, preening them and licking their eyes when their sight began to fail.

Magick and Folk Medicine

The entrails of the hoopoe were long believed to hold magical properties. They were said to sharpen vision and memory and to serve as an aphrodisiac, while the dried innards worn around the neck guarded against the evil eye. Among the Arabs the bird was held to be a doctor able to cure almost any sickness, and its blood and heart featured in many folk remedies. The blood of the hoopoe was prized as a potent ink for the writing of spells.

Weather and Omens

The hoopoe is one of the birds said to foretell the weather, and storms in particular. A recent discovery lends an old superstition new weight: the bird can sense the minute piezoelectric charges in the atmosphere that herald a storm or an earthquake up to ten hours before it arrives.
