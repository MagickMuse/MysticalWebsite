Symbolism

A nocturnal animal that lives a solitary and private life, the lynx symbolizes hidden mysteries, occult knowledge, and clairvoyance. It is credited above all with keen sight, and people once believed it could see straight through tree trunks, walls, and even mountains. This is a fine example of an animal taking on symbolic traits that owe nothing to the truth and everything to appearances, for the lynx has no extraordinary eyesight at all, yet it came to stand for clear-sightedness in both the literal and the metaphorical sense.

The lynx was once far more common in the British Isles than it is today, and it shares the root of its name with that of the great British God of Light, Lugh. The harp was a deeply significant instrument for the Celts and others, and strings made from lynx-gut were said to give music that was especially divine and sacred, carrying the animal's magical properties in its melodies.

One of the stranger beliefs about the lynx was that its urine hardened into precious stones, which the creature then buried in the ground so that no one could find them. Pliny the Elder and others who held to this odd theory argued that the habit added to the lynx's reputation for greed.
