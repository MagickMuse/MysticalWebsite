Symbolism

The praying mantis takes its name not from any spiritual nature but from its shape and stance. The female has a sinister reputation for eating her mate, so it is no surprise that she stands for trickery and deception, though in certain parts of Africa the mantis represents the Creator.

Its trickster reputation is deepened by the way it feigns indifference, sitting motionless for long stretches in its distinctive praying posture before striking like lightning. The Japanese name for the creature, Kamakiri, captures this perfectly, since it means sickle cut. The fighting skill of the mantis is renowned, and an Asian martial art called Praying Mantis is said to have drawn its inspiration from the insect's prowess. Because it can kill creatures much larger than itself, the mantis became a symbol of power, set upon Samurai swords, buckles, and other artifacts.
