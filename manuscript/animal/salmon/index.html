Symbolism & Medicine

Salmon embodies unwavering determination and purposeful navigation. She represents the journey home to one's true self, swimming thousands of miles undaunted by obstacles, and always finding her way back to where she belongs.

Behavioral Wisdom

Salmon swims against incredible odds, facing eagles, bears, and waterfalls. When she is at her weakest and most exhausted, her route turns mostly upstream. This is not by accident or confusion. Salmon is one of the finest navigators to swim the Seven Seas and can always find her way home.

Magical Applications

Salmon helps you plumb the depths of your own unconscious to discover the energy and determination needed to reach your soul's destination. Call on Salmon to navigate life with determination, meaning, and purpose, and to quit griping and get going when obstacles seem overwhelming.

The Salmon of Knowledge

Irish legend tells of an ordinary salmon who consumed nine hazelnuts fallen into the Well of Wisdom. Infused with the well's power, those nuts held all worldly knowledge, and the salmon who ate them transformed into something far greater, the Salmon of Knowledge, the ultimate spiritual prize.

The ancient Irish understood that Salmon's extraordinary journey, navigating vast oceans by starlight, swimming upstream against impossible currents, returning unerringly home, mirrors the seeker's path toward enlightenment. But possessing the potential for wisdom is not the same as embodying it.

Salmon teaches that wisdom requires the full journey: diving deep, persevering through resistance, following inner knowing even when exhausted, and offering yourself completely to the process of transformation. The hazelnuts must be eaten, the journey must be swum, the price must be paid.

Symbolism

One of the most sacred creatures of the Celts, the salmon is a symbol of wisdom and esoteric knowledge, and within that culture it is arguably the very epitome of the fish symbol. To eat its flesh was once akin to a shamanic act, a way of taking in not only its wisdom but its reputed powers of second sight. Celtic folklore brims with tales of the gifts this sacred fish bestows, and it is commonly pictured living in holy wells and feeding on magical fare such as hazelnuts and rowanberries.

Finn, one of the most celebrated heroes of Celtic myth, was credited with immense wisdom and farsightedness that he gained quite by accident from the salmon. While he was cooking the fish for his master, hot cooking oil splashed onto his thumb, and when he licked it the knowledge passed to him.

The salmon is also bound up with rebirth and reincarnation, since it returns faithfully to its breeding ground, leaping upstream against the river's current to reach it. Even after the arrival of Christianity the salmon held on to its standing as a sacred symbol, helped no doubt by the use of the fish as an emblem of Christ.
