Vitriol

◇Notes/Warnings
An acronym for the Latin Visita Interiorem Terrae Rectificando Invenies Operae Lapidem.
◇Type
Emblem & Device

Overview

To call someone "vitriolic" is to say they are cruel or caustic in speech, but the word begins in alchemy. Vitriol, once called Oil of Vitriol, is sulfuric acid, a name given by the eighth-century alchemist Jabir ibn Hayyan.

The Hidden Formula

Sulfuric acid was held to be a prime ingredient in making the Philosopher's Stone, the substance reputed to grant immortality. A clue to its preparation was hidden in the letters of the word itself, an acronym for the Latin phrase: Visita Interiorem Terrae Rectificando Invenies Operae Lapidem. This translates roughly as "Go down into the bowels of the Earth; by distillation you will find the stone for the Work." As with all alchemical sayings, more than one meaning is folded in: beyond the literal making of the Stone, it also carries a philosophical and metaphysical sense.
