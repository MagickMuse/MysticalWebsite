Pentagram

Notes/Warnings

Overview

The pentagram is the five-pointed star, among the most widespread of all magickal signs. It may have been recognized in early astronomy in the Tigris-Euphrates region around six thousand years ago, and pottery bearing it survives from roughly four thousand years ago. It was Pythagoras who gave the star the prominence it still holds.

The Number Five

Five combines the feminine 2 and the masculine 3, making it the number of harmony, of the union of opposites, and of marriage. It is also the number of humankind, matching the five points of the body. Cut an apple across its equator and the seeds form a perfect pentagram, which ties the symbol to the Apple of Knowledge that Eve offered Adam.

Uses and Traditions

The pentagram is used in spell casting and the revelation of secrets. Pythagoras held that its five points stood for the elements earth, air, fire, water, and ether. Followers traced it over the body as a sign of protection, much as pagans still do and as Christians use the Latin Cross. In the Kabbalah it represents the upper five sephiroth of the Tree of Life, whose qualities are justice, mercy, wisdom, understanding, and splendor. Freemasonry calls it the Blazing Star.

Orientation

For most of its history the direction of the star did not matter, since like the circle it has no beginning and no end. The inverted pentagram later took on a darker reputation and appears in the symbol of Baphomet.

Venus

The pentagram is the sign of Venus. Over the course of four years and a day the planet traces a pentagram across the sky, the only planet to draw so clear a figure.
