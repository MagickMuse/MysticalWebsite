Overview

The name Paisley seems incongruous for a motif of Eastern origin. The original pattern, known as the buta in Persia and the Mankolam in India, traveled across continents on the shawls and fabrics brought back by Scottish soldiers returning from the colonies in the nineteenth century. Weavers in the small Renfrewshire town of Paisley, already known for their woven and printed cloth, copied the designs, and the name stuck.

Origins of the Teardrop

Several theories explain the familiar curving teardrop shape, but it is generally thought to derive from plant or fruit forms. The mango that inspired the mankolam is an emblem of fertility and good luck, and the motif may also descend from the leaf of the sacred Bodhi tree under which the Buddha gained enlightenment.
