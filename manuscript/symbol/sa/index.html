Symbol Grimoire

Sa

◇Notes/Warnings
Egyptian hieroglyph for 'protection' and 'amulet.' Shaped like an oversized knot, possibly representing a bundle of reeds folded into a loop and lashed together at the ends. May have originated as a simple life preserver for workers on the Nile. The sa reinforced the protective function of all amulets.
◇Type
Sacred Word & Glyph
◇Culture
Ancient Egyptian

Symbolic Meaning

The sa is the Egyptian hieroglyph for "protection" and was used as the general word for "amulet." Its form, a large looped knot, embodies the protective principle that underlies all Egyptian amuletic magick.

Form and Origin

The sa appears to represent a bundle of reeds folded into a loop and lashed together at the ends. Some scholars suggest it originated as a simple flotation device or life preserver for workers on the Nile, who were not typically proficient swimmers. If so, its evolution from physical life-saver to magickal protector is a natural one.

Function in Magick

As the hieroglyph for "protection," the sa reinforced the meaning and power of any amulet. It reminded wearers and viewers that the purpose of amuletic magick was to guard against danger, malice, and misfortune. The sa symbol might appear alongside other protective symbols or as a standalone amulet.

Connection to Knotting

The sa's knotted form connects it to the broader Egyptian practice of binding magick into physical objects through knots. The loop-and-bind structure echoes the tyet, ankh, and shen, all of which may derive from or relate to amuletic knots.

Correspondences

Protection, amulets, safety, warding, binding magick, preservation of life
