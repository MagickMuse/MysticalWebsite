Overview

The Ouroboros brings together two potent symbols, the Circle and the Serpent. Most often it is a snake curved into a circle or oval, swallowing the end of its own tail. Sometimes it is drawn as a dragon rather than a serpent, or as a pair of snakes each swallowing the other's tail; the dragon and the serpent are closely linked, and both often guard treasure.

Antiquity and Name

The Ouroboros is a primal symbol of great antiquity. It first appears in Ancient Egypt around 1600 BC and is likely older still. The Greeks named it, and the word means "self-devourer." It speaks of motion, continuity, and self-fertilization. The concept appears far beyond Greece: in Norse myth the great serpent Jormungandr is so vast that he can circle the planet and still grasp his tail in his teeth, and in Hindu myth the serpent goddess Nahusha creates the world. It also echoes the Orphic Egg encircled by its coiled snake.

Meaning

Most obviously the Ouroboros symbolizes the cyclical nature of time and the seasons and the eternal cycle of rebirth, fitting since the serpent's habit of shedding its skin makes it an ideal emblem of renewal. For the Gnostics it stood for eternity, while also reminding the viewer of the confines of the material world and the aspiration to a higher state. The serpent is a creature of the Earth, while the circle is a sign of completion and spiritual perfection, so the Ouroboros unites opposites, Heaven and Earth in harmony. A double ouroboros twists into the Infinity symbol, and in alchemy the figure speaks of purity, wholeness, and infinity.
