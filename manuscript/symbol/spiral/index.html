Symbolic Meaning

The spiral represents growth, evolution, and transformation. This dynamic shape embodies the journey inward and outward, cycles of expansion and contraction, and the continuous path of spiritual development.

Magickal Properties

  • Continuous growth - represents ongoing evolution and development
  • Life cycles - symbolizes birth, death, and rebirth in an eternal pattern
  • Energy movement - spirals channel and direct magickal energy
  • Journey - represents the inward path to self-knowledge and outward expansion

Associated Concepts

  • The journey of the soul
  • Seasons and natural cycles
  • DNA and the structure of life
  • Labyrinth and spiritual pilgrimage
  • Expansion and contraction of consciousness

Origins and Forms

The spiral shown in the Tibetan tradition describes the origins of the Universe. Turning clockwise, following the path of the Sun, it stands for the seed of potential energy, much like the coiled tension held within a spring. The emblem resembles a snail shell, and the form recurs throughout the natural world in both flora and fauna. It has inspired artists, mathematicians, and philosophers alike. The unfurling fronds of the New Zealand tree fern shaped the spiral motifs of Maori art, and the whirlwind shows the same contained energy as a force of nature.

Across Cultures

Spiral motifs appear worldwide, from the Neolithic cave complexes of Europe and Paleolithic carvings of the Goddess to the Celtic stonework at Newgrange. In the Hindu pantheon the spiral appears as the Kundalini Serpent, uncoiling from its tightly wound state and rising up from the base of the spine. An African sign for the Sun shows a cooking pot ringed by three red spirals. The Aztec feathered serpent god Quetzalcoatl shared the same emblem as the Tibetan seed of life, while the warrior god Huitzilopochtli bears a coat of arms of five spirals set within a circle.

The Double Spiral

The double spiral winds in one direction and then back in the other, a reminder of death and rebirth. Spiral movement is also enacted in dance, from the Turkish whirling dervish to the in-and-out folk dances that circle a central point, an idea echoed again in the spiral dances of Native Americans. In Hindu temples pilgrims walk a spiral path around shrines and ritual objects such as the lingam and yoni, a meditative circuit said to charge the energy of the place.

The Mayan Zero

For the Maya, the winter solstice marked the start of the year, the moment just before ordinary time began. The symbol that represented this concept was the snail, whose spiral shell is said to have inspired the Mayan sign for zero.
