Urim and Thummim

Notes/Warnings
The names are generally translated as "light and perfection" or "light and truth."
Type
Emblem & Device

Overview

The Urim and Thummim are objects so wrapped in secrecy that their exact nature is still debated. What is certain is that they were tied to the breastplate of the High Priest of the ancient Hebrews, kept in a hidden pocket behind the breastplate, or "essen."

Theories

Some hold that they were knucklebones or small stones carried out of Egypt by the Israelites. Others suggest they were sacred names inscribed on plates of gold to act as talismans. A further idea is that they were not objects at all, but the name for a method of divination through which the will of God could be sought, a form of augury. What is agreed is that the names translate as "light and perfection" or "light and truth."

Divine Communication

By tradition, when a slip bearing the Tetragrammaton, the name of God, was placed beneath the Urim and Thummim, the twelve jewels of the breastplate began to glow. This glow signaled that the breastplate was active and tuned in, working like a receiver for messages from God to His people. Eleazar is named as the last priest able to draw such direct messages, allowing Moses to commune with God. After his time the gift seems to have been lost, and the Urim and Thummim were used only as a more indirect tool of divination.
