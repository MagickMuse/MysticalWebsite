Numerology

Within numerology, three carries spirit, communication, and wisdom. In the Yantra system of Hindu numerology, three marks sprouting.

Symbolism of Three

Just as a man and a woman together hold the potential to create a third, one and two combine to make three, a neat turn of the same idea. When a third vertical line joins the other two, it forms the Roman numeral for three and opens the potential to make both a triangle and a circle, the circle appearing when the points of the triangle are joined by three arcs. Because three offers this potential circle, and with it a new dimension, it becomes the first true magickal number.

Groups of three appear everywhere. There is the Holy Trinity of Father, Son, and Holy Ghost, the Triple Jewel of Buddhism in which Buddhists take refuge, namely Buddha, Dharma as ultimate truth, and Sangha as virtue, and the trio of faith, hope, and charity. The Hindu pantheon holds Brahma, Vishnu, and Shiva as the Trinity that is God. Ancient Babylonia named Anu, Bael, and Ea as Heaven, Earth, and the Abyss or Hell. There are also the Three Wise Men who visited Christ, the three Fates, and the three parts of time as Past, Present, and Future.

A deep satisfaction surrounds things that come in threes, the sense of being third time lucky and of completing a cycle. Fairy stories lean on this pattern, as in Goldilocks and the Three Bears or the three wishes granted by a benevolent being such as the genie of Aladdin's lamp. Under Muslim law, a man can divorce his wife simply by saying I divorce thee, yet the phrase must be repeated the magickal three times for it to carry meaning.

The Pythagoreans held three to be the first true number. The Triad stands as the first equilibrium of unities, and for this reason the god Apollo gave his oracles from a tripod. Three was regarded as the number of wisdom, understanding, and knowledge.

In the Tantric system three is called Trayah, and here too several threefold ideas appear. There are three sources of Karma and three types of Karma, along with three vanities that can bind a person to the wheel of rebirth. The Ayurvedic system describes three qualities, or doshas, present in human beings in varying proportions: Vata as wind, Pitta as bile, and Kapha as phlegm.

In the Tarot, three is the number of the Empress, the embodiment of the female principle that symbolically links the Heavens with Earth and who carries within her the triple aspect of the Goddess as virgin, mother, and crone.
