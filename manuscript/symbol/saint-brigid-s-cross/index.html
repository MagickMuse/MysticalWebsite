Saint Brigid's Cross

◇Notes/Warnings
Woven from rushes on the eve of Saint Brigid's Feast. Rushes must be pulled (not cut). Weave left to right. Place above the door for household protection and blessings. Replace annually with a new cross.

A traditional Irish protective amulet crafted from rushes in honor of Saint Brigid. The cross has four arms radiating from a woven square center, creating a distinctive pinwheel pattern quite different from the standard Christian cross.

  • Gather rushes on the eve of Saint Brigid's Feast (January 31st)
  • Rushes must be pulled from the ground, not cut
  • Weave from left to right
  • The finished cross features four equal arms extending from a square center

Hang above the doorway to protect the household and invite blessings. The cross remains in place for one year, then is burned or buried and replaced with a freshly woven one on the next feast day.

The four-armed design may represent the sun, the four seasons, or the cardinal directions. Some interpret the weaving motion as symbolic of the eternal cycle of life. The cross bridges pagan solar imagery and Christian symbolism, much like Saint Brigid herself bridges the goddess and the saint.
