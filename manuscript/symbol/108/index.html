Symbolism

One hundred and eight is a sacred or perfect number in Hinduism, Buddhism, and the other Dharmic faiths, and so it appears in many places. Malas, or rosaries, always carry 108 beads to aid the repetition of a mantra, as do the juzu prayer bracelets of Zen priests. Hindu deities are each said to have 108 names. It is the number of sins in Tibetan Buddhism and of earthly temptations in Japanese Shinto, where temple bells are rung 108 times to welcome the New Year and bid farewell to the old, and to remind people to beware of these transgressions. The Angelus bells of the Catholic Church are likewise tolled 108 times.

Numerical Harmonies

The number is also the product of 12 x 9, representing the nine planets and the twelve months of the year, and of 4 x 27, symbolizing the four quarters of the Moon across the 27 "lunar mansions." Pranayama yoga, the yoga of breath control, reckons that a person takes 21,600 breaths in a 24-hour period, so the 12 daylight hours account for 10,800 of them.

Martial Arts & Beyond

The sacred weight of 108 reaches into the martial arts as well. One of the major Karate disciplines, the Suparinpei, translates as "one hundred and eight," and the South Indian and Chinese schools count 108 pressure points on the body. Taoist and Chinese astrology recognize 108 sacred stars, and Shiva's cosmic dance is made up of 108 poses.
