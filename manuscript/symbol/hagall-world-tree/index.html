Interpretation

Cosmic pattern of Yggdrasill, the snowflake, protection, and magickal working by and through the laws of the world.

Runic Context

The Hagall or World-Tree ideograph encodes the structural pattern of Yggdrasill itself, the axis around which the Nine Worlds are arranged. Its crystalline form recalls the snowflake, a natural expression of the same cosmic geometry. As a symbol of protection, it shields through alignment with universal law rather than brute force. In talismanic work, it provides a framework for magickal operations that function in harmony with the deep structure of the multiverse.

The Sacred Tree

It is not difficult to see why the tree is universally revered as a sacred, living being. Trees are not only beautiful but benevolent, giving food in the form of nuts, fruits, and syrups, and medicines (aspirin, for example, comes from the bark of the willow). Their timber builds houses, ships, and carriages, and shapes weapons and tools; it also furnished gallows, from whose branches the condemned were hanged. The tree combines all the elements within it: its roots lie in the Earth that nourishes it; its sap is the water element, the "dew of Heaven" as well as its lifeblood; its leaves and branches rise high into the air; and fire is produced when its sticks are rubbed together, fed by the wood itself. One of its most vital functions is the processing of carbon dioxide, for trees are the lungs of the planet, and the destruction of the rainforests is akin to a vital organ being removed from the body. By the Doctrine of Signatures, the ancient idea that a plant carries the qualities of the thing it resembles, this rings true: stripped of its leaves, the tree's branches look like a great upturned lung.

Tree Spirits and Worship

Our ancestors believed that each tree had its own powerful spirit, an idea that has never wholly gone away. Bringing an evergreen into the home at Christmas continues it, as did the Beltane festivals of pre-Christian Britain, when trees were decked with ribbons and danced around (a rite the Romans banned, later revived as the maypole). Before felling a living tree, its spirit had first to be appeased, a practice that has by no means died out. The Druids, whose name shares a root with the word for "oak," the tree they most revered, are famed tree worshippers, though tree worship has been practiced the world over. At Dodona the birds in the sacred trees carried messages from the gods, and the rustling of the leaves was read in a form of divination called phyllomancy.

World Trees of the Nations

Different peoples each hold their own most sacred tree, what might be called their World Tree. For the Scandinavians it is the great ash, Yggdrasil, that unifies the cosmos; the Siberians venerate the birch, and the Germans the lime, which they called the Linden. The Buddha sat beneath the Bodhi tree when he received enlightenment, and the Chinese sage Lao Tzu was said to be born beneath a plum tree.

The Cosmic Axis

The World Tree, a giant tree growing through the cosmos to link the Heavens, the Earth, and the Underworld, is a wholly universal archetype, a primal image with parallels in the Axis Mundi and the Omphalos. It represents not only the striving toward the Heavens but the endless cycle of regeneration, of life, death, and rebirth; the birds in its highest branches symbolize the souls of unborn beings. Its reach is far: it stands for the idea of a family, the Kabbalah itself is imagined as a tree, and in alchemy the World Tree appears as a naked woman crowned with sprouting, fruiting branches, wands in her hands, the Sun and Moon at her right and left.

The Inverted Tree

The inverted tree, with its roots in the air, is a sacred symbol that has largely been forgotten. It is set physically at the center of the Green Man Maze in Wales, and expresses the teaching of the Upanishads that the whole Universe is an upside-down tree, its roots in the Heavens and its branches embracing the Earth. The same image appears in Kabbalistic lore, in the ten spheres of the Sephiroth and their twenty-two connecting paths.
