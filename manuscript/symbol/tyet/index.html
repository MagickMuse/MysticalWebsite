Knot

Symbolic Meaning

The tyet, also called the Knot of Isis or Blood of Isis, is a knotted cloth symbol associated with the goddess Isis. It resembles an ankh with its arms folded downward, suggesting a garment tie or ceremonial knot.

Connection to Isis

The tyet embodies Isis's protective and life-giving powers. It was traditionally depicted in red, the color of blood, emphasizing its connection to:

  • Isis's grief and the blood she shed mourning Osiris
  • Menstrual blood and female creative power
  • The life force that Isis used to resurrect Osiris and protect Horus

Protective Function

Tyet amulets were placed on mummies, particularly at the throat, to invoke Isis's protection for the deceased. The spell accompanying the tyet amulet (Book of the Dead, Spell 156) states: "You have your blood, Isis; you have your power, Isis; you have your magick, Isis. The amulet is a protection for this Great One."

Pairing with the Djed

The tyet pairs with the djed pillar to represent the union of Isis and Osiris, female and male, the complementary forces that overcome death and generate new life. This pairing appears throughout Egyptian funerary art.

Knots and Funerary Stones

The folded form also recalls a knotted length of cloth, including the cloth tied during menstruation, which links the emblem to female power and to the binding force of the knot itself. Like many peoples, the Egyptians held that a knot could hold magick fast or set it loose. For burial the tyet was carved from a blood-red stone such as carnelian or red jasper and fastened at the throat of the deceased, lending protection to the spirit as it crossed into the life to come.

Correspondences

Isis, protection, blood, female power, resurrection, motherhood, magickal authority, funerary blessing
