Numerology

Within numerology, zero stands for emptiness that paradoxically contains everything. As the mysterious "no number," its discovery ranks among the most significant developments in human history — it was the last "number" to be recognized as such.

Spiritual & Cultural Meanings

Conceptually, zero is the symbol that stands for "no thing."

  • Buddhism & Taoism — It represents the Void that existed before Creation.
  • India — The concept was called Sunya, the Sanskrit word for void or vacuum.
  • Islam — Zero represents the essence of divinity.
  • Pythagoras — Held that zero indicated perfect form.
  • Kabbalah — Placed after a number, zero multiplies it tenfold, hinting at the infinite and limitless possibilities held within so simple a symbol.

History & Origins

  • A blank space standing for zero appears in India as early as the fourth century BC; the Egyptians similarly used a gap.
  • The Babylonians marked zero with a pair of slanted lines.
  • The Mayans were using zero at least a thousand years before anything similar appeared in Europe. In their calendars and codices it took the form of a humble snail-shell, whose spiral inspired the symbol — first known use around 36 BC.
  • The Romans used the word nulla ("nothing"), abbreviated to the letter N by around AD 720.
  • Zero in the oval form we know today belongs to the Indian numerological system and appears in a Jain text dated AD 458.
  • The Indian and Greek concepts of zero were amalgamated by the Persian mathematician Al-Khwarizmi, and our modern zero descends from this source.

The Debate Over Nothing

Whether zero can truly be a number at all has been fiercely debated. The question so vexed the philosophical Ancient Greeks that it sparked intense arguments about existence itself:

Must nothing also be something? How could nothing be something?

Tarot

Its circular shape — so like the Cosmic Egg, or a seed — makes zero a fitting emblem of potential and possibility. In the tarot it belongs to The Fool, the card of enthusiasm, innocence, and guilelessness.
