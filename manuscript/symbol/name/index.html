🌟Greatest Name📛Magickal Name📜Tetragrammaton

Symbolism

A rose by any other name, the old line goes, would smell as sweet, but would it truly still be a rose? A name carries the very essence of the power and spirit of the one who bears it. For this reason many faiths hold the true Name of God to be a profound mystery, veiled in oblique reference and rarely spoken aloud. In the Jewish tradition only the High Priest pronounces the Name of God, for it is believed that its power is such that the whole world would be struck dumb were it shouted aloud, and so it must be kept secret. In Islam there are ninety-nine names of God and one further, hidden name, the Greatest Name; God and the Name of God are held to be identical, and to know all these names is to be able to enter Heaven.

The Ancient Egyptians regarded the name as a living thing, an inseparable part of the person it named. Often, on joining a religion, a person receives a further name at initiation, a sign of rebirth and a new identity conferred by allegiance to the faith (see also the Magickal Name).

The power of a name lies behind the old storytelling habit of leaving a dreaded villain's name unspoken, named only as "you know who," and behind the fairy tale in which learning the name of the gnome Rumpelstiltskin grants power over him. An old superstition holds that a child without a name is somehow without a soul, and so left open to evil influences.

Among the Celtic peoples, a person's name and their trade or function were one and the same. The custom survives today, especially in parts of Wales, where someone may be known by both name and occupation, as in "Dai the Post."
