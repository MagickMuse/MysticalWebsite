United States Dollar Bill

◇Notes/Warnings

The reverse of the United States one-dollar bill is crowded with esoteric imagery, and its design has stayed largely constant even as the note has been revised over the years. The most prominent device is the Great Seal of the United States.

The Pyramid and the All-Seeing Eye

The seal shows a pyramid with its capstone removed and replaced by a triangle enclosing a single eye, the All-Seeing Eye. Its associations with Freemasonry and with the Illuminati have made it a favorite of conspiracy theory. Around the top runs the motto 'Annuit Coeptis,' loosely 'favor has been given to the things begun,' implying work still unfinished, while the banner below reads 'Novus Ordo Seclorum,' roughly 'a new order of the ages.' The use of Latin, a language few now read and officially counted as dead, works as a quiet sign in its own right, lending gravity to the statements and an air of antiquity to a young nation whose Declaration of Independence was signed in 1776.

The Eagle and the Hidden Birds

The Bald Eagle, the country's official bird, grips an olive branch of peace in one talon and a cluster of arrows in the other, ready for war; the thirteen leaves and fruits of the branch are matched by thirteen arrows. The dove, bird of peace, is sometimes named as America's covert emblem and a fitting counterweight to the eagle, though it does not actually appear on the note; aptly, the Latin name for the dove is Columba. An owl, creature of wisdom, is also said to be concealed somewhere in the design.

Thirteen Stars and Solomon's Seal

Above the eagle floats a crown of thirteen stars, the number of states that first formed the Union. Arranged as they are, the stars can be joined to form the Seal of Solomon, counted among the most potent of all symbols. That they combine into a second emblem neatly echoes the motto on the banner beneath, 'E Pluribus Unum,' 'out of many, one,' a reference to the many states united as a single nation.
