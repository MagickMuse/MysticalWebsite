Symbol Grimoire

Voudon Veves

◇Notes/Warnings
Also spelled vèvè or veve; the sacred emblems of Vodou (also rendered Voudon or Voodoo), an African diaspora religion of the Caribbean. Each loa has its own veve, traced on the ground in cornmeal by a mambo (priestess) to summon that spirit. These are sacred ritual symbols within an initiatory tradition and should be approached with respect.
◇Type
Seal & Sigil

Vodou took shape among Africans brought to the Caribbean toward the close of the seventeenth century. At its heart lies the belief that the spirit animating the living body endures after death, growing over time until it attains the standing of a divinity, or loa.

Syncretism with the Saints

Catholic missionaries worked to suppress the tradition, but it proved resilient, and its followers gradually blended their own spirits with the Catholic saints. The fit was close enough that a visiting priest might be reassured by a household shrine filled with the expected Catholic statuary, while the household saw in those same figures its own preferred spirits in another guise. Even pieces of the Catholic liturgy found their way into ceremonies.

Calling the Loa

Contact with the loa is central to the faith. The spirit is invited to take possession of a living person, and much of the ritual is arranged toward that end.

The Veve

Every loa has its own emblem, the veve. A mambo, or priestess, traces it on the ground in fine cornmeal as a welcoming summons to the spirit, and the figure must be rendered correctly even though many are highly intricate. Alongside the veves of the deities there are veves for particular petitions, a vast repertoire covering needs of every kind, from the collecting of debts to the increase of potency. The example honored here belongs to Erzulie Freda Dahomey, the loa of dreams and love.
