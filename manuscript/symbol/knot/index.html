Overview

Because a knot binds, it carries powerful symbolic and magickal meaning, and that binding reaches the spirit as well as material things. In some pagan wedding rites the hands of the bride and groom are bound together as a sign of the vows that join them body and soul. For Buddhists, untying a knot means an unbinding from material things and liberation, and so it can stand for death.

Knots at Sea

Knots matter greatly to fishermen, which is no surprise since nets are built from them, and those who trust their lives to the elements tend toward superstition. Some still carry a cord tied with three knots: untie the first for fair winds, the second for storms, and the third holds those storms in check.

Protection and Prohibition

Arabic tradition holds the knot a powerful talisman against the Evil Eye, so a knot may be tied into the beard, yet pilgrims to Mecca are forbidden to wear any knots in their clothing. The Quran preserves a charm for the making and undoing of knots, taking refuge with the Lord of Daybreak from those who blow upon knots, a reference to charging an object with magickal power through the breath.

Witches and the Fates

Because weaving and knitting are knot-filled crafts, and because women knot their hair, knot magick is closely tied to witches; the warning against those who breathe on knots was read as a charge against spellcraft. The Fates of Greek myth weave and knot the threads of existence, and midwives made sure a birthing mother carried no knots that might hinder the birth.

The Knot of Isis

The Egyptians held the knot a symbol of eternal life and tied their sandals so the knot left its imprint on the ground, a reminder of the Knot of Isis, also known as the Tyet.
