Overview

Also called the Parasparopgraho Jivanam, this sacred symbol of the ancient Jain faith, an offshoot of Hinduism, is built from several smaller signs.

The Lok

The outline of the symbol is the Lok, which represents the Universe. Its lower part recalls the concept of the Seven Hells, the central part stands for the Earthly plane, and the upper part for the Heavens.

Siddhasila and the Siddhas

At the top a curved arc represents the Moon and is called the Siddhasila, the final resting place of souls freed from the karmic wheel of death and rebirth. Such souls are the Siddhas. The dot, or bindu, within the arc stands for the zero, the everything and the no thing combined, and for the Siddha itself.

The Three Jewels

Below the arc sit three more bindu, the Three Jewels of Jainism, the rules for freeing the soul:

  • Right Faith (Samyak Darshan)
  • Right Knowledge (Samyak Jnan)
  • Right Conduct (Samyak Charitra)

The Swastika and the Ahimsa Hand

Below the three sacred dots sits the swastika, a very ancient solar symbol. Here its four arms stand for the four realms into which a soul may be reborn: as a heavenly being, a human being, an animal, or a hellish being.

Beneath the swastika is an upraised hand, the universal sign for stop. Within the hand is the word Ahimsa, a core tenet of both Hinduism and Jainism meaning nonviolence, and the word itself sits inside a wheel. Together the hand, the word, and the wheel are a reminder to pause and think before acting, and to do nothing that could harm any living creature. Otherwise the wheel of birth and rebirth keeps turning and the soul is never liberated.
