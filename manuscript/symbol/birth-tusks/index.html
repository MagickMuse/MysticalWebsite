Magickal Wands of Hippopotamus Ivory

Birth tusks are curved wands made from hippopotamus ivory, incised with protective spirits and demi-gods. Sometimes they bear the names of mother and child. These time-honored magickal tools protected women during childbirth and their children afterward.

Physical Evidence

Birth tusks have usually been found in burials, including the Ramesseum magician's tomb, but they were clearly used in life:

  • Many have been repaired after breaks
  • Show signs of wear and re-working along their sides
  • Pointed ends are well-rubbed, suggesting they marked protective circles during magick rites

They are often associated in tomb scenes or burials with women identified as caring for children. These tusks were valued parts of a magician's or midwife's equipment.

Incised Imagery

The images on birth tusks were not decorative but magickal. They include:

Animals from the magickal menagerie:

  • Lions and baboons
  • Lion-hippo hybrids
  • Frogs and turtles
  • A fantastic winged wildcat with a falcon's head

Human-animal hybrid figures:

  • Aha: A Bes-like dwarf figure ('the fighter')
  • A female figure with breasts grasping snakes in either hand (resembles figures on the Abydos birth brick)

Dating and Significance

The similarity of images across many tusks, and how they echo other magickal objects of similar date, suggests a coherent cosmic realm of protective forces that magicians could invoke during the centuries around 2000-1500 BCE.

Broader Application

Although much of this symbolism connects to childbirth, protection against harm was something everyone needed, starting with the smallest and newest members of the community. The tusks' protective powers extended beyond 'female concerns' to universal human vulnerability.
