The Lovers

Overview

Pythagoras observed that the Greek letter Y, or upsilon, resembles a forked path, and to those in the know this simple shape became laden with hidden meaning. The two arms of the Y stand for two roads: earthly wisdom and vice to the left, and divine wisdom and virtue to the right.

The Choice

The traveler must choose a path where the two branches meet, a point that represents the arrival of adulthood. The image captures a concept central to classical philosophy, the free will to choose one's own course. A verse long attached to the symbol describes the right-hand path as a hard, steep ascent that rewards the weary with rest at its summit, while the broad left-hand road looks easier yet leads the deluded to a fall among the rocks.

The Forked Cross

In the Middle Ages this form, also called the Forked Cross or Furka, became a sign for a thief, since it also recalls the forked tongue of the serpent.
