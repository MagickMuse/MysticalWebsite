Overview

The emblem of the Theosophical Society, founded by Helena Blavatsky in 1875, is an almost overwhelming gathering of some of the most significant sacred symbols. Blavatsky held a deep interest in and an extensive knowledge of sacred signs, so each element was chosen with care for its meaning to the organization, which keeps headquarters in countries all over the world.

A Symbolic Puzzle

Many of the components are themselves built from other symbols, such as the Seal of Solomon formed from two interlocking triangles. To examine the emblem closely is to unravel an extensive puzzle, with each piece explained more fully under its own entry.

Component Elements

The design gathers the Seal of Solomon with the quintessence marked at its center, the swastika, significant letters of the Hebrew alphabet, floral devices, the Circle, the Ouroboros, the crown, and various carefully placed bindhu or dots. The emblem is also said to hold within it every number from one to ten, which together contain all possible numbers. A rectangle forms the base of the crown, though the square does not appear directly as a symbol, except perhaps in the many expressions of the number four.
