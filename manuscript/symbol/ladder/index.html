Overview

An everyday object, the ladder nonetheless carries esoteric meaning as an image of the relationship between Heaven and Earth. At its simplest it stands for ascension, transcendence, and the fulfilment of potential; the Greek word for ladder is "climax." It is also a symbol of communication between the worlds. The idea that the material and spiritual worlds were once joined by a ladder that was later removed or broken runs through many cultures, though Shinto tradition holds that the goddess Amaterasu, who borrowed the magical ladder, kept the connection.

Initiation and the Seven Rungs

The ladder shown on the tracing boards of Freemasonry signifies initiation and the ascent into the higher levels of the cosmos, and a Rainbow can likewise serve as a ladder between the celestial realms and those below. The number of rungs matters: the Buddha has a ladder of seven colors, and in the Mithraic religion there was a ceremonial ladder of seven rungs, each made of a different metal tied to one of the seven known planets, from lead, the base metal, to gold, the most sublime. To climb it was to ascend through the Seven Heavens.

The Axis Mundi

The ladder is one of the many vertical forms that signify the Axis Mundi.
