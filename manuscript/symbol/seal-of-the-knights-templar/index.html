Seal of the Knights Templar

◇Notes/Warnings
The traditional seal of the Knights Templar, the order founded in 1118 to protect pilgrims to Jerusalem. Engraved in stonework such as at Rosslyn Chapel, its presence marks the Templars.

The Knights Templar were founded in 1118 with the charge of protecting pilgrims on their way to Jerusalem, and were granted leave to quarter themselves on the Temple Mount. Several seals belong to the order; the traditional one is described here. It can be found cut into stonework, especially in churches and chapels such as Rosslyn Chapel in Scotland, and wherever it appears it marks the presence of the Templars.

The Two Sides

The seal bears images on both faces. The front shows two knights riding a single galloping horse. This carries several meanings at once: unity in action and common purpose, since the two ride together in one direction; a reminder of the vow of poverty, since the two share a single mount; and the Spirit of Christ, recalling the promise in Matthew's gospel that wherever two or more believers gather, Christ is among them. The reverse shows the dome of the Church of the Holy Sepulcher in Jerusalem.
