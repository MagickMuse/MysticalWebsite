Saltire

◇Notes/Warnings
Also called the St Andrew's Cross. A diagonal cross; the white saltire on a blue field is the national flag of Scotland.
◇Type
Cross
◇Culture

The Saltire, also called the St Andrew's Cross, is a diagonal cross. Legend holds that when Andrew, one of Christ's disciples, was to be crucified, he judged himself unworthy to die in the same manner as his messiah, so a diagonal cross was used instead.

How a man born near Galilee more than two thousand years ago became the patron saint of Scotland, his diagonal cross adopted as the nation's flag, was seemingly an accident. Legend tells of a Greek monk, possibly St Rule, who was warned that Andrew's remains were to be moved and was charged with carrying them to the ends of the Earth for safekeeping. The monk was shipwrecked at a place later renamed St Andrews, where a chapel was raised to hold the relics and, later, a cathedral.

The Scottish flag, a white saltire on a blue field, is among the oldest national flags in the world and dates to the ninth century.
