Symbolic Meaning

The star represents guidance, hope, and divine illumination. This celestial shape embodies spiritual light, aspiration, and the connection between earthly and heavenly realms.

Magickal Properties

  • Divine guidance - lights the path through darkness and uncertainty
  • Hope and inspiration - represents wishes, dreams, and aspirations
  • Spiritual illumination - symbolizes enlightenment and higher consciousness
  • Protection - the pentagram shields and wards against negative energies

Associated Concepts

  • Navigation and direction
  • Celestial wisdom and divine connection
  • Wishes and aspirations
  • The five elements (pentagram)
  • Inner light and spiritual awakening

The Star as Spirit

Our ancestors believed that each star carried its own spirit, whether that of a deity, an unborn soul, or the soul of a person who had lived a particularly notable life. This idea sits easily with the way we still call the famous and admired stars. The light of a star shows only against the darkness that surrounds it.

Stars as Omens

An unusually bright or strange star was read as the herald of an important or auspicious event. The Three Wise Men knew the ancient prophecy recorded in the Book of Numbers that a star should come out of Jacob. Yet the birth of Christ was not alone in being announced this way, for the Buddha and the Hindu fire god Agni were said to share the privilege.

The Pole Star

The Pole Star offers a reliable aid to navigation. Fixed and unmoving at the center of the great wheel of the Heavens, it stands as a symbol of the world axis and, for many, a reminder of the constancy of God. The Yakut of Siberia held that the stars were windows of the Universe, opened and closed in turn to ventilate the spheres of Heaven.

The Number of Points

The number of points shapes a star's meaning. An eight-pointed star can stand for eternity and regeneration, in keeping with the number 8, while a nine-pointed star may recall the nine Muses or the Nine Worlds of Norse myth. The pentagram, the hexagram, and the seven-pointed Elven star each carry their own meanings under their own entries.

Garden Magick

This slightly more complicated shape is great with or without the circle around it. This can be a Witch's Garden, meaning anything grown inside is useful for magickal ritual or spells.

You can divide a circle into a Pentacle, but keep in mind that plants need room to grow - if you go that route, plan on making it bigger than you already have in mind.

Practical Applications:

  • The points of the star can be used for the five points of a pentacle: earth, air, fire, water, and spirit
  • The whole star can be assorted protection plants that strike your fancy
  • The shape of the star will imbue the plants grown inside with the blessings of the elements
  • If you go the star-within-a-circle route, the blank spots can be universal plants good for a wide variety of magickal uses like Rosemary and mints
🌿
About Mint: Mint is invasive. Some gardeners caution never to plant mint directly into the ground because it spreads. If you're worried, cut the bottom off a five-gallon bucket and plant the bucket down two-thirds of the way into the ground. This keeps the runners to a minimum and ensures the mint isn't extending its boundaries.

On the other hand, plant mint wherever you can, as it makes a great tea!
