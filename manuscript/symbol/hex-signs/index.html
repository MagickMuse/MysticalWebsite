Hex Signs

Overview

In south-eastern Pennsylvania lives a community of European settlers, drawn largely from the Rhine region and from a range of religious groups including Lutherans, Moravians, Quakers, and Mennonites. For all their devout faith, many share a thriving belief in witchcraft, known as Hexerei, from the German Hexen, meaning witch.

Barn Signs

The community paints or carves a whole series of protective symbols onto the sides of their barns and houses. Called Hex Signs or Barn Signs, they serve many purposes: averting evil, drawing fertility and prosperity, promoting health, and even influencing the weather. Many are individually designed and become tied to a particular family, much like a coat of arms, and are sometimes tooled into the leather covers of family bibles.

Common Motifs

The designs are richly decorative and draw on widely familiar symbols:

  • Hearts for love
  • Stars for good luck
  • Oak leaves and acorns for strength and growth
  • The distelfink, a finch that lines its nest with thistledown, for good fortune

The double distelfink is said to bring double the luck.
