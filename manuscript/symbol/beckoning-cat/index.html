Maneki Neko

A friendly little statuette with a warm welcome found all over Japan and China. The cute little Maneki Neko, or beckoning cat, is ubiquitous in both homes and offices, and can be seen in restaurants around the world. For many people it is the ultimate symbol of prosperity and good luck.

Colors

The Maneki Neko comes in different colors, each of which signifies a different meaning. For example, a red cat will protect from illness, and a black one will ward off evil.

Paw Positions

The position of the paws carries a message:

  • Right paw raised — brings money and happiness to home and workplace
  • Left paw raised — attracts new customers for a business
  • Both paws raised — the jackpot: both home and business will be happy and profitable, attracting good luck, friends, prosperity, and new clients

Origin Story

The beckoning cat is also the symbol of a small Buddhist temple in Tokyo, where the original incident is said to have happened. The temple was a lowly place whose impoverished priest would regularly share what little food he had with his pet cat. One day some Samurai were passing and noticed the cat with one paw raised as though to say hello. The warriors stopped, intrigued, and went into the temple just as a horrendous rainstorm started. They believed that paying attention to the cat's invitation had prevented them from being struck by lightning. Thereafter, the fortunes of the priest, the temple, and of course the cat, started to change for the better.
