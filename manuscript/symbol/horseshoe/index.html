Overview

The horseshoe gained its meaning not from its use but from its shape and the metal it is made of.

Shape

It echoes the arc, one of the earliest sacred symbols, representing the vault of the heavens. Turned upside down it resembles the Omega, the last letter of the Greek alphabet. Flipped the other way, it looks like the crescent Moon, and so invokes the protection of the Moon Goddess. It also recalls the yoni, deepening its link with the Goddess.

Iron and Protection

The iron of the horseshoe strengthens its protective power, since iron is a guarding metal that evil entities avoid.

Good Luck

The horseshoe is a well-known emblem of good luck, appearing on greetings cards and wedding keepsakes, and nailed above doorways for the same reason. There is some disagreement over how to hang it. One view says it should rest on its curved end to hold the luck in, which would otherwise pour away if reversed. Older pre-Christian belief says it should sit so that it resembles the sky, and also the yoni.

Vulva Symbolism and Protection

As a crescent, the horseshoe is one of the traditional symbolic stand ins for the female genitals, the most protective of all images. Display it in the home or wear it as jewelry to borrow that protective power. See Vulva.
