Overview

The I Ching is an ancient Chinese system of philosophical divination, possibly dating back to the eighth century BC, and still in use today.

The Book of Changes

Also called the Book of Changes, it was the only book to escape destruction when the Chinese philosophical works were burned in the third century BC. Its tools are dried yarrow stalks or special coins, usually pierced with a square hole, together with knowledge and a good measure of intuition.

The Two Lines

The system rests on just two lines, one unbroken and one broken. The most profound symbols are usually the simplest, and these two lines hold the universal source of all things, known as the tau, as in Taoism. The unbroken line stands for the positive: male, Sun, fire, heat, action, odd numbers, yang. The broken line stands for its opposites.

Trigrams and Hexagrams

Combined, the lines form 64 possible hexagrams. They also build a set of eight trigrams representing air, water, fire, and earth, with the four sub-elements of breath, sea, thunderbolt, and mountain. The readings are beautifully oblique, and the philosophy behind the system is about finding balance. Trigrams from the I Ching appear on the flag of South Korea.

The Pa Kua

The eight trigrams are also arranged into the Pa Kua (or Ba Gua), an octagonal figure. These trigrams are sometimes carved onto wood to frame the bagua mirror, a protective tool of Feng Shui that turns away bad influences from every direction.
