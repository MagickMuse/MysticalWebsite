Symbolism

Broken into its parts, 21 gives 2 + 1 = 3, itself a magical number, the first to broaden our perspective with a new dimension. Add that 21 is 3 x 7 (or 7 x 3), with seven among the most sacred of numbers, and the significance of 21 begins to come into focus.

It is no accident that the age of majority was traditionally set at 21 in many countries, the birthday on which a person was figuratively handed the "key to the door," a symbol of adult responsibility and an initiatory unlocking. In the Old Testament, 21 is the number of perfection and of the attributes of wisdom. Solomon, famed for his own sagacity, wrote of wisdom:

I preferred her before scepters and thrones, and esteemed riches nothing in comparison of her ... neither compared I unto her any precious stone, because all gold in respect of her is as a little sand, and silver shall be counted as clay before her. (The Wisdom of Solomon, 7:9)

Tarot & Kabbalah

In the tarot, 21 is the World, the final card of the major arcana. It shows an androgynous, female figure standing within a vesica piscis of laurel leaves, surrounded by an ox, an angel, a lion, and an eagle, which represent earth, air, fire, and water as well as the cardinal points. The whole forms a victorious, triumphant image. In the Kabbalah, 21 represents the path of conciliation and the blessings of God.
