Serpent Cross

Notes/Warnings
A Tau cross, or Christian cross, with a serpent draped about it. It signifies the triumph of the cross over the serpent and the rise of matter into spirit, and in alchemy it marks the elixir of mercury.

The Serpent Cross, a Tau cross or Christian cross with a serpent draped around it, is an ancient sign carrying several meanings. The Christian faith adopted it to signify the triumph of the cross over the serpent, the victory of good over evil, pointing back to the serpent coiled around the tree in the Garden of Eden. It also marks the turning of the material into the spiritual, as the serpent leaves its usual place on the ground and climbs upward toward enlightenment.

The Book of Numbers tells how Moses, leading the Children of Israel through the desert, met with such complaint and faithlessness that a plague of snakes was sent in punishment. Following divine instruction, Moses fashioned a brazen serpent and set it upon a pole as a charm against snakebite. This serpent was called Nehushtan and was later worshipped as an idol.

The Serpent Cross also serves as an alchemical sign for the elixir of mercury, a potent healing substance once its toxin, figured by the snake, had been killed.
