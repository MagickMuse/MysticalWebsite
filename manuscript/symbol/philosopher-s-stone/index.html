Philosopher's Stone

Notes/Warnings
Also called Lapis Philosophorum. Closely tied in lore to the Emerald Tablet and likened to the Holy Grail. It has no single fixed emblem and is treated as a metaphysical attainment as much as a physical substance.

Emblem & Device
Culture

Other Symbols1
Smaragdina Tablet

Few symbols hold as much esoteric weight or arcane mystery as the Philosopher's Stone. It has no fixed emblem of its own, existing instead within a web of veiled clues and deliberate linguistic ambiguity drawn from alchemy.

The Great Work

Making the Stone is said to require seven steps, with oblique instructions for them preserved in the Emerald Tablet. Like the Holy Grail, the Stone exists on a metaphysical plane as much as any physical one, so those instructions must be read on several levels at once.

Powers

To the alchemists the Stone was a substance that could transmute lead into gold, and indeed turn any substance into another, wielding a power much like a magick wand. It was also believed to restore youth and grant eternal life.

Revelation

Tradition holds that the means of producing the Stone are not won through hard labor but disclosed by angels to a chosen few. Such angelic intervention often stands for a flash of inspiration or intuition that reaches beyond ordinary logic.

Seekers

A handful of figures across history claimed to have found it. Paracelsus proposed in the sixteenth century that the Stone was an undiscovered element from which all other elements were formed. Isaac Newton studied alchemical processes closely and pursued its mysteries in depth.
