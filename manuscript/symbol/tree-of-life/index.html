Tree of Life

Overview

The Tree of Life is the most famous graphic of the Kabbalah, a deceptively simple form that holds all of nature within its many layers of meaning. Its ten spheres, the sephiroth, stand for the ten numbers, and they are joined by twenty-two paths that stand for the twenty-two letters of the Hebrew alphabet. Together the numbers and letters make up the thirty-two paths of wisdom described in the Sefer Yetzirah, the very early kabbalistic text said to have been written by Abraham. The Tree is also said to be found within the Flower of Life.

The Three Pillars

The three columns of the Tree are symbols in their own right. The central pillar represents balance and equilibrium. The right-hand pillar is Jachin, male energy, and the left-hand pillar is Boaz, female energy. These are the Columns, Jachin and Boaz that stood in the great Temple in Jerusalem, that shaped the design of Masonic temples, and that appear again in the Tarot.

The Ten Sephiroth

Each sphere, or sephira, adds a new dimension to the unfolding of creation:

  1. Keter, the Crown. The uppermost sphere and the start of manifestation, positive but undefinable, since it bears no relation to anything else yet.
  2. Hokhmah, Wisdom. To the right and just below Keter; it allows a line to be drawn between itself and the One.
  3. Binah, Understanding. To the left of Hokhmah; it forms a triangle so the three numbers give one another definition. These first three sephiroth belong to Atziluth and the element of fire.
  4. Hesed, Mercy. Below Hokhmah; now the Universe can hold four elements and four directions and form a square.
  5. Gevurah, Strength. To the left of Hesed; it introduces the idea of time, without which nothing can happen.
  6. Tiferet, Beauty. It adds the notions of past and future and the consciousness of the self, alongside above and below.
  7. Netzah, Victory. Below Hesed; the emotional nature and the pure joy of existence, the soul glad to be made manifest in human form despite the chance of suffering.
  8. Hod, Splendor. The intellectual nature, or thought.
  9. Yesod, the Foundation. A sense of being, joining the emotional and intellectual natures to give a sense of reality.
  10. Malkhut, the Kingdom. At the base of the Tree, where the zero becomes manifest and self-consciousness is fully developed in the physical world; it is the sphere of all life on Earth.

The Triple Veils of the Negative

Before the first sphere lies the threefold nature of zero. In the Kabbalah, nothingness, or zero, is called Ain, the void. It holds two further ideas: Ain Soph, Infinite Space without limit, pictured as the dot or bindhu that contains the seed of everything; and Ain Soph Aur, the Limitless Light, a kind of time-space continuum. Together these abstract ideas form the Triple Veils of the Negative.

The Twenty-Two Letters

Like other magickal alphabets, the Hebrew letters are seen as the symbols with which God created the physical world through the sounds they carry. They fall into three groups: three "mother" letters for the elements of air, water, and fire; seven "double" letters for the seven traditional planets; and twelve "simple" letters for the signs of the Zodiac. The letters also stand for numbers, giving each a double meaning, and they appear on the Tree as the twenty-two paths that link the spheres.

The Five Parts of the Soul

Kabbalists tie the five parts of the human soul to the four elements plus ether, the quintessence:

  • Nephesch, the animal soul, of earth, the realm of instinct and the senses.
  • Ruach, of air, the intelligence and the breath, with the same sense as Prana in Hindu thought.
  • Neshamah, of water, the intuition.
  • Chiah, of fire, the essence of life, akin to Chi.
  • Yechidah, the Highest Self, encompassing the whole and symbolizing spirit.

The Mesopotamian Tree of Life

Some hold the Mesopotamian tree to be the "original" Tree of Life, the near-universal symbol that appears across cultures. It was pictured as a colossal tree growing at the center of the world, from whose roots flowed the Apsu, the primordial waters from which all life emanated.
