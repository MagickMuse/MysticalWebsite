Overview

The earliest recorded use of the word "labyrinth" comes from descriptions of the maze that housed the Minotaur in Greek myth. The name means "house of the double axe," a reference to the Labrys that was a powerful emblem of Minoan culture. Although labyrinth and maze are often used as if they were the same, a maze has many branching paths and dead ends, while a true labyrinth has a single winding path that leads inevitably to the center and offers no choice. Its space is used ingeniously so that the path runs as long as possible. A maze still carries the symbolism of a metaphorical journey, and can even be grown as a living puzzle, such as a hedge maze shaped in the likeness of the Green Man.

The Journey of the Soul

The deepest meaning of the labyrinth is the journey of the soul to its center and back out again, the cycle of death and rebirth, a metaphysical pilgrimage of the spirit. The center represents the womb, and to reach the center of the "labyrinth of life" is to attain initiation and enlightenment. In this the labyrinth has parallels with the mandala and with the Spiral patterns of the Celts, such as those carved on the great stones at the entrance to Newgrange in Ireland.

An Ancient and Universal Pattern

Labyrinth patterns are universal and older than the myth itself, appearing on fragments of amulets from ancient Egypt, on Mycenean seals, and on Etruscan vases. They were later adopted by Christian churches, the earliest at the Reparatus Basilica in Algeria, dating to the year 324.

Sacred Numbers

The number of layers in the path and its length are held to be significant. The famous labyrinth at Chartres Cathedral has eleven concentric circles and is exactly 666 feet long. Though 666 is often misread as evil, it is in truth a sacred number, the number of humankind. At the center of the Chartres labyrinth is a six-petaled flower that conceals the hexagram, or Seal of Solomon, within it, a reminder that labyrinths are sometimes called Solomon's Maze.

Sun and Moon

Because the winding trail moves both clockwise (deosil) and anticlockwise (widdershins), the labyrinth symbolizes the course of the Sun and the waxing and waning of the Moon.
