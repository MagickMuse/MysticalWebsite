Valknutr

◇Notes/Warnings
Runic ideograph of Viking origin, found on rune stones and in carvings. Also known as the Knot of the Fallen, the Knot of the Chosen, the Knot of Death, or the Knot of the Slain.

Interpretation

The Nine Worlds embodied in the three realms in eternal unity, expressing the evolutionary law of arising, being/becoming, and passing away to new beginning.

Runic Context

The Valknutr (knot of the fallen, or the chosen) is one of the most profound ideographs in the runic tradition. Its three interlocking triangles represent the nine corners of the Nine Worlds, bound together in an unbreakable pattern of cyclical transformation. The symbol encodes the fundamental evolutionary law: all things arise, exist in a state of becoming, pass away, and begin anew. It is strongly associated with Odin and the mysteries of death and rebirth, appearing frequently on funerary stones and in contexts connected to the chosen slain.

Protective Promise

Like other knot symbols, the Valknutr was most likely a protective device. To the warrior who fell in Odin's name it carried a promise: that Odin would guard the fallen spirit, and that the warrior would soon be reborn. Its three interlocking triangles invite comparison with the triquetra and the triskele.
