Birth Bricks

Notes/Warnings
Four sun-baked clay bricks used during Egyptian childbirth, associated with the four cardinal directions. Women squatted or leaned on them while giving birth. Collectively personified as the goddess Meskhenet ('bricks'). Also placed in four walls of New Kingdom royal burial chambers, linking birth to rebirth. The Abydos birth brick (excavated 2001) shows blue-haired divine women with infant, Hathor emblems, and protective figures resembling those on birth tusks.

Sacred Bricks of Childbirth

Birth bricks are sun-baked clay bricks that supported women during labor and delivery. Conventionally there were four, associated with the four cardinal directions.

Divine Personification

The bricks were collectively given divine form in the goddess Meskhenet, whose name literally means 'bricks.' In the tales told to entertain King Khufu, Meskhenet is one of four goddesses who arrive to help Redjedet deliver triplets.

Meskhenet sometimes appears in the form of a brick next to the balance scales in Book of the Dead judgment scenes, emphasizing the connection between birth and rebirth.

Use in Childbirth

A seated or squatting position was the usual position for delivery. The bricks may have been used:

  • For women to squat or lean on during delivery
  • To define a zone of protection around the laboring woman
  • Lined up to form a protective platform for the newborn baby

Funerary Connection

Birth bricks were placed in the four walls of royal burial chambers during the New Kingdom—another example of the conceptual link between the birth of a child and the rebirth of the dead.

The Abydos Birth Brick

A rare decorated example was excavated at Abydos in 2001:

  • Made of mud, formed in a mold and sun-dried
  • Short sides painted with protective animals and demi-gods
  • Largest surface depicts an elegant woman seated on a throne with an infant, attended by two other women
  • All three women have blue hair (lapis lazuli color = divine status)
  • Framed by Hathor cow-face emblems
  • Some protective figures have blue hair or bodies, including a lion-faced female holding snakes resembling figures from the Ramesseum magician's grave and birth tusks
