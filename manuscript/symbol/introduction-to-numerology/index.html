Introduction to Numerology

What Numerology Is

Numerology assigns meaning to the symbolic vibration of the digits 1 through 9, suggesting traits, strengths, and weaknesses for every day of the year. It rests on the idea that the universe is mathematically constructed and that the vibrational energy of people, places, and things can be expressed through numbers. Reducing birth dates and names to numbers allows a person's personality and destiny to be discerned.

Origins

Numerology likely originated in ancient Babylonia and among the early Hebrews, and many different systems developed across different parts of the world. The most familiar lineage traces back to the fifth-century BC Greek mathematician and philosopher Pythagoras. Pythagoras taught that mathematical connections linked the gods, mortals, and numbers, and that recurring number patterns could be codified to predict fate. He held numbers as the source of energy in the world, with 1 through 9 representing the nine stages of life, and said that the world is built upon the power of numbers.

How the System Works

Every number in numerology reduces to a value between 1 and 9, and each digit also corresponds to a letter of the alphabet. Any number larger than 9 is reduced by adding its digits together. For example, 123 becomes 1 + 2 + 3 = 6, so the qualities of 123 align with the symbolic number 6. Using single digits as the guide, the patterns of different dates and a person's name can be analyzed to describe character and forecast events.

The Nine Core Numbers

Each number carries a primary current and a shadow. The shadow names the challenge associated with that number. When the challenge is faced and integrated, it becomes a source of strength.

  • 1: Independent, creative, ambitious, extrovert. Shadow: selfish with tunnel vision.
  • 2: Sensitive, domestic, imaginative, musical. Shadow: timid and gullible.
  • 3: Scientific, powerful, knowledgeable, multi-talented. Shadow: superficial and hedonistic.
  • 4: Practical, stable, honest, trustworthy. Shadow: stubborn and overly serious.
  • 5: Energetic, sensual, daring, flirtatious. Shadow: difficulty committing.
  • 6: Perfectionist, creative, artistic, compassionate. Shadow: supersensitive and overemotional.
  • 7: Intellectual, philosophical, imaginative, intuitive. Shadow: impractical and secretive.
  • 8: Practical, just, trustworthy, powerful. Shadow: opinionated, impatient, intolerant.
  • 9: Spiritual, humanitarian, visionary, healer. Shadow: self-serving, possessive, volatile.

Numerology Alongside Astrology

Where astrology works through stars and Sun signs, numerology works through numbers. Both are symbolic systems and tools for understanding the self and life purpose. No sign is better than another in astrology, and no number is better than another in numerology. Every number carries both potential and shadow, and the shadow is simply the challenge the number invites you to face.

Your Birth Date Number

The date of birth carries a permanent influence on the life. Names may change over time, but the birth date number stays constant. To find it, add the digits of the month and day and reduce to a single digit. Someone born on April 17 has a birth date number of 1 + 7 = 8.

Numbers as Universal Symbols

On the surface, numbers are practical tools for counting. Beneath that plain function lies a deep tradition of philosophy and symbolism that recurs across nearly every culture and belief system in strikingly similar forms. The idea that numbers express not only quantity but also the inherent qualities, laws, and powers of the universe is extremely old. It runs through the mystery traditions, including those of the Pythagoreans, Babylonians, and Hindus, and through the Kabbalah. A number's form, its conceptual nature, and its symbolic meanings were all held to carry sacred significance, a hidden code that could reveal how the universe is constructed and where humanity stands within it.

Numbers demand precision and full understanding to be wielded accurately, and what they accomplish as mathematical instruments is inseparable from their symbolic meaning. Whether the universe is truly governed by numbers, and whether they were discovered rather than invented, the cosmos would carry on regardless. The Sun would still rise and set, the seasons would still turn, and the Moon would still wax and wane without our measurements. What numbers give us is a way to make sense of the cosmos and to locate ourselves within a far larger whole. Time illustrates this neatly. Sixty seconds make a minute, sixty minutes make an hour, and twenty-four hours make a day. The Sumerians devised this sexagesimal system roughly four thousand years ago, and it remains in use today as a neatly integrated framework, however much of a construct it may have been at the start.

Who Invented Numbers?

Unlike letters, which many myths describe as gifts from the gods, numbers are generally credited to human invention alone, despite their mystical importance. There is debate over whether numbers first arose in India or China, but it is clear that they came before letters. At some point a person recognized that a group of animals could be matched to the fingers of the hands, and counting began from there. It likely started in fertile, agrarian regions, where surplus produce had to be tallied so it could be traded or guarded against theft, since the worth of livestock or grain depends on knowing how much there is.

Children learn numbers the same way, counting fingers and then toes before moving on to tokens that extend the range. The Greeks and Romans did likewise, using small pebbles called calculi, from a word that originally meant limestone. This tally method gave rise to a system still in use. Roman numerals grew out of an earlier Etruscan practice of drawing lines to represent fingers, thought to descend from Italian shepherds notching wood to count sheep. One line stood for 1, two for 2, three for 3, and four for 4, followed by V for 5, which could be traced on the hand by running a line down the thumb to the wrist and back up to the little finger. Two V shapes joined end to end formed the X for 10.

Because this is a Base 10 system, every tenth V or X received an extra stroke. C, from centum meaning hundred, marked 100 and survives in the modern word cent. At this stage there was no symbol for zero, though the Romans named the concept nulla, meaning nothing. The absence of a zero symbol seriously hampered Roman numeral notation, which is why the Hindu and Arabic system, believed to date back as early as 400 BC, eventually became the most widely used way of writing numbers in the world.

The Esoteric Standing of Numbers

Numbers have long been described as a universal language given to humanity as a confirmation of truth. Numerology is the belief in a mystical, esoteric relationship between numbers and living things, physical objects, ideas, and concepts. It ranks among the oldest of the symbolic sciences, and Plato regarded it as the highest level of knowledge. The Greeks, Romans, Indians, Chinese, and Kabbalists all treated numerology as essential to understanding how the universe is constructed. Among the Fulani of Africa, numbers were held to carry such power that quantities tied closely to a person, such as their age in years or the size of their herds, were never spoken aloud, since naming them was thought to drain a little of that power.

Numerology is easy for modern skeptics to dismiss alongside fortune telling, yet it should not be set aside so quickly. Many Hindu families will not name a child until the numerology of the chosen name sits in harmony with the numerology of the birth date. Pythagoras declared that numbers rule all things, echoing an ancient Chinese conviction that numerology holds a key to the harmony between spirit and matter, humanity and the divine, microcosm and macrocosm. The I Ching points toward the same correspondence. Pythagoras went further, proposing that mathematics, music, architecture, and color all resonate together in a balance that numbers can help define. In this view, numerology does not strip numbers of their practical, mathematical role, it raises them to the standing of arbiters of a deeper truth.

Many of the ancient meanings attached to numbers reach back as far as the Chaldeans. The early Christian Church did not accept them, regarding the art with the same suspicion it directed at astrology and other forms of magick, and judging it ungodly. Over time the Church worked to undermine the concepts of numerology, and much of its mystery was lost, though a portion of the secret language of numbers has since been pieced back together.

A Key to the First Eleven Numbers

Although several systems of numerology exist, there is broad agreement on the meanings of certain numbers. The brief key below covers the first eleven numbers, including zero, the mysterious no number whose discovery ranks among the most important in human history.

  • 0: Nothingness, and yet also everything.
  • 1: The oneness of all, and the individual.
  • 2: Duality, balance, and separation.
  • 3: Spirit, communication, and wisdom.
  • 4: Earth, solidity, understanding, and order.
  • 5: Harmony, justice, protection, and love.
  • 6: Marriage, the union of male and female, and the balance of opposites.
  • 7: Spirituality, the occult, and intelligence.
  • 8: Eternity, infinity, and justice.
  • 9: The limited and the limitless, experience and virtue, completion.
  • 10: Death and rebirth, perfection.
