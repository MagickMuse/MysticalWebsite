Black Stone

Notes/Warnings
Also called al Hajar ul Aswad. A sacred relic set in the corner of the Ka'aba at Mecca and revered as one of the holiest objects in Islam. ⚠️ This is a living sacred object of an active religious tradition and should be treated with cultural respect.

Gabriel

The Black Stone, known as al Hajar ul Aswad, is among the holiest objects in Islam, and its presence is a chief reason that Mecca stands as one of the foremost places of pilgrimage in the world.

Origins

The provenance of the stone is unknown, though it is widely believed to be a meteorite. Another tradition holds that it was given to Ishmael, the son of Abraham, by the angel Gabriel. In one account the angel who had failed to stop the serpent from tempting Adam and Eve was imprisoned within the stone, said to be a Zircon turned black by the kisses of sinners. On the Day of Judgment this angel would be released to bear witness for all who had completed the pilgrimage to Mecca. Beyond the myths, the one certainty about the stone is its great age.

In Pilgrimage

Pilgrims circling the Ka'aba seven times will try to kiss the stone if they are able, and when that is not possible they point toward it on each circuit. The stone is regularly anointed with perfumed oil.

Appearance

The stone is of modest size, roughly 30 centimeters across. Legend tells that it was once pure white and darkened as it took in the sins of humankind. It is now cracked, damage attributed either to its theft around AD 930 or to a siege in AD 638. A silver frame of oval shape holds it together, and the whole object resembles a large unblinking eye.
