Overview

The word mandala is Sanskrit for "circle." A mandala may be enclosed in a square or may itself hold squares, triangles, and other shapes, along with animals, flowers, and plants, and despite its name it need not always be circular. An important symbol in the Hindu tradition, it is now admired everywhere for its beautiful colors and pleasing patterns, even where its religious meaning is not understood.

A Map of the Universe

A mandala can be as simple or as elaborate as its maker wishes. Monks made the first mandalas at least two and a half thousand years ago as part of their spiritual training. It is a mystical map of the Universe, designed so that the attention is drawn continuously inward to the center and then back out to the frame.

Meditation and the Self

As a focus for meditation its shapes and patterns are open to each person's interpretation, yet every element, including the colors, carries meaning. The unfolding petals of the lotus, in whose heart the yogi imagines sitting, form a mandala, and a perfect physical example is the Temple of Borobudur in Java, whose whole structure, seen from above, is a circular mandala set in a square frame with gates at each quarter; to walk it in the right sequence is itself a meditation. Carl Jung valued making mandalas in his psychological work and called them "a representation of the unconscious self."
