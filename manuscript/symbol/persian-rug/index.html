Persian Rug

◇Notes/Warnings
Woven motifs include the paisley and the tree of life. Each weaving tribe carries its own distinctive patterns, so a rug's design can reveal its origin.

A Persian rug carries an elaborate hidden language within its colors and patterns, so that a household can walk daily across an encoded wealth of meaning.

Patterns

No two Persian rugs are ever identical. Even where a design has been repeated for centuries, the weaver works in a deliberate flaw so the pattern is never perfect, an acknowledgment that perfection belongs to Allah alone. The rugs fall into two broad families, one built from curving floral motifs and one from more geometric design. Inspirations include the architecture of mosques, flowers, trees and other vegetation, animals, spirals, and the paisley motif. Some patterns were drawn in a single unbroken line, the designer's hand never leaving the page. Pattern also records provenance, since each tribe weaves its own distinct designs.

Hidden Meanings

Certain woven motifs carry specific meanings. The parrot signifies love, the pomegranate abundance, the tree of life eternity, the carnation happiness, and the camel wealth.
