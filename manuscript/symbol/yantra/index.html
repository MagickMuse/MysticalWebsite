Overview

In Hinduism and Buddhism, the yantra is a linear geometric figure that embodies a spoken chant, or mantra, within a single symbol. In essence it is an image of the cosmos. The practice of drawing and contemplating yantras reaches back more than two thousand years and is the Hindu counterpart to the Buddhist mandala.

Elements

The yantra serves as a focus for concentration and meditation, and its parts are rich symbols in their own right. It usually holds a triangle, upright for male energy or inverted for female energy, and sometimes the interlocking triangles of the Shatkona. It includes a circle and a lotus flower, all enclosed within a square whose "gateway" points stand for the solidity of the Earth. Sanskrit letters may be added, giving further definition and meaning to the shape.

The Sri Yantra

The most important is the Sri Yantra, the "Mother of all yantras," formed from nine interlacing triangles around a central space implied as the dot, or bindhu. To contemplate the Sri Yantra is to make a symbolic pilgrimage, each step of its construction drawing the seeker toward the center and the spiritual goal of unity. When sand is spread on a taut surface and the "aum" sound is chanted, the vibrating sand is said to settle into the shape of the yantra.

Hindu Numerology

The word yantra also names the Hindu system of numerology, which combines a person's date of birth with the letters of their name to reveal personality, destiny, favorable omens and conditions, and any traits to stay aware of. The meanings of the numbers one through nine in this system follow the turning of the seasons and the progress of humankind across the Earth.

Zero appears as the bindhu, the dot, like the one that marks the third eye at the center of the forehead. To find a person's life path number, the digits of their birth date are added together and then reduced to a single digit. A birth date of August 7, 1963 gives 8 + 7 + 1 + 9 + 6 + 3 = 34, which reduces to 3 + 4 = 7.
