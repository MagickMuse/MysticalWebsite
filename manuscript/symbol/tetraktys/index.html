Overview

The Tetragrammaton, the four-letter code for the secret name of God, is sometimes drawn as a Tetraktys, a word that means "fourfold" in Greek. This Pythagorean figure looks simple but holds a complex meaning. Made of ten dots arranged into a pyramid, it was said by Pythagoras to symbolize the whole Universe, embracing the principles of the natural world, the harmony of the cosmos, the ascent to the divine, and the mysteries of the divine realm.

Reading Upward

Starting at the base and rising through the rows:

  • The four foundation dots represent the four elements, the seasons, and the cardinal directions of North, South, East, and West.
  • The next three stand for Earth, Heaven, and Hell, and also mind, body, and spirit.
  • The next two are male and female, light and dark, yin and yang.
  • The single dot at the apex is the Godhead.

Reading Downward

Working down the pyramid, Pythagoras linked the first dot to the intellect, the second row of two to science, the third row to opinion, and the base row of four to sense. Altogether its ten dots symbolize the entire Universe that exists in the now, along with the Universe that is yet uncreated. See the Y of Pythagoras for a related Pythagorean emblem.
