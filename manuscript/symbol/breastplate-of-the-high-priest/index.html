Breastplate of the High Priest

◇Notes/Warnings
Also called the Essen. A sacred vestment worn by the High Priest of ancient Jerusalem, set with twelve gemstones in gold mountings that stand for the twelve tribes of Israel.
◇Type
Emblem & Device
◇Culture
Hebrew/Jewish

The breastplate of the High Priest is an artifact of the ancient world that became the original source for much of the importance later attached to certain gemstones.

History

The first High Priest of Jerusalem was Aaron, who lived around 1200 BC. He was the first to wear the breastplate of righteousness and prophecy, also called the Essen. The breastplate was tied over the robe-like garment of the High Priest and was connected with the mysterious objects Urim and Thummim, held to be the powers of judgment and prophecy.

The Twelve Stones

Set into the breastplate in gold mountings were twelve stones, arranged in a sequence described in the Book of Exodus. Centuries of changing names have left the identity of some stones uncertain. In four rows of three, working downward, they are given as sardius, identified by some as Carnelian, Topaz, and carbuncle, identified by some as Garnet; Emerald, Sapphire, and Diamond; ligure, identified by some as jacinth, Agate, and Amethyst; Beryl, Onyx, and Jasper. Each gem was inscribed with the name of one of the twelve tribes of Israel and also carried a link to the twelve months of the year and the twelve signs of the Zodiac.
