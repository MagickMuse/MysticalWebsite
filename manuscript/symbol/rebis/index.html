Rebis

Notes/Warnings
Also called the Twofold Matter. An alchemical emblem of the hermaphrodite and the union of opposites, related in meaning to the yin yang and the yab yum.

The Rebis, also called the Twofold Matter, is one of several symbols expressing the idea of the hermaphrodite, a perfection of being formed from a perfect balance of opposites. The yin yang of Chinese tradition and the yab yum of India carry the same idea.

The rendering most often seen comes from alchemy and, like many alchemical drawings, is packed with nested symbols.

The Image

The oval frame suggests the egg, emblem of the cosmos and of new potential. Within it stands a single body bearing two heads, one male and one female. The figure rests on a winged, fire breathing dragon, which in turn crouches upon the orb of the planet. The dragon stands for manifestation, and the fire from its mouth for spirit. The planet is winged as well, marking the marriage of Earth with the Heavens. Inside the planet a cross spreads four arms for the four directions and the four elements, while a central dot marks the fifth element, the quintessence. A square and a triangle also sit within the circle.

Number and Stars

Five stars surround the figures. Five is the union of the feminine two with the masculine three, the joining of opposites and the number of marriage. Each star carries six points, and six here stands for the male element of fire shown by the upward triangle joined to the female element of water shown by the downward triangle. The star also speaks the principle of as above, so below. The Sun sits above the man's head and the crescent Moon above the woman's.
