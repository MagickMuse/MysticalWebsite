📅Numerological Day 6

Numerical Correlations

Letters: F, O, X

Element: Earth

Influences: Sociable and Emotions, Cats, Unity of Triple Goddess and Triple God, Allure, Decisions

Divination Meaning

Harmony, domesticity, peaceful, happiness, kindly, reliable, friendly, loyal, conscientious, idealistic, affectionate, wholesome, conventional, eventual success, artistic, teacher, thorough, fussy, smug, gossipy, conceited, obstinate.

Numerology

Within numerology, six carries marriage, the union of male and female, and the balance of opposites. In the Yantra system of Hindu numerology, six marks budding.

Symbolism

Because six splits into two groups of three, it is seen as a number that can go either way, as likely to be evil as good. This precarious balance recurs again and again in its symbolism. The figure itself is ambivalent: flip it over and it becomes a 9. Six is the number of the marriage of opposites, an image and its reflection making up the whole.

Cultural Traditions

In China, 6 is an extremely auspicious number and the number of heaven. The I Ching is built on two sets of three diagrams that combine into a hexagram, and its 64 hexagrams form a kind of predictive dictionary of the human condition, describing various elemental combinations.

In Tantra, 6 is called Shat. Several important sixes recur: six noble virtues, six revealed views of the Universe, six lower chakras, and six higher ones. In the tarot, 6 is the Lovers card, which shows a young man at a crossroads trying to choose between two women of equal merit, while Cupid hovers in the background, bow and arrow ready to make the decision for him. The image captures the dilemma that belongs to the number perfectly.

The Hex & Sacred Union

The witch's curse, or hex, takes its name from six because the number represented copulation, the union of the Triple Goddess with her mate; the word was also the original root of "hag." The Egyptian hieroglyph for six showed male and female genitalia, underlining the number's sexual connotations. For the Egyptians, 3 was the number of the Goddess and 6 meant her union with God. The fairy-tale command "Open, Sesame" is in fact a Sufi love charm, "sesame" being a corruption of seshemu, the Egyptian word for intercourse, and the symbolic "cave" to be opened is the yoni, or female genitalia. Early Christian authorities branded 6 the number of sin because of this association with physical love.
