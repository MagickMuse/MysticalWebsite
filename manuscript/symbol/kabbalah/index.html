Overview

Most systems of faith have an exoteric, outer level meant for everyone and an esoteric, inner level kept for the priests and initiates. The Kabbalah is the deeply mystical, esoteric aspect of Jewish doctrine. Its name comes from the Hebrew root QBL, meaning to receive, or mouth to ear, or the unwritten law, and like most mystery traditions its secrets were first passed on by word of mouth. It shares a root with the word Cabal, a secret intrigue.

A Short History

The secrets, so the story goes, were given by God to the Archangels, who passed them to Adam after his expulsion from Eden so that he might regain God's favor. They travelled through Noah to Abraham, who shared the mysteries with the Egyptians, and from there the Kabbalah spread across the world. Moses received kabbalistic instruction directly from God, spending forty days learning the secret doctrine from the angels in the desert, then concealed those teachings within the first books of the Old Testament.

In the first century, Rabbi Simeon Ben Jochai hid in a cave with his son for twelve years to escape execution for criticizing Rome. There he taught his son the secrets of the Kabbalah, and those teachings later appeared as the Zohar, published in thirteenth-century Spain. The Zohar remains the cornerstone of kabbalistic doctrine.

A Universal Influence

Because its ideas are so universal, the Kabbalah has been taken up by many traditions. The Middle Ages saw a surge of interest in esoterica and the rise of a Hermetic Kabbalah that blended kabbalistic teaching with Greek Hermeticism. Alchemy, Rosicrucianism, and Freemasonry were all shaped by its secrets, the Tarot draws on it, and the Golden Dawn built its symbolic language upon it.

The Doctrines

The teachings encompass the Four Worlds and the Tree of Life, and the Tree in turn holds the Ten Numbers and the twenty-two Letters.

The Greatest Name, Jehovah or IHVH, attaches an element to each of its letters, and those letters also stand for the Four Worlds:

  • Atziluth (emanation), the world of pure spirit and the archetypal realm of the Gods, where there is no division. It belongs to fire and the letter I, and from it the other worlds are born.
  • Briah (creation), the next world, of the letter H and water, where separation begins though it is still formless.
  • Yetzirah, of the letter V and air, the realm of imagination and thought that corresponds to the astral world just before the physical.
  • Assiah (to do), of the final letter H and earth, the material here and now, built from the finer worlds that precede it.
