Zoso

Notes/Warnings
Commonly called the Zoso, although the emblem was never meant to carry a name at all; the label comes from the way the first of the four sigils appears to spell the word. The mark is also bound up with the planet Saturn and the sign Capricorn.

Zoso stands as a fine modern example of how magickal emblems are pieced together from older fragments, a single sequence of marks that hints at the way many emblematic amalgams come to be. It first reached a wide audience on the cover of Led Zeppelin's fourth album, the record most people simply call Led Zep 4, when the band chose to set down four personal sigils in place of any printed words or title.

Jimmy Page's Sigil

The first of the four belongs to guitarist Jimmy Page, long known for his fascination with the occult and with the work of Aleister Crowley in particular. Page has never spoken openly about the parts that make up his sign, but it is widely thought to come from an old alchemical grimoire of the sixteenth century. The sigil draws a line between the planet Saturn and Page's own birth sign of Capricorn: the opening "Z" is a familiar shorthand for the planet, while the trailing "oso" is read as the element of mercury, itself linked to Saturn.

The Four Symbols

Each member of the band chose a mark of their own. Bassist and keyboardist John Paul Jones took the triquetra. Drummer John Bonham picked the Tripod of Life, also known as the Borromean rings. Singer Robert Plant settled on a feather set within a circle. The feather recalls the feather of justice that Ma'at, goddess of truth, weighed against the souls of the dead, and it most likely speaks of transcendence as well, while the circle around it stands for completion and wholeness.
