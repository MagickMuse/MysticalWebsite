Symbolism of Sixty

The Babylonians used the same symbol for sixty as they did for one, and for this reason sixty remains a crucial number both in symbolism and in practical applications today. There are sixty seconds in a minute and sixty minutes in an hour, and six times sixty gives the number of degrees in a circle, three hundred and sixty.

In Judaism, sixty represents transformational power, and there is a rule in the Torah called the Law of Nullification, which sets down a ratio of one to sixty. This means that if a fragment of non-Kosher food finds its way into the cooking pot, its effect is rendered obsolete provided the rest of the contents are sixty times greater than the errant ingredient.

Sixty is represented by the letter Samech, which is itself symbolized by a circle, and conceptually it carries the idea of an all-inclusive state and the transcendental light of God. It is said that fire comprises only one sixtieth of Hell, that a sixtieth part of Manna is honey, and that a dream is a one-sixtieth part of prophecy. In Ancient Egypt, sixty represented longevity.
