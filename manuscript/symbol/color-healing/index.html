Color Healing

Color healing, also known as chromotherapy, operates on the principle that specific colors can stimulate energy in different parts of the body, enabling healing to occur. This ancient practice has been used for thousands of years across cultures and is experiencing a modern revival as science confirms many traditional beliefs.

Historical Foundation

Ancient Egypt

Archaeologists have discovered special rooms in ancient Egyptian temples designed to break up sunlight into rainbows. Patients would sit in rooms corresponding to the specific color they needed, demonstrating that color healing principles have been known for millennia.

India & Ayurveda

Color healing has been practiced in India for thousands of years through work on the aura and chakras. The Kurma Purana, a sacred Hindu text, refers to the creator as "Grandfather," visualized as millions of colored rays reaching every part of the universe, providing light and life.

Chinese Medicine

Traditional Chinese medicine has used color to promote healing for thousands of years, integrating chromotherapy into their holistic approach to health.

Ancient Greece

Greek physicians studied color healing extensively. The principles formulated by Pythagoras and Hippocrates remained unchallenged in the West until the Middle Ages.

Medieval & Renaissance Period

Avicenna (980–c.1037), an Arab physician, wrote Canon of Medicine describing his color therapy system. He prescribed:

  • Red flower potions for blood disorders
  • Yellow flower potions for bile duct problems

Paracelsus (1493–1541), the Swiss-German physician also known as Theophrastus Bombastus von Hohenheim, used light and color extensively in his medical practice during the Renaissance.

Modern Revival

By the nineteenth century, Western medicine had shifted to treating only the physical body, ignoring mental and spiritual aspects. Color healing declined until three pivotal books appeared in America during the 1870s:

The Foundational Texts

1876: *Blue and Sun-lights* by Augustus Pleasanton

  • Claimed to heal animals and people through blue light exposure
  • Popular with the public but ignored by medical establishment

1877: *Blue and Red Lights* by Dr. Seth Pancoast

  • Written by an eminent physician
  • Endorsed color healing principles, lending medical credibility

1878: *The Principles of Light and Color* by Edwin S. Babbitt

  • Monumental work that became hugely successful
  • Considered Babbitt a miracle worker
  • Established different schools of color therapy still active today

Edwin Babbitt's Contributions

Babbitt is arguably the father of modern color healing. He identified red, blue, and yellow as the main healing colors, each with a complementary color that could restore balance. For example, green (complementary to red) would calm hostile or aggressive patients, as it counters red's negative traits.

20th Century Developments

Colonel Dinshah P. Ghadiali, a Hindu scientist, published Spectro-Chrome Metry Encyclopedia in 1933. He discovered that every organ could be either stimulated or inhibited by a specific color, enabling targeted healing when you know the affected organ and its corresponding color.

Contemporary Practice

Today, organizations like the International Association of Colour ensure practitioners receive proper training. Research continues, and color healing is regaining recognition as a valuable means of healing body, mind, and spirit.

Scientific Validation: Dr. Robert Gerard, a Los Angeles psychologist, confirmed that blood pressure, respiration rate, eye blinks, and brain wave patterns increase with red exposure but decrease with blue exposure.

Biochemistry of Color: Research in the 1980s demonstrated that colored light triggers biochemical reactions in the body. Later work confirmed that blues and greens carry a soothing effect, lowering stress, brain-wave activity, and blood pressure, while warm colors such as orange and red stimulate. Many psychologists now use color to produce beneficial effects in homes, workplaces, and hospitals.

How Color Healing Works

Through the Aura

Most color healing works through the aura, particularly the chakras. Although chakras are aspects of consciousness, they profoundly influence physical functioning.

Healthy Aura: When someone is healthy in mind, body, and spirit, their aura glows with vitality and energy.

Diseased Aura: Dis-ease appears in the aura long before manifesting physically. Most illness (aside from accidents) stems from years of negative thinking, visible in the aura before affecting the body. Mental and emotional states constantly affect the aura for better or worse.

Healer's Aura: Interestingly, color healers' auras change during sessions. Spiritual healer Ethel de Loach's aura shifted instantly when beginning healing, with blue streams around her hands changing to vivid orange.

Diagnosis Methods

Aura Reading

Color therapists who can see auras examine a patient's aura to determine imbalances and needed colors.

Pendulum Dowsing

A pendulum is frequently used to determine which colors would be most beneficial for the patient.

Methods of Administering Color

Colored Light Therapy

Healers shine lights of needed colors on affected areas. These lights contain special quartz filters to produce colors of varying wavelengths and frequencies.

Oils & Flower Essences

Different colored oils and flower essences are used. Dr. Edward Bach arranged his floral remedies into seven groups, each associated with a different color.

Solarized Water

Water is poured into colored glasses and exposed to light for four hours. This treated water lasts up to two weeks refrigerated. Patients receive color benefits by drinking the water.

Crystal Therapy

Crystals of different colors are placed on patients' bodies to facilitate healing.

Color Clothing

Patients may be advised to wear clothing of certain colors to aid healing.

Color Breathing

Visualize yourself breathing in needed colors:

  • Red, Orange, Yellow: Visualize flowing into your body from the earth
  • Green: Visualize flowing horizontally into the solar plexus
  • Blue, Indigo, Violet: Visualize flowing into your body from the top of your head

Best Timing: Before or after breakfast, or before/immediately after evening meal.

Colored Foods

Stimulate deficient colors in the aura through diet:

  • Red: Meat, beets, peppers, grapes, red-skinned fruits
  • Orange: Oranges, carrots, pumpkins, sweet corn, apricots
  • Yellow: Butter, egg yolks, grapefruit, melons, yellow fruits/vegetables
  • Green: Green vegetables and fruits
  • Blue: Blueberries, plums
  • Indigo: Grapes, plums, blueberries, blackberries
  • Violet: Beets, eggplants, grapes, blackberries, purple broccoli/asparagus

Visualization Healing

Healers send specific colors to patients through visualization. Swami Panchadasi described this process nearly a century ago in The Human Aura:

"A nervous, unstrung patient may be treated by bathing him mentally in a flood of violet or lavender auric color; while a tired, used up, fatigued person may be invigorated by flooding him with bright reds, followed by bright, rich yellows, finishing the treatment with a steady flow of warm orange color."

Sessions conclude by visualizing the patient surrounded by great white light: "This will leave the patient in an inspired, exalted, illuminated state of mind and soul, which will be of great benefit to him, and will also have the effect of reinvigorating the healer by cosmic energy."

Self-Application:

  • Exhausted? Breathe in red
  • Stressed? Breathe in green

Crystals and Gemstones for Chakra Healing

Crystals and gemstones that relate to the colors of the specific chakras are frequently used for healing purposes. They should be placed on the area of the specific chakra for about twenty minutes a day. Gemstones for the crown chakra are usually placed on the floor or bed directly above the head of the patient.

If the specific gemstones are not available, rock crystal and rutilated quartz can be used as substitutes for any chakra.

Crystals and gemstones can also be used to provide a protective shield when performing healing work. Placing several green gemstones on the floor to surround the area you will be working in creates a protective boundary. Any crystals or gemstones can be used for this purpose, though green is particularly suited as it relates to healing.

Safety and Professional Guidance

Color therapy should be used only as an adjunct to whatever your medical doctor advises. Your doctor should always be your first health professional, as they have undergone years of study and training.

Be careful when selecting a color therapist. Ethical color therapists are cautious in their claims. Be wary of anyone who claims they can use color therapy to heal someone at a distance, or who claims instant healings. Absent, or distant, healing can be done by spiritual healers, but this does not usually include sending color to the person. Instant healings can occur, but usually color therapy takes time. Color therapy will not cure any illness caused by a deficiency in the system.
