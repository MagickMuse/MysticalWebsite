Latin Cross

◇Notes/Warnings
Also called the Christian Cross. When the body of Christ appears on it, it becomes a crucifix.
◇Type
Cross
◇Culture
Gnostic/Early Christian

The Latin Cross, or Christian Cross, is the familiar cross shape that stands as the major symbol of Christianity in all its forms. When the body of Christ is shown upon it, it becomes a Crucifix. So great is its impact that the faith's major places of worship are themselves built in the shape of a cross when seen from above. It is a symbol of the victory of life over death, and the benediction traced in the air at the sign of the cross both names the Father, Son, and Holy Ghost and echoes a far older use of the cross as a sign of protection.

The cross was not always the emblem of the Church, and Christ may not even have been crucified on this shape; accounts speak of a tau cross, a simple straight beam, or even a living tree. Because it was a gruesome instrument of torture, it made an unlikely emblem for a new religion, so the Chi Rho, or Labarum, was used instead. By the third century, however, the cross had been accepted.

The form holds a quiet secret. Drawn to the right proportions, four square parts in the upright to three in the crossbar, sharing the square where they meet for six squares in all, the shape folds into a perfect cube, and with diagonal folds it yields other geometric forms. The Freemasons have explored several of these.
