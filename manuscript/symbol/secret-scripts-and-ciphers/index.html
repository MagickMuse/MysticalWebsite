Secret Scripts and Ciphers

Overview

Almost as soon as most of us could share information by reading and writing, we began inventing different alphabets so that some things could still be kept secret. The urge to conceal seems to come naturally; even children invent little changes to their speech or writing meant to hide what they really mean.

Many of these secret alphabets and ciphers were devised by closed societies, such as the Freemasons, or by practitioners of magic who wished to keep knowledge within their own circle of trust. A good number are mere substitution ciphers: they swap the ordinary letters of an alphabet for different symbols while keeping the underlying rules of grammar intact. Others, such as the Enochian script, are true languages in their own right.

Secret scripts, codes, and ciphers are not the preserve of sorcerers and magicians alone. Codes are essential in wartime to keep information from the enemy. During the Second World War, for instance, the Navajo language was used as a code in the Pacific campaign; its complexity and rarity, spoken only by the Navajo and perhaps thirty other peoples at the time and never written down, meant that enemy forces never broke it. Beatrix Potter, likewise, kept her wry observations on the society and politics of her day to herself by inventing a code in which she wrote her journals from the age of fifteen until thirty; it was so intricate that it was deciphered only some twenty years after her death.

It is worth remembering that even our "ordinary" alphabets are full of arcane secrets that reach far beyond their role as simple visual symbols for sounds.
