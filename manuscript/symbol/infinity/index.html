Overview

The infinity sign, the figure of eight, and the lemniscate all name the same flowing shape, one of the simplest symbols to hold the richest meaning. It even appears on the everyday camera as the infinity focus mark.

Form and Feel

Twist a circular, flexible loop such as an elastic band a single time and you have the lemniscate. That one twist lends a flat circle a whole new dimension. As a mathematical device the sign was set down by John Wallis in 1655, but its meaning as a sacred symbol is far older, with roots in the Arabic numerals that came in turn from India.

Balance of Opposites

Drawn in one continuous seesaw of clockwise and counterclockwise loops, the sign mirrors the balance of opposites: male and female, day and night, dark and light. Sitting side by side, the two loops imply equality between these forces, meeting at the central convergent point. It speaks of sexual union, of two becoming one, and of wholeness and completion. The same looping curves appear in Arabic calligraphy of the Name of God, pointing toward eternity.

In the Tarot

The lemniscate floats above the Magician's head in the Rider Waite Smith deck, while other decks hide it in the brim of the hat, a fitting touch for so mysterious a figure.
