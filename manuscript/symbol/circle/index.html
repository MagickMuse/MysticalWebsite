Origin and Nature

The next logical magickal symbol after the dot, the circle is effectively an expansion of it. The circle represents the spirit and the cosmos. It is constructed from "some thing" (the unbroken line) and "no thing" (the space inside and outside this line). The circle therefore unifies spirit and matter. The structure itself has great strength, as demonstrated by the cylindrical shape of a lighthouse, built to withstand the fiercest assault of a stormy sea.

The physical and spiritual strength of this symbol exist because the perfect circle has no beginning and no end. It is unassailable. This power is the reason why the circle is used in magickal practices such as spellcasting. The magick circle creates a fortress of psychic protection, a physical and spiritual safe haven where unwanted or uninvited entities cannot enter.

Hermes Trismegistus said of the circle:

God is a circle whose center is everywhere and circumference is nowhere.

Sun, Moon, and Time

Where would ancient peoples have seen the most important circles? In the Sun and the Moon. As the Sun, the circle is masculine; as the Moon, it is feminine. Because the passage of time is marked by the journey of the Sun, Moon, and stars in orbit around the Earth, the circle is a symbol of the passage of time. In this form, it commonly appears as the wheel.

Equality

Because the circle has no divisions and no sides, it is also a symbol of equality. King Arthur's Round Table was the perfect piece of furniture for a fellowship of knights who were each as important as each other. Similarly, the Dalai Lama has a "circular" Council.

Symbolic Meaning

The circle represents unity, wholeness, and infinity. This eternal shape embodies cycles, divine perfection, and the interconnectedness of all things.

Magickal Properties

  • Infinite continuity - no beginning or end, representing eternity
  • Protection - creates sacred boundaries and shields from harm
  • Wholeness - symbolizes completion and totality
  • Cycles - represents the wheel of the year, life cycles, and natural rhythms

Associated Concepts

  • Divine perfection and the sacred
  • The cycles of nature and time
  • Protection and sacred space
  • Unity and interconnectedness
  • The infinite and eternal

Garden Magick

This popular (and easy to replicate) shape has a world of uses, pun absolutely intended. Think of it as The World card in the tarot. In your protection magick, you are casting this circle on the ground to mark the achievement of your family, home, and self-protection. You are empowering the wheel to keep spinning in your favor.

Practical Applications:

  • Divide into sections that get rotated each year to keep the energy moving
  • Illustrate the cycle of life, death, and rebirth
  • Coven protection: Include the health of your coven as a whole, the group mind as well as individual members
  • Deity devotion: Fill the space with plants, flowers, or vegetables associated with the deity you are honoring
  • Solar garden: Plant solar herbs for male mysteries or male-aligned deities
  • Sunflower sanctuary: Plant a circular bed of giant Sunflower with a small doorway left unplanted, makes a nice summer shady spot for children, pets, wildlife, or yourself
💡 Sun-chokes (related to sunflowers) have edible roots resembling galangal, potatoes, and other root vegetables, perfect for an edible solar garden.
