Solomon's Knot

Notes/Warnings
An ancient interlacing sign resembling two links of a chain set at right angles. Belonging to no single people, it was adopted by the Comacine stonemasons as their hallmark and, like many knot signs, offers protection. It also recalls the swastika as an old Sun symbol.

Solomon's Knot looks like two links of a chain set at right angles to one another. The sign is especially ancient and belongs to no single people, though the Italian stonemasons known as the Comacines, held to be forerunners of the Freemasons, took it as their hallmark and filled it with mystical meaning.

The designs of King Solomon's Temple remain a great influence on Freemasonry, which may be how the Comacines came to name the sign; it also appears in the abstract patterns within synagogues, perhaps another source of the name. Like many knot symbols it offers protection, and in form it recalls the swastika, an ancient Sun symbol.
