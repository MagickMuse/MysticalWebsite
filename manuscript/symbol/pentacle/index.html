"Pentacle" and "pentagram" are now frequently used synonymously but technically a five-pointed star is a pentagram: a pentacle is a small flat disc on which a pentagram has been engraved or inscribed. So the pentagram is the geometric shape and a pentacle is a round amulet or magickal tool that displays a pentagram. Many people use the words interchangeably, however, and do not distinguish between the two.

Ancient Origins

Pentacles are ancient; earliest surviving images date back to over four thousand years before the Common Era. The pentagram within a circle appears on rings worn by members of the Pythagorean brotherhood. Pentagrams, both with and without surrounding circles, appear in Kabalistic writings, and they are among the sacred, magickal images associated with Onmyoji, the wizards of Japan.

Historical Significance

Over the centuries, pentagrams, five-pointed stars, have been used to represent witches and to protect others against them. The pentagram has been used to symbolize Jesus Christ and also to represent Satan. Talk about contradictions!

A German folk name for pentagram is drudenfusz, "witch's foot." German folk tradition indicates the use of pentagrams as protective talismans against evil spirits. During the Middle Ages and the Renaissance, pentagrams were painted on homes or mounted within them to protect against evil spirits and witches.

The Pennsylvania Dutch hexafoos also indicates "witch's foot" and is sometimes used as a synonym for hex signs. It also names a specific architectural motif involving an arch or decoration painted beneath a barn window that was expected to protect against witches.

Materials and Construction

Pentacles can be formed from clay, wax, bone, and wood; most frequently they are made from metal. A practitioner on a low budget could cut a round pentacle from construction paper and inscribe it with a pentagram.

Pentagrams may be drawn in the air, with an athame or other ritual knife, in each of the four directional points to consecrate a magick circle.

Symbolic Interpretations

The pentacle may be interpreted in various ways:

Human Figure: The pentacle may represent a human figure

Parturient Woman: The pentacle may represent a parturient (birthing) woman. The uppermost point is her head, and the other four are her arms and legs spread wide in labor. This image radiates exceptionally strong spiritual protection: hold the laboring woman in mind while drawing pentacles wherever protection is needed.

Spirit and Elements: The pentacle's solitary point may represent spirit while the others indicate the four elements (air, earth, fire, water)

Human Will: Magus Eliphas Levi suggested that the pentacle represented the triumph of the human will over the power of the four elements

Five Elements: East Asian cosmology perceives five elements (air, earth, fire, water, metal) not four, and so the pentacle may represent the eternal interplay of the elements.

Orientation

The solitary point may be considered the pentacle's head. A pentacle may be positioned head up, down or sideways.

Satanists have adopted the inverted pentagram but not because of any witchcraft associations. Many historians suggest that Satanists adopted the inverted pentacle as their emblem because early Christians used the pentacle as a symbol of Christ with the uppermost point indicating his head. Thus turning the pentacle upside down was perceived as a hostile, desecrating act.

Protective Properties

Pentacles are traditionally a protective talisman. In much of the ancient world, especially the Middle East and North Africa, the number five radiates protective energy and is the number most associated with protective spells. The pentagram may, thus, be understood as related to five-fingered hand-shaped amulets like the Hand of Fatima or the Hamsa, which symbolizes the all-protecting Five Fingers of God.

Master magus Cornelius Agrippa explains that every pentagram reveals the ideal qualities represented by the number five: it demonstrates five triangles, five obtuse angles, and five acute angles and is an excellent symbol for counteracting demons or malicious spirits.

Wearing the Pentacle for Protection

A worn pentacle also serves a specific protective purpose during conjuration, sending, or greater divination work. Should raised power run short or rebound on the operator, the pentacle helps earth that force, which matters most when the power in question comes from the practitioner's own deep mind rather than an outside entity, since the boundary of a magic circle only restrains external forces. Cautious practitioners choose to wear their pentacle even for minor workings like casting the runes or a simple charm. Instructions for making your own personal pentacle appear in a later chapter of this Manuscript.

Wiccan Usage

The pentacle is one of Wicca's elemental tools, representing Earth, usually serving as a protective talisman. It has evolved into the religious emblem of Wicca in the manner of the cross for Christians and the hexagram (Star of David) for Jews.

Tarot Association

Pentacles are one of the Tarot suits where they are also called coins. They correspond with the playing card suit of Diamonds and represent the feminine element Earth. Pentacles represent Earth's bounty, abundance, and protective energies. A parallel image would be the cornucopia, the horn overflowing with fruit and wealth.

Jungian Correspondence: Sensation

In Jungian psychology, the pentacle corresponds to the Sensation function, the psychic tool that tells us a thing exists. Connected to our physical senses (touch, taste, hearing, sight, smell), sensation grounds energy in tangible form.

On the Qabalistic Tree of Life, manifestation flows from Kether (pure undifferentiated potential) down to Malkuth (elemental earth, discrete form). Without the sensation function, energy remains ungrounded; ideas never solidify into reality. The pentacle completes the manifestation cycle by anchoring intention in the here and now.

In manifestation work, the pentacle asks: How specific is your goal? How will you know when you've achieved it? Vague aims produce vague results. The pentacle's tangibility (you can hold it, see the pentagram inscribed upon it) mirrors the sensory specificity successful manifestation requires.

Opposite function: Intuition (Wand)

Bar Witchery: The Cutting Board

In bar witchery, the cutting board is the pentacle's extension, the place where our spirits take root in the physical world. Just as the pentacle is the microcosm of the macrocosm, the cutting board becomes the altar space where ingredients are prepared and transformed.

The pentacle is a disk used for consecrating ritual items, including all tools for the altar before they are used in ritual the first time. Each point references something in the outer world: earth, air, fire, water, and spirit.
