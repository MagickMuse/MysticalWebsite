Overview

The key, or clavicle, is a symbol of access to something hidden or secret. Yet keys lock as well as unlock, so a deity shown holding a key may signal the need to keep something private and occluded. A key need not be physical: a code can be the key to a secret language or cipher, and holy words were thought such powerful locks that they were rarely spoken, as with the "Open Sesame" that opened the treasure cave.

Sympathetic Magick

Our ancestors used the physical key as a charm or talisman in sympathetic magick whenever something needed to be unlocked. Jewish midwives placed the key to the synagogue in the hand of a woman in labor, hoping the association would help the baby unlock the door of the womb, and keys were buried with the dead so the gates of the Underworld would open easily.

The Crossed Keys

Saint Peter holds the keys to the pearly gates of Heaven, and these crossed keys became a Papal symbol of the powers given by Christ. One key is gold, the Sun and male energy, and the other silver, the Moon and feminine energy; the gold binds or releases in Heaven, the silver does the same on Earth. Before the Pope, these keys belonged to the Roman god Janus, whose two faces let him look both ways at once and who unlocked the gates of the solstices.

Initiation and Power

Initiation, too, hands over a key that unlocks secret knowledge, a visible sign of attainment. To be given the keys to a town or city is a great honor carrying power and ownership, echoing the medieval custom of handing over keys to grant authority. The "key of the door" once given on the twenty-first birthday marked the coming of age, and the Tarot card numbered twenty-one is the World.
