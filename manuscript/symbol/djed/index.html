Djed

◇Notes/Warnings

Symbolic Meaning

The djed pillar is the Egyptian hieroglyph for "stability," "endurance," and "permanence." It represents the cosmic principle of lasting strength, the backbone that holds things together through time and change.

Association with Osiris

The djed is closely associated with Osiris, Lord of the Dead. It may represent:

  • Osiris's backbone or spine
  • The tree trunk in which Osiris's body was hidden
  • His lost phallus, restored through Isis's magick

Whatever its origin, the djed became Osiris's primary emblem, appearing on sarcophagi, burial shrines, and funerary equipment to ensure the deceased's stability in the afterlife.

Pairing with the Tyet

The djed pillar pairs with the tyet (Knot of Isis) to represent the union of male and female creative forces. Together, Osiris (djed) and Isis (tyet) embody the complementary energies needed for creation, resurrection, and the continuation of life through death. Alternating djed and tyet symbols decorated Tutankhamun's burial shrine.

World Axis Symbolism

As with other pillar-like symbols the world over, the djed also signifies the World Axis and the World Tree, connecting the earthly realm to the heavens.

Ritual Use

The "Raising of the Djed" was an important Egyptian ritual symbolizing Osiris's resurrection and the triumph of order over chaos. Priests would ceremonially erect a djed pillar, reenacting cosmic renewal.
