Overview

The Vesica Piscis, whose name translates as the Vessel of the Fish, is one of the most important shapes in sacred geometry. It is formed by the overlap of two circles, the pointed oval where they intersect. The Pythagoreans saw in it the meeting of the spiritual and material worlds, a sacred doorway between two states of being. The name and shape together also led to its reading as the vagina, a doorway through which life enters the world.

The Egyptian Ru

The Egyptian counterpart is the Ru symbol, which likewise represents the vagina as a doorway through which a spirit passed into the material world, an apt metaphor that supports the Pythagorean idea.

Mandorla

Because of its almond shape it is also called the Mandorla, the Italian word for "almond." It is often used to frame sacred figures in religious iconography, and it appears around the central figure of the World, the twenty-first card of the Major Arcana. Places where the Goddess is honored tend to carry the shape as well; it is seen, for example, on the cover of the well at Glastonbury.
