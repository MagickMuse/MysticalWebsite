Overview

The Khanda is the emblem of the Sikh faith. It represents the four aspects of Sikhism and holds the four sacred weapons within its shape.

The Elements of the Symbol

The double-edged sword at the center, the khanda that gives the symbol its name, stands for the creative power of God and the knowledge of the divine. The circle around it is the chakkar, a wheel whose name shares a root with chakra; it is itself a medieval weapon, and like all circular forms it stands for eternity and unity. The two crossing daggers at the sides are the kirpans, said to have belonged to the guru Hargobind, and they symbolize the balance of spirit and matter.
