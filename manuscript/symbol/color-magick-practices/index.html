Color Magick Practices

The History and Symbolism of Color

Colors are proven to have a profound effect on the human psyche and on our moods. They resonate with the elements, the directions, the seasons, the planets, and astrological signs as well as holding huge significance in their own right.

Colors and particular shades of them confer an immediate identity and make a strong statement. For example, in the green, purple, and white of the Suffragettes (green for hope, purple for dignity, and white for purity), or in the red and white stripes of the old-fashioned barber's pole (where the white represented the color of flesh and the red was the blood sometimes drawn by the cut-throat razor).

The significance of colors is proven by the high value that our ancestors placed on certain plants or substances that could be made into dyes, such as the Imperial Purple of Rome that was produced from a mollusk valued more highly than gold, or the saffron crocus that produced the sacred color of the same name. Prior to the development of chemical dyes, the creation of colors that did not fade in the Sun or wash away was a combination of art, science, and magick, akin to an alchemical process.

The impact of the Sun shining through stained glass, painting the interiors of churches with living colors that shimmered and danced, in a medieval world where color was often a privilege of the wealthy few, can only be imagined. Warriors in Ancient Britain daubed themselves in blue pigments in order to look more fearsome to their enemies. The power of red was once so powerful that corpses were daubed in red ochre in the belief that the color had the same life-giving properties as blood.

The seven colors of the rainbow, which break down into 700 shades visible to the naked eye, are associated with the seven planets, the days of the week, the Seven Heavens, and the seven notes of the musical scale.

The Power of Color

Color therapy operates on the principle that colors create beneficial effects on different organs and systems in the body. By exposing yourself to specific colors, you can support healing, shift emotional states, and align your energy with your intentions.

Practitioners often do this instinctively, wearing green after a breakup to encourage forward movement, or choosing red when confidence is needed. Color therapy simply brings this natural tendency into conscious practice.

A note on black:

Black has many negative connotations to the general public like death, mourning. To witches, however, black is a color of power, for it contains all the other colors. It's also reminiscent of mystery and the night, the time when witches often gather to work magick. In spellworking, black is associated with the planet Saturn and used for banishing, endings, and inner strength.

Practical Applications

Wearing Color

The most direct way to work with color energy is through clothing:

  • Red for energy, courage, and confidence
  • Violet to enhance divine connection
  • Green for healing and moving forward

If a needed color doesn't suit your outfit or situation, wear it as an undergarment or carry a swatch of fabric in your pocket.

Candle Magick

Burning colored candles infuses your space with specific energies:

  • Red candles build confidence for challenging situations
  • Yellow candles encourage outgoing, conversational energy at gatherings
  • Violet candles create introspective, meditative atmospheres

Choose candles intuitively based on what feels right, or deliberately select colors that align with your needs.

Methods for Identifying Needed Colors

Physical Color Collection

Create a set of color samples, one for each rainbow color plus white and black. Sources include:

  • Hand-painted cardboard squares
  • Magazine clippings
  • Paint manufacturer color charts
  • Colored tiles or fabric swatches

Practice: Sit quietly with your collection. Notice which color draws you without conscious decision. This is what your body needs. Gaze at the chosen color for 20-30 minutes, breathing deeply and allowing your system to absorb its energy. Your body signals completion when you feel restless or lose interest.

Daily variation: Each morning, choose the color tile that resonates and keep it visible throughout your day. The color works both when you actively look at it and when you absorb it subliminally while working.

Imagination Method

When physical samples aren't available, work with color through visualization:

  1. Close your eyes and mentally go through each color
  2. Ask yourself: "Do I need more red? Orange? Yellow?" and so on
  3. When you identify the right color, imagine breathing it in as pure light
  4. Continue until you feel saturated with that color's energy

This technique works as effectively as physical samples once you've familiarized yourself with the practice.

Pendulum Dowsing

Use a pendulum to identify which colors your body needs:

  1. Establish responses: Hold your pendulum and determine which movements indicate "yes," "no," "I don't know," and "I don't want to answer"
  2. Test accuracy: Ask questions you know the answers to (e.g., "Am I male?")
  3. Query colors: Systematically ask if you need each color: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet
  4. Note results: You may need multiple colors depending on your current state

Once the pendulum reveals your needs, absorb those colors through any of the methods described.

Key Principles

Colors affect practitioners constantly, whether consciously acknowledged or not. The difference between color therapy and everyday life is simply awareness and intention.

Physical exposure (wearing, viewing, surrounding yourself) works powerfully, but imagination and visualization can be equally effective once you've trained your sensitivity.

Your body knows what it needs. Trust the colors you're drawn to, through intuition, pendulum, or simple attraction, and absorb their energies through whichever method feels most natural to you.

Closing Reflection

The world is vibrant with color, each hue carrying its own energy, medicine, and teaching. While you may naturally gravitate toward certain colors, every color has value and purpose.

Some colors resonate based on personal preference. Others connect through name, birth date, and astrological correspondences, becoming personal colors that carry special significance in magickal and healing work.

Color and Moon Magick

Cultural background and personal experience shape how color is experienced.
