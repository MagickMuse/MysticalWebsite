Symbolic Meaning

The ankh is the Egyptian hieroglyph for "life" and "to live." It is among the most recognizable and powerful Egyptian symbols, representing not merely physical existence but eternal life, the animating force that distinguishes the living from the dead.

Magickal Uses

Gods and goddesses are depicted holding the ankh, often extending it toward the pharaoh to confer divine blessing and life force. The expression di ankh ("given life") followed royal names as both blessing and protection. Some practitioners carried or wore ankh amulets for vitality, protection, and connection to divine life force.

Form and Origin

The ankh may represent an amuletic knot, similar to the tyet, with a loop at the top and short projections to the sides. Some Egyptologists propose it represents a sandal strap seen from above, connecting it to the ability to stand and walk. Its shape also recalls a key, leading to interpretations as the "key of life."

Connection to Mirrors

Ankh was also the Egyptian word for mirror. Bronze mirrors were sometimes crafted in ankh shape, the polished disc corresponding to the loop. The mirror's circular form recalled the sun, linking ankh symbolism to Ra and solar power.

The Union of Opposites

The volume of meaning that can be squeezed from such a simple symbol is awe-inspiring. The Ankh represents the male and female genitalia, the Sun coming over the horizon, and the union of Heaven and Earth. This association with the Sun means the ankh is traditionally drawn in gold and never in silver, which relates to the Moon.

Key to the Afterlife

Its resemblance to a key gives a clue to another meaning of this magickal symbol. The Egyptians believed that the Afterlife was as meaningful as the present one, and the ankh provided the key to the gates of death and what lay beyond.

Beyond Egypt

Powerful symbols frequently cross into other cultures despite their origins, and the ankh is no exception. Because it symbolizes immortality and the Universe, it was borrowed by fourth-century Coptic Christians who used it to reinforce Christ's message that there is life after death. The Rosicrucians use it as well. Even though its actual invention is shrouded in thousands of years of mystery, the ankh symbol can be bought in any high street jewelry store anywhere in the world.
