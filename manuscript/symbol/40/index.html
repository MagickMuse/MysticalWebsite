Symbolism of Forty

Forty is generally agreed, among most religions and belief systems, to be the number of symbolic death, initiation, trial and testing, preparation, and waiting. Both Moses and Mohammed received their call from God at this age, as did the Buddha, the very epitome of the idea that life begins at forty. Muslims hold that the Qur'an should be read every forty days. It is said to be unwise to attempt the study of the Kabbalah before the prospective student has reached forty.

Understanding the symbolic meaning of this number gives a deeper appreciation of the forty days and nights that Christ spent in the desert being tempted by the Devil, and the forty years during which the Children of Israel wandered in the wilderness. Other examples that reveal a period of trial or tribulation are the forty days of the deluge, the forty days that Moses spent on Mount Sinai, and the forty days of denial or fasting during Lent. The Ancient Egyptians also fasted for forty days, in memory of the forty days between the death of Osiris and his reappearance, the same number of days between the crucifixion of Christ and his ascension to Heaven. For the Babylonians, forty days was the length of time between the disappearance and reappearance of the Pleiades, a season of floods, storms, and other dangers.

In Africa, when the deceased is over one hundred and five years old, the funeral lasts for forty days. In other cultures the period after which the soul may safely be assumed to have left the body is forty days, after which the mourning customs are relaxed, and an Altaic widow, for example, was free to remarry after this time. ⚠️ Among some Native American peoples a second burial is customary, taking place forty days after the first interment; this is a closed practice sacred to those traditions and should only be observed by those within the originating culture. In Islam, a memorial is held forty days after a death. The period of isolation for suspected plague victims during the Middle Ages was set at forty days, which gave rise to the word quarantine.
