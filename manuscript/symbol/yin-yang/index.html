Overview

The yin yang, also called the Ta Ki, is Chinese in origin and comes from the Taoist tradition, though its meaning has spread across the world. Two identical shapes nestle inside a circle, divided by an S-shaped line, with a dot set in the fuller part of each. The two are often drawn in opposing colors.

Balance of Opposites

The shapes express the interplay of opposing forces. Yin is the female, the Moon, coldness, and passivity; yang is the male, the Sun, heat, and action. The dot, or bindhu, in the fuller part of each shape borrows its color from the other, signifying balance and harmony even between forces that appear to be at odds.
