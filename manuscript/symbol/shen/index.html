Symbol Grimoire

Shen

◇Notes/Warnings
Egyptian hieroglyph meaning 'to encircle' and implying protection and eternity. Represents a looped rope or cord. The cartouche that surrounds royal names is an elongated shen. Birds of prey (especially falcons associated with Horus) are depicted holding shen rings in their talons. Encircling as an act of protection and binding.
◇Type
Emblem & Device
◇Culture
Ancient Egyptian

Symbolic Meaning

The shen is an Egyptian hieroglyph representing a looped and knotted rope or cord. As a verb, shen means "to encircle." The symbol carries meanings of:

  • Protection through enclosure
  • Eternity and infinite duration
  • Cosmic cycles that have no beginning or end

The Cartouche

The cartouche, the oval frame surrounding royal names in hieroglyphic inscriptions, is an elongated shen. By placing the pharaoh's name inside this protective circle, the shen shielded the king's essence from harm. Since names carried a person's vital force, protecting the royal name was protecting the king himself.

Iconographic Use

Deities represented as birds, particularly the falcon Horus, are often depicted holding shen rings in their talons. This imagery conveys divine protection extended over what the shen encircles. The shen also appears supporting figures of gods or expressions of time, emphasizing eternity.

Magickal Principle

The shen embodies the protective power of encircling. Wrapping, binding, and surrounding something with a cord or cloth creates a boundary that guards what is within. This same principle underlies knotting magick: the act of circling cord around itself "ties" the incantation into physical form.

Correspondences

Protection, eternity, encircling, binding, royal names, Horus, infinite cycles, cosmic order
