🌙Ramadan

🍽️Fasting

Ramadan is the ninth month of the Islamic year and, for Muslims, a season of physical and spiritual detoxification given over to contemplation, reflection, and fasting. The name refers both to the month itself and to the religious observances that fill it.

A Lunar Observance

The Islamic calendar is lunar, and each new month begins only when the crescent Moon is first sighted, so the timing of Ramadan shifts from year to year when measured against the solar calendar. It is held to be the most propitious month of the year: during it the great adversary Satan and his demonic helpers are said to be locked away by God, unable to press their evil influence on humankind, so that any malice held in thought or carried out in deed falls squarely on the person responsible. Most significant of all, it was in this month that the Qur'an was revealed to Mohammed.

Fasting, Prayer, and Self-Improvement

Joining prayer to fasting is believed to bring renewed clarity and calm, drawing body, mind, and spirit together in the pursuit of self-improvement and closeness to God. Because the whole community keeps the observance together, the month carries a strong current of unity and mutual support, and old grudges and quarrels are set aside in a shared search for enlightenment.

The first prayer of the day comes before dawn and the last at sunset, when the daily fast is broken. Life shifts toward the dark hours as a result, and in places with large Muslim populations the markets and restaurants stay open late. Abstinence from sex is also kept during the fasting hours. Throughout the month the faithful immerse themselves in the teachings of Islam, often rereading the Qur'an from end to end, and devote themselves to good works while the concerns of the material world give way to the spiritual.

Charity and the Feast

Ramadan is a season of charity and of sharing with those who have less. A tax known as the Zakaat, reckoned as a portion of one's unused wealth, is calculated and given at this time, in effect a charitable donation. The fast comes to a close when the next new crescent Moon is sighted, and the day that follows is a feast: people put on new clothes, and families and friends pass the day in one another's company.
