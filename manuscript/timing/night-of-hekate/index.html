November 16 is the night dedicated in honor of Hekate, the ancient Greek goddess of magick, witchcraft, and crossroads. While this is not the only night of the year sacred to her, Hekate claims dominion over all dark moon nights as well as the final day of each month, with October 31 (the Feast of All Hallows) being particularly sacred. The festival on November 16 recalls that Hekate was once a great goddess complete with temples and shrines, venerated by many, not only her spiritual daughters, witches. Some traditions observe Hekate Night from sunset on November 15 through sunset on November 16.

Why November 16?

No one really knows why this specific date was chosen. However, November 16 falls during the time when the Sun passes through Scorpio, the eighth zodiac sign. Scorpio rules aspects of life sacred to Hekate: death, transformation, gateways between realms, and occult knowledge.

About Hekate

Hekate is a powerful goddess. Being the goddess of magick and witchcraft in Greek mythology, as well as the ruler of the three realms, has earned her great respect. Some have feared her. However, if you did good works during your life, she would help you and treat you with great kindness.

It was Hekate who helped the grieving goddess Demeter in her search for her daughter, Persephone, after Persephone was abducted by Hades and taken to the Underworld. Hekate, holding her torch (one of her symbols), led Demeter in the darkness to find Persephone.

Hekate is also known to fight without mercy to protect the innocent and those living on the fringes of society: the poor, the homeless, the abandoned, and the abused. To the unfortunate and to those who help them, she shows great compassion.

Triple Nature

Some believe her to be the goddess of heaven, earth, and the Underworld. Others associate her with the three realms: the sky, the seas, and the earth. She is also said to represent the three phases of a woman's life: Maiden, Mother, and Crone. It is no surprise that she is referred to as the Triple Goddess.

Nocturnal Observance

Hekate keeps nocturnal office hours and this festival is no exception. It begins at nightfall. Animal sacrifices were once offered in Hekate's temples in what are now Greece, Turkey, and Georgia; however those rites have been lost and are no longer appropriate.

Sacred Symbols

If you wish to honor Hekate on her special night, be aware of some of her symbols. These are sacred to her. It is said if you encounter any of these symbols or images at any time, not just on her night, it means she is near and wants to help you or is answering a request you have made of her:

  • Three faces or heads: Hekate has been depicted since ancient times as the Triple Goddess, with three bodies or three heads
  • Torches: Her most consistent attribute, representing her role as guide through darkness
  • Keys: Symbol of her role as keyholder and guardian of thresholds between worlds
  • Dogs: Her sacred animals, whose howling announces her presence. Black dogs are especially connected to her
  • Crescent Moon: Her crown is often shaped like a crescent with points facing up
  • Crossroads: Especially three-way intersections
  • The number three

Being the goddess of the realms also means Hekate is associated with transitions and portals, not only between realms but also on Earth. Paths, gates, doors, and crossroads have meaning to her.

Colors

Her colors include black, gray, silver, and deep midnight navy blue.

Signs of Her Presence

Pay attention for signs that she is nearby helping. If you see the number three more frequently, or a stray dog appears in your yard and does not leave, these are signs she has heard your requests. Also, expect a prophetic dream. If you are ever followed by dogs, especially a black dog, Hekate is near and is reaching out to you.

Remember the Less Fortunate

Hekate is also known as the protector of those who are marginalized by society, both human and nonhuman. Here are some ways you or your coven can honor Hekate and do some good works at the same time:

  • Volunteer at or donate to a homeless shelter
  • Donate to food banks, not only during the holiday season
  • Remember Hekate's relationship with dogs: foster a pet, volunteer to walk dogs at a shelter, or donate supplies like paper towels, toys, and food

Hekate Suppers

Hekate Suppers were once held as part of this sacred night's observances. Celebrants share a feast in Hekate's honor, with a full plate for the goddess left at a crossroads.

Important: Whatever is left is considered given. Do not use your finest china plate unless it is intended as part of the offering. It is forbidden to take anything back that has been given to Hekate. Once offered, the gift belongs entirely to her.

Traditional Foods

Appropriate foods for the ritual dinner include:

  • Cheese
  • Honey
  • Garlic and garlic-flavored dips
  • Eggs
  • Mushrooms
  • Fish, including red mullet (a scavenger which was taboo elsewhere)
  • Dark olives
  • Breads cut into crescent moon shapes
  • Honey cake and small pastries for dessert

Toast her with dark grape juice or deep red wine.

The Offering Ritual

Leave the offering for Hekate at a crossroads and do not look back. If someone else picks it up, whether human or animal, this is wholly appropriate and Hekate's desire. Should you hear a dog bark, it is highly auspicious: this is Hekate's sacred animal acknowledging the offering.

Hekate's Procession

Allegedly, Hekate roams the Earth on this night with her pack of hounds and wolves, accompanied by a host of ghosts, blessing those who left offerings for her. This is a night of divine favor for those who honor the Queen of the Crossroads.

Walk with Hekate

If you have time on this Night of Hekate, finish the night by walking with her. Walk outside into the darkness. On this late autumn night, the Great Wheel of Time is turning, moving us closer to winter. Now is when you can truly experience Hekate, the Queen of the Night. Listen as the wind howls through the pines and bows the naked maples. Walk with Hekate and experience the Great Mysteries of eternity: the stars, the Moon, the clouds moved by the wind and the enduring hills, old as time.

Now is the time to dream, to seek the answers about those mysteries. Now is the time to listen: Hekate is speaking to us. She tells us if we are to understand the Great Mysteries, we must work our magick. But we must listen.

Rituals for Hekate Night

Preparing for Hekate Night depends on the type of ritual you wish to perform. Before any ritual, cleanse your space and cast your circle first to keep out unwanted energies.
