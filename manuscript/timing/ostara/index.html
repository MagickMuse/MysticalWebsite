Historical and Cultural Context

The Holiday's Significance

The spring equinox marks the first day of spring and the start of the busy planting season in agrarian cultures. Farmers till their fields and sow seeds. Ostara, therefore, is one of the fertility holidays and a time for planting seeds-literally or figuratively. Because day and night are the same length on the equinoxes, these holidays also signify balance.

Pagans and witches celebrate Ostara when the sun enters 0 degrees of Aries, around March 21. In the Northern Hemisphere, the spring equinox ushers in warmer weather, days that are longer than nights, and life reawakening. Birdsong fills the air and new buds sprout on bare tree limbs; baby animals are born and the greening of the earth begins.

Christianity adopted this joyful period of the year for the celebration of Easter (which usually falls near the spring equinox). Ostara gets its name from the German fertility goddess Ostare; the word Easter derives from the same root. Both holidays celebrate the triumph of life over death.

Origins of the Easter Bunny

In an old German story, a rabbit laid some sacred eggs and decorated them as a gift for the fertility goddess Ostara. Ostara liked the beautiful eggs so much that she asked the rabbit to share the eggs with everyone throughout the world.

Some popular Easter customs have their roots in Ostara's symbolism. Eggs represent the promise of new life, and painting them bright colors engages the creative aspect of the sabbat. Some cultures connect the egg's golden yolk with the sun. Rabbits, of course, have long been linked with fertility.

Ways to Celebrate Ostara

On Ostara, sow seeds that you want to bear fruit in the coming months. This is an ideal time to launch new career ventures, move to a new home, or begin a new relationship. If you're a gardener, you'll start preparing the soil and planting flowers, herbs, and/or vegetables now. Consider the magickal properties of botanicals and choose plants that represent your intentions. If you don't have room for a garden, you could plant seeds in a flowerpot to symbolize wishes you hope will grow to fruition in the coming months.

You might also enjoy decorating eggs with magickal symbols, such as pentagrams and spirals.

Modern Ostara Practice

Celebrating an Ancient Holiday in a Modern World

Spring arrives with bursting flowers, nesting birds, and baby animals in meadows. After winter's confinement, people feel an urge to reconnect with nature through gardening, spring walks, and outdoor activities. Spring cleaning, both physical and spiritual, becomes a way to clear old energy and make space for new growth. For many Pagans, the equinox marks a shift from planning to action, turning winter's intentions into tangible goals and projects. Relationships also blossom as love and connection fill the air, and many embrace new practices like meditation, yoga, or divination to cultivate personal growth alongside the awakening earth.

Modern Pagan Celebrations

Many Pagans honor the goddess Eostre with symbols of fertility: rabbits, eggs, chicks, and pastel colors reflecting spring's palette. Rituals often include blessing seeds, both literal garden seeds and figurative intentions for the year ahead. Activities embrace youthful play and renewal, from finger painting to outdoor games, honoring the Maiden Goddess and young God. Popular traditions include egg hunts (for all ages), egg decorating with natural dyes, cascarones filled with blessings, and various egg games from different cultures.

Some traditions focus on the equinox's balance symbolism. Night and day stand equal, reflecting yin and yang, inner and outer work, contraction and expansion. The sun's victory over darkness begins, encouraging practitioners to grow stronger alongside the lengthening light. Honey and maple syrup serve as sacred offerings, awakening with spring as bees emerge and tree sap runs. The butterfly symbolizes transformation and rebirth, emerging from winter's cocoon to spread new wings. The lamb represents the young god in Aries, though Pagan focus remains on renewal rather than sacrifice.

The Natural World at Ostara

Spring landscapes transform dramatically. Trees bud, grass greens, and early flowers like crocuses and daffodils push through snow. Birds nest and baby animals appear in fields and forests. The air fills with the scent of melting snow, damp earth, and blooming plants. For those in transitional climates, walks reveal the sound of melting snow, running water, and awakening streams.

Gardens come to life as people till soil and plant seeds. Container gardening flourishes on balconies and windowsills for urban dwellers. Farmers' markets reopen with early spring offerings. Even challenging weather like mud, lingering snow, or spring rain, invites engagement with the elements and renewal through water's cleansing power.

The Prevailing Energies and Energetics of Ostara

Ostara's energy pulses with action and expansion. After winter's introspection and planning, it's time to implement, create, and grow. The world physically expands as people venture farther from home, reconnect with community, and welcome new relationships. Spring's restlessness drives movement, change, and the shedding of what no longer serves.

Balance remains central: light and dark, passive and active, death and life, inner and outer. The equinox teaches grounding while reaching skyward, like kite flying, where intentions soar while feet stay planted. It's a season for challenging oneself with new beginnings, embracing playfulness, and remembering that growth requires consistent nurturing, just as seeds need more than a day or two of water to flourish.

Moon Magick at Ostara

The spring equinox mirrors the first quarter moon. Both hold a near-equal balance of dark and light, and both mark the turning point where the light half of the cycle takes over. Just as the first quarter moon sits at the crossroads of manifestation, calling for energy, decision-making, and forward momentum, the equinox asks the same of us as we step fully into spring.

The spring moons herald beauty, youth, new beginnings, and fertility, with the first stirrings of romance and love weaving in as well. There is a felt balance between the material world and the realm of spirit, and this equilibrium favors cocreation and partnership. Whatever seeds were planted in winter's darkness are now ready to push through the soil.

Spells that use eggs, flowers, and seeds align beautifully with lunar work during this season, blessing your intentions with the fertile energy of the equinox. Anything can blossom under the spring moons, so bring vitality and decisiveness to your magick and trust that the growing light will carry your efforts forward.

The Merging of Traditions

Ostara overlaps significantly with Easter and spring celebrations worldwide, sharing symbols of eggs, rebirth, and renewal. Many Pagan families blend traditions, honoring both the equinox's balance and cultural spring festivities. Egg activities, spring cleaning, and garden blessings transcend religious boundaries, allowing practitioners to celebrate openly while maintaining personal spiritual practices.

The season welcomes diverse expressions: some honor specific deities like Eostre, others focus on astronomical balance, and many simply celebrate nature's awakening. Field trips to zoos, farms, or planetariums offer educational and spiritual connections. Whether through ritual, play, gardening, or simply dancing in spring rain, Ostara invites everyone to shed winter's cocoon and embrace transformation.

Traditional Ostara Activities

Color Hard-Boiled Eggs

Add symbols for the God and the Goddess, fertility, the Sun, unity, fire, water, agriculture, prosperity, growth, strength, wisdom, spring, love, affection, and protection.

Make Natural Dyes from Herbs

  • Green: coltsfoot and bracken
  • Yellow-green: carrot tops
  • Yellow: turmeric
  • Orange: onion skin
  • Red: madder root
  • Blue: blueberries
  • Bright blue: red cabbage

Consecrate the Eggs

Speak:

In the Name of the Goddess of Spring; And the ever-returning God of the Sun; By the powers of the Elementals Earth, Air, Fire, and Water; I do consecrate these Eggs of Ostara.

Point athame at eggs and make the sign of the pentagram. See energy flow into the eggs:

New life lies within as new life shall enter the soil. Let those who seek this life find it and consume it, for all life feeds on life.

Ostara Egg Hunt

Hide the consecrated eggs and commence the Ostara Egg Hunt. Make some of the found eggs into deviled eggs for supper.

Make Hot Cross Buns

Cut the X with the bolline, honoring Earth and Sun for Spring, then bless the cakes.

Ostara Eve Blessing

Light a purple or violet candle and burn Patchouli incense. Carry both through the house:

Farewell to Wintry spirits and friends;

On morrow we greet the spirits of Spring.

Our blessings to thee as your way you wend;

And merry we'll meet next Winter again.

Blow out the candle:

Merry Meet, Merry Part, and Merry Meet Again!

Traditional Meal

Serve hot-cross buns and eggs for breakfast; ham for supper.

Eggshell Garden Blessing

Toss crushed eggshells into the garden:

For Fairy, for flowers, for herbs in the bowers; the shells pass fertility with Springtime showers.

Other Activities

  • Wear colorful or pastel clothing
  • Bless seeds to be planted in the garden
  • Empower an egg with a desired quality, then eat the egg and envision it bringing what was sought
