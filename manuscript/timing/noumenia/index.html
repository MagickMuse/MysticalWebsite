🌑The Noumenia Ceremony

The Noumenia is the first day of each Hellenic lunar month, marking the first sighting of the new moon after the dark phase. In contemporary terms it is also called Protominia. Tradition holds it as the most sacred day of every month.

It falls under the patronage of Apollon Noumenios, Artemis Noumenia, Zeus, Hermes, Hera, Hekate, and the Household Gods. The Noumenia and the seventh of the month both belong to Apollo.

On a normal calendar. The Noumenia falls on the evening when the first sliver of the waxing crescent moon becomes visible after sunset, typically one to two days after the astronomical new moon. The Hellenic month begins here, so this date shifts each cycle relative to the Gregorian calendar.
