The word "esbat" is believed to derive from the Old French s'esbattre, which means "to frolic and amuse oneself" or "to celebrate joyfully." Esbats are among the sacred, celebratory days of Wicca.

Historical Origins

At present, there is only one known pre-twentieth-century reference to an "esbat." It derives from the memoirs of the witch-hunter Pierre de Lancre, published in 1613. The word is used in a quotation from a witch.

Margaret Murray picked up the reference and used it in her writings, which were to have tremendous influence on Gerald Gardner. Esbats are now an integral part of Gardnerian Wicca and the word has entered the general witchcraft lexicon, although it is not used in a consistent fashion.

Modern Definitions

The modern definition of "esbat" is somewhat loose and one cannot assume that everyone defines the word identically. At its least rigid definition, esbats refer to any scheduled ritual. It is most often intended to indicate the meeting of a coven, however independent practitioners also celebrate esbats, and will do so in solitary fashion if they choose.

How and When Esbats Are Celebrated

How and when esbats are celebrated depends upon how each tradition, coven or individual defines the word:

Synonym for Sabbat: Some use "esbat" as a synonym for "sabbat" or to refer to one of the four lesser Wiccan sabbats.

Lunar Devotions: Esbats may be specifically identified with lunar devotions. In this case, esbats are celebrated in conjunction with either the new or full moon, so that there are 13 annual esbats (or if the full moon is observed, the occasional additional blue moon, too).

When esbats are associated with the full moon, some prefer celebrating sky-clad (without clothes) so that moonlight is better able to charge the body with its magical energy, however this depends upon coven and individual.

Regular Meetings: Some covens use the term "esbat" to refer to any regularly scheduled meeting.

The Esbat Lunar Cycle

A solar year of twelve months can contain up to thirteen Full Moons, which means one month will see two Full Moons. The Esbat lunar cycle commences with the Full Moon nearest to Yule (around December 21st), known as the Oak Moon. Each subsequent Full Moon carries its own traditional name, following in sequence from this starting point. The doubled Full Moon most commonly falls during July or August.

Special Descriptive Moon Names

Three special descriptive names overlay the standard moon names when specific conditions occur:

Blood Moon: A relatively rare phenomenon where the Full Moon appears russet to deep wine-red in color. Most common between August and October, it amplifies the month's Full Moon with additional Power, Energy, or Aggressive force.

Blue Moon: The second Full Moon occurring within a single calendar month. This moon adds Spiritual Energy to the regular monthly Full Moon it accompanies.

Sidhe Moon (also called Otherworld Moon): The second Dark Moon occurring within a single month. When present, it enhances that Dark Moon with heightened Intuitive Power, Psychic Energy, or Occult Wisdom.

New Moon and Dark Moon Esbats

New Moon Esbat: The New Moon may be honored at the final sliver of visible light, just before the moon turns completely dark. This liminal moment marks the threshold between waning and renewal.

Dark Moon Esbat: When the moon shows no light at all, this phase is traditionally observed but not used for magical workings. It is a time of rest, introspection, and stillness rather than active spellcraft.

Names in Esbat Practice

During Esbat rituals, practitioners typically use their given name or their Craft Name. A practitioner's Working Name or Secret Name should only be spoken aloud when working in complete solitude.
