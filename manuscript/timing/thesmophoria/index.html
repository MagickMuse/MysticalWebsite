Overview

The Thesmophoria was an ancient Greek women's mystery festival held in honor of Demeter and Persephone. This was one of the most widespread festivals in the Greek world, celebrated annually in autumn.

Ritual Practices

During the Thesmophoria, cakes shaped like female genitalia and snakes dedicated to Demeter and Persephone were thrown into chasms or underground vaults inhabited by sacred snakes. This practice connected the festival to the myth of Persephone's descent into the underworld. Cakes served as offerings to the goddesses and food for the sacred serpents guarding the underground chambers. The snake and genitalia shapes symbolized fertility, regeneration, and the chthonic powers of the earth.

Because it was a mystery cult festival, few details survive. However, we know that during the ritual, pigs, cakes, and pine branches were thrown into underground chasms (or vaults), which were then covered so that they were contained within Earth.

Snakes lived in these grottoes as guardians; they may have consumed much of the offerings. During the next year's festival, the decayed remains were removed and incorporated into ritual. This ritual may have reproduced Persephone's descent into the Underworld and her ultimate emergence.

Women's Mysteries

The Thesmophoria was exclusively a women's festival. Men were forbidden from participating or even observing the rites. This was one of the few occasions when women could gather independently and conduct their own religious ceremonies.

Connection to Agriculture

The festival was intrinsically tied to agricultural fertility and the cycle of planting. The decomposed offerings from the previous year were believed to have fertilizing properties and were mixed with seed grain for planting.

Reference: The Three Days at Athens

In the Athenian calendar, the Thesmophoria falls on the eleventh through the thirteenth of Puanepsion. It is an all-female agricultural festival in honour of Demeter and Persephone, held at the hillside sanctuary of Demeter.

The First Day

The women climbed the hill and made camp, sleeping on the ground in huts.

The Second Day

The women sat on the ground and fasted from all solid food, taking only pomegranate seeds, in sympathy for the mourning of Demeter. They taunted one another in iambic verse, in imitation of Iambe and Demeter.

The Third Day

A torch-light ceremony was held, in commemoration of Demeter's search for Persephone by torch-light. This is likely when the deposited objects were retrieved from the earth by purified priestesses and placed upon the altars of the two Goddesses. The retrieved matter was later mixed with the seed grain to be sown in the following month. The remainder of the day was given to joyous celebration.

On a normal calendar. The Thesmophoria falls on the eleventh through the thirteenth of Puanepsion, generally landing in October of the Gregorian calendar, and shifts each cycle.
