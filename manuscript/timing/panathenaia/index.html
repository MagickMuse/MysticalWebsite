🦉Panathenaia

The Panathenaia is the celebration of the birthday of Athena, held on the twenty-eighth day of Hekatombaion in the Athenian calendar.

Ritual Procedure

  • A vigil was kept through the night preceding the Panathenaic procession.
  • At sunrise a sacrifice was made to Eros and Athena at the altar of Eros within the Academy.
  • A torch race then carried the sacred fire from the Academy to the altar of Athena.
  • Sacrifices were also offered to Athena Hugieia (Health) and to Nike.
  • The three or four days following the procession were given to contests of sport and of art.

The Greater Panathenaia

Every fourth year the festival was celebrated in an expanded form known as the Greater Panathenaia. A new peplos, the sacred robe of Athena, was woven for the Goddess. A great procession carried the peplos to Her statue in Her temple, where the robe was laid across Athena's knees and later stored in the treasury. The formal re-robing of the statue then took place at the Plunteria.

On a normal calendar. The Panathenaia falls on the twenty-eighth day of Hekatombaion, usually landing in late July or early August of the Gregorian calendar, and shifts each cycle.
