🛡️The Ceremony of Triakas

The Feast of Hekate, known in the ancient Athenian calendar as the Deipnon, is the rite of Hekate held on the last day of every lunar month, the dark moon.

The last three days of each Athenian month were given over to the chthonic deities as a unified sequence, and the final day, the dark moon, was reserved for Hekate specifically. On the Deipnon, offerings were left at the household crossroads or before the herm at the door, and the house itself was purified in preparation for the coming Noumenia.

On a normal calendar. The Feast of Hekate falls on the last day of each lunar month. In the Athenian reckoning this is the day of the dark moon preceding the first sighting of the new crescent, and it recurs monthly through the calendar year.
