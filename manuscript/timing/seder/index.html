✡️Passover

The Seder is the ritual meal of the first night of Passover, in which every food on the table is laden with symbolic meaning. It is a fine example of how a shared meal can be made into an act of remembrance, each item chosen to carry a layer of meaning, here recalling two faces of the Exodus story, slavery and freedom: before midnight and the plague the people were slaves; after it, they were free.

Religious Associations

Seven symbolic foods serve as reminders of this time:

  1. Maror and Chazeret — bitter herbs symbolizing the bitterness of slavery. Maror means "bitter," and horseradish, endive, or dandelion leaves may be used; Chazeret is itself a particularly bitter leaf.
  2. Charoset — a crumbly mixture of chopped nuts, grated apple, and cinnamon, symbolizing the mortar used to build the Egyptian storehouses.
  3. Karpas — a cooked vegetable dipped in salt water; the water recalls the tears of the Israelite slaves, and the dipping is a symbol of royalty and of newfound freedom.
  4. Z'roa — roast lamb, symbolic of the paschal lamb.
  5. Beitzah — a roasted egg, recalling an offering once made in the Temple at Jerusalem.
  6. Matzoh — a plate of unleavened bread. Because no fermented food is eaten during Passover, the dough is allowed to rest no longer than eighteen or twenty minutes so that it cannot ferment. Eaten throughout the holiday, it recalls the first bread of the early Israelites and symbolizes humility, since it is not "inflated" in any way.

In addition, four glasses of wine or grape juice are drunk in the second part of the meal, symbolizing the four promises God made to the people: "I will bring out," "I will deliver," "I will redeem," and "I will take."
