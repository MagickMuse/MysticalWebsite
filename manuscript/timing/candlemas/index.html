Etymology and Name

Candlemas is the informal English name given to the Roman Catholic feast of the Purification of the Blessed Virgin. The name reflects the festival's central tradition of blessing candles. In Spanish-speaking countries, the festival is known as Candelaria.

Historical Origins and Timing

Candlemas is the oldest of the festivals specifically honoring the Blessed Mother, with traditions in their current form safely dated to the eleventh century. The festival's timing varies depending on which calendar version is used: Candlemas falls on either February 2nd or February 15th, although always beginning the previous eve.

Candlemas is generally understood as an attempt to replace the Lupercalia, Rome's festival of purification and fertility that officially began on February 13th. The Lupercalia was officially banned in 494 CE, although it's believed to have survived in secret for longer.

Connection to Purification Festivals

Candlemas coincides in time with other purification festivals dedicated to divine mothers, creating a network of February celebrations focused on cleansing and renewal:

Brigid and Imbolc: The Celtic purification festival honoring the goddess Brigid.

Juno and the Lupercalia: The Roman purification and fertility festival dedicated to Juno.

This convergence of purification festivals during early February reflects deep spiritual significance attached to this time of year across multiple spiritual traditions.

End of the Christmas Season

Candlemas marks the official end of the Christmas season. This is when Yule greens and decorations are taken down, symbolically closing the winter holiday cycle and transitioning toward spring.

Modern Wiccan Usage

Despite its ecclesiastical name, the name Candlemas is often used to refer to the modern Wiccan sabbat Imbolc, perhaps because of positive association with candle magic (and maybe simply because many find it easier to pronounce). In other words, although the name Candlemas is used, rituals and practices belong specifically to Imbolc.

This naming convention creates an interesting bridge between Christian and Pagan traditions, with modern practitioners using the Catholic festival's name while performing distinctly Pagan observances focused on Brigid and the return of light.

Neo-Pagan Candle Celebrations

Other Neo-Pagans understand Candlemas as a celebration of candles, now standard everyday witchcraft tools. Traditionally candles are set ablaze in every window and the night is considered ideal for candle magic and divination.

This focus on candle blessing and illumination connects the Christian tradition of blessing ceremonial candles with the older Pagan traditions of welcoming back the light during late winter's darkness.

Candelaria and Oya Syncretism

Candelaria is the equivalent of Candlemas in Spanish-speaking countries. Within African diaspora religions, Oya, the warrior orisha of storms, is syncretized to the Virgin of Candelaria and shares her feast day.

Oya sweeps the atmosphere clean using the powerful hurricane winds that blow annually from Africa toward the Caribbean. As Queen of the Cemetery Gates and ruler of transformation through storm and change, her association with this purification festival creates a powerful spiritual convergence.

Oya's traditional Candelaria offerings include nine purple candles, nine small purple eggplants, and a glass of red wine. These offerings honor her role as cleanser and transformer, aligning with the purification themes central to all the February festivals.
