Ritual Grimoire

Witches' Working Tools

The Basic Set

Every practicing witch needs a foundational toolkit before ritual work can begin. The four essentials are a white-hilted knife, the Athame, the Chalice, and the Censer.

The Full Complement

Rounding out to eight tools, the complete set is traditionally known by these paired names:

  • The Witches' Knife: also called the Athame or Bolline
  • The Witches' Cord: the Girdle Cord, or cingulum
  • The Witches' Censer: the incense burner, also known as the thurible
  • The Witches' Cup: the chalice
  • The Witches' Spellbook: the Book of Shadows, workbook, or Liber spirituum

From this base toolkit, a witch can go on to craft further magical items: a wand, a speculum, candlesticks, a pentacle, a mandragore or alraun, along with talismans, philters, incenses, images, and amulets.

Exorcism: Purifying Raw Materials

Before any raw material becomes a working tool, it's purified, or exorcised, using salt, water, and incense, which together stand in for the four elements (earth, water, fire, and air). This step symbolically clears the material of outside influence before it's charged with the witch's own will and power, turning a plain object into a magical instrument. This purification process, referred to throughout as exorcism or "passing something through fire and water," comes before the charging step described for individual tools and jewels. See The Charging of Water and Flame for the full formula.

A Witch's Initial Supplies

Beyond the eight core tools, a well-stocked witch also keeps a working supply of everyday materials on hand. Buy these new rather than haggling over price, keep them apart from everyday use, and exorcise each one with fire and water before it's called into a spell:

  • Charcoal blocks for the thurible, self-igniting for preference, though the ordinary kind lit with a few drops of alcohol will do
  • Plain white Candle, six to nine inches long, in beeswax, tallow, or stearin; other colors are called for individually by spell
  • Salt, ground rock salt for preference, though table salt suits most exorcisms
  • Water, tap water for exorcisms and distilled water for philters and elixirs
  • White Masking Tape, handy for temporarily marking out a circle or triangle, especially for group work
  • Plain white Drawing Paper for talismans, or sheepskin parchment if it can be afforded
  • A Geometry Set (compass, protractor, ruler, and set square) for drawing talismans and sigils
  • A small sewing kit: Scissors, Needle, and Thread
  • A Dip Pen, kept as a pen of art, for drawing talismans and writing in the workbook
  • A good quality Paintbrush for painting runes onto magical tools
  • Thick black waterproof Ink, kept as an ink of art, which can be blended with a fitting herb, such as powdered basil for love or cinnamon for intelligence, when a spell calls for it. Tradition-minded witches often brew their own instead of buying it, following a recipe such as Gall Nut Ink or Peach Kernel Ink
  • Household Glue
  • Black and white paint, an enamel that takes well to ceramic, glass, and metal, used as a paint of art for inscribing runes on working tools
  • A small table set aside for practice, sometimes called the table of practice or altar
  • An assortment of jars and Bottle for philters and powders
  • Adhesive Labels to keep every preparation clearly identified
  • A sharp, white-hilted knife for preparatory cutting and engraving, the Bolline

These form only a starting stock. Build it up gradually as spells call for special herbs or extra supplies, and keep everything magical together, ideally in a dedicated cupboard, closet, or room.
