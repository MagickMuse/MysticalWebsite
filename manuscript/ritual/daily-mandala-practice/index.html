A spontaneous daily drawing practice that creates a visual record of your inner state over time. By drawing without intent and allowing your hand to guide you, you bypass conscious control and reveal what is truly present in your psyche.

Quantities

A pencil
Paper

Directions

  1. Drawing a mandala every day is a useful way to gain experience of the subject.
  2. Over a period of time the mandalas you draw will provide a fascinating record of what was going on in your life at the time you drew them.
  3. Carl Jung drew a mandala every day using this very approach.
  4. The Key Principle:
    The essential element of this practice is spontaneity. You are not creating art or trying to make something beautiful. You are allowing your unconscious mind to express itself through your hand.
  5. Preparation:
    Spend a few moments in a state of casual, calm awareness before picking up a pencil and starting to draw. It is important that you do not focus much attention on what you are doing.
  6. The Drawing Process:
    Draw spontaneously. Observe what your hand is doing with mild interest, but draw as little as possible with intent.
  7. Let Your Hand Lead: Allow your hand to draw anything it wants, in the manner it wants to.
  8. Your hand will decide what shapes to make, your hand will know when to put one pencil down and pick up another, your hand will decide what colors to use, and your hand will also decide when you are finished.
  9. Your Role: You are simply the witness.
  10. Watch with gentle curiosity as your hand creates the mandala.
  11. Do not judge, do not direct, do not plan.
  12. After Completion:
    You may want to examine your mandala right away to see what insights it provides. However, many practitioners find their mandalas easier to understand when there is a period of time between the drawing and interpretation. Consider putting your mandala aside for at least a few hours before analyzing it.
  13. Interpreting Your Mandala:
    Sometimes the meaning of the mandala will be obvious. Emotional States: If you are feeling depressed, your mandala is likely to contain darker colors and have jagged-looking edges to the designs. If you are feeling happy you will probably use lighter colors and your creation will seem joyful and high-spirited.
  14. Patterns Over Time: The real power of this practice emerges when you can look back at weeks or months of daily mandalas and see the patterns, cycles, and transformations in your psyche.
  15. Building the Practice:
    Commit to drawing one mandala every day. Keep them in a notebook or folder so you can review them periodically. You will be amazed at the insights that emerge when you can see the progression of your inner state over time. This is not about artistic skill. Some of your most revealing mandalas may be the simplest or least "artistic." What matters is the honesty of the expression.
