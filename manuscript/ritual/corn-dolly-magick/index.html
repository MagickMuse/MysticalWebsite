A Mabon poppet working that creates corn dollies to bring harvest blessings for the year. Different decorations serve different intentions: wedding attire for love/marriage, water rituals for fertility/abundance, stones and work symbols for money. Feed offerings throughout the year, then burn or bury at next autumn.

Quantities

  • Corn husks or dried corn stalks
  • For love/marriage: wedding decorations, wedding cake samples, mini champagne bottles, honeymoon pamphlets
  • For fertility/abundance: water for daily pouring
  • For money: stones for weighting, ribbons, work uniform miniatures or symbols (apron, pens, keys, etc.), offerings of honey, bread, beer, apples
  • Optional: acorns, late season herbs, colored leaves, palm leaves, sand, or succulent stems (use local materials)

Directions

  1. Make a corn dolly from corn husks or dried stalks.
  2. The dolly will hold the key to your harvest intention for the coming year.
  3. For love or marriage: Make two corn dollies and dress one like a bride and one like a groom (or two brides or two grooms, as appropriate).
  4. Leave each one offerings of wedding cakes (you can buy sample pieces at most bakeries) or perhaps leave small offerings of mini champagne bottles.
  5. Sing the bridal march to them and tuck pamphlets for honeymoon locations around them.
  6. If the new partner does not emerge in that year, burn them at the next autumn and start over again with new dolls.
  7. For fertility, abundance, or creativity: Pour water over the dolly once a day.
  8. In some parts of England, the last reaper carried the corn dolly home and along the way, other villagers threw water on the reaper and on the dolly or sometimes dunked the dolly and the reaper together in a nearby stream.
  9. For money: Weigh down the center of the dolly with stones and weave ribbons into its body to represent hard work.
  10. If you practice a profession with a specific uniform, dress the dolly in a miniature version of that uniform.
  11. You might want to give it an apron, pens, computer or typewriter keys, or any other small symbol that imitates the work you do.
  12. Ritually feed it over the year by leaving it small offerings of honey, bread, beer, and apples.
  13. Disposal: At the end of the year, dispose of the dolly with burial or burning.
  14. Give the dolly a place of honor for the year, then bury or burn it at the beginning of the next harvest to complete the cycle.
  15. Regional variations: Use what you have on hand.
  16. If you live in an area with lots of oak trees, use acorns, late season herbs, and colored leaves.
  17. If you live in an area with palm trees, use sand, palm tree leaves, or the broken stem of a succulent.
  18. You can also simply buy a package of cornhusks at a grocery store and stuff them with the other magickal symbols that serve your purpose.
