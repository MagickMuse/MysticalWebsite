An elaborate plant-gathering ritual recorded in Alexandria, Egypt during the first centuries of the Common Era. It combines personal purification, fumigation with pine resin and kyphi, prayer, milk libation, and a seed-and-honey offering returned to the earth to ensure harvested plants retain their full magickal potency.

Quantities

  • Natron, baking soda, or Dead Sea salt (enough to sprinkle over the harvest area)
  • Pine resin
  • 1 censer
  • Kyphi incense
  • Milk (enough for a libation)
  • 1 pure white linen cloth
  • 7 wheat seeds
  • 7 barley seeds
  • Honey (enough to blend with the seeds)

Directions

  1. Begin by performing personal cleansing spells over a period of several days.
  2. During this preparatory window, purify the body by abstaining from sexual activity and the consumption of meat.
  3. Once the cleansing period is complete, sprinkle natron over the area where the plant will be harvested.
  4. Natron is the natural salt central to the ancient Egyptian mummification process; baking soda is a close modern substitute, and Dead Sea salt may also be appropriate.
  5. Next, place pine resin inside a censer and use the smoke to fumigate the space surrounding the chosen plant, circling it three times.
  6. Burn kyphi incense and pour a libation of milk while simultaneously offering prayers and petitions related to the purpose of your working.
  7. When the moment is right, pull the plant from the earth while invoking the name of the deity to whom the herb is sacred, requesting their assistance with the goal of your spell.
  8. Wrap the uprooted plant carefully in a pure white linen cloth.
  9. Finally, blend seven wheat seeds and seven barley seeds together in honey.
  10. Place this mixture into the hole left behind by the uprooted plant and fill it with dirt, returning nourishment to the ground in exchange for what was taken.
