🕯️Playing with the Faeries

A playful outdoor Midsummer rite, child‑friendly and lighthearted. Welcome the fae with simple protections, quarter calls, gifts or crafts, bubbles, dancing, and shared treats.

Quantities

  • God and Goddess candles (gold/silver or yellow/white) in fire‑safe holders
  • Four quarter candles: yellow (East/Air), red (South/Fire), blue (West/Water), green (North/Earth)
  • Sage smudge or incense in a fire‑safe holder (for simple protection)
  • Bottles of bubbles for each participant
  • Cakes and ale: cheerful cookies and fruit juice (or mead for adults)
  • Altar table and cloth, plus a small table or cloth in the circle center for faery gifts
  • Optional gifts for the fae: small bowl of honey, pretty stones, rose petals, daisies, marigolds, carnations
  • Optional craft supplies: tissue or colored paper, pipe cleaners, scissors, tape/thread, white glue and brush, glitter or glitter glue; ribbon or thread for garland

Directions

  1. Setting: Noon, dawn, or dusk outdoors among greenery and flowers (Midsummer Day or Midsummer Eve).
  2. If with very young children, skip formal casting and do simple protections.
  3. Simple protection and cleansing:
  4. Light sage or incense and walk clockwise, wafting smoke to clear the space. Then let each person cleanse from head to feet (adults can assist little ones).
  5. Optional circle (very simple):
  6. Pass a flower around the circle, each saying: “With this flower, we cast the circle.”
  7. Quarter calls (invite kids to help; light each candle if safe):
  8. East/Air (yellow): “I call the guardian of the east, the power of air, upon which the faeries fly.”
  9. South/Fire (red): “I call the guardian of the south, the power of fire, which lights the faeries’ way.”
  10. West/Water (blue): “I call the guardian of the west, the power of water, like the magickal Midsummer dew where the fairies like to bathe.”
  11. North/Earth (green): “I call the guardian of the north, the power of earth, where the faeries love to play.”
  12. Invocations:
  13. “Lovely Aine, goddess of the faeries, come and join our circle today!” [Light goddess candle.]
  14. “Handsome Belenus, who causes the flowers to grow, come and join our circle today!” [Light god candle.]
  15. Gifts for the fae:
  16. If bringing gifts: Each person places theirs on the center cloth: “Here is a gift for you, faeries!”
  17. If crafting gifts: Make tissue‑paper flowers or star garlands together. Decorate lightly with glitter; place finished gifts on the faery table or hang where sunlight sparkles.
  18. Play with bubbles and dance:
  19. Give everyone bubbles. Leader: “We can make magick, too, with water and light and air! Come join us, faeries, and let us dance together!” Blow bubbles and dance merrily.
  20. Thanks and treats:
  21. “Thank you, faeries, for joining us today! Huzzah for the faeries of Midsummer Day!” Share cakes and juice, saving a little for the fae.
  22. “Faerie folk, please join us for some treats. Thank you lord and lady for your presence here today. Thank you lord and lady for helping us to play.” Snuff deity candles.
  23. Close (choose age‑appropriate):
  24. Quarter thanks (kid version): “Thank you, north! Thank you, west! Thank you, south! Thank you, east! Goodbye! Goodbye!” Snuff quarter candles.
  25. Circle close (formal): “The circle is open, but never broken. Merry meet, merry part, and merry meet again!”
  26. Or with very young children: “We’re done! That was fun!”
