A learned hymn sung three times to consecrate sacred space, purify the reader's inner self, and invite helpful spirits. Rite 6 of Book I.

Quantities

1 pen, 1 sheet of paper, 1 bowl for spring water and salt, matches, 3 white candles, myrrh incense with a censer or heat-safe holder, cinnamon for dusting the hands, spring water, salt, a small branch of yew needles.

Directions

  1. Learn this hymn by heart in advance, ideally by the evening six days before the new moon, so that it can be sung from memory on this, the third night before.
  2. As usual, wash thrice, dust with cinnamon, draw the sigil, and light three white candles and myrrh incense. This time also set a small bowl of spring water and salt to the left of the sigil, and a small branch of yew needles to the right.
  3. Breathe yourself to stillness. Once centered, take one of the lit candles and tip it over the bowl so that a little hot wax drips in, saying as you do: "Let fire and water blend; / Let all impurities end; / Let the veil rend, / As the song I sing is sung."
  4. Dip the yew branch in the water and asperge (sprinkle) the four corners of the room.
  5. Sing the hymn three times, feeling the sandy path beneath your feet and sawgrass brushing your hands, smelling sea salt, hearing distant thunder, and visualizing the crossroads with its torches, pole, and masks. With each verse, picture the song as a banner of fire streaming from your lips, splitting and weaving into a helix of blue flame around you. This hymn carries its own simple traditional melody and may be sung solo or as a round:
    "Ouranian, Einalian, Chthonian / Everlasting Goddess; / Ouranian, Einalian, Chthonian / Everlasting Goddess; / Ouranian, Einalian, Chthonian / Everlasting Goddess / Of mine: / See within this circle, gathered in your honor; / See within the circle three black dogs; / See them lift their muzzles to the black moon, howling: / Howling down your favor and love. / Howling down your favor and love. / Howling down your favor and love. / Ouranian, Einalian, Chthonian / Everlasting Goddess / Of mine."
  6. When the hymn ends, let the stillness linger; sit for several minutes in the presence of the three flames, noticing whether the space, and you within it, feels different now that it has been consecrated. Extend your senses, and calmly acknowledge whatever you notice, whether sound, sensation, image, or nothing at all.
  7. When ready to end the vigil, say aloud: "Thank you for your company. Now go in peace." Burn the sigil and blow out the candles.

Context

The sixth of nine nights leading to the new moon dedication, three days before it, in which the reader sings a learned hymn of consecration — celebrating Hekate as celestial (Ouranian), oceanic (Einalian), and earthly (Chthonian) — to purify herself and her working space and to invite helpful presences into it.

Recommended Reading

The Hymn to the Waning Moon from the Greek Magical Papyri, a millennia-old invocation depicting Hekate in her most fearsome lunar aspect.
