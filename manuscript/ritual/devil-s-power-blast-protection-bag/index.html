A conjure bag packed with devil-named botanicals and curios that calls on the devil's power for protection against lesser malicious spirits. ⚠️ Devil's Power Blast Protection Bag is a Hoodoo working. Hoodoo is a closed practice meant only for those within the originating culture.

Quantities

Devil's pod (Trapa bicornis)
Devil's dung (asafetida)
Devil's bit
Devil's nettle (yarrow)
3 devil's claws
5 devil's joy blossoms (vinca)
9 devil's shoestrings of equal length
Conjure bag
Flying Devil Oil
Devil candle, in whatever color feels most protective (typically black, green, or red)

Directions

  1. Fill a conjure bag with devil's pod, devil's dung (asafetida), devil's bit, devil's nettle (yarrow), three devil's claws, five devil's joy blossoms (vinca), and nine devil's shoestrings of equal length.
  2. Anoint the bag with Flying Devil Oil.
  3. Carve and dress a devil candle in whatever color feels most protective.
  4. Activate the bag by placing it beside the candle.

Lore

Frequent references to the devil in folk magick do not make witchcraft a form of Satanism. A wholly malevolent devil locked in battle with the Creator is a Christian idea, built on ancient Persian beliefs about warring forces of good and evil; few other spiritual systems recognize deities divided so starkly. The figures Christian authorities labeled the devil are usually one of the less sympathetic angels, a crossroads spirit, or a horned spirit of primal male generative power. Each can be dangerous and must be handled with care, but none is inherently evil, hostile, or bent on stealing souls, and many, such as Elegba or Maximon, show great kindness toward people when they choose to. Without the fear of an eternal evil, and because crossroads spirits tend to be tricksters, devil imagery in folk magick is often playful: the Lucky Devil rather than an evil Satan.

In magickal terms, the devil is seen as the biggest and fiercest spirit around, an ally worth having against lesser malicious spirits. This bag calls on that power.
