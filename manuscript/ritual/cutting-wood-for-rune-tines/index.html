A ceremonial method for selecting and cutting wood from a living tree to fashion into rune tines, honoring the wight of the tree with invocation and thanksgiving.

Quantities

Rune knife
A living tree suited to the tine's purpose

Directions

  1. Settle on a clear talismanic design, then scout the area for a tree whose sympathetic qualities suit the tine's aim.
  2. Choose a favorable time: dawn, noon, or twilight for most workings, or midnight for negative rituals and curses.
  3. Select a branch, twig, or root bending toward the quarter or eighth of the heavens sympathetic to the purpose, since its orientation channels the corresponding forces into the material.
  4. Return to the tree armed with the rune knife; stand to the north or east of the trunk facing outward and perform the Hamarssetning or another protective opening rite, envisioning the whole tree within the holy stead.
  5. Position yourself before the branch, twig, or root to be cut, and speak the invocation to the tree's wight, naming the tree and the runes to be used.
  6. Cut the chosen portion while humming or singing the names and galdrar of those runes throughout.
  7. Give thanks to the wight of the tree with a thanksgiving verse.
  8. Trim and prepare the branch to receive the stave forms; load the tine at once, or save it for later loading in your usual ve.

Adaptation

This ritual process may be adapted for the selection and preparation of other materials to be fashioned into holy objects by the runic art. The essential elements (protective rite, invocation of the material's wight, ceremonial cutting with galdr, and thanksgiving) apply regardless of the medium.
