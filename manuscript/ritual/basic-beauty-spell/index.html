A preparatory altar working performed before creating any herbal beauty preparations. This spell consecrates the herbs and aligns the practitioner's intention with the plant world's life force, setting the energetic foundation for all practical beauty workings that follow.

Quantities

  • Stick incense
  • 1 cup water
  • White candle
  • Powerful image (a deity you resonate with or an aesthetically pleasing figure)
  • Small white cloth
  • Herbs you will be using in your preparation

Directions

  1. Set up your altar by placing each material at one of the four cardinal points: incense at one quarter, water at the next, the candle at the third, and your chosen image at the fourth.
  2. The center of the space is left open for the work itself.
  3. Lay the white cloth at the center and distribute your herbs across it.
  4. White holds all colors of the spectrum and stands for clarity of purpose.
  5. Lower your hands over the herbs and draw yourself into a calm, receptive state.
  6. Breathe steadily, and as you do, feel into the living relationship between your body and the plant world.
  7. Let genuine gratitude surface for the vitality and power your herbs carry, then release that feeling downward through your palms and into the plant material.
  8. As you continue breathing, call to mind streams of light moving through you from crown to root.
  9. This light is the seat of your inner beauty, something you project outward for it to expand and return.
  10. Let your intention sharpen until it is completely clear, then speak it aloud in whatever combination resonates:
  11. I come to the altar of power seeking beauty
    I am that which I seek
    Beauty is within me
    Beauty surrounds me
    What I seek I have discovered within myself
    May it be pleasing to all
    May it be deeply felt
    I am the source and keeper of this beauty
    I radiate it outward for the benefit of all beings
  12. Close with: So mote it be.
  13. Your herbs are now consecrated and charged.
  14. Move into the practical work that follows using these herbs, preparing in small batches to keep preparations fresh and potent.
