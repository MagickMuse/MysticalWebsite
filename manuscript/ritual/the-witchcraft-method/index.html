The Witchcraft Method

Some feel that science and magick are antithetical, but scientists make great witches. In a way, both witches and scientists use the Scientific Method. The Witchcraft Method mirrors this approach.

Step 1: State the Need

This could be a spell, or a small part in a more extensive ritual incorporating many elements including candles, quarter calls, and the like. "Money" is acceptable, but better results come from more specific intention: "I need money for a new car."

Whenever possible, state the need in the positive rather than negative. "I don't have money for a new car" is not as magickally effective as "I need money for a new car." The theory is that the mind does not grasp negative words when doing magick, which can impact the working.

The best wording, however, is "I have money for a new car." You are acting as though the goal has already been attained. This leaves room in the universe to provide what you need, or something better.

Step 2: Conduct Research

You will need correspondences for the stated need: plants associated with prosperity, stones for money, and the like. Write them all out first, then narrow the focus from there.

This method serves two purposes: it makes it easier to see patterns, and it has been proven that more information is retained when handwritten rather than typed. Use blue ink for a memory boost, as it differs from black typeface and stands out in the mind for easier recall.

Step 3: Write the Spell or Ritual

First, decide how complicated the working should be. Is this a simple issue with a simple solution, like a candle spell? Or is this a complicated matter that requires an in-depth ritual to build the power needed?

Step 4: Do the Thing

The minute you decide a spell needs to happen in Step 1, the clock starts ticking. Every moment you spend researching materials, choosing the working, and shopping for materials required is energy that goes toward the intended outcome. The more power, the more likely the intended result.

Step 5: Make Observations

The data you collect during and after the spell gets recorded in your Book of Shadows, just like a scientist would record findings. "A week later, I won the office birthday pool" or "thirteen days later, still no results."

Even if the spell does not work, it can be illuminating to look at the potential reasons why. Perhaps you look at the time the working started and find out the moon was void of course, or between signs. That can muddy the magickal waters, so try not to do workings when the moon is between signs. Your magickal calendar or ephemeris might have it marked as "Moon v/c 6:04 p.m."

Step 6: Form Your Conclusion

If your intention came to pass the way it was designed, excellent. If it did not, many factors could have contributed to the failure of the ritual.

Before you declare it null, wait for at least a full lunar cycle (twenty-eight days) for the intention to come to pass. You do not want to decide a working failed before it has a chance to work.

Step 7: Communicate and Replicate

The Witches' Pyramid says: "To Know, To Will, To Dare, To Keep Silent."

Exclaiming to your circle of friends that you have completed the "coolest spell ever" is not the best idea. It can introduce doubt into the spell from outsiders, from yourself if they are not 100 percent supportive, or from magickal practitioner friends who do not see the outcome the same way you do. Wait before declaring success.

When communicating the results of a spell, await the full results or 28 days before declaring it a failure. Spell work takes time.

Unintended complications from magick can happen. For example, you needed cash, but after the money came in, an unexpected bill came up. Perhaps in addition to keeping magickal records, you want to start an online journal, a blog, or even a channel to speak to other witches about your experiences. Being a magickal practitioner can feel lonely if you do not know anyone. If you feel that way, there are thousands of others like you who feel isolated and could benefit from your experiences.
