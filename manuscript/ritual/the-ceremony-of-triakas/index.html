🌅Sunset📅Last Day of the Month🔱Feast of Hekate

A monthly Hellenic household ceremony honoring Hekate and the ancestors with a shared portion of the evening meal.

Quantities

Red wine, for libations during the Prayer to the Ancestors
A complete dinner shared among the household
A separate portion of the same meal, dished up as an offering for the dead
Optional: an outdoor crossroads or place at the edge of the property for the dead's portion

Directions

  1. Before sunset on the 29th or 30th day of the lunar month, recite the Invocation of Helios and Hekate while preparing the evening meal.
  2. Dish up a separate portion of the meal for the dead and place it on the table alongside the household's portions.
  3. Recite the Prayer to the Ancestors over the offering, pouring libations of wine.
  4. Close with the Hymn to Hekate, or, if taking the offering to an outdoor crossroads, the Orphic Hymn to Hekate.
  5. Sit down and share the meal with the household.
  6. After dinner, carry the dead's portion outside and leave it for nature to receive; never discard or eat it.

This monthly rite, the Triakas (tree-AH-kas), is dedicated to Hekate and the dead. In ancient times it was marked by women's rites known as the Hekatea Deipna (the Dinners of Hekate), in which food offerings were left at three-way crossroads or family graves. Modern Hellenes who cannot reach a true crossroads adapt the practice by reserving a special portion of the household dinner for the dead and setting it on the table alongside the living.

Not to be confused with the burial ceremony of the same name.

Ceremony Script

Begin before sunset on the 29th or 30th day of the lunar month, while the evening meal is still being prepared. Stand before the household altar or hearth and speak the Invocation of Helios and Hekate, asking the sun and the goddess of the crossroads to bless the offerings about to be prepared and to keep evil away from the home:

Lord Helios (HEH-lee-os), sacred fire,

O' spear of Enodia (eh-NO-dee-ah),

which She wields as She journeys to Olympos (oh-LEEM-pos)

and while travelling amongst the sacred crossroads of the Earth.

She who is crowned with oaken wreaths

and coils of serpents,

Bless on this last day of the month

the offerings which I am about to prepare for the venerable ancestors

and keep all evil away from us.

Finish preparing the meal. When it is ready, dish up a separate portion of the same food specifically for the dead and place it on the dinner table alongside the household's portions. Stand over the offering and recite the Prayer to the Ancestors, pouring libations of wine (called khoês, KHO-ess) in their memory:

I honour the Venerable Ancestors with my prayers.

O' Glorious Founding Fathers,

I invoke your illustrious name.

It is through you that we have seen our Sacred earth,

It is you who guide us as we follow the road of Honour and Virtue

May we be worthy of the glory of your generations.

To hold unfadingly your living memory with great Fortitude!

I pour libations [khoês, KHO-ess] in memory of you,

strengthening, with this wine,

our sacred bond!

May you always be a support and a solid foundation

to our household, O' Immortal Ancestral Souls!

Close the ceremony with the Hymn to Hekate, drawn from the Hellenic magical papyri, addressing her as three-faced Selene (seh-LEH-neh), the four-named goddess of the four roads, danced among the Kharites (KHAH-ree-tess), Dike (DEE-keh), and the threads of the Moirae (MEE-rye):

Come to me, O' beloved lady, three faced Selene (seh-LEH-neh),

listen, with kindness, to my sacred chant.

Youthful, exulted Jewel of the Night, bringing light to mortals;

child of early dawn, riding joyfully on fierce bulls,

O' Queen, driving the chariot along the path of Helios (HEH-lee-os);

In triple form, You dance, three-headed, with the three Kharites (KHAH-ree-tess),

revelling with the stars, and Dike (DEE-keh) and the threads of the Moirae (MEE-rye);

Klotho (KLO-thoh), and Lakhesis (lah-KHEH-sees) as well as Atropos (AH-tro-pos), and Persephone (per-SEH-fo-nee), Megaira (meh-GHY-rah)

and Allecto (ah-LEK-toh).

Multi-formed, your hands, armed with the dreaded dark lamps,

your mane of terrifying serpents shakes upon your brow,

from your mouth is heard the roaring of bulls.

Solitary, night-crier, bull-faced, bull-headed,

you have the eyes of bulls, you have the voice of dogs,

you hide amongst lions by taking their shape.

Your ankles are wolf-shaped, fierce dogs are your friends,

Hence they call you Hekate (heh-KAH-teh), illustrious Mene (MEH-neh),

you dart through the air as arrow-shooting Artemis (AR-teh-mees),

four-faced goddess, of the four names, of the four roads.

Artemis, Persephone, deer-shooting, night-shining, triple striking,

triple sounding, triple headed, triple named Selene; three-pronged,

triple-faced, triple-necked and of the triple roads,

in triple containers you keep the eternal flame,

You who rules over the triple decades and frequents the three road crossings.

Come, graciously hear my call,

You who oversees the wide world of the night,

darkness voyaging Goddess; famed, easy-birthing, bull-faced, horned,

mother of Gods and men, Nature's All-Mother.

You frequent Olympos (oh-LEEM-pos), traversing the broad and boundless abyss,

You are the beginning and the end, You alone rule all.

Hail Goddess, smile as You hear this praise,

Your fragrance floats upon the breeze, arrow-shooting child of Zeus (ZOOS),

heavenly, Limeniti (lee-meh-NEE-tee, title of Artemis),

You who roams the mountains, nocturnal Goddess of the netherworld,

You of the chaste darkness, quiet and frightening,

You alone hold the feast of the graves, Nyx (NEEKS), Erebos (EH-reh-bos),

wide Khaos (KHAH-os), O' inescapable Anagke (ah-NAHN-keh), O' lavish Moira (MEE-rah),

O' Erinye (eh-ree-NEE-yeh), O' Dike (DEE-keh).

When the hymn is finished, sit down with the household and share the meal in the company of the Ancestors. After dinner, carry the dead's portion outside (to a true three-way crossroads if available, or to a quiet place at the edge of the property) and leave it for nature to receive. The portion is never thrown away or eaten; it must remain outdoors until the next Noumenia (noo-meh-NEE-ah) ceremony begins.

If placing the offering at an outdoor crossroads, the Orphic Hymn to Hekate from the Full Moon Ceremony may be recited in place of (or in addition to) the closing PGM hymn while setting the portion down.

Notes

The Invocation of Helios and Hekate is adapted from a surviving fragment of Sophocles' lost play Rhizotomoi: "Lord Helios, sacred fire, who is the governing power of Hekate of the roads, accompanying her from lofty Olympos to the sacred crossroads of the earth, crowned with an oaken wreath and coiled serpents around her shoulders." The closing Hymn to Hekate is selected from the Hellenic magical papyri (PGM IV 2785-2870). Many of the figures named alongside Hekate in the closing hymn (Klotho, Lakhesis, Atropos, Megaira, Allecto) are tagged here through their parent collectives, the Moirae and the Erinyes, since they do not have standalone Entity entries.
