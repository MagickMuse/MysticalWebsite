A guide to crafting dream pillows, small herb-filled sachets placed under the cheek or beside the pillow to stimulate, enhance, or direct dreaming. Dream pillows can serve many purposes: basic dream stimulation, incubating specific types of dreams (erotic, financial), sleep enhancement, nightmare prevention, or spiritual protection during sleep. Adjust the botanicals to match the intended goal. Crafting the pillow is itself a spell: charge and consecrate materials, focus your intention while working, and treat every knot as an opportunity for a knot spell.

Standard size: 12" x 8" (30 cm x 20 cm), filled with approximately 8–10 oz (300 g) of dried herbs. A small sachet works just as well. One of the Dream Pillow methods.

Quantities

  • Two matching pieces of soft fabric (muslin or similar natural material)
  • 8–10 oz dried botanicals chosen for your intention, ground to a fine powder
  • Optional: essential or fragrance oils
  • Optional: small charms or harder roots

Directions

  1. Cut two matching pieces of soft, smooth fabric. Sew three sides together to form a bag. Fill from the open end with your botanical blend, then sew the final side closed.
    Construction tips:
  2. Botanicals must be completely dry before sewing in. Wet or damp herbs will rot and produce an acrid smell that causes nightmares rather than pleasant dreams.
  3. Unless very soft, grind all ingredients to a fine powder. Rose petals are preferred over buds, which are pointy. Bay leaves are powerful dream enhancers but must be crushed first or they will protrude and poke you.
  4. If adding essential or fragrance oils, blend them into the dried herbs thoroughly using a wooden chopstick or twig. Let everything dry fully before sewing the pillow closed.
  5. If adding hard charms or roots, place them at the center of the pillow and surround them with softer material so nothing sharp presses against your face.
  6. The fragrance must be pleasing or at minimum neutral to you. Discomfort will keep you awake. Be aware that in the dark, deprived of other stimulation, a scent will register more intensely than in daylight. Adjust accordingly.
