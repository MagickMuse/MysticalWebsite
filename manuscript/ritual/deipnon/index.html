Ritual Grimoire

🌑Deipnon

◇Tradition
Greek
◇Solitary or Group
Solitary
◇Form
Offering RiteVerbal Working
◇Setting
Outdoor
◇Category
Ritual
◇Difficulty

◇Quantities
Wine: small bottle, for libation Milk: small bottle, poured in a circle Honey: a drop, offered at closing Cinnamon: a dusting, for the hands before knocking Simple meal: lentils, eggs, fish, garlic, or a loaf of bread (one or more) Knife: the Tartaric Machaira (Infernal Knife) Black Candle: 3, set in dog skulls Bone: 3 dog skulls
◇Directions
1. At midnight on the new moon, bring wine, milk, honey, cinnamon, and a simple meal to a secluded forking path. 2. Dust the hands with cinnamon and knock three times at the threshold of the path. Await a sign. 3. Raise the hands and call aloud to Hekate. 4. Pour milk in a circle around the working space. 5. Use the Tartaric Machaira to carve a circle in the earth, then cross it with lines connecting all cardinal directions, overworld, underworld, within and without. 6. Throw the blade into the center of the circle. 7. Set three dog skulls with black candles along the perimeter of the circle. Light each candle, invoking Kerberos with each. 8. Invoke Hekate by her twenty-seven sacred names. 9. Kneel within the circle and chant: "Ela mazi mou, sto amorpho scotadi" until her presence is felt. 10. Speak from the heart. 11. When the working feels complete, offer the meal with incantations. 12. Blow out each candle in turn, dismissing Kerberos with each extinguishing. 13. Pull the blade from the earth to unbind the circle. 14. Draw a line across the circle's perimeter with the blade to close the space. 15. Depart without looking back. Thank the woods at the edge of the path.
◇Description
A monthly Hekatean offering rite performed at the new moon, in which the Devotee brings a meal of traditional foods (lentils, eggs, fish, garlic, or bread) to a secluded crossroads or forking path, invokes Hekate by her twenty-seven sacred names, shares communion with her presence, and makes libations of wine, milk, and honey before departing without looking back.
