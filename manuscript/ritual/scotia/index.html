Scotia Sigil

Meaning: Dark One. A darkness-communion practice combining witchbreath with the Scotia sigil, used to navigate the dark, conceal objects, induce dreamless sleep, or draw the spirits of darkness. Day 19 of the Hekate Devotee cycle.

Quantities

  • A piece of paper, for writing the sigil

Directions

  1. Cover your left eye with the palm of your left hand, and your right eye with the palm of your right hand, literally invoking Scotia - the Darkness.
  2. Practice witchbreath: slowly inhale for a four-count, hold the breath for a four-count, release it for a four-count, and remain empty of breath for a four-count. Repeat, contemplating the four seasons of the earth and the four corresponding seasons of life. This sustains and strengthens the Devotee, allowing Hekate's darkness to enfold, fill, inspire, and heal.
  3. Trace the Scotia sigil gesturally over both eyes to safely navigate dark territory and quickly find what is hiding, hidden, or lost.
  4. Mark an object with this sigil so that it will avoid notice.
  5. An insomniac who places this sigil beneath her pillow will find dreamless sleep.
  6. Sit in darkness with both fists enclosing this sigil to attract the spirits which abide in darkness, which will gently devour the Devotee's outermost, mundane shell, just as ants devour the outer coating of the peony in spring, allowing it to bloom.

Arcana

Hekate is present in the darkness not because she wishes to hide, but because she is a Prodigy of Night. "Prodigy" has three meanings: first, a miraculous child; second, a marvelous embodiment of a certain virtue; and last, as a natural wonder. All three apply to Hekate, who is the primal Nightchild (and as such, a sibling to Death and the Fates), the true embodiment of liminalities and magic, and a wonder of timeless, tireless earthforce that defies all limits and definitions.

Hekate is Melaina (the Black One). Therefore, to be in the dark is to be in her; to be in actual contact with her sacred, secret self; to be intimately connected with her divine nature. Her Devotees for this reason draw energy from residing, as she does, in the gloom. For this reason they do not despise the darkness, but seek it out. And the fear they feel, if any, is holy, for it derives from the divine paradox of the darkness's double nature: it is both formless void and the embodiment of her ineffable form.

To be in darkness, then, and mindful of being so, is to partake in Hekate's holy shadow-substance and to sacramentally engage with the ever-living Queen of Night who accompanies, advances, terminates, and renews everything and everyone.

Thracian images of Hekate are unique in their portrayal of her as a mother bearing a child. The image has value: as a mother nurses an infant with her life-force in the form of milk, so Hekate nurtures her Devotees with her own life-force, which is shadow and flame. Hekate's flame is the Formless Fire which pours continually from her sacred crux, enlivening, animating and purifying her worshippers. Her shadow is the fertile, soil-dark earth-energy that emanates from the Underworld. This chthonic substance, like black milk from a black goat, strengthens and vitalizes the Devotee, increasing her earth-power, earth-wisdom, and earth-sight, thus revealing to her the true nature of life, spirit, and magic - which is the mediating force between all things.
