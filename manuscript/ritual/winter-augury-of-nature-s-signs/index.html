Traditional winter augury practice reading omens in clouds, bare branches, meteors, bird flights, and snow formations. This comprehensive divination method uses multiple natural phenomena to answer questions or reveal hidden knowledge.

Quantities

  • Outdoor location with sky view
  • Journal and pen
  • Warm clothing and blanket
  • Thermos of hot beverage (optional)
  • Field guide to winter birds (optional)
  • Flashlight for night observations

Directions

  1. Preparation:
  2. Formulate a clear question or open yourself to general guidance.
  3. Choose your time: dawn, dusk, or night for different energies.
  4. Dress warmly and bring your journal.
  5. Find an outdoor location with a clear view of sky and land.
    Cloud Reading (Nephomancy):
  6. Winter clouds are stark against the sky; look for shapes and movements
  7. Fast-moving clouds: quick changes coming
  8. Slow clouds: patient approach needed
  9. Dark clouds: hidden factors, caution
  10. Bright clouds: clarity, positive omens
  11. Animal shapes: consult animal symbolism
  12. Direction of movement: where energy flows
    Bare Branch Patterns:
  13. Observe the skeletal structure of winter trees against sky
  14. Look for natural symbols formed by branch intersections
  15. Runes, letters, numbers, crosses, Y-shapes (choices)
  16. The tree species adds additional meaning
  17. Note which direction you face when viewing
    Meteor Observations:
  18. Winter nights offer clear viewing for shooting stars
  19. Direction of streak indicates flow of fortune
  20. Brightness shows intensity of coming events
  21. Make a wish or note the timing for major workings
  22. Multiple meteors: significant changes ahead
    Bird Augury:
  23. Winter birds are easier to spot against bare branches
  24. Flight direction: toward you (approaching), away (leaving)
  25. Number of birds: count carries numerological meaning
  26. Species: crows (magick), cardinals (vitality), jays (communication)
  27. Calls and songs: messages to decode
  28. Solitary vs. flock: individual or community focus
    Snowbank Formations:
  29. Wind-sculpted drifts reveal shapes and symbols
  30. Animal-like formations: consult animal meanings
  31. Geometric patterns: structured approach needed
  32. Chaotic drifts: embrace flexibility
  33. Snow depth: how buried or visible your issue is
    Recording Your Observations:
    Note date, time, weather, moon phase, your question, and all signs observed. Patterns emerge over repeated practice throughout the winter season.
