🛡️Garlic Wreath at the Sickbed

◇Solitary or Group
Either
◇Form
Charm
◇Setting
Indoor
◇Method
Herbal MagickProtection
◇Category
Spell
◇Difficulty
Beginner
◇Quantities
- One large bundle of fresh garlic flowers, or two whole heads of garlic broken into peeled cloves - Length of cotton string or strong thread for the wreath - A second handful of cloves or flowers reserved for rubbing - Optional: a small bowl to catch crushed bits
◇Directions
1. Prepare the room in early evening, before the sleeper goes to bed. 2. Latch every window securely. 3. Take a handful of the garlic flowers or crushed cloves and rub them across each window sash so that any breath of air entering the room carries the garlic scent. 4. Rub the same wisp across the door jamb above, below, and on each side. 5. Rub around the fireplace if there is one. 6. Do not skip an opening. 7. The seal is only as good as its weakest edge. 8. Next, weave the wreath. 9. Thread cloves or whole flowering tops onto the cotton string and tie the ends so that the wreath sits comfortably around the throat without binding. 10. Set it aside while the sleeper makes ready for bed. 11. When the sleeper is in bed, place the wreath yourself around their neck. 12. Speak plainly that this is for their protection and that they are not to disturb it, and that even if the room feels close they are not to open the window or door until morning. 13. Keep the wreath whole through the night. 14. In the morning, if all is well, the wreath may be taken outside and buried, burned in a clean fire, or returned to running water. 15. Replace nightly for as long as the threat persists. 16. If the wreath is found broken, scattered, or removed in the night, repeat the working immediately and consider stronger measures.
◇Description
A folk apotropaic working for a sleeper who is being visited at night, drained of energy, or harassed by an unseen presence. Garlic is rubbed across every opening of the bedroom and then woven into a wreath worn at the throat until morning. The work is done in seriousness. Silence and obedience are part of the rite.
