🌙Saint Camillus Addiction Release Ritual

A candle ritual invoking Saint Camillus to help overcome addiction. Camillus himself was a gambling addict who lost everything before rebuilding his life through service to the sick.

Quantities

  • 1 white candle
  • Cash (whatever amount feels meaningful to you)
  • A symbolic item representing your addiction (casino chips, playing cards, racing forms, lottery tickets, dice, a bottle, etc.)

Directions

  1. Gather your materials: a white candle, some cash, and an object that symbolizes the addiction you wish to overcome.
  2. Set up your space with the white candle in the center and the cash and addiction symbol nearby.
  3. As you light the candle, speak to Saint Camillus.
  4. Tell him about your struggle; he understands addiction intimately, having lost everything to gambling before finding redemption.
  5. Ask him for strength to overcome your addiction and rebuild your life as he rebuilt his.
  6. Let the candle burn completely; do not extinguish it.
  7. Once the candle has burned down, give the money to the first beggar, charity, or needy person who asks for help.
  8. This act of charity mirrors Camillus's own transformation from desperate beggar to servant of the needy.
  9. This ritual can be repeated as often as needed.
  10. The addiction symbol may be disposed of after the ritual, or kept and used again until you no longer need it.

Background

Saint Camillus knows addiction from the inside. As a young man, he was a professional gambler and cardsharp who lost absolutely everything—including the shirt on his back—and wound up begging in the streets. His transformation from rock bottom to sainthood makes him the perfect intercessor for those struggling with addiction.

Best Timing

  • 14 July (Saint Camillus's feast day)
  • Saturday (traditional day for banishing work)
  • Waning moon (for releasing and letting go)

Why It Works

The giving away of cash at the end transforms the petitioner from someone in need to someone who gives. This mirrors Camillus's own journey from beggar to servant of the sick and needy. The act breaks the cycle of addiction by embodying generosity and service to others.
