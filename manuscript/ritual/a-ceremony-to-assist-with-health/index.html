Close by saluting the Healing Gods together, libate red wine, and seal the working.

This ceremony draws on the great physicians of the Universe: Apollon, the healer Asklepios, the much-desired Hygeia, and Telesphoros. The opening epigram from Hippocrates' On Diet (4.87), "indeed prayer is proper but action is also good," frames the ceremony's central rule: this working is never a substitute for medical science. Asklepios is invoked to help diagnose the cause of disease in the body, Apollon to understand its psycho-spiritual causes, Hygeia to preserve health and keep a calm mind, and Telesphoros, added during recuperation, to complete the healing and lead mortals back into the light.

Ceremony Script

Perform the ceremony early in the morning, after bathing and before the day's obligations begin. Set the wine, frankincense manna, herbs, olive branch, and laurel branch on the altar within easy reach.

Open with the Invocation of the Healing Gods, calling Apollon, Asklepios, Much-Desired Hygeia, and (during recuperation) Telesphoros to terminate disease and restore health to body and mind:

Come, O' Healing Gods,

Apollon (ah-POL-lon), Asklepios (ah-SKLEH-pee-os), Much Desired Hygeia (hee-YEH-ah), (and you, Telesphoros, teh-LEH-sfo-ros).

May you terminate dire diseases

granting health to our body and mind.

Libate red wine.

Take the olive and laurel branches in hand and recite the Paian to Apollon and Asklepios, the hymn that Amphepoleites of Macedon wrote on the instructions of the god. The phrase Ie Paian (EE-eh PY-an) recurs as a refrain and may be sung or chanted with feeling at each occurrence:

We praise the silver-bowed Delian (DEH-lee-an), Zeus's (ZOOS) beautiful quiver,

Silently holy and compassionate, Ie Paian (EE-eh PY-an).

Like the Aglaion ÊRnos (ah-GLY-on AIR-nos, the Athenian youth),

the supplicant pledges with olive and laurel branch in hand, Ie Paian.

Calling upon the leader of the Muses,

singing pure hymns to the far-shooting son of Leto (LEH-toh), Ie Paian.

Asklepios, kindly youth,

defender against any born disease, warder of mortal woes, Ie O Ie Paian.

To Kentauros (ken-TOW-ros), the pain warder of humans,

You taught all the secret healing arts on the peaks of Mt. Pelias (PEH-lee-as), Ie Paian.

Child of Koronis (ko-ROH-nees), mild man and modest spirit, Ie Paian.

Adorning the Hellenic lances

of the young soldiers of the Fleet-footed Machaeis (mah-KHY-ees), Ie Paian.

Children of Epione (eh-pee-OH-nee) and noble Hygeia,

Iaso (ee-AH-so), Akeso (ah-KEH-so) and Aigle (EYE-gleh) and Panacea (pah-nah-KEH-ah), Ie Paian.

Khaire (KHY-reh), illustrious spirit, great benefit of mortals, Ie, O Ie Paian.

You are gentle, Makar (MAH-kar, Blessed),

eliminate dread diseases, Ie, O Ie Paian.

Come Blessed Paian (PY-an) and grant to your supplicant [insert name here]

much-desired health.

Libate red wine.

Light the frankincense manna and recite the Hymn to Asklepios, asking him as the honoured child of Phoibos (FY-bos) Apollon to dispel disease and grant the supplicant an end to pain:

Asklepios (ah-SKLEH-pee-os), Healer of all,

who dispels the diseased

sufferings of people.

Mighty, Soothing, Healer of disease

honoured child of Phoibos (FY-bos) Apollon (ah-POL-lon)

terminate dire illness.

Come O' Blessed Saviour,

and grant health to your supplicant [insert name]

and an end to pain.

Libate red wine.

Add the chosen dried herbs to the frankincense manna and recite the Hymn to Hygeia, asking the goddess of health to save the supplicant from ill-fated disease and to fill the home with joy and flourishing crafts:

Come, O' Blessed Hygeia (hee-YEH-ah)

Much desired, beloved Queen of all.

All mortal diseases are destroyed by You,

and from You every home blossoms and is filled with joy,

Even the crafts flourish from you,

For it is You that people desire, O' Queen

Ever-flourishing, much-praised, repose of mortals.

Come, Goddess, without You

all else is futile,

save your supplicant [insert name]

from ill-fated disease.

Libate red wine.

If the working is being performed during the recuperation phase, add the Hymn to Telesphoros next, asking the imperishable young shoot of Asklepios to lift mortals from weariness and lead them back into the beautiful light:

Telesphoros (teh-LEH-sfo-ros), we praise your virtues,

imperishable young shoot of Asklepios,

all-wise son of the god who heals all pain,

illustrious Daimon (DY-mon),

Lift the generations of mortals from their weariness,

repelling painful disease.

The long-haired Paian (PY-an)

is overjoyed to have you, young shoot, O' Telesphoros,

Many times, you lead mortals into the beautiful light

after baneful illness.

Khaire (KHY-reh), Telesphoros, precious healer

bring laughter, befitting the sacred joy

to our faces.

Close the ceremony by saluting the Healing Gods together and asking them to banish disease to the ends of the Earth:

Khairete (KHY-reh-teh), Healing Gods,

avert from us disease and ailments

and banish them to the ends of the Earth.

Libate red wine and seal the working with ΓΕΝΟΙΤΟ (yeh-NEE-toh, it is done).

Notes

This ceremony acts in conjunctive support of medical care and is never to be used as a substitute for medical science. The Paian to Apollon and Asklepios is attributed in the source to Amphepoleites of Macedon, who wrote it on the instructions of the god. The Hymn to Telesphoros is a surviving devotional hymn to a deity who appears mainly in later antiquity as a companion to Asklepios; he completes the recuperation that Asklepios and his attendants begin. Telesphoros is typically depicted as a young child wearing a cloak or mantle, and in Roman art is shown sitting at the feet of Asklepios. He is also considered a protector of infants and young children.

Several figures named in the Paian do not have standalone Entity entries and are tagged here through their parent or related deity: Iaso, Akeso, Aigle (daughters of Asklepios and Epione, attendants of Hygeia), Epione (wife of Asklepios), Koronis (mother of Asklepios), and Machaon/Machaeis (son of Asklepios). The Aglaion ÊRnos refers to a young Athenian supplicant figure used as a model in the hymn. Kentauros refers to Chiron, the centaur who taught Asklepios the healing arts on Mt. Pelias.
