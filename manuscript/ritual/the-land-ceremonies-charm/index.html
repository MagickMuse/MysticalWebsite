🌙The Land Ceremonies Charm

An elaborate Anglo-Saxon pagan ritual from the Lacnunga manuscript for blessing and consecrating land to bring prosperity, abundance, and fruitfulness before cultivation.

Quantities

  • Four sods of earth (one from each cardinal direction)
  • Oil
  • Honey
  • Yeast
  • Milk
  • Herbs
  • Frankincense
  • Fennel
  • Salt
  • A seed
  • A loaf of bread made with milk and holy water
  • A cross inscribed with the word "Grow" (or rune stones)

Directions

  1. This ritual is to be performed at night before dawn.
  2. Dig up four sods of earth, one from each of the four cardinal directions of the land to be blessed.
  3. Chant over the sods:
  4. "Grow and multiply and fill the earth."
  5. Prepare a potion of oil, honey, yeast, milk, and herbs.
  6. Drip the potion three times onto each sod.
  7. Carry the sods to a sacred place such as a henge or stone circle and allow them to be touched by the first rays of sunrise.
  8. Fill each hole left behind with either a cross marked with the word "Grow" or with rune stones, forming a magickal circle of protection around the field.
  9. After the sods are replaced, face east and bow nine times while reciting:
  10. "We ask that the swelling crops be wakened for our worldly need, that the fielded earth be filled, and the green fields made beautiful."
  11. Turn sunwise three times, then lie upon the ground in supplication and continue the invocative charm, asking the earth to become fertile and green for the benefit of the land's owner and all who are subject to them.
  12. Before ploughing, consecrate the tools by rubbing a paste of frankincense, fennel, and salt onto the plough.
  13. Place a seed within it while chanting:
  14. "Erce, Erce, Erce, Mother of Earth, may the Almighty, the Eternal Lord grant you fields growing and thriving."
  15. When the first furrow is turned by the consecrated plough, place a loaf of bread made with milk and holy water within it, symbolically returning to the earth a portion of the blessed yield.
  16. This completes the ritual.
