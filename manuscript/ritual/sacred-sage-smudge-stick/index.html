⚠️ Cultural Sensitivity: Smudging is a sacred practice rooted in Indigenous American spiritual tradition. Unless you are of Native heritage, this practice is not yours to claim. Approach it with genuine respect, humility, and acknowledgment of its origins. With that understanding, a practitioner may create and use their own herb bundle as an act of reverence rather than appropriation. Burning bound herbs clears stagnant energy, prepares sacred space, and carries intention through smoke. Common smudging herbs include sage, cedar, sweetgrass, and tobacco. Sage is the most widely used because it continues to smolder after the flame is extinguished.

Quantities

  • Sage (primary herb, 6 to 10 inches long, enough for a 1.5-inch-diameter bundle)
    Optional additions:
  • Rosemary (creativity, memory, ancestor veneration)
  • Lavender (healing, calm)
  • Basil (joy, prosperity)
  • Mugwort (psychic attunement)
  • Pine (purification)
    Scissors
    Thin cotton string

Directions

  1. Gather your chosen herbs by the stem, ensuring they are clean, dry, and free of debris. Do not wash them as moisture causes rot. Trim the stems with scissors so all herbs are roughly the same length, between 6 and 10 inches. Arrange them into a bundle about 1.5 inches in diameter.
  2. Tie the string tightly around the base of the bundle, leaving a long tail. Begin wrapping the long end upward around the bundle at a slight diagonal, keeping tension consistent throughout.
  3. When you reach the top, wrap twice around to secure, then continue wrapping back down in the opposite diagonal direction, creating a crosshatch pattern. At the base, tie the working end to the tail with a firm knot.
  4. Set the smudge stick in a dry location and allow it to air-dry for approximately two weeks, or less for smaller bundles. Use with respect, intention, and an acknowledgment of the tradition from which this practice comes.
