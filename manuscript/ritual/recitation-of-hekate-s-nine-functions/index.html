A liminal-hour recitation of Hekate's nine sacred functions and epithets, paired with self-reflection on what one truly seeks. Rite 3 of Book I.

Quantities

1 pen, 1 sheet of paper, 1 bowl, matches, 1 white candle, myrrh incense with a censer or heat-safe holder, cinnamon for dusting the hands.

Directions

  1. At a liminal hour, wash your hands thrice and dust them with cinnamon. Gather pen, paper, a bowl, matches, a white candle, and myrrh incense in a private space.
  2. Sit, breathe deeply, and still your mind. Draw Hekate's sigil on a clean sheet of paper, contemplating its meaning, and write your initials on all four sides as before.
  3. Light the white candle and set it at the sigil's center. Light the myrrh incense; once the smoke begins to rise, turn out all other lights, leaving only the candle.
  4. Gaze into the flame, breathing deeply until your mind stills. Reflect honestly on what you seek, healing, power, profit, freedom, respect, pleasure, wisdom, control, or transformation, and how you came to be without it. Consider which of Hekate's nine functions, recited below, speaks most to that need.
  5. Close your eyes and picture yourself again on the sandy path at the crossroads, the sea to your left, the dark wood to your right. See the first mask shift aside as the second mask slowly turns to face you; look at it, accept it, and breathe its essence in and out.
  6. Inhale, and see the sigil glow beneath your feet; breathe in and out three times as it rises and rotates upright along your spine. Look up and watch clouds spiral into a storm cell overhead; see lightning strike the crown of the trident above your head, purifying every limb, vein, artery, and nerve with divine fire.
  7. Exhale slowly and open your eyes, returning fully to the room. Cover the sigil with your hands and read the hymn below aloud twice, taking care over each Greek epithet, since their careful recitation is said to compel divine attention:
    "She exists! She exists! Ever and always, Hekate exists, / And so the gods rejoice, for she enables their reign; / And so the titans rejoice, for she ennobles their bondage; / And so the mortals rejoice, for she conducts each soul to its destination; / And so the Fates rejoice, for she is their Instrumentality, their Spindle, Loom and Shears; / And so we rejoice, for her existence embodies nine sacred functions; namely, // To destroy old and outworn forms, in order to make way for the new, and therefore is she known as REXICHTHON, Earth-Splitter; // To ensoul the noetic mind-force that emanates from Lion-Faced Aion, the scion of Eternity, and to take such ensoulments from the Moon to Earth, whereby she ensures the souls' enfleshment in the bodies of men and women, and therefore is she known as ZOOTROPHOS, Nurturer of Life. // To recover the lost, and re-unite the departed, and accompany the absent on their journey, and therefore is she known as ATALOS, Compassionate One. // To reap the souls of the Restless Dead, and escort them to their peace, past Kerberos, Minos, Charon, Nyx, Styx, Aklys, and Aidoneos, the King of the Dead, to the place deep within the Earth where they receive their final judgment, and from whence they drift back to the Moon, her kingdom, where their lower, hylic souls are flensed from their higher, noetic minds, which then return to Aion in the Realm of the Stars, and therefore is she known as PSYCHOPOMP, Soul-Guide. // To bind, restrain and secure her fellow titans imprisoned in the black pit of Tartaros, which is as far below Earth as Heaven is above it, and therefore is she known as TARTAROUKHOS, the Lady of Tartaros. // To grant increase, secure success, and send surcease from suffering, to benefit the breeder, the believer, the soldier, sportsman, lawyer, warrior, wife, witch, mage, mother and thief, and therefore is she called APOTROPAIA, Shield from Harm. // To visit the unjustly imprisoned, and negotiate the terms of their release, and therefore is she called SOTEIRA, Savior. // To devour that which is cast off; to want the unwanted; to seek out the unsought; to reach the unreachable and catch the uncaught; to touch the untouchable and claim the unclaimable, and therefore is she called BORBOROPHORBA, Eater of Filth. // To revel in her own existence, celebrating her ineffable self, and therefore is she called EPIPHANESTATE, the Most Manifest One. // KHAIRA KHAIRA KHAIRA // HEKATE!"
  8. When the words have been spoken, close your eyes once more; sense the warmth and light of the candle before you, then extend your awareness left, right, above, below, and behind you, seeking any presence beyond the candle's flame. If you sense one, simply register it without acting; if not, accept that too.
  9. Gradually withdraw your awareness. When ready, open your eyes, blow out the candle, then kiss and burn the sigil and store the ash.

Context

The third of nine nights leading to the new moon dedication, six days before it, in which the reader recites Hekate's nine sacred functions and their associated names, then turns inward to identify what she herself seeks before the working continues.

Recommended Reading

The Hymn to Selene from the Greek Magical Papyri — a passionate ancient invocation of Hekate-Selene drawn from a trove of spells excavated from a Greco-Egyptian sorcerer's tomb, offering a real historical example of titanic powers being summoned by a practicing magician nearly two millennia ago.
