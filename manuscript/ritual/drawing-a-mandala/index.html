🎨Drawing a Mandala

◇Tradition
Eclectic
◇Solitary or Group
Solitary
◇Form
Altar Working
◇Setting
Indoor
◇Method
MeditationVisualization
◇Category
Meditation

◇Quantities
Colored pencils, markers, or crayons White paper A circular template, such as a plate Optional: music, candles, or incense
◇Directions
1. Drawing a mandala is a powerful form of creative meditation that reveals your moods and feelings, helps you release pain and trauma, and enables you to make contact with your higher self. 2. The process bypasses your logical left brain and deals directly with your soul. 3. Materials: - Colored pencils, markers, or crayons - White paper - A circular template (such as a plate) - A well-lit place where you will not be interrupted for at least 30 minutes 4. Optional Atmosphere: Gentle music playing in the background, candles, incense. 5. Some practitioners prefer silence, while most enjoy having gentle music playing in the background. 6. Choose what feels right for you. 7. Preparation: Start by drawing a circle on the piece of paper. Use a plate or other circular object as a template. Pause for a few moments before starting. Take a few deep breaths and focus on relaxing your body. 8. The Drawing Process: Once you feel relaxed and centered, begin drawing inside the circle. Let your intuition guide you. There is no right or wrong way to create your mandala. 9. What to Draw: Allow images, colors, and patterns to emerge naturally. 10. You might draw geometric patterns, organic shapes, symbols that have meaning for you, abstract designs, or representational images. 11. The mandala will reveal your inner state at this moment in time. 12. Traditional Approach: If you prefer structure, consider the traditional Tibetan approach, begin from the center and work outwards. 13. This symbolizes creation from a single cell to an entire world. 14. Use colors that call to you (traditional Tibetan colors are white, red, yellow, green, and blue). 15. What the Mandala Reveals: The mandala you create clearly reveals your moods and feelings at the time you made it. By expressing these images, they lose their hold on you and can be released, allowing you to move forward again. Your mandala might show current emotional states, hidden feelings or blockages, spiritual insights, or the ever-changing pattern of your life. 16. After Completion: When your mandala feels complete, sit with it for a few moments. Notice what you feel looking at it. You may experience a sense of release or relief, insights about your current state, a feeling of peace or wholeness, or contact with your higher self. 17. Optional Daily Practice: Consider drawing a small circular mandala every morning in a notebook. 18. This allows you to observe your psychic transformations from day to day, as Carl Jung did in his own practice. 19. Closing: Take a few deep breaths, stretch if needed, and return to your day with the knowledge that you have released what needed to be expressed and made contact with your soul.
◇Description
A creative meditation practice where you draw a circular mandala to reveal your inner state, release blockages, and contact your higher self. The process is both revealing and therapeutic, bypassing the logical mind to work directly with the soul.
