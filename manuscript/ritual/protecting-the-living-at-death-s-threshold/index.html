🛡️Protecting the Living at Death's Threshold

A collection of traditional protective practices performed immediately following a death, for the safety of both the living and the newly deceased. The period immediately after death is understood as a threshold moment as vulnerable as birth, with psychopomps present and the risk of metaphysical confusion. Three core practices are observed: First, all mirrors in the room are immediately turned to face the wall or covered. Capturing the reflection of an angel of death is considered harmful, and it is believed dangerous for the soul of the deceased to see their own post-death reflection or lack thereof. Second, the deceased is never left unattended from the moment of death until funeral rites are complete. In areas where malevolent sorcerers may attempt to exploit the dead, a vigil may continue after burial for a period of time, ensuring the soul departs and the body begins to return to the earth. Third, candles are kept burning near the deceased throughout the vigil. Some traditions ring the body with candles; others place one at the head and one at the foot; others keep them burning beneath the bed.

Quantities

Cloth or covering for all mirrors in the room
Candles, kept burning near the body

Directions

  1. Immediately upon death, turn all mirrors in the room to face the wall or cover them completely.
  2. Do not allow the mirrors to reflect anyone in the room.
  3. Ensure the deceased is attended at all times throughout the preparation and funeral period, never leaving them alone.
  4. Keep candles burning near the body continuously.
  5. The placement varies by tradition: a ring of candles around the body, one at the head and one at the foot, or candles burning beneath the bed are all valid forms.
