🌽Corn Wheel

◇Tradition
Wiccan
◇Solitary or Group
Solitary
◇Form
CharmConsecrated Item
◇Setting

ElementalConsecration
◇Category

◇Quantities
- 8 small ears of colorful Indian corn with husks - 1 willow wreath, metal circle, or wicker doily - Wire - Ribbon in Sabbat-appropriate colors - Hot glue gun and glue sticks (optional) - Cardboard circle (optional, for metal rim method) - Decorations (scallop shell, silk sunflower, etc.)
◇Directions
1. Lay a circular frame flat, such as a wicker doily, willow wreath, or metal circle. 2. Arrange eight ears of colorful Indian corn on the frame so they all touch at the center. 3. Secure the ears in place with wire or hot glue, then tie with ribbons. 4. Choose colors that evoke each Sabbat for each ear, or colors that distinguish between Quarters and Cross-Quarters. 5. Decorate the center where the tips meet with husks or other decorations such as a scallop shell or silk sunflower. 6. Gently fan out the husks of each ear for a more decorative appearance. 7. If using a metal rim, carefully wrap a portion of the husk from either side of each ear of corn around the rim closest to it. 8. Make a small circle of cardboard to fit under the tips and hot glue them in place. 9. Add a decoration over the center if desired. 10. Consecrate with the Basic Consecration Ritual. 11. Hang the wheel as a reminder of the God's protection: indoors over a door or next to the entrance, over an altar area, or from a tree in the yard. 12. Traditionally displayed starting on Hogmanay (New Year's Day).
◇Description
A home or outdoor charm representing the Sabbats of the Wheel of the Year. Eight small ears of colorful Indian corn are arranged on a circular frame such as a willow wreath or wicker doily, touching at the center, then secured and decorated with colored ribbons evoking the Sabbats. The finished wheel is consecrated with the Basic Consecration Ritual and hung over a doorway on Hogmanay (New Year's Day) as a year-long invocation of the God and Goddess's protection. It also serves as a family teaching tool for the spiritual path of the Sabbats.
