💖Mabon Ritual for a Couple

A ritual for partners to honor one another through gratitude and shared ceremony. Partners wash each other's hands and feet, read letters of gratitude to each other, and share food and drink while acknowledging their interdependence and partnership at the Autumn Equinox.

Quantities

  • Bowl of water
  • Two clean washcloths
  • Two clean hand towels
  • Two letters (one by each person to their partner)
  • Small plate of food (bread, fruit, cheese, etc.)
  • Bottle of any beverage (water, wine, cider, etc.)
  • Picnic blanket (optional)
  • Wand, athame, or similar item for circle casting

Directions

  1. Purpose:
    This ritual is a way for partners to take time out to honor one another. In addition to being thankful for the harvest bounty, this is a way to show each person gratitude for the other. If it's been a difficult year, this is a good way to acknowledge each other as partners and as a team, to help fortify one another for the challenges of the coming winter.
  2. Setting:
    Perform this ritual outside or inside, at any time of day when you can avoid interruption. A city park, a backyard, a living room floor, or, in a pinch, a parking lot near a storm drain will do.
  3. Pre-Ritual Preparation:
    Write a heartfelt letter to your partner. The choice of paper and pen doesn't matter. Be simple with a ballpoint pen, fanciful with typed fonts, or magickal with quill and ink. Make a list of everything you are grateful for in that person, from the trivial to the gigantic. Carry it close to your heart until called to produce it in the ritual. You may want to fasten it inside your shirt with a safety pin.
  4. Each person should take a bath or shower and change into loose, comfortable clothing.
  5. This ritual should be relaxed.
  6. Go barefoot if possible.
  7. Assemble the plate of food with small items you can both safely eat.
  8. Fresh, just harvested foods are ideal; whatever is easily available to you is very good.
  9. Put down the picnic blanket if you have it.
  10. In the center, place the bowl of water, washcloths, and towels on one side.
  11. On the other side, set the plate of food and the beverage (and glass for pouring, if desired).
  12. Decide between you who casts a circle, calls the quarters, and makes the first move once the space is established.
  13. One partner should cast the circle and the other call the quarters.
  14. If you need to, flip a coin.
  15. The Ritual:
    The first partner performs a circle casting, walking deosil (clockwise), while saying:
  16. Circle, full circle we come to this space,
    in a time without time,
    in this place between places.
    Together we are safe both outside and in,
    as we share heart-to-heart what lies deep within.
  17. Then the second partner performs quarter calls by saying:
  18. Hail to the east, may we speak our truths well!
    Hail to the south, may we feel true warmth for one another!
    Hail to the west, may we flow well with one another!
    Hail to the north, may we live together in trust!
    So mote it be!
  19. Settle yourselves on the blanket across from each another, with the bowl of water and the plate of food between you.
  20. Gaze into each other's eyes for a full minute.
  21. Meditate on the person before you, the meaning he or she has to you, and what you see inside that person.
  22. First, one partner should pick up a washcloth, dip it in the water, wring it out, and then proceed to wash his or her partner's hands and feet.
  23. Be gentle and thorough.
  24. When done, dry off and set the cloths away from the blanket.
  25. Take the letter out from where you've been keeping it.
  26. Read it to your partner.
  27. Take your time.
  28. When finished, hold a moment of silence.
  29. Then the partner who has not yet read should reciprocate the process, washing the hands and feet, drying them, and reading his or her letter of gratitude.
  30. Once you have completed these actions, the partner that washed first should set the bowl of water and cloths outside the immediate ritual area.
  31. The other partner should then pick up the plate of food and gently feed a bite to the other partner, saying: May you never hunger.
  32. The partner just fed should in turn feed his/her partner, and say: May you never hunger.
  33. The partner who just ate should open the beverage bottle and pour it into the glass, offering it and saying, May you never thirst.
  34. The partner who just received should offer it back, also saying: May you never thirst.
  35. Finish the plate of food and take a few more moments to enjoy one another's company.
  36. Tuck the letters away someplace safe to read in times when you most need or miss one another.
