Dadophorous Sigil

Meaning: Torch-Bearer. A spirit-sight rite that produces an anointing cream to see spirits and to invoke Hekate's epithets to manifestation. Day 15 of the Hekate Devotee cycle.

Quantities

  • Yew bark, for the sigil
  • Olive oil
  • Melted tallow (fat)
  • A crushed leaf of vervain
  • Crossroads dirt taken from a cemetery
  • Ashes of a Hekate sigil burnt during nine-day preparation
  • Myrrh, to burn
  • One black candle

Directions

  1. To see spirits, write the Dadophorous sigil upon yew bark and burn it.
  2. Combine the ash with olive oil, melted tallow, a crushed leaf of vervain, crossroads dirt taken from a cemetery, and the ashes of the Hekate sigil burnt during the nine-day preparation.
  3. Gently rub the cream on and around your left eyelid.
  4. Say three times aloud, "Hekate, Dadophorous, Torchbearer, as you revealed all to Kore on her descent, so reveal the unseen world to me, by Maskelli Maskello etc."
  5. Close your right eye and open your left. If spirits are present, you will glimpse them.
  6. To invoke any of the twenty-seven epithets to manifestation: purify yourself and apply the cream to your left eye, then burn myrrh.
  7. Having consecrated your space at a liminal hour, and having whirled the Iynx toward you (counter-clockwise) while speaking the Maskelli Maskello formula, call upon any one of the twenty-seven epithets, saying its name repeatedly as a mantra.
  8. Do this in a dark space lit by one black candle, saying after nine recitations, "Come to me." This may take some time, but eventually your skin will rise in gooseflesh and the hairs at the back of your neck will prickle, signifying the spirit's arrival.
  9. When the epithet manifests, greet it respectfully, but without groveling. You may remain silent in its presence, converse with it, or bid it to aid you in some manner consistent with its function.
  10. When finished, say farewell and spin the Iynx in the opposite direction (clockwise, away from you) to speed its departure.
  11. Afterward, keep a careful record of the image or sensations that accompanied its arrival, as these are the keys by which the epithet may be identified again.
  12. If the epithet outstays its welcome, say: "Return to your source, the Virgin-Serpent-Bitch herself, immediately, immediately! I adjure you by Ablanathanalba, by Akrammachamarei, and by these seven shudderful names" - then close your eyes and intone the seven vowels of the Lyre of Apollon. The spirit will depart.
  13. Carefully remove all of the cream and flush your left eye when the ritual is done.

Arcana

The two torches that Hekate bears have a dual function. First, they serve to illuminate the darkness as she leads night-travelers to their destination, be it to a foreign land, the spheres of the upper air, or the depths of Hades. Second, they represent the weapon she used against her own kind when she sided with the gods at the end of the Age of Titans, and again during the Gigantomachia (War of Giants). The same flames that led Persephone out of Hades burnt the rebel giant Clytius to ash. As Torchbearer, she is thus protector and destroyer; revealer and obliterator; liberator and incinerator.

Hekate's torches have a third, more subtle function. As in the perfect darkness of the deep sea the anglerfish's bioluminescent light draws its prey, so too does the light from her twin torches draw to her those human seekers who will become Devotees, Adepts, Initiates, Hierophants, and then ultimately, her Hounds. In this way, all attempts made by the Devotee to share Hekate's radiance with others serve as dadophoric acts in which the Devotees themselves become, briefly, the flickering flames emanating from Hekate's brilliant torches.
