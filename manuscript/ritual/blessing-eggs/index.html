A customizable Ostara craft creating blessing eggs filled with herbs, oils, and glitter corresponding to desired blessings. Eggs are blown out, filled with symbolic ingredients, painted and decorated, then blessed on altar. Can be displayed or planted to 'grow' blessings. Examples: green glitter and oak moss for money, blue glitter and lavender for sleep.

Quantities

  • Eggs (raw, to be blown out)
  • Bowl for egg contents
  • Herbs corresponding to desired blessing
  • Flowers corresponding to desired blessing
  • Oils corresponding to desired blessing
  • Colored glitter corresponding to desired blessing
  • Small piece of masking tape
  • Craft paints
  • Paintbrushes
  • Optional: planter or garden space for planting

Directions

  1. This working uses blown eggs that are refilled with herbs, oils, and other items to symbolize a blessing to receive or to gift.
  2. Make two holes in the egg, one at each end; keep one small and make the other just large enough to refill later.
  3. Blow into the small hole to push the yolk and white out of the larger hole into a bowl, then carefully rinse the shell.
  4. Choose herbs, flowers, oils, and glitter that match your intent using Ostara correspondences.
  5. Examples include green glitter with oak moss for money or career, and blue glitter with lavender for sleep support.
  6. Fill the egg as much as you can and seal the larger hole with a small piece of masking tape.
  7. Paint and decorate the shell to match your blessing, adding words, symbols, or runes to boost the effect.
  8. Example: paint the egg green for money and add words like money and abundance and a dollar sign.
  9. Place finished eggs on your altar during your Ostara rite, ask your deities to bless the eggs and the recipient, and if gifting, give the egg after the rite.
  10. For use, either display the egg where it is seen often, or plant it so the blessing may grow, such as in a garden or planter.
  11. You may also create two identical eggs, one to display as a reminder and one to plant so the blessing can take root.
