🧹The Broom on the Door

A home protection ritual for crafting and hanging a handmade herbal broom on the door. Made from foraged wood and bundles of protective herbs bound with jute and copper wire, the finished broom is blessed with an incantation and optionally used to asperge the home with salt water and sage oil.

Quantities

For the broomstick:
Sturdy foraged stick of oak, linden, or hawthorn, 3 to 4 feet long and approximately 1.5 inches in diameter
Wood rasp or file
100-grit sandpaper

For the brush:
Long-stemmed herbs: sagebrush, mugwort, rosemary, and/or Scotch broom
Broom corn (optional, for structure)
Jute twine
Heavy scissors or garden shears
Staple gun and staples
12-gauge copper wire
Pliers
Wire cutters

For the asperging wash (optional):
A chalice of water
3 pinches of salt
3 drops sage essential oil

Directions

  1. Prepare the broomstick by using the rasp or file to round off one end, which will remain exposed as the handle. Smooth away any rough edges and strip any bark from the shaft. Follow up with sandpaper to prevent splintering.
  2. Gather the herbs into 8 to 10 bundles of equal length, with a mix of each herb in every bundle. Secure each bundle with jute twine using a tight half knot on one side, then flip and tie another half knot on the other. Trim the twine.
  3. Position the bundles around the unfinished end of the broomstick, about 4 inches from the end. Staple them in place alternately, one bunch toward the top and the next toward the bottom, until the stick is fully ringed.
  4. Bend the end of the copper wire to a 90-degree angle, matching the depth of one herb bundle. Insert the bent end between two bundles near the top and wrap the wire tightly around the herbs and broomstick. Make at least three passes, then snip and tuck the end under the herbs with pliers so it does not show.
  5. Gather the herbs together below the concealed broomstick end and tie them tightly with jute twine in a double knot. Trim any excess. Wind a second coil of copper wire over the twine to finish. The broom is now ready to hang.

The broom is one of witchcraft's most enduring emblems. It carries associations with flight, astral projection, cleansing, and protection. A handcrafted herbal broom hung on the door guards the threshold against unwelcome energy and guests. As the herbs dry naturally in place, they release their protective properties into the home continuously.

Blessing Charm

To empower the broom, recite aloud as you hang it:

Stalk and stem
Leaf and wood
Used for power
Used for good
By light of day
Or dead of night
Unwanted energies
Now take flight
My sacred space
Is now this room
Protected by
My magick broom.

Asperging the Home

To extend the broom's protective work throughout your space, combine a chalice of water with three pinches of salt and three drops of sage oil. Dip the herb ends of the broom into the chalice and use it to sprinkle the four corners of each room, the doorways, and the boundaries of your property. This act of asperging seals the protection into the space itself.

Magickal Notes

The species of wood for the handle carries its own symbolism: oak for strength and endurance, hawthorn for fierce protection and the fae, linden for peace and rest in the home. Choose according to your intention. Copper wire is not merely decorative; copper conducts and amplifies energy, making it a powerful binding agent for magickal crafts. As the herbs age and dry, their essence releases slowly into the home, making this a long-acting, continuous working.
