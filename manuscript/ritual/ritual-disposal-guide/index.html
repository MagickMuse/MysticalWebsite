Ritual Disposal Guide

After ritual work is complete, you're left with physical remains: candle wax, herb bundles, petition papers, jar contents, worn-out tools. What you do with these materials matters both magickally and practically.

The Five Primary Methods

Bury

When to use:

Grounding energy, long-term workings, growth intentions, binding that needs to stay hidden, anything you want to take root or remain stable.

How:

Dig at least 6 inches deep on your property, at a crossroads, or in a natural area where you have permission. Biodegradable materials only. Mark the spot mentally if you need to retrieve it later.

Magickal reasoning:

Earth absorbs, grounds, and transforms. Burial keeps energy localized and stable. Use for protection anchored to a location, prosperity that needs to grow, or bindings you want hidden and immobilized.

Examples:

Protection jars for the home, prosperity pouches, grounding work, binding someone's harmful behavior.

Burn

When to use:

Release, transformation, banishing, sending intentions skyward, destroying connections, purification.

How:

Use a fireproof container or outdoor fire pit. Never burn plastics, synthetic fabrics, toxic plants, or anything that produces dangerous fumes. Let it burn completely. Dispose of cool ash by scattering, burying, or flushing.

Magickal reasoning:

Fire transforms matter into smoke and ash, releasing energy upward and outward. Use for letting go, severing ties, sending petitions to spirits or deities, or destroying what no longer serves.

Examples:

Cord-cutting remains, banishing work, petition papers to deities, releasing past relationships, cleansing materials.

Keep on Altar or Person

When to use:

Ongoing work, talismans, charms, objects that need regular charging, workings that require sustained energy.

How:

Store on your altar, in a dedicated drawer, or carry with you. Refresh or recharge periodically. Cleanse if energy feels stagnant.

Magickal reasoning:

Keeping materials maintains the energetic link. Use for protection you wear daily, ongoing prosperity work, relationship charms, or any working that needs continuous presence.

Examples:

Protection charms, love talismans, prosperity pouches carried in wallet, altar jars that remain active.

Crossroads Disposal

When to use:

Banishing, removing unwanted energy from your life, sending work away from you, offerings to crossroads spirits, disposing of particularly heavy or dark materials.

How:

Take materials to a physical crossroads (intersection of two roads or paths). Leave them without looking back. Do not return to that crossroads for several days. Some traditions require you to walk away in silence.

Magickal reasoning:

Crossroads are liminal spaces where energy disperses in multiple directions. Use for banishing illness, bad luck, unwanted attachments, or anything you need completely removed from your sphere.

Examples:

Banishing work, illness transfers, bad luck removal, offerings to Hekate or Legba, cleansing materials from heavy work.

Running Water

When to use:

Carrying intentions away, cleansing, releasing emotions, sending work downstream, dissolving connections.

How:

Use biodegradable materials only. Natural bodies of water preferred (river, stream, ocean). Toss materials into moving water, ideally flowing away from you. Do not use for toxic materials or anything harmful to ecosystems.

Magickal reasoning:

Water flows and carries energy away. Use for releasing grief, washing away negativity, sending intentions to water spirits, or cleansing after difficult work.

Examples:

Grief release rituals, cleansing after baneful work, offerings to water spirits, dissolving emotional ties.

Special Cases

Non-Biodegradable Materials

The problem:

Many ritual materials can't be buried or released into nature: glitter, synthetic fabrics, plastic, metal hardware, glass jars with non-removable labels, electronic components.

Solutions:

  • Disassemble first. Separate biodegradable from non-biodegradable. Bury the herbs, dispose of the jar in trash.
  • Cleanse and reuse. Wash glass jars, metal containers, synthetic fabric pouches. Cleanse with smoke, salt, or sound. Reuse for future work.
  • Trash disposal with intention. If it must go in the trash, acknowledge that the work is complete. Say a closing statement: "This work is done. The energy is released." Then dispose normally.
  • Recycle when possible. Glass, metal, and clean paper can be recycled. The transformation into new material serves as symbolic release.

Wax Remains

Free-standing candles:

If the candle burned completely, bury or dispose of small wax remnants. If significant wax remains, it may indicate blockage or resistance. Read the wax shape for divination if you practice that. Then dispose based on the working type.

Container candles:

Clean out wax with hot water or freezing method. Bury the wax or dispose in trash. Cleanse and reuse the container.

Wax with herbs or petition papers:

If you can separate them, do so. Burn the paper and herbs separately. Dispose of wax in trash or bury if small amounts.

Jars You Want to Keep Active

Ongoing protection, prosperity, or relationship jars:

Leave sealed on your altar or hide in a relevant location (protection jar near front door, prosperity jar near cash box, relationship jar in bedroom). Refresh periodically by shaking, anointing with oil, or placing a candle on top.

When to dispose:

If the jar breaks, the working feels complete, or the intention no longer applies. Disassemble contents and dispose using appropriate methods for each material.

Poppets and Dolls

After healing or positive work:

Thank the poppet. Disassemble it respectfully. Burn natural fabrics and stuffing. Bury biodegradable elements. Dispose of synthetic materials in trash.

After baneful or binding work:

The disposal method depends on intent. Bury to keep the person bound and hidden. Burn to destroy their influence over you. Crossroads to remove them from your life entirely. Some practitioners keep binding poppets sealed in a box to maintain the binding.

Offerings Left for Spirits

Food and drink:

Leave for a set period (overnight, three days, a week). Then return the offering to nature by burying, composting, or leaving in a wild area. Some traditions allow you to consume offerings after the spirit has taken the essence. Know your tradition's rules.

Non-food offerings:

Some stay on the altar permanently. Others are buried at sacred sites. Follow the guidance of the specific entity or tradition.

Toxic or Dangerous Materials

Plants:

If you used toxic plants (belladonna, datura, oleander, hemlock), do not burn them. Do not compost them. Wear gloves. Seal in a bag and dispose in trash, or bury deep where animals and children cannot access.

Bodily fluids:

Blood, sexual fluids, urine, or other biological materials should be buried, burned (if dried and safe), or flushed. Never leave in public spaces.

Broken glass or sharp objects:

Wrap securely before disposal. If burying, mark the location so no one gets hurt later.

Environmental Responsibility

Never dispose in nature:

  • Plastics, synthetics, glitter, or non-biodegradable materials
  • Toxic substances, chemicals, or treated materials
  • Glass or sharp objects in unmarked locations
  • Anything that harms water, soil, or wildlife

Biodegradable does not mean instant:

Even natural materials take time to decompose. Paper takes months. Fabric takes years. If burying on public land or in a natural area, use minimal amounts and choose a discreet location.

Salt warning:

Salt is used in many rituals but is not environmentally neutral. It can kill plants and leach into groundwater. Dispose of salt in trash or down the drain, not in soil or natural areas.

When the Ritual Specifies Disposal

Some rituals include disposal instructions as part of the working. Follow those instructions. The disposal method is part of the magick.

If no disposal method is specified, use the chart below.

Quick Reference Chart

Intent → Disposal Method

  • Growth, stability, long-term → Bury
  • Release, transformation, sending upward → Burn
  • Ongoing work, daily use → Keep
  • Banishing, removal, sending away → Crossroads
  • Cleansing, releasing emotions → Running water
  • Binding (keep effect active) → Bury or hide
  • Binding (end effect) → Burn
  • Protection for home → Bury on property or keep inside
  • Offerings to spirits → Follow tradition, then nature
  • Toxic materials → Sealed trash or deep burial

Closing the Work

Before disposal, acknowledge completion:

  • "This work is done."
  • "The energy is released."
  • "I thank the materials and release them."

Then dispose without doubt or second-guessing. The work is complete when you say it is.
