A monthly fertility petition to Yemaya, Yoruba orisha of the sea and the mother of mothers, anchored to the menstrual cycle. A blue seven-day candle is burned in her honor at the beginning of each period. If pregnancy is achieved, a shrine to Yemaya is maintained throughout the pregnancy; she may also be petitioned for a healthy gestation and a smooth birth.

Quantities

A blue seven-day candle
Seashells, sea glass, or a goblet of salted water (for altar, if not at the beach)

Directions

  1. When your period begins each month, light a blue seven-day candle dedicated to Yemaya.
  2. Petition her for fertility, speaking your desire aloud.
  3. Maintain the candle through its seven days.
  4. If you do not have access to the beach, the most powerful place to petition her, call her instead with a goblet of salt water set with seashells and sea glass.
  5. Decorate her shrine with ocean imagery: fish, ships, nets, anchors, shells.
  6. Should you conceive, keep a shrine to Yemaya active for the duration of the pregnancy, and extend your petitions to cover her domains of healthy pregnancy and safe delivery.

Yemaya is the Great Mother of Yoruba cosmology. Her name translates as The Mother Whose Children Are Fish — an indication not of their nature but of their number: innumerable. She is the mother of most of the orishas and the embodiment of all facets of maternity in their fullest expression.

Tying the working to the onset of menstruation is deliberate. The cycle itself is Yemaya's territory — salt water, tidal rhythms, the body's monthly correspondence with the moon. Beginning the candle at that threshold acknowledges the cycle rather than mourning it, and frames the petition within the natural time that is already moving.

Blue is her primary color, and seven her number. A seven-day candle in blue covers her full span — one complete cycle of devotion within a single vessel of flame.

⚠️
Yemaya is a Yoruba orisha venerated within Santería, Candomblé, and related African Diaspora traditions. Approach with respect for the tradition's origins.
