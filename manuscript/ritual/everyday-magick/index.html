Everyday Magick

Celebrating the sacred in each moment.

The practice of witchcraft is intensely personal. It comes with traditions, paths, and practices, but the way these speak to each practitioner and the way they are or are not integrated into daily life is deeply individual, perhaps even unique.

If you choose to join a firm tradition or practice, you will be following their dictums. But otherwise, you can and should feel free to forge your own path, integrating selected tools and practices into your own life rather than changing your ways to fit someone else's idea of "correct" practice. You can work with what speaks to you and set the silent bits aside. You can shape your own daily life into intentional, sacred space.

Where to Start

Begin by thinking about who you are and what aspects of the craft seem most meaningful to you. If you are new to witchcraft, what drew you to it? What jumped out and made noise to get your attention?

  • Are you fascinated by the ideas and intricacies of spellwork?
  • Is your heart in the kitchen, working magick with a tea kettle and wooden spoon?
  • Do you feel a strong magickal connection when you work in your garden or walk through the woods?
  • Are you drawn to creating a home space filled with magickal, liminal spaces?
  • Have you always had a knack for divination or prescience?

That which calls you to a certain path or place is where you should set your work in motion.

Three Rules for Everyday Practice

1. Keep It Simple

If you can find a tool in something you already own and something important to you, or if you can make your own, why spend money on a tool created elsewhere?

2. Be Aware of Need vs. Want

There is really very little we need to practice magick, and there is no need to crowd our practices with dozens of items we will never use. Ideally, every tool or item you acquire should have meaning and purpose.

3. Make It Fun (Or Meaningful)

If you are dealing with a challenging practice that really is not fun, at least make it meaningful. Do not do magick because of someone else's expectation or because the calendar says it is time. Do it because it means something to you. Do it because it enriches your magickal life.

Tools and Items to Begin With

Candles

Candles are a good place to start, as they are aesthetically pleasing and have multiple purposes. Use them in spellwork aligned with color and scent correspondences, gaze into the flame as a scrying tool, or simply allow a flickering candle to provide ambience.

You do not need fancy candles that cost thirty dollars each. A tealight might serve the purpose. Or make your own candles and carve runes, moon phases, or whatever speaks to you into each one, filling in the carvings with permanent marker.

Want to go rogue? Try LED candles. Who says witches cannot move into the modern age?

Wands

A wand's purpose is to direct energy, but you can do this equally well with your own arm, hand, and fingers. If you really want a wand, you could make your own by peeling bark off a branch, sanding it, and rubbing it with beeswax.

Or be creative:

  • A kitchen witch could use a wooden spoon as a wand, using a wood burner to engrave inscriptions
  • A witch who loves to write might use a prized pencil as a wand
  • A green witch might call upon a garden tool, like a dibbler

Athame

The athame (AH-thuh-may) is a small or medium-sized knife used to direct energy, create or manipulate ritual space, or for other purposes. An athame need not be fancy and can simply be a knife that you already know and love.

Herbalist Supplies

Think practical. Mason jars are great for herbal works, and a small, sharp knife for collecting herbs is as effective as a boline (the traditional sickle-shaped cutting tool).

Robes and Garb

If they speak to you and are appropriate, go for it. But are they necessary? Not unless a group requires them. Many practitioners prefer to work in everyday clothing, as it best represents their magickal selves.

An inexpensive option is to create a shawl from a piece of decorative fabric. Drawn around your shoulders or draped over your head while working, the shawl creates a feeling of magickal space.

Where Do I Practice?

Choose a location that aligns with your practices, one that means something and feels natural to you. This might be your kitchen, your backyard, the local woods, your altar space, or someplace else. Make your practice space intentional, not incidental.

Altars

Most witches invariably end up with one or more altar spaces. Depending on your focus, yours might be quite formal, complete with altar cloth, deity figures, candles, stones, and any number of magickal correspondences.

But an altar can also be informal: a corner of a bookshelf, space on a side table, or even a quiet spot in the garden. One or two items, placed intentionally in a significant location, become an altar.

Once it has been created, use your altar regularly, whether for spellwork and ritual or simply for contemplation, meditation, or a moment of quiet.

When Do I Practice?

Here is where the everyday witch is someone different. Rather than basing practice on the occasional ritual or holy day and otherwise remaining magickally inactive, everyday witchcraft sees every day as sacred in its own way and full of magickal potential, centering spiritual practice in a simple, grounded approach that embraces the magick in each moment.

Ideas for Celebrating the Sacred in the Everyday

  • Upon waking: Pause, take three deep breaths, and speak aloud words of gratitude
  • Morning greeting: Spend five minutes outdoors with a cup of coffee or tea, welcoming the new day and feeling its fresh magick
  • Morning cuppa: Make the brewing of your morning beverage into a simple ritual
  • Bathing: Dip under the water three times and imagine negative energy being washed away
  • Before eating: Wash your hands and face, then offer thanks for the gift of food
  • Seasons: Follow the seasons, tracking the equinoxes and solstices
  • Cooking: Stir deosil (clockwise/sunwise), in the direction of the sun, and envision the substance gathering vitality
  • Working magick: Prepare by washing hands and face and, if appropriate, donning fresh clothing. Make your magickal actions special and unique to you. Take your time.
  • Before bed: Reflect on the day and offer gratitude
  • Prayer: Offer prayers to your favorite deity, the universe, or whatever appeals. Pray or petition for help, understanding, or answers, or pray to offer thanks. Yes, witches can pray.
In creating your own witchery, make it thoughtful, make it meaningful, make it a regular part of your everyday life, and make it yours.
