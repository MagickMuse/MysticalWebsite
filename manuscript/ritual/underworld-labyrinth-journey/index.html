🕯️Underworld Labyrinth Journey

A group ritual journey to the underworld. This raises awareness of activity beyond the veil and builds understanding of death mythologies and chthonic gods. Draws on labyrinth symbolism combined with haunted house motifs.

⚠️ Sage and sweetgrass are sacred to Indigenous peoples and should only be used by those within Indigenous traditions.

Quantities

  • Costumes (one per character)
  • Indoor space (large enough for labyrinth)
  • Labyrinth template
  • Masking tape
  • Dividers (cardboard or clotheslines)
  • Flashlights or LED lamps
  • Small altars (one at center, one at each major turn)
  • Manual scale
  • Feather
  • Heart-shaped charm
  • Bowl
  • Representation of Lord and Lady
  • Two bowls (one empty, one with water)
  • Coins (gold or silver colored)
  • Hourglass
  • Tarot deck
  • Blindfold
  • Mirror
  • Jar
  • Paper and markers
  • LED tea lights (multiple stacks)
  • Sage, frankincense, or camphor tea
  • Light snacks (nuts and apples)
  • Notebooks and pens

Directions

  1. This ritual requires extra preparation, from a few days up to a week.
  2. First, pick an underworld mythology (Egyptian, Greek, Norse, or create your own).
  3. Part of the group takes the role of guides and characters, others are souls touring the labyrinth.
  4. Right before the ritual, walk the labyrinth inside and out, smudging with white sage, frankincense, or asperging with camphor and sage tea.
  5. Set up the center altar with representations of the Lord and Lady, goblet, beverage, incense, and candles (or LED alternatives).
  6. The priest or priestess performs the opening invocation:
  7. Hail to the elements of nature!
    Be gentle here on these paths,
    as we are all still learning.
    Hail to the living things,
    those that live in the in-between!
    We greet you in this middle place tonight.
    Hail to our ancestors,
    to our mighty dead!
    Be gentle in what you teach us tonight,
    we remember you, and forgive us for grieving our losses,
    grief is, after all, part of being alive.
    We greet the Goddess, who has descended,
    and we greet the God, as we visit him in his realm.
    Blessed Be!
  8. Each traveler waits outside the entrance.
  9. At the entrance are two bowls, one filled with water on the right and one empty on the left.
  10. When the guide appears, bid the soul to drop a coin in the empty bowl and wash their hands in the water bowl.
  11. As they wash, say: "You are water and water is you. When you cross this water, you cross over it and on into the world between veils."
  12. At the first turn, meet the Crone.
  13. She greets the traveler and directs them to talk to their dead first.
  14. At the next turn, an altar holds pictures of ancestors decorated with flowers, books, candles, and heirlooms.
  15. The guide sets an hourglass and the traveler pays respects until the sand runs out.
  16. They must light an LED light before leaving.
  17. At the next bend, a woman shuffles tarot cards while blindfolded.
  18. The traveler picks one card, looks at it closely, and returns it.
  19. At the next turn, an antlered man in dark green holds up a mirror.
  20. The traveler gazes at their reflection until the hourglass runs out.
  21. At the next bend, a person dressed as the Grim Reaper blocks the path and leads the traveler to a table with jar, paper, markers, and LED light.
  22. The traveler writes their maladies on paper and drops it in the jar.
  23. The reaper says: "From this point, you must go alone."
  24. At the center altar, a manual scale holds a feather and heart charm in balance.
  25. Signs read: "Meet darkness with your light" (pointing to LED candles) and "To return to the land of the living, something must be left for the land of the dead." The traveler leaves an offering in the bowl.
  26. On the return path, a sphinx blocks the way and demands: "Tell me a story, or I'll give you a riddle!" The traveler must tell a story or answer correctly (or pay tribute).
  27. At the next bend, the Grandfather teaches a simple lesson (tying a knot, whistling, telling a joke, holding a pocketknife) and gives the traveler something from the lesson.
  28. Just before the entrance, the Great Mother steps into the path and says: "To me all things are created, and to me all things shall return." She anoints the person's head with water and steps aside.
  29. Travelers are guided to a table with snacks and notebooks to record their experiences.
  30. When the last traveler completes the journey, the priest or priestess asperges the entrance and center, saying:
  31. To the Lord and Lady,
    we live in gratitude for what you taught us this night.
    To the Mighty Dead, we are honored you have walked with us.
    To the forces of nature, we are grateful for your gentleness.
    We release each archetype, each spirit, each presence
    that came for the night's work,
    and we gladly return to ourselves!
    So mote it be!
  32. Everyone shares a meal together.
  33. Disassemble the labyrinth.
  34. Give coins to charity or trick-or-treaters.
  35. Seal other offerings in a bottle with wax and bury it.
