This ritual has two purposes: to give thanks for the blessings that you already have and to draw additional blessings into your life. As with all rituals, the more often it is performed, the stronger, more effective, and more meaningful it becomes.

This is not a fixed, immutable ritual. You can create it anew each time you perform it, incorporating whatever blessings are present or needed. You can enact this ritual whenever you like, but it is best performed on sunny days.

Preparation

Count your blessings. What are you grateful for? Good health, prosperity, freedom, family... a list of blessings will be different for each of us, according to the circumstances of our lives.

Next, identify two or three blessings that you would like to ask the gods for. Be reasonable about this. Most of us would love to win a major lottery jackpot, but it is more realistic to request abundance, prosperity, or a good job.

Once you know how many blessings will be incorporated into your ritual, assemble that number of leaves and the honey. Get into a magickal mood, and create sacred space around the area where you will be working.

Lay out the bay leaves on the container. Spoon or drip some honey into the center of each leaf. As you dress a leaf with honey, name aloud the blessing for which that leaf will be offered.

Optional Herbal Offerings

You can increase the magickal power of your ritual by incorporating offerings for each blessing sought. Stick an appropriate charm into the honey on the leaf that represents that blessing. Charms should be nontoxic, biodegradable, and small enough to fit on the leaves.

Correspondences for blessings:

  • Abundance: orange peel, raw popcorn kernels
  • Beauty: jasmine, lavender, lily, orchid, or rose petals
  • Comfort: cloves, fir tips, marjoram, sage
  • Courage: garlic, black pepper, black tea
  • Fertility: banana chips, dark blue flower petals, pine nuts, rice, wheat flour
  • Friendship: drop of aloe juice, pink carnation petals, geranium leaf, lemon zest
  • Good Fortune: dill seeds, mint, pomegranate seeds, poppy seeds
  • Good Health: acorn, apple peel, ginseng, marjoram, nutmeg
  • Good Luck: allspice, basil, catnip, clover, cornmeal
  • Happiness: apple peel, baby's breath, lemon zest, marigold petals, sunflower seeds
  • Healing: pearl barley, cedar shavings, eucalyptus leaf, lime zest, thyme
  • Inspiration: hazelnut, purple or violet flower petals
  • Justice: cedar chips or shavings, pinecones or pine needles
  • Love: chamomile, ginseng, hibiscus, jasmine, lavender, red rose petals
  • Peace: chamomile flowers, lavender, lemongrass, drop of olive oil, white rose petals
  • Prosperity: allspice, cloves, nutmeg, sage, tangerine peel
  • Protection: birch bark, garlic, pepper, kosher salt, nettles, thorns
  • Strength: red or orange carnation petals, garlic
  • Success: allspice berry, cinnamon, orange or purple flower petals, ginger, lemon balm
  • Wealth: acorn, almond, basil, moss, saffron threads

The Ritual

Take the prepared leaves to the offering site you have selected: a river, stream, or other watercourse. Adapt to your location: if you live in a desert, bury offerings in sand; in a frozen place, give offerings to the wind; at the seaside, offer them to the ocean.

Raise power and reverently cast the leaves, one by one, into the moving water. As you offer each leaf, say:

To (name of deity), for (blessing).

You can invoke your matron goddess, your patron god, or any other deities. You can also make the invocations more general:

To Mother Earth, for rain.

To the Lord and the Lady, for love.

To the Goddess, for prosperity.

Concentrate on your intent as you make each offering. When giving thanks for a blessing, focus on the gratitude you feel. When working to attract a blessing, focus on your need or desire.

Deity Correspondences by Pantheon

Celtic: Sulis (health), Brigid (healing), Angus (love), Epona (abundance), Rhiannon (truth), Cernunnos (good fortune), the Morrigan (justice), Danu (all blessings)

Egyptian: Isis (peace), Hathor (love), Bast (abundance), Ma'at (justice), Sekhmet (healing), Ra (strength), Thoth (success), Osiris (all blessings)

Greek: Aphrodite (love), Hygeia (health), Zeus (protection), Apollo (healing), Nike (victory), Demeter (prosperity), Hestia (peaceful home), Pandora (all blessings)

Norse: Thor (strength), Odin (victory), Freya (longevity), Sjofna (love), Fulla (abundance), Njord (wealth), Heimdall (protection), Eir (healing)

Roman: Venus (beauty), Minerva (peace), Jupiter (justice), Mercury (healing), Vesta (happy home), the Lares (household protection), the Penates (safeguarding family)

Closing

When you finish making your offerings, stand quietly for a few moments. You are in the presence of the gods you have invoked, so this is a good time to commune with them. You can ask for answers or guidance as well as blessings.

Focus on the blessings. Feel them around you, or approaching you. Give thanks to the gods for your blessings and for accepting your offerings. Ground the power that you raised.
