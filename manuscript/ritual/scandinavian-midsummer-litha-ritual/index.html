A between June 21 and 23 or December 21 and 23 spell for Scandinavian Midsummer ritual called Litha in old Anglo-Saxon calendar, weaving seven flowers into wire circle at noon then dancing around tree nine times clockwise for dearest secret wish.

Quantities

  • Seven kinds of flowers, in a basket
  • A small circle of wire for each person present
  • Red, yellow, and green threads

Directions

  1. At dawn, preferably on the longest day of the year, set your basket of seven different kinds of flowers where they will catch first light.
  2. At noon, weave the flowers onto the wire, attaching them with the threads.
  3. You can do this with friends, if you wish.
  4. As you weave, whisper your dearest secret wish.
  5. When you're finished, hang the circlet(s) on a tree.
  6. Walk or dance around the tree nine times clockwise, chanting: "With seven flowers I/we meet, and when I next dance the Midsummer tree, I hope to see joy fulfilled of seven flowers sweet." At sundown, take your wreath from the tree and hang it on the wall over your bed.
  7. Leave the wreath on the wall of your bedroom until it fades.
  8. Perform between June 21 and 23 (St. John's Eve) in the northern hemisphere, and between December 21 and 23 in the southern hemisphere.
