Pomegranate

A fertility petition to Yemaya delivered via a honey-dressed pomegranate containing a written message. The pomegranate is halved, both halves coated in honey, and a paper bearing the petitioner's name and message is sandwiched inside before the fruit is sealed back together. The message is then delivered to the ocean, or to a river, stream, or waterfall as an offering to Oshun, who will carry it to Yemaya. If delivering via Oshun's waters, a gift for Oshun is also customary.

Quantities

1 pomegranate
Honey
A piece of paper and something to write with
An offering for Oshun (if delivering to fresh water)

Directions

  1. Cut the pomegranate in half.
  2. Coat both cut surfaces with honey.
  3. Write the name of the person seeking to conceive on a piece of paper, along with any message to Yemaya.
  4. Place the paper between the two halves and press them back together.
  5. Carry the sealed pomegranate to the ocean and release it into her waters.
  6. If the sea is not accessible, a river, stream, or waterfall may be used instead; these belong to Oshun, Yemaya's daughter or sister depending on the tradition, who will pass the message along.
  7. If using Oshun's waters, bring her a gift as well.
  8. Yemaya is the elder and does not return the favor of message-passing.

The pomegranate is one of the most universal symbols of fertility across the ancient world — its chambers full of seed, its flesh red as blood, its crown like a many-pointed star. Honey seals the petition with sweetness, asking that what is within the fruit be received with the same.

The postal logic of this working is elegant: the pomegranate becomes a sealed letter addressed to Yemaya and physically delivered to her domain. The ocean is the direct address; rivers and waterways are routed through Oshun, who holds authority over fresh waters.

The relationship between Yemaya and Oshun is familial and asymmetric. Oshun is younger — daughter or sister depending on which path of the tradition you follow — and will intercede on a petitioner's behalf. But Yemaya, as elder, operates on her own terms and does not carry messages in the other direction. Bringing Oshun a gift when routing through her waters acknowledges her as more than a postal system: she is a power in her own right, generous but deserving of recognition.
