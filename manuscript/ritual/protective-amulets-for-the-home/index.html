Traditional practices for placing protective amulets throughout the home to guard against evil, malevolent spirits, and negative energy. Includes horseshoes, mirrors, protective woods, and frightening amulets.

Quantities

A horseshoe, or a blue glass horseshoe amulet
Small round mirrors, optionally set in a wooden disk
Hawthorn, blackthorn, yew, or rowan wood, optionally with red thread
A gargoyle, chimera, or Garuda image
A Devil Nut (water chestnut) seed

Directions

  1. Horseshoe Placement
  2. The horseshoe is a ubiquitous protective amulet made of iron.
  3. Legends about proper placement conflict.
  4. Some believe the shoe must be hung with points up or else the luck will run out.
  5. Others believe it must be hung point down so luck pours into you.
  6. Trust your intuition and inclination when choosing orientation.
  7. The origin of the horseshoe's use as an amulet is lost in antiquity.
  8. Some believe it originated as a clandestine symbol of Lunar Goddess worship connected to underground witch cults.
  9. Another legend attributes its power to Saint Dunstan, a blacksmith who became Archbishop of Canterbury.
  10. The story says he was asked to shoe the Devil's horse and instead placed a shoe on the Devil's hoof itself.
  11. He would only remove it if the Devil promised to never bother a home that had a horseshoe hanging as an amulet.
  12. Blue glass horseshoe amulets with eyes on them serve as versions of malocco amulets.
  13. These can be hung in the home or car for protection.
  14. Mirror Protection
  15. Mirrors are very popular for repelling evil from the home.
  16. Their use is prevalent in China, where bagua mirrors surrounded by the eight trigrams are placed over doors and out windows to repel evil.
  17. In Morocco, large mirrors in the shape of hamsa hands or eyes repel evil in the same way.
  18. To use mirrors in protecting the home, purchase small round mirrors from a craft store and place them near doorways and windows to reverse negative energy sent to you.
  19. For more complex protection, set the mirror in a small wooden disk and surround the glass with protection symbols.
  20. Use this spoken charm when placing the mirror at its post:
  21. Mirror shield, where you are set
    No spell may pass, nor ill beget
    All devils captured in thy face
    Are driven backwards from this place
    By the name of fearsome Hekate
    And by my will, so shall it be.
  22. Protective Woods
  23. Certain woods protect against occult attacks.
  24. In parts of England, hedges of hawthorn and blackthorn are grown to keep away spirits.
  25. Hawthorn is also the traditional wood used to make stakes to impale vampires upon.
  26. In Bosnia, hawthorn is placed upon the navel to keep corpses from returning to life.
  27. Yew trees are rumored to keep the dead at bay and are planted in graveyards for that very reason.
  28. Hanging thorny branches of almost any kind over a doorway to keep away witches is a custom spread across Europe and also exists amongst Native Americans.
  29. In England, rowan crosses bound with red thread are another popular amulet against witchcraft.
  30. All of these protective woods can be bound to the rafters and beams of a house to strengthen the structure against magickal attack.
  31. Frightening Amulets
  32. Some amulets protect by frightening away demonic spirits rather than creating barriers.
  33. Gargoyles or Chimeras are the best known, seen on many buildings and churches in almost any major city.
  34. While used architecturally to direct rainwater, their fearsome appearance makes them protectors of the building and its inhabitants.
  35. In Tibet and Nepal, a Garuda image of metal or wood is often seen above the door.
  36. The Garuda is a fearsome mythical bird that routs out troubles caused by evil Nagas, most often seen with a serpent in its mouth to symbolize this.
  37. Another amulet is the Devil Nut (Trapa bicornis), the seed of a Chinese plant sometimes called the water chestnut.
  38. It looks somewhat like a Devil with horns or a bat.
  39. This seed is used for protection in Nepal and America, where it is known as a devil nut or bat nut.
  40. It is hung above the door to frighten away evil in the same way that the Garuda and gargoyle do.
