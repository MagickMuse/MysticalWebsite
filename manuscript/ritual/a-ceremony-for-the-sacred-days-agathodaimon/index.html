Hellenic household ceremony for the second day of the lunar month, the day immediately after Noumenia, which is dedicated to Agathodaimon, the household guardian who determines the fortune of the people of the home and stands beside Zeus Ktesios as the bringer of wealth and prosperity. The structure of the rite is generic on purpose, written so that the same shape, opening to the ancestral Gods, hymn at Hestia's hearth, hymn or prayer to the God of the day, and the closing thanksgiving, can be reused for any sacred day of the lunar calendar by simply substituting hymns appropriate to whichever God or Goddess is being honoured.

Quantities

  • Red wine for libations at every station
  • Generic fragrance incense for Hestia (rose, jasmine, sandalwood, or any household fragrance the home keeps)
  • Olybanum (frankincense) incense for Agathodaimon
  • A cup or phiale dedicated to Agathodaimon for the libation
  • Optional honey, barley, milk, or bread offerings to set on Agathodaimon's altar
  • A clean cloth and fresh water if statues or shrine items are being tended

Directions

  1. Begin in a settled state of mind, bathed and in clean clothing.
  2. Set the altar with the wine vessel, the dish of Agathodaimon (ah-gah-tho-DY-mon), the burner with charcoal, and the fragrance incense for Hestia (HEH-stee-ah).
  3. Stand at the altar in stillness, then speak the opening:
  4. Come O' Esteemed and Ancestral Gods;
    Gods honoured in the homes of our forefathers.
    Come O' Immortal Gods, rulers of the holy days,
    that You may be venerated as is your due.
    Come with benevolence, as Friends and Protectors of our household,
    as You were to the line of our forefathers.
  5. Libate red wine and set the offerings on the altar.
  6. Move to Hestia's hearth or hearth-shrine.
  7. Kindle the fragrance incense and speak the hymn:
  8. Hestia (HEH-stee-ah), royal daughter of almighty Kronos (KROH-nos),
    You who holds the central abode, the magnificent perpetual fire,
    Elevate the respectful suppliants who worship You in their holy ceremonies,
    Grant to them ever-flourishing happiness, glad and pure,
    O' dwelling of the Blissful Gods, steady support of mortals,
    Eternal, glow-formed, needed by all,
    Come, smiling Blessed Goddess, accept these offerings with kind heart,
    Inspire happiness and gentle health.
  9. Libate red wine.
  10. Return to Agathodaimon's altar, kindle the olybanum (oh-LIB-ah-num), and raise the prayer:
  11. I address myself to You, O' Bountiful guardian of our Household
    who determines the fortune of people.
    You who are at the centre of our home,
    bringer of wealth, vigilant counsel of mortals.
    Fertile, gentle god,
    companion of Agathe Tyche (ah-gah-THEH TEE-keh),
    mighty foundation of happiness.
    To You, I raise my cup, pouring a sacred libation.
  12. Libate red wine.
  13. Then continue:
  14. May You, the footprint of the Immortals on earth,
    be the mentor of our Household.
    That prosperity, good fortune and wealth
    never be absent from our home.
    Welcome Agathodaimon (ah-gah-tho-DY-mon),
    companion to Zeus Ktesios (ZOOS k'TEH-see-os).
  15. Close the rite at the central altar:
  16. Khairete (KHY-reh-teh) O' Blessed Gods,
    Be with us always,
    Never leave,
    Cease all illness
    and drive away sorrow.
  17. Libate red wine.
  18. Seal the working with the closing word:
  19. ΓΕΝΟΙΤΟ (yeh-NEE-toh, it is done).
  20. Afterwards, leave the offerings in place for the day.
  21. Return spent incense, ash, and the libated portion to clean ground or running water so the offerings may pass back to the Earth.
  22. Any shared bread, honey, or barley is eaten at the household table, since the food carries the blessing of Agathodaimon forward into the days that follow.
  23. This ceremony is a template.
  24. To honour any other sacred day of the lunar calendar, keep the opening, the hymn at Hestia's hearth, and the closing intact, and substitute the prayer to Agathodaimon with a hymn proper to the God or Goddess of that day.
