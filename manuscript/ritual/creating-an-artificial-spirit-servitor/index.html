◇Description
A method for creating an artificial spirit (servitor, fetch, egregore, or artificial elemental) for magickal defense and protection purposes. The spirit is shaped from energy and programmed with specific functions, then either bound to an object or sent to carry out its mission.

An artificial spirit (servitor) is an autonomous thought form created from energy, shaped and programmed by the practitioner to carry out specific magickal tasks independently. Unlike helper spirits or deities that exist independently, servitors are manifestations of the practitioner's will given form and function.

Planning Your Servitor

Before creating a servitor, plan thoroughly. Journal, meditate, or use divination to clarify your design. Consider what you want your servitor to do, how you want it to manifest, how you will feed it to maintain it, and how you will discontinue it (whether self-terminating or manually decommissioned).

Function: Define the problem or task clearly. Be specific about what the servitor will address. The clearer your intention, the better the servitor will function. All the rest of the creation process hinges on what you are trying to accomplish.

Form: Choose a form that indicates the spirit's function. A servitor made to warn of danger could take the shape of a cloud of eyes and ears. A servitor meant to guard a door could take the form of a knight in armor. Be aware that the servitor may take on the characteristics of its form. If you want a servitor to charge into battle, do not make it cute and cuddly. If you are concerned about causing harm, do not make it into the form of a bear.

Name: Choose a name that represents its function. You can jumble letters from one word or condense a combination of words. The word "protector" could become "Rectoport." The words "binder of harm" can become "Binderham" by removing the word "of" and the repeating letter "r" from harm. An artificial spirit created primarily from an elemental or planetary force can be named with a word that recalls that force. Madim (Hebrew name for Mars) could be used for a servitor made with energy from that planetary sphere.

Sigil: Construct a sigil from traditional methods by combining letters into a symbol where individual letters are not immediately apparent. You can trace a sigil upon kameas or Rose Cross Lamen if you know how. You can also make a chart of your own construction and trace a sigil on it.

Physical Link: Though not strictly necessary, writing the name and sigil on paper forms a magickal link and home for the spirit. This paper can be used to feed the servitor and issue further instructions. The spirit's orders and symbols appropriate to the working can be added, as can oils and powders consistent with the spirit's nature. For long-term spirits, engrave the sigil and name onto a statue or other object that will serve as a powerful guardian.

End Date: Decide if you want to give your servitor an expiry date or if you will be in charge of disassembling it when it no longer serves its function. Some practitioners give servitors a traditional time span of a year and a day. You can also tie the end date to completion of the task. Servitors can be ongoing helpers, but temporary servitors with clear end conditions are safer for beginners. Use astronomical events to mark time: the next equinox, the new moon, or when the Sun moves into a new sign.

The Creation Process

You can make your creation ritual as elaborate or as simple as you desire. Some practitioners simply ground, create sacred space, light a white candle, and work with visualization. If you prefer more intricate ceremony, include crystals such as lapis lazuli, apatite, clear quartz, and rainbow moonstone to aid creative energies. Use purple or silver candles carved with your intention or the word "servitor." Burn vanilla, cinnamon, or ginger incense to boost creative force.

Preparation: Banish the space you are working in or set up a circle according to whatever methods you normally use. Set a place a few feet in front of you where the spirit will manifest. Place a triangle in this spot to help the manifestation. If you are using paper or another object to serve as a connection to the spirit, place it in the spot where the spirit will manifest.

Drawing Energy: Begin by drawing whatever type of energy you will be using into yourself. Earth may be chosen for providing protection, Water for smoothing over tense situations and engendering understanding, Fire for aggressive defense, and Air for communication and awareness. The forces of the planets can be breathed into the body as well by performing the ritual during the proper planetary day and hour while concentrating on the colors and symbols of the planet. When the body is filled with force, you are ready to create.

Manifestation: Set your gaze upon the space set aside for the spirit to manifest within. Eject the force through your navel and see it shoot from you and form a cloud in front of you. Through force of will, command the cloud of energy to take the shape you have determined ahead of time. The more detail you put into this, the better the spirit will manifest. If you are very talented at visualizing, you can imagine the energy taking the form of microscopic versions of the spirit's sigil, which then form the cells of the being and eventually coalesce into the form of the servitor.

Naming and Ordering: Once you see the spirit in front of you, it is time to cut the connection, name it, and give it its orders. A simple "I name you [name]; you are [function]" will suffice for the naming. Its orders should be succinct and to the point. If the spirit is meant for temporary use, you must give it an order to dissipate at a future time whether it has accomplished its task or not.

Animation: Imbue the servitor with a bit of your energy to give it life. Visualize a small ball of white energy leaving your body and entering the servitor. This is the life spark that will animate it.

Release or Binding: After you have issued orders, command it to depart and carry them out. If you are binding the spirit to an object, see the servitor sink into the object. If you are not binding it, simply see the servitor fly off to carry out its mission.

Warnings

If you are planning to keep the servitor around permanently, you must be vigilant in feeding it energy and reinforcing its programming at regular intervals. Neglected permanent servitors can begin to take nourishment and programming from elsewhere, as with Alexandra David Néel's runaway tulpa that shifted from good-natured to sinister when it changed outside her control.
