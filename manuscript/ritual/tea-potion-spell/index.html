◇Description
The master ritual for brewing magickal tea potions. Tea is inherently magickal because it incorporates all four elements: boiling water (Fire and Water), herbs (Earth), and aromatic steam (Air). To make any cup of tea with magickal intent is to brew a potion. To drink a potion is to cast an indrinking spell, drawing the magickal power of the brew into yourself.

The brewing of magickal potions is a traditional skill of witches. Images of the cackling witches in Macbeth might come to mind with their bubbling cauldron, or one may think of Cerridwen, whose legendary potion had to brew for a year to yield three drops of magickal elixir. In reality, potions can be made quickly, simply, and from ordinary ingredients.

Preparation

These potions are best made in privacy when you are calm, centered, and feeling able to enchant the potion with the power of your intent. You could use them when you have specific needs, or on a regular basis to maintain positive conditions in your life.

Whenever possible, use your finest china, your best table linens, your good silverware. If you use the potions regularly, make a small ceremony of their preparation. Such rituals are comforting, centering, and meaningful. They encourage the flow of magick through you and throughout your life.

Ethics

These potions are meant to be made by you for yourself. It is unethical to administer a potion to someone without that person's knowledge and informed consent. To violate someone in that way is to invite negative repercussions to rebound upon you.

The Spell

A potion spell begins when you first think about making it. As you gather everything you need, focus your magickal attention on the effect you want your potion to have. Visualize the changes you would like the potion to manifest.

Get into a magickal mood. Create sacred space where you will make and drink the potion. Empty your kettle of standing water and fill it fresh. Heat until the water is at a full boil with steam pouring from the spout. Regard the boiling kettle and say:

Powers of Water, Powers of Fire,
Bring to me what I desire.

Put the herbs in the pot, or the tea bags into the cup, and pour the boiling water over them. Allow the brew to steep. Inhale its steam and say:

Powers of Earth, Powers of Air,
Draw to me all that is fair.

Remove the tea bags or pour the tea into a cup. Raise power. Hold your hands over the cup, focusing on infusing the potion with your magickal intent. This is the most important part of the spell, so use full power and give it your complete attention. Stir the tea deosil and say:

This cup of tea is now enchanted,
with seeds of [your intention],
firmly planted.
Aromatic magickal potion,
As I drink, set my spell in motion.

Stirring and Symbols

Always stir deosil (clockwise) because you are working to attract vibrations that will help you achieve your objective. Some potions call for symbols such as hearts or runes to be stirred into them. First stir deosil, then trace the shape of the symbol. You may substitute an alternate symbol such as a different rune. Focus on your magickal objective while drinking.

Ingredient Notes

Water: Tap water is fine, but spring or filtered water is preferable. You may collect water from natural sources like rain, melted snow, wells, streams, or springs. Only use water you are certain is safe to drink.

Tea: When black tea is specified, it means ordinary tea such as orange pekoe. Lemon balm tea means Melissa officinalis. Ginger tea is brewed from freshly peeled ginger root. Cinnamon tea is brewed from cinnamon sticks or chips. Rose tea is made from rose petals, rose hips, or both. For potions calling for multiple teas, use two tea bags in the cup or a spoonful of each herb per cup in the pot.

Sweetener: Honey has the magickal power of attraction, making it the ideal sweetener. Candied ginger also makes a good sweetener with its many magickal qualities. Sugar works, or no sweetener at all. Artificial sweeteners have no magick in them.

Citrus: Lemon has the magickal powers of repelling and averting, so avoid using it unless specifically listed. When the peel of any citrus is called for, it means the zest, the colorful outer part of the skin. Citrus peel may be fresh or dried. Do not add milk to potions containing citrus, as it will curdle.

Crystals: When a potion calls for a crystal, place it in the cup with the tea but DO NOT INGEST IT. Wash the stone after drinking and reserve for future use.

Cinnamon Sticks: Use to stir the tea, then discard after drinking.

Customization

Taste is individual, so you may not care for all potions. Do not use a potion whose taste you dislike or one containing ingredients you are allergic to. Wherever possible, more than one recipe is given. Feel free to experiment and create your own potions.
