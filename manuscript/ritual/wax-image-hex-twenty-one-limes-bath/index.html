An ancient Semitic formula to antidote a wax-cast curse. Twenty-one limes are prayed over, fumigated with benzoin and frankincense, and used to bathe the afflicted person standing on large leaves or brown paper. The limes and wrapping are deposited in the sea or far from home. A variant calls for three limes each of seven different species, or seven different citrus fruits emphasizing sour, bitter, or acidic varieties. One of the Antidote remedies.

Quantities

21 limes (or 3 limes × 7 species, or 7 different citrus fruits, emphasize sour/bitter/acidic)
Benzoin (for fumigation)
Frankincense (for fumigation)
Large leaves or brown paper (to stand on)
Salt (optional, for disposal)

Directions

  1. Obtain twenty-one limes.
  2. Pray over them and charge them with their purpose.
  3. Fumigate the limes with benzoin and frankincense.
  4. Ideally, have the person stand on large leaves; if unavailable, stand on brown paper.
  5. Squeeze the fruits into a bowl.
  6. Bathe the person with the fruits and juice.
  7. Drop the squeezed-out limes onto the brown paper.
  8. When ritual bathing is complete, have the person hop off the paper.
  9. Wrap up the limes in the paper.
  10. Deposit the limes and leaves or paper in the sea; alternatively, sprinkle with salt and dispose of far from home via a circuitous route.
  11. As an alternative, use three limes each of seven species, or seven different citrus fruits with an emphasis on sour, bitter, or acidic over sweet.
