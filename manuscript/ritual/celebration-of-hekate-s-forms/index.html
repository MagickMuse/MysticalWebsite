⬜Celebration of Hekate's Forms

◇Tradition
Eclectic
◇Solitary or Group
Solitary
◇Form
SigilConsecrated Item
◇Setting
Indoor
◇Method

◇Category
Invocation
◇Difficulty
Advanced
◇Quantities
1 pen, 1 sheet of paper, 1 bowl, matches, 2 white candles, myrrh incense with a censer or heat-safe holder, cinnamon for dusting the hands.
◇Directions
1. At a liminal hour, wash thrice and dust your hands with cinnamon. Draw Hekate's sigil as before, and burn myrrh incense. 2. Light two white candles this time: one at the sigil's center as before, and a second on the trident point that faces away from you. 3. Recall that Hekate is both Many-Formed (POLYMORPHOS) and Many-Faced (POLYPROSOPOS), and also AZOSTOS, the Unconstrained One who defies definition. Declaring some of her traditional forms aloud aligns the reader with those who have named her before. 4. Decide in advance whether you will speak the three lines beginning "CALLEO SEH" ("I call you; hear me, Queen"). Speak them only, and only with full force, if you truly wish to draw her attention; omit them if you are uncertain. 5. Close your eyes and steady your breath. Picture yourself again standing at the sigil's center on the sandy path, the sea to your left and the dark wood to your right. Watch the storm cell wheel overhead; inhale and exhale three times as the sigil rises beneath you, aligning with your spine and crowning your brow with its trident. Feel the lightning crack and the flame course through you once more. 6. Look down: the earth has vanished, and you hover above a churning sea that begins spiraling into a whirlpool, mirroring the storm above. Recognize this as approaching communion with a vast, impartial force, an act that cannot be undone. Continue only if you are willing to enter both the storm above and the vortex below. 7. Return your attention to the crossroads path and feel the weight of your own body. Acknowledge your strengths and flaws without shame; what is asked of you here is determination, courage, and clear-headed resolve, not self-abasement. 8. Turn to the pole at the crossroads' crux and look at the third mask. Take it in your hands, press it to your brow, eyes, and lips, and recognize it as your own face. 9. Open your eyes with a slow exhale and become present in the room again. Cover the sigil and read the hymn below aloud thrice, with force and clarity, pronouncing each name distinctly: "O Indomitable Darkness that dwells / In the heart of Light; / O Formless Fire that informs / The womb of Night; / O woman astride an open grave / Giving birth to life; / O child with a dog's face / Whose left hand is a knife: / POLYMORPHOS POLYPROSOPOS / APHRATTOS APLETOS / ANESSA ANESSA / HEKATE! // O mile-high Bull who pulls the plow / Through a field of stars; / O Companion on the journey / Whose touch heals journey's scars; / O infernal Flame whose heat / Begins celestial wars; / O Sound of three dogs barking / With no apparent source: / CALLEO SEH CLOUTHI MEU ANESSA! / CALLEO SEH CLOUTHI MEU BASILEA! / CALLEO SEH CLOUTHI MEU HEKATE! // O Radiant Serpent who sheds the night / To reveal her true complexion; / O Six Hands crowning a flaming eye, / Extending in every direction; / O Mirror taking what's without, within / To forge unseen connection; / O Tongue that tells the secret names / With intimate inflection; / POLYMORPHOS POLYPROSOPOS / APHRATTOS APLETOS / ANESSA ANESSA / HEKATE!" 10. Close your eyes once more; sense the heat, light, and form of both candles before you, then extend your awareness left, right, above, below, and behind you, seeking any other presence in the room. Take your time. 11. Open your eyes, extinguish both flames, then kiss and burn the sigil and store the ash.
◇Description
A liminal-hour invocation celebrating Hekate's many forms and formlessness, culminating in taking on her third mask as one's own. Rite 4 of Book I.

Context

The fourth of nine nights leading to the new moon dedication, five days before it, in which the reader declares aloud several of Hekate's traditional forms and epithets and, if ready, speaks three lines of direct summons before taking on the likeness of the crossroads' third mask as her own.

Recommended Reading

The Homeric Hymn to Demeter, which recounts Persephone's abduction by Hades in detail and emphasizes Hekate's role as companion and ally to Persephone and Demeter.
