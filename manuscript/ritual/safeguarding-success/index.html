Lughnasadh

A Lughnasadh protection charm that purifies and fortifies the ‘valuables’ of your life, money, home, work, relationships, by passing their herbal stand‑ins through consecrated water, then sealing with an outdoor offering.

Quantities

  • Fresh herbs chosen to represent each asset you wish to protect (use sprigs or whole pieces; bundle powders in colored cloth)
  • Large bowl three‑quarters full of water (spring, rain, river, ocean, or tap)
  • One apple (for the closing offering)
  • Optional: cloth squares and thread for bundling powdered herbs

Directions

  1. Preparation:
  2. Make a list of assets/valuables to protect. Choose a matching fresh herb for each (e.g., basil or oregano for wealth, cinnamon stick for success/courage, rosemary for home, lavender or rose for love, dandelion blossoms for joy). Favor whole sprigs or sticks; bundle powders in a colored cloth that matches the intent.
  3. Line the herbs along the front edge of your altar. Place a large bowl of water at center.
  4. Consecrate the water:
  5. Invite the element of Water into the bowl, visualizing river, ocean, and storm. Feel purification and protective flow gathering.
  6. Work each herb in turn:
  7. Hold the first herb and concentrate on the asset it represents. Speak:
    “This is not [HERB], but it is my [ASSET] that I hold in my hand.”
  8. Slowly drag the herb through the water, envisioning obstacles washed away and protection infused. Speak:
    “By the power of water, by river and sea, what I have is protected! So mote it be!”
  9. Place the consecrated herb on the far side of the bowl. Repeat for all herbs.
  10. Seal outdoors:
  11. Bring the wet herbs and bowl outside to a natural spot. Place the herbs on the ground and set an apple atop them, stem upwards. Pour the bowl water in a circle around the herbs and apple, saving a few drops to sprinkle on top. Offer thanks for the continued welfare of your assets and leave the herbs and apple as gratitude to the land and spirits.
  12. Notes: This rite echoes historic Lughnasadh protections (livestock passed through waters) applied to modern valuables.
  13. Adjust herbs as intuition and tradition guide.
