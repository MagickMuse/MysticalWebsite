Ritual Grimoire

🌀Physical Labyrinth Journey Underworld

◇Form
Circle Rite
◇Method
InvocationElementalDirectional MagickDivinationAncestor WorkMeditation
◇Category
Ceremony
◇Closed Practice
Indigenous (Sage & Sweetgrass)
◇Quantities
- Indoor space (house, park building, church, library, or museum basement) - Labyrinth template - Masking tape - Dividers (tall cardboard, clotheslines, or waist-height boxes) - Flashlights or LED lamps - Small altars (one at center, one at every major turn) - Costumes for characters (Crone, Grim Reaper, Sphinx, Grandfather, Great Mother, Antlered Man, Tarot Reader) - Two bowls (one with water, one empty) - Coins (participants bring 2+ each) - Pocket items for offerings (buttons, candy, safety pins, small toys) - Ancestor altar: pictures, flowers, books, candles, heirlooms, LED lights, pillows - Tarot deck - Blindfold - Mirror - Hourglass - Scythe prop - Jar - Paper and markers - Manual scale - Feather - Heart-shaped charm - Central altar: representations of Lord and Lady, goblet, beverage, incense, candles - Notebooks and pens - Snacks (nuts and apples) - Bottle and wax for burial - White sage, frankincense, or camphor and sage tea for cleansing
◇Directions
1. PURPOSE: This ritual raises awareness of activity beyond the veil, and builds an understanding of why we need death mythologies and chthonic gods. It also draws on labyrinth symbolism and combines it with the popular motif of the haunted house. Each soul that journeys walks a set path that requires the same steps to go inside and out. This makes the labyrinth the symbol of spiritual work. 2. SETTING: Indoors, you will be putting together a complicated path; best not to struggle with the elements while you're at it! 3. PRE-RITUAL PREPARATIONS: This ritual will take extra preparation, from a few days up to a week. Setting it all up will be a lot of work. First, pick an underworld. Different ancient cultures had different ideas about what the afterlife looked like. 4. In this ritual, part of the group takes the role of guides in the underworld chosen; others are the souls that come to the dead. 5. You can perform this ritual on a large, theatrical scale or on a small, personal scale. 6. Those who take the role of a character in the underworld or the role of a guide should have more experience with ritual practice than those who tour the labyrinth. 7. When assuming the role of a specific god or goddess in a ritual context, sometimes the deity represented will manifest their personality within the body of the person playing that role. 8. This is called aspecting. 9. Make sure someone stays in normal consciousness to act as a spotter for those assuming archetypal roles. 10. Those who choose to travel the labyrinth should wear street clothing with pockets. 11. Advise them to have at least two coins colored gold or silver and to fill their pockets with buttons, candy, safety pins, small toys, or other oddments. 12. These will be offerings at different points of the labyrinth. 13. Use masking tape to lay down the total path of the labyrinth on the floor. 14. You will need to divide the path through the middle of the labyrinth using tall cardboard, clotheslines, or waist-height cardboard boxes with tall items to obscure paths at corners. 15. THE RITUAL: Right before the ritual, the person who acts as the tour guide should walk the labyrinth inside and out, smudging with white sage, frankincense, or asperging a tea of camphor and sage onto the path. 16. The center of the labyrinth should have the main altar with representations of the Lord and Lady, a goblet, a beverage, incense, and candles (or LED alternatives). 17. Someone acting as priest or priestess should go to the labyrinth center to perform invocation: 18. Hail to the elements of nature! Be gentle here on these paths, as we are all still learning. Hail to the living things, those that live in the in-between! We greet you in this middle place tonight. Hail to our ancestors, to our mighty dead! Be gentle in what you teach us tonight, we remember you, and forgive us for grieving our losses, grief is, after all, part of being alive. We greet the Goddess, who has descended, and we greet the God, as we visit him in his realm. Blessed Be! 19. JUST OUTSIDE THE ENTRANCE: Two bowls: one filled with water on the right and the other empty on the left. Guide bids the soul to drop a coin in the empty bowl and wash hands in the bowl of water. 20. As the soul submerges hands in water, guide says: "You are water and water is you. When you cross this water, you cross over it and on into the world between veils." 21. FIRST TURN, THE CRONE: "How now traveler?" Soul should greet the Crone politely and ask her blessing. 22. She points round the next bend: "Go on, you need to talk to your dead first!" 23. NEXT TURN, ANCESTOR ALTAR: Tray with pictures of ancestors or old heroes, decorated with flowers, books, candles, and small heirlooms. Seating pillows and LED lights. 24. Guide: "Sit down and speak your peace. Your ancestors have been waiting to hear from you. Make sure you light their way before you go." 25. Guide produces hourglass, sets it at center: "Get on with it, they haven't got long!" 26. Allow soul to pay respects until hourglass runs out. 27. Guide snatches it up: "Along with you! They haven't got all eternity!" Person turns on LED light and leaves it on the tray. 28. NEXT BEND, THE TAROT READER: Woman shuffling cards says nothing but dons blindfold and holds out fanned deck. Traveler picks one card, looks at it closely, then returns it to the deck. 29. NEXT TURN, THE ANTLERED MAN: Dressed in dark green, holds up mirror and smiles: "Look! This is your truth!" Guide produces hourglass. Traveler gazes upon themselves in dimmed labyrinth until sand runs down. 30. NEXT BEND, THE GRIM REAPER: Blocks path with scythe: "I must speak to the soul." Guide stands aside. 31. Reaper: "Come with me." Takes traveler to table with jar, paper, markers, and LED light. 32. Reaper gestures to table: "Leave it here." When soul has named their maladies on paper and dropped it in jar, reaper says: "From this point, you must go alone." 33. CENTRAL ALTAR: Manual scale, representation of Lord and Lady, bowl, feather, and small heart-shaped charm (anatomically correct if possible). Feather and heart balance on either side of scale. 34. Sign: "Meet darkness with your light." Arrow points to stack of LED tea light candles. 35. Sign in front of bowl: "To return to the land of the living, something must be left for the land of the dead." 36. Traveler decides what to leave in bowl (hair, coin, shoelace, button, or memento). 37. ON THE WAY OUT, THE SPHINX: Blocks path: "Tell me a story! Tell me a story, or I'll give you a riddle!" 38. When traveler has told story or answered riddle, sphinx lets them pass. 39. If person picks riddle and answers wrong, sphinx demands tribute (any random item). 40. NEXT BEND, THE GRANDFATHER: Steps in path: "It's time to teach you." Gives simple lesson, tying a knot, whistling, telling a joke, how to hold a pocketknife properly. 41. If person doesn't catch on first try: "It all takes practice. You'll get it." Hands person something from lesson and sends soul on its way. 42. JUST BEFORE EXIT, THE GREAT MOTHER: Steps into path: "To me all things are created, and to me all things shall return." Anoints person's head with water then steps aside as person exits labyrinth. 43. Spotter guides travelers to table away from those who haven't entered. 44. Table has light, earthy snacks (nuts and apples). 45. Small notebook and pen at each place for travelers to jot down experiences and insights. 46. CLOSING: When last traveler completes journey, priest or priestess asperges entrance/exit and center with water. At center says: 47. To the Lord and Lady, we live in gratitude for what you taught us this night. To the Mighty Dead, we are honored you have walked with us. To the forces of nature, we are grateful for your gentleness. We release each archetype, each spirit, each presence that came for the night's work, and we gladly return to ourselves! So mote it be! 48. Everyone sits down to meal together. 49. After eating, disassemble labyrinth. 50. Give coins to charity or trick-or-treaters. 51. Other odd things travelers left go inside bottle that is sealed with wax and buried.
◇Description
A complex group ritual creating a physical labyrinth journey through the underworld. Participants encounter archetypal characters (Crone, Grim Reaper, Sphinx, Grandfather, Great Mother) at various stations. Combines sacred space work, ancestor veneration, divination, and personal transformation through symbolic death and rebirth journey. ⚠️ Sage and sweetgrass are sacred to Indigenous peoples and should only be used by those within Indigenous traditions.
