Tea Leaf Reading

A method of reading symbols formed by tea leaves in a cup to answer questions and glimpse future events.

Choosing Your Tea

Before beginning a reading, choose your tea with intention. Each variety carries its own energetic signature and will shape the quality of the information that comes through.

Black Tea

Black tea aligns with the energy of the crone, the waning moon, and the threshold between the living and the dead. It is the tea of choice when seeking wisdom from ancestors, messages from those who have passed, or when working within a New Moon ritual context. The fermentation process strips away the softness of the fresh leaf, transforming it through solar energy and time into something more potent. Witches understand death not as an ending but as a passage, and black tea carries that knowledge in its cup.

Green Tea

Green tea carries the energy of optimism, growth, and renewal. Use it when your question involves change: a decision to be made, a move being considered, a pattern ready to be released and replaced. Its fresh-leaf origin aligns it with emerging possibilities rather than established conditions.

Moroccan Tea

Moroccan tea is brewed as a green tea infusion poured over freshly chopped mint. The addition of mint brings its prosperity and fortune associations into the reading, making this blend ideal for questions about money, career, and financial direction.

First and Second Flush

Flush refers to the harvest timing of the tea leaves, not the variety. First flush teas are gathered from the earliest spring growth, connecting them to maiden energy and new beginnings. Use first flush green tea when seeking insight on romantic possibilities and the opening of love. Second flush teas are harvested in the fullness of summer and carry a more mature, settled energy. Use second flush when reading on established relationships, long-term commitments, or family dynamics.

Oolong

Oolong sits between green and black tea, only partially fermented. This in-between nature makes it especially suited to readings about crossroads and mixed conditions. When you are weighing two clear options, navigating ambiguity, or finding that cards like the Chariot or the Tower keep appearing in your spreads without resolution, oolong is the tea to brew.

How to Read Tea Leaves

When preparing tea for a reading, do not strain the leaves. Leave them loose in the cup.

The Process

  1. Focus clearly on your question while drinking the tea.
  2. Drink all but a small amount of liquid, leaving the leaves behind.
  3. Swirl the remaining liquid and leaves gently around the cup.
  4. Turn the cup upside down onto the saucer, positioning the handle at the six o'clock position.
  5. Rotate the overturned cup three times on the saucer.
  6. Return the cup to its upright position.

Reading the Symbols

Read the symbols that appear in the cup by moving clockwise from the handle. Events expected soonest will appear closest to the handle, with timing extending further around the cup as you go.

See the Symbol Grimoire for interpretation of common shapes and figures.

Symbol Types

Tasseomancy is highly intuitive and relies on a strong command of symbolic association. The shapes that appear in the leaves fall into five general categories:

  • Numbers
  • Letters
  • Specific objects
  • Identifiable animals
  • Mythical creatures or beings

Cup Positions

Where a symbol appears is as significant as the symbol itself. Read from the handle clockwise:

  • The rim: The present moment, the questioner's current frame of reality.
  • The sides: The near future, the sphere of influence just about to come to pass.
  • The bottom: The distant future, an outcome that will take time to materialize and will be shaped by the actions the questioner takes or does not take.
  • The handle: The questioner's immediate environment, the circumstances under which they are currently living and working.

The Reading

The diviner prepares the tea and the questioner drinks in a meditative state, allowing a clear question to form inwardly. The questioner need not share the question; a skilled reader works through the herbs' connection to source energy and truth surfaces regardless. Sharing the question, however, allows the diviner to contextualize symbols more precisely and interpret both their type and placement together.

Customizing Your Blend

Tasseomancy draws on all four classical elements: the herbs carry earth and air, the water comes from the kettle, and fire heats it. You can deepen this elemental alignment by choosing herbs that correspond to your specific question. Use loose-leaf tea that is chopped but not heavily processed so the leaves form readable shapes in the cup.

A few starting points:

  • Rosemary for questions touching on past memories or ancestral themes
  • Rose to amplify readings focused on love and relationships
  • Mint for questions about luck, fortune, and financial direction

Coffee Grounds Reading

Reading coffee grounds (tasseography) is a companion practice originating in Turkey and extending across the Middle East, where it has been practiced since the 1600s. Turkish coffee's thick, sediment-rich preparation makes it a particularly readable medium.

Preparing the Coffee

The coffee must be ground to a very fine powder; the degree of grind matters more than the quality of the bean. Combine the ground coffee and sugar (if desired) in a small copper Turkish coffee pot, fill with water, and bring to a medium boil. When foam accumulates at the top (within about 5 minutes), spoon the foam into a white cup, boil the grounds a few seconds longer, then pour everything over the foam.

The Reading Process

The questioner drinks the coffee, then places a saucer over the rim. They swirl the cup three times, invert it onto the saucer, and allow it to rest for about 10 minutes. The diviner then reads the symbols in the grounds, using the same intuitive and symbolic approach as tea leaf reading.

Cup Positions

The coffee cup is read along two axes:

PositionEnergy
NorthNegative influence
SouthPositive influence
EastNear future
WestDistant future

Both the cup and the saucer are read: the cup reflects the questioner's outer life (work, commerce, livelihood), while the saucer reflects their inner and home life (relationships, matters of the heart). Note the size, intensity, and distribution of symbols as well as any significant trails or shapes in the sediment.
