📜Fra Geremia's Healing Ritual

A 16th-century healing ritual that brought the Franciscan friar Geremia Da Udine local celebrity (and also an invitation to speak with the Inquisition). This method was intended to provide healing and identify an ailing person's patron saint simultaneously. The ritual involves writing saints' names on paper, selecting one by lot, and placing it beneath the patient's pillow.

Quantities

  • 12 pieces of paper (one for each saint, or another number of your choosing)
  • A pen
  • A hat or container for mixing the papers
  • A pillow

Directions

  1. Begin with a set number of saints.
  2. Fra Geremia relied exclusively on the Apostles, so this spell is traditionally cast with twelve.
  3. You can adopt his method exactly using only the twelve Apostles, or select any twelve saints, or choose another number entirely.
  4. Bibliomancy techniques can determine the saints' names, or you can select from among your favorites.
  5. This works well when you do not know who will help.
  6. Write each saint's name on a separate piece of paper, one name per slip.
  7. Fold each paper.
  8. Place the folded slips into a hat and shake them up.
  9. Select one paper and place it beneath the ailing person's pillow.
  10. Should the condition improve within twenty-four hours, the saint whose name is on the paper receives credit and is identified as the person's patron.
  11. The healed person (or a parent, if the patient is a child) is then required to honor that saint, although only after recovery is complete.
  12. In Fra Geremia's day, the traditional gesture of respect was to fast on that saint's feast day, but other offerings work as well.
  13. If there is no improvement within twenty-four hours, burn that paper and choose another.
  14. If after twelve days (each paper having been attempted) there has been no improvement, a different solution or approach must be found.
  15. Note: Fra Geremia achieved tremendous success with this technique, which caught the eye of the Venetian Inquisition in 1590.
  16. What troubled them?
  17. Burning the papers was perceived as disrespectful and potentially coercive, similar to the modern practice of turning statues of Saint Expedite upside down to make him hurry up and deliver.
  18. Conceivably, a saint might provide a cure just to prevent having a name burned or reputation besmirched.
