Ritual Grimoire

Ritual Types

The Ritual Database organizes workings by their physical form and method. Understanding these categories helps you choose the right approach for your intention and find similar workings across traditions.

Types of Magick (per Family Tradition)

Sympathetic Magick: "This IS That."

One object is treated as being another. Energy directed into the magickal object passes into the object it represents. Examples include poppets, sigils, and candle-burning spells.

Comparative Magick: "This REPRESENTS That."

A relationship is established between a magickal object and another object, typically using the word "as." Energy directed into the magickal object also enters the other. Examples include charms, connections, and growth spells.

Directive Magick: "This AFFECTS That."

The most common type. Magickal energy is raised, focused on a goal, directed into an object, and released for accomplishment. Examples include candle spells, herbal pouches, and stones and crystals.

Transference Magick: "This ENTERS That."

Negative energy is moved from an affected object into another. Common tools include onion or garlic braids, garlic cloves, and salt. In rare cases, deadly energy can be moved into a willing host, which may die as a result, such as a plant in a hospital room or a willing animal pet or familiar. Willingness is determined through intuitive communication.

Ritual Types and Techniques

Ancestor Work / Necromancy

What it is:

Magick specifically involving the dead. Communication with deceased ancestors, offerings, elevation work, graveyard rituals. Distinct from general spirit work.

When to use:

Seeking ancestral guidance, honoring the dead, requesting assistance from deceased family, elevation rituals for stuck spirits, graveyard work for specific intentions.

Examples:

Ancestor altar offerings, cemetery rituals, spirit elevation work, ancestral petitions, Samhain rituals.

Note:

Many graveyard and necromantic practices are closed by culture. Research before practicing.

Astral / Spiritual Journeying

What it is:

Intentional travel in non-physical realms for guidance, messages, problem-solving, or entity contact. Not the same as meditation.

When to use:

Seeking guidance from other realms, entity contact, problem-solving through spiritual travel, retrieving lost parts of self, exploring non-physical spaces.

Examples:

Hedge riding, shamanic journeying (closed in many cultures), trance-based movement between worlds, pathworking.

Note:

Requires specific techniques, tools, and safety guidelines. Practice with care.

Banishing

What it is:

Magick that removes something or someone from your life or space. Can target people, spirits, energies, or situations. Generally considered defensive rather than harmful. Focuses on removal, not harm.

When to use:

Toxic person leaves your life, unwanted spirit departs, negative energy clears from space, bad habit breaks, illness leaves the body.

Duration:

Immediate effect, but may require reinforcement.

Bath / Wash Magick

What it is:

Magickal baths, floor washes, cleansing washes, foot baths. Massive historical category across cultures.

When to use:

Road opening, spiritual cleansing, attracting luck, blessing a space, removing negativity, preparing for ritual work.

Examples:

Road opener baths, cleansing baths with herbs, Florida Water washes, floor washes, uncrossing baths.

Common methods:

Herbal infusions added to bath water, bathing with intention, pouring wash over threshold, wiping down surfaces with charged water.

Binding

What it is:

Magick that restricts or attaches. Can be defensive (restricting harmful actions) or positive (binding yourself to good things, binding willing participants together).

When to use:

Defensive: Stopping someone from causing harm, preventing gossip, neutralizing magickal attacks.

Positive: Securing prosperity, strengthening consensual relationships, binding yourself to success or healthy habits.

Duration:

Ongoing while the binding object remains intact or buried.

Ethics note:

Binding another person without consent removes free will, even if intended positively. Binding yourself to positive things or binding willing participants together is generally accepted.

Blessing

What it is:

Calling down positive energy, divine favor, or beneficial power onto a person, place, object, or situation. Can invoke deities, spirits, ancestors, or universal forces. Opposite of cursing.

When to use:

Welcoming new baby, blessing a home, consecrating ritual tools, sending good wishes, invoking protection and prosperity, marking life transitions, gratitude offerings.

Duration:

Can be momentary or lasting depending on intent and method.

Common methods:

Spoken prayers or invocations, anointing with oil or water, smoke cleansing, laying on of hands, sprinkling blessed water, marking with symbols.

Candle Magick

What it is:

Magick performed using candles as the primary tool. One of the largest categories in modern practice. May involve dressing with oils, carving, color correspondences, moving candles, flame reading, glass reading, or multi-candle arrangements.

When to use:

Nearly any intention. Candle magick is versatile, accessible, and foundational to many traditions.

Examples:

7-day candles, reversing candles, dual-color candles, moving candle spells, candle petitions, vigil lights.

Common methods:

Dressing candles with oils, carving names or symbols, color selection based on intent, burning in specific patterns, reading flame behavior and wax remains.

Amulet

What it is:

A protective object worn or carried to ward off evil, danger, or negative energy. Amulets are primarily defensive. They repel or shield rather than attract. Historically placed in tombs, carried into battle, and worn for safe travel.

The early Greeks called amulets amylon, meaning "food," suggesting people used food offerings to ask gods and goddesses for protection, perhaps eating or carrying a small bit as a token.

When to use:

Protection from harm, warding off evil spirits, shielding from negative energy, safe travel, guarding against illness or misfortune.

What can be used:

Many items found in the natural world have been made into amulets: plants, carved stones, metal objects. Amulets were often chosen for their shapes or where they were found:

  • Holey stones (any stone with a natural hole): European ancestors carried these to guard against malicious fairies, who would get trapped in the hole
  • Crystals found near sacred wells: Carried to protect the bearer's well-being
  • Amethyst: Long worn against drunkenness, as it increases self-control

Placement:

Amulets can be worn, placed with valuable items, affixed on pet collars, hung in windows, planted in gardens, or put anywhere you want protection. A horseshoe hung above a doorway is a familiar example.

Combining elements:

An amulet may be a single gem or contain several items, just make sure each relates to your intention and holds meaning for you. Going to a party? Amethyst earrings (self-control) plus amber perfume (protection against unwanted advances) creates layered protection.

Historical note:

Ancient Greek soldiers carried amulets into battle. Early Egyptians placed them in tombs of royalty to ensure safe passage into the afterlife

Charm

What it is:

Originally, charms were spoken spells: verbal incantations. The word comes from the Latin carmen, meaning "incantation" or "song." In a time when few could read or write, witches used verbal spells that rhymed or had distinct rhythm, making them easier to memorize.

Today, "charm" also refers to a portable object charged for a specific purpose: carried, worn, or placed in a location. Can be natural (stone, root, bone) or crafted (knotted cord, inscribed metal, assembled object).

When to use:

When you need magick that travels with you or protects a specific location. When the working requires sustained presence. When you want an object you can recharge over time. When speaking your intention aloud strengthens your focus.

Examples:

  • Verbal charms: Rhyming incantations, spoken spells, repeated affirmations
  • Physical charms: Protection stones carried in pocket, knotted cords, carved talismans, crystals programmed for intent

Creating verbal charms:

A charm is like a poem. Many rhyme or have distinct rhythm. The important thing is that it expresses your wish clearly and is easy to remember. Repeat it often to give it more energy and make the magick work faster.

Example:

Leaf of ash, I do thee pluck
To bring to me a day of luck.

You can add physical actions to spoken charms. As you speak "pluck," take a leaf from a tree whose properties match your intention, then carry it with you.

Affirmations vs. Incantations:

  • Affirmations are positive statements in present tense, as if the condition already exists. Keep them short, clear, and precise. Only include what you desire.
    • ✓ "I am healthy in body, mind, and spirit."
    • ✗ "I don't have any illnesses or injuries."
    • ✓ "I now have a job that's perfect for me."
    • ✗ "I want to get a better job."
  • Incantations follow the same rules but are written as rhymes. The catchy phrasing makes them easy to remember and gives them musical power.

Example love incantation:

As the day fades into night
I draw a love that's good and right.
As the night turns into day
We are blessed in every way.

Creative uses:

  • Write on a slip of paper and insert into a talisman or amulet
  • Place under your pillow at night
  • Repeat throughout the day: in the shower, driving to work, before sleep
  • Write on colored paper, decorate with meaningful images, post where you'll see it often

Talisman

What it is:

An object charged to attract specific energies or outcomes. Unlike amulets (which repel), talismans draw things toward you: love, wealth, success, health. Often inscribed with symbols, words, or sigils.

Many old stories say spirits lived in talismans, and whoever possessed the talisman could command the spirit to do specific tasks. Aladdin's lamp holding a genie who granted wishes is a mythological example of a talisman.

When to use:

Attracting love, drawing prosperity, manifesting success, invoking specific planetary or deity energies, enhancing personal power.

Examples:

  • Planetary seals, inscribed metals
  • Jade (Chinese tradition): worn for health, strength, and good fortune
  • Lockets with lover's hair: worn for centuries across cultures
  • Gemstones programmed for attraction
  • Written petitions sealed into objects

Timing:

Fashion talismans while the moon is waxing (between new and full). The waxing moon encourages increase and development.

Placement options:

  • Wear in a cloth or leather pouch
  • Place in a wooden box on your altar
  • If you know feng shui, place in the part of your home that relates to your intention

Creating a talisman:

Create essentially the same way as an amulet, with one or more ingredients that relate to your objective. Consider:

  • The purpose of the spell
  • Your own associations with the objects
  • If carrying: small, lightweight ingredients
  • If wearing: comfort, convenience, and beauty

Tokens as talismans:

Many simple talismans are ordinary objects infused with meaning by their owners. A pretty pebble found during a meaningful walk, touched fondly throughout the day, placed where you'll see it. This token now holds positive energy. The good feelings and thoughts associated with such objects give them their power. Objects from sacred sites are especially ideal.

Lifespan and maintenance:

Because talismans actively send out energy to attract what you desire, they burn out faster than amulets. If you don't get what you want in a reasonable time, you may need to "feed" your talisman to reinforce its strength. Dab a little essential oil on it. Choose an oil from a plant that relates to your talisman's purpose.

Historical note:

Aztec priests used gemstones as talismans to invoke deities and for prophesying. The tradition spans virtually every culture worldwide.

Key distinction:

  • Amulet = defensive, repels harm (passive until activated)
  • Talisman = attractive, draws desired outcomes (actively sends energy, burns out faster)
  • Charm = can be either, originally referred to spoken spells

Cleansing

What it is:

Removing stagnant, negative, or unwanted energy from places, objects, or bodies. One of the most-used categories in every magickal system.

When to use:

After illness or conflict, moving into new space, after unwanted visitors, clearing ritual tools, removing negativity, refreshing stagnant energy.

Examples:

Smoke cleansing (non-sacred herbs), sound cleansing, floor washes, salt bowls, egg cleansings (note: egg limpia is closed practice).

Common methods:

Burning herbs and passing objects through smoke, ringing bells or using sound, washing with salt water, placing salt in corners, sweeping with intention.

Consecration

What it is:

Making something sacred. Setting an object, space, or person apart for spiritual or magickal use. Purifies and charges simultaneously. Often involves blessing, anointing, and dedication to a specific purpose or deity.

When to use:

New ritual tools, altar setup, dedicating sacred space, preparing objects for magickal work, claiming items for spiritual use, temple or shrine creation.

Duration:

Generally permanent unless the item is deconsecrated or the energy needs refreshing.

Common methods:

Cleansing with salt, smoke, or water, then anointing with oil, speaking words of dedication, passing through elements (earth, air, fire, water), invoking deity or spiritual force, charging under moon or sun.

Cord Cutting

What it is:

Severing energetic connections between yourself and another person, situation, or energy. Uses physical cords, thread, or string as sympathetic representation. Cut, burn, or untie the cord to release the connection.

When to use:

Ending toxic relationships, releasing past trauma, breaking unhealthy attachments, moving on from situations that no longer serve you, cutting ties with harmful people.

Intent:

Release and freedom. Not necessarily harmful, often healing. Can be done with love and gratitude for what was while still choosing to move forward separately.

Common methods:

Two candles with cord between them (burn through cord), knotted cord cut with ritual knife, braided cord representing relationship unbraided and burned, visualization paired with cutting physical cord.

Crossing

What it is:

Deliberately blocking someone's path or luck. Laying a spiritual obstacle in their way. Can be done through physical items placed where they'll cross them (crossroads magick) or through ritual work.

When to use:

Blocking opportunities for someone causing harm, preventing someone's plans from succeeding, creating obstacles for toxic people.

Duration:

Until the crossing is removed or walked off.

Curses

What it is:

The most severe form of harmful magick. Intended to cause serious, lasting harm. May span years or even generations in some traditions. Requires significant energy and often has consequences for the caster.

When to use:

Extreme situations where other methods have failed. Many traditions consider curses taboo.

Duration:

Long-term to permanent unless specifically broken.

Warning:

Carries significant risk and ethical weight. Consider consequences carefully.

Decay / Weathering Magick

What it is:

Magick using rot, rust, mold, or decomposition as the spell engine. Niche but historically present.

When to use:

Banishing through decay, transformation through rot, breaking down old patterns, letting something waste away naturally.

Examples:

Leaving written names outside to rot and dissolve, burying biodegradable items to decompose, rust work for corrosion of harmful influences.

Common methods:

Writing names on biodegradable material left to weather, burying items to rot, using rust or decay as sympathetic magick.

Divination Ritual

What it is:

Rituals where cards, runes, scrying, pendulums, or bones are the core magickal act, not just tools for information. Fully functional spells, not just readings.

When to use:

Drawing a card to seal a spell's outcome, casting runes for manifestation, scrying to make contact with entities, using pendulum to direct energy.

Examples:

Drawing tarot card to determine spell method, runic casting for manifestation, scrying for entity contact, bone reading for decision-making magick.

Why it's distinct:

The divination method itself performs the magickal action, not just provides information.

Dream Work

What it is:

Casting spells to influence dreams, induce visions, receive messages, or travel astrally. Distinct from dream interpretation.

When to use:

Seeking guidance through dreams, inducing prophetic visions, lucid dreaming for magickal work, receiving messages from entities or ancestors, astral travel preparation.

Examples:

Sleep sachets under pillows, mugwort teas before bed, poppets or sigils under pillows, pre-sleep invocations, dream incubation rituals.

Common methods:

Herbal preparations (mugwort, lavender, chamomile), charged objects under pillow, spoken intentions before sleep, keeping dream journal as magickal record.

Elixir

What it is:

Liquid preparation meant for consumption or external application. May be charged through intention, moon water, crystal infusion, or spoken words. Must use non-toxic ingredients if consumed.

When to use:

When you want to take magick internally or apply it to skin. When water is the primary carrier for your intention. When you need something that can be shared or distributed.

Examples:

Moon water for various purposes, flower essence remedies, crystal-charged water, herbal infusions for specific intents (using safe herbs only).

Freezer Magick

What it is:

Placing a representation of a person or situation in water, then freezing it. Stops action, slows energy, creates distance, puts things on hold.

When to use:

Stopping gossip, slowing someone's harmful actions, putting distance between you and a person, pausing a situation until you're ready to deal with it, cooling hot tempers.

Common methods:

Name or photo in water in a jar or bag, frozen in back of freezer. Can add herbs, pepper, or vinegar. Leave frozen as long as needed. Thaw and dispose when ready to release.

Hex

What it is:

Magick intended to cause misfortune, bad luck, or general harm to a target. Less severe than a Curses but more aggressive than a Jinx. Often described as "crossing" someone.

When to use:

When someone is causing harm and other methods haven't worked. When consequences are needed but not devastation.

Duration:

Typically temporary unless sustained. Fades over time or when conditions are met.

Hot Foot

What it is:

Making someone leave a location or your life. Traditional Hoodoo technique using specific powders or oils. Forces someone to move, quit, leave town, or exit your sphere.

When to use:

Removing unwanted neighbor, making toxic coworker quit, forcing abuser to move away, getting rid of unwanted romantic attention.

Common methods:

Powders sprinkled where target will walk, oils on their shoes or doorstep, petition work with movement symbolism.

Jar

What it is:

Container magick using glass jars, bottles, or sealed vessels. Ingredients are layered or mixed inside, then sealed. Can be kept active indefinitely, buried, or disposed of when the work is done.

When to use:

When you want to contain, sweeten, sour, protect, or trap energy. When the magick benefits from ingredients combining and steeping over time. When you need a working that stays hidden or sealed.

Examples:

Honey jars for sweetening, vinegar jars for souring, protection jars with nails and herbs, witch bottles buried at property line, freezer jars for binding.

Jinx

What it is:

The mildest form of harmful magick. Causes minor inconvenience, irritation, or temporary bad luck. Often spontaneous or spoken ("jinxing" someone with words). Easiest to cast and easiest to break.

When to use:

Minor annoyances, temporary setbacks for someone who needs a small lesson.

Duration:

Short-term, often self-resolving.

Kitchen / Hearth Magick

What it is:

Magick performed through cooking, stirring, baking, or home tending. One of the most accessible and widely practiced forms of magick.

When to use:

Daily magick, feeding family with intention, blessing the home, creating abundance, protection through domestic work.

Examples:

Stirring clockwise for abundance, baking protective bread, simmer pots, enchanting meals, blessing ingredients.

Common methods:

Stirring direction (clockwise for increase, counterclockwise for decrease), speaking intentions while cooking, choosing ingredients by correspondence, sharing food to share blessings.

Knot Magick

What it is:

Tying knots in cord or string to trap, hold, or release intention. Each knot can hold a specific intention. Untying releases the magick.

When to use:

Storing spells for later release, binding intentions into physical form, creating portable magick, setting conditions for spell release, building power through multiple knottings.

Common methods:

Witch's ladder (9 knots with feathers or beads), speaking intention into each knot, carrying knotted cord as charm, untying one knot per day to release serial magick, sailors' wind knots.

Meditation / Energy Work

What it is:

Magick performed entirely through non-physical means. Includes visualization, breathwork, energy raising, grounding, centering.

When to use:

When physical tools aren't available or appropriate, personal energy work, preparing for ritual, cleansing your own energy, raising power.

Examples:

Chakra clearing, energy balls, aura cleansing, guided journey visualizations, breathwork for power raising, grounding and centering.

Common methods:

Visualization techniques, breath control, energy manipulation through intent, mental focus and direction.

Mirror Box

What it is:

A box lined with mirrors (or reflective material) facing inward, containing a representation of the target (photo, name, poppet, personal item). Reflects their negativity back to them. Traps and contains harmful energy.

When to use:

When someone is actively harming you and you want their actions to backfire. When returning energy to sender. When you need to trap and contain someone's influence.

Ethics note:

Reflects what someone sends out. If they're sending harm, they receive harm. If they stop, the reflection stops. Some consider this defensive rather than harmful.

Oil

What it is:

Blended oils for anointing, dressing candles, charging objects, or applying to body. Typically a carrier oil base with essential oils, herbs, resins, or other ingredients added. See also Recipe Grimoire for oil blending instructions.

When to use:

When you need to anoint candles, dress tools, apply to body, or consecrate objects. When scent is important to your working. When you want a working that can be reused for the same purpose.

Examples:

Protection oil for anointing doors, prosperity oil for money candles, love oil for relationship work, banishing oil for cleansing tools.

Petition / Manifestation Work

What it is:

Workings driven by written or spoken petitions, letters, written intentions, command phrases, or affirmations charged magickally. Appears across Hoodoo, folk magick, chaos magick, and modern witchcraft.

When to use:

When you need to state your intention clearly, when writing is your strongest form of focus, when petitioning spirits or deities, when working with specific phrasing or commands.

Examples:

Petition papers folded toward/away from you, burning petitions, placing petitions under candles, writing intentions on bay leaves, letter magick, spoken affirmations.

Common methods:

Writing full petition statements, folding toward you (attraction) or away (banishment), burning to release, placing under candles or in jars, speaking petitions aloud with power.

Poppet

What it is:

Doll or figure representing a person, animal, or spirit. Used for sympathetic magick: what you do to the poppet affects the target. Can be made from fabric, clay, wax, roots, or other materials.

When to use:

When you need to work magick on a specific person (with or without consent, depending on your ethics). When healing work requires directing energy to body parts. When binding or controlling someone's actions.

Examples:

Healing poppets with herbs in afflicted areas, binding poppets wrapped in cord, protection poppets for children, love poppets (ethically questionable), harmful poppets (use with extreme caution).

Related technique:

Taglocks (hair, nails, personal items) used to link poppet to specific person. Doll babies in Hoodoo are a specific closed practice unless culturally connected.

Potion

What it is:

Prepared mixtures for magickal purposes, broader than elixirs. Can be liquids, powders, pastes, or salves. May include ingredients not meant for consumption. Used externally, sprinkled, poured, or buried.

When to use:

When you need a prepared substance for a specific magickal purpose that isn't strictly an oil or consumable elixir. When combining multiple ingredients into a finished product.

Examples:

Magickal inks, ritual baths, floor washes, powders for sprinkling, salves for anointing.

Pouch

What it is:

Fabric bags filled with herbs, crystals, petition papers, and other materials. Typically tied or sewn shut. Can be carried, worn, placed on altar, hidden in location, or disposed of. Also called mojo bags, gris-gris, or medicine bags (note: some of these terms belong to specific closed traditions).

When to use:

When you want portable magick that holds multiple ingredients. When the contents need to be in contact with each other. When you need something more discreet than a jar but more durable than loose materials.

Examples:

Protection pouches carried in purse, prosperity bags kept in wallet, sleep sachets under pillow, healing pouches worn on body, banishing bags buried off property.

Protection

What it is:

Shielding, warding, blocking, reinforcing one's energy or space. Foundational magick distinct from banishing or binding.

When to use:

Daily shielding, protecting home or space, guarding against magickal attack, reinforcing personal boundaries, protecting loved ones or pets.

Examples:

Wards, protective sigils, barrier jars, iron nails, sound wards, mirror wards, personal shields.

Common methods:

Creating energetic shields, placing protective objects, using protective symbols, invoking protective entities, layering multiple protection methods.

Reversal

What it is:

Sending magick back to its source. Reversing a hex, curse, or negative energy back to whoever sent it. Can also reverse your own magick if you change your mind.

When to use:

When you're under magickal attack and want to return it. When you suspect someone hexed you. When you cast something and regret it.

Common methods:

Reversing candles (black on outside, red on inside), mirror work, reversal oils, speaking incantations backward, turning photos or names upside down or backward.

Ritual (Default)

What it is:

The catch-all category for general spellwork that doesn't fit into more specific types. Includes spoken invocations, ceremonial workings, symbolic actions, multi-step procedures that combine multiple methods.

When to use:

When the working doesn't fit neatly into another category. When combining multiple magickal methods. When following traditional ceremonial structures.

Examples:

Circle casting, invocations to deities, full moon rituals, sabbat celebrations, initiations, multi-step ceremonial work.

Sigil

What it is:

Symbol-based magick using drawn, painted, carved, or inscribed marks. The sigil itself holds and focuses the intention. Can be activated through various methods: burning, carrying, burying, charging with energy, or visual meditation.

When to use:

When you want a condensed, abstract representation of your intent. When you need portable magick that can be hidden or destroyed easily. When working with entities that respond to symbolic language.

Examples:

Created sigils from statement of intent, traditional magickal symbols, runes arranged in specific patterns, geometric designs charged with purpose.

Souring

What it is:

Magick that turns someone's attitude or situation bitter. Makes relationships go bad, ruins someone's mood, causes discord. Often uses vinegar or lemon as sympathetic materials.

When to use:

Making someone unpleasant to be around, turning a relationship sour, causing plans to fall apart through discord.

Duration:

Temporary to medium-term.

Sweetening

What it is:

Using honey, sugar, or sweet substances to make someone sweet toward you. Can be done to improve relationships, soften attitudes, win favor, or calm conflict.

When to use:

Improving relationship with boss, softening someone's attitude, court cases where you need favor, family conflict resolution, customer service situations.

Common methods:

Honey jar with target's name and herbs, petition paper folded toward you and placed in honey, sugar bowl workings, speaking sweetly while stirring honey clockwise.

Warding

What it is:

Constructing magickal barriers around home, body, objects, or pets. Architectural form of magick, distinct subset of protection.

When to use:

Setting up new home protections, creating long-term barriers, protecting thresholds and boundaries, guarding specific rooms or areas.

Examples:

Threshold protections, door sigils, iron nails in corners, energetic wards, boundary lines, protective circles around property.

Common methods:

Physical barrier placement (salt lines, iron nails), energetic barrier construction, sigil placement at entry points, invoking guardian spirits, layering protections at boundaries.

Weather Magick

What it is:

Workings that call, change, or influence weather: rain, storms, wind, fog. Ancient category recognized across multiple magickal traditions globally.

When to use:

Calling rain for crops, dispersing storms, raising wind, calling fog for concealment, working with weather spirits.

Examples:

Storm jars, wind knot magick, rain petitions, weather working with entities.

Note:

Weather magick affects entire areas and other people. Consider ethics and consequences carefully.

Choosing the Right Type

What form should the magick take?

  • Need portability? Charm or pouch
  • Need containment? Jar
  • Need simplicity? Sigil or candle
  • Need to direct energy at specific person? Poppet
  • Need something consumable? Elixir, kitchen magick
  • Need ongoing altar work? Keep as ritual
  • Need to work without physical tools? Meditation, energy work

How long should it last?

  • Immediate, one-time effect: Ritual, sigil (destroyed), petition (burned)
  • Ongoing protection or work: Jar, charm, pouch, wards (kept)
  • Long-term with maintenance: Altar objects, rechargeable charms, wards

What's the intent?

Work with what's available. Most intentions can be achieved through multiple formats.

Final Notes

The type you choose matters less than your clarity of intent and commitment to the working. A simple candle ritual with focused will outperforms an elaborate jar with scattered energy.

When in doubt, start simple. You can always repeat or escalate if needed.
