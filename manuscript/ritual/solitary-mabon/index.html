Mabon

A simple solitary libation ritual for Mabon focused on gratitude and attunement to the Autumn Equinox. Pours offerings to deity and the four directions, followed by meditation on the cycle of growth and harvest.

⚠️ White sage and Palo Santo are sacred materials in Indigenous traditions. Consider rosemary, cedar, or plain frankincense incense as alternatives if you are outside those cultures.

Quantities

  • Smudge stick (sage, Palo Santo wood, or frankincense incense stick) or standard kitchen broom
  • 5 bowls (if performing indoors)
  • Wine, cider, water, or beer for libation
  • Decorations: walnuts, sunflower seeds, autumn foliage, acorns
  • Tray to hold ritual pieces
  • Circle casting tool (athame, wand, or favorite pen)
  • One or more candles (optional)

Directions

  1. Purpose:
    This ritual attunes you to the Autumn Equinox through simple gratitude and libation offerings. It encourages deeper relationship with deity and nature while marking the transition from harvest work to winter rest.
  2. Setting:
    Outdoors is preferable for less mess. Indoors can be done over a sink or tub.
  3. Pre-Ritual Preparation (Outdoor):
    Wear clean, comfortable clothing that fits close to your body. Prepare your tray with libation liquid (water, grape juice, cider, beer, or wine) and decorate with nuts, leaves, or Mabon symbols.
  4. Take your tray outside and place it where you can walk completely around it.
  5. Use smudge stick or broom to clear the sacred space.
  6. If smudging, light your bundle and walk clockwise, waving up and down, telling the smoke to cleanse the air and land of conflicting energies.
  7. Walk north to south, then east to west in a cross pattern.
  8. Wave above your head and below your knees.
  9. Imagine the space brightening like sunlight.
  10. Optional chant:
    Cloud of smoke borne of sagebrush,
    bring this space to a quiet hush;
    scrub it of all pain and meanness,
    let these rites go forward clean.
  11. Give yourself about two feet more space than needed.
  12. Snuff out sage safely.
  13. Place bundle on tray.
  14. Sit on the ground, feel the air, notice the sky's effect on your mood, and reflect on changes since last Mabon.
  15. Take several deep breaths.
  16. Begin when relaxed.
  17. Pre-Ritual Preparation (Indoor):
    If sweeping with broom, go clockwise stating: "I sweep out any energies that conflict with my intentions." For intensive chant:
  18. Cleanse the air and free the dirt,
    move out of here what lays inert.
    Whip out all conflicts, dust up disruptors;
    let peace reconstruct this space!
  19. Place five bowls in sacred space, one at each cardinal direction and fifth on center tray.
  20. Surround center bowl with autumn foliage, acorns, walnuts, and sunflower seeds.
  21. The Ritual:
    Cast circle by saying:
  22. At Autumn Equinox, I name this place
    a sacred time and sacred space.
    Within it I now give my thanks,
    with protection granted by God/ess grace!
    The north grants ground to walk upon.
    The east grants winds that gyrate.
    The south grants fire so we live on.
    The west grants fluids to sate us.
  23. Take one swig from the drink container to show the beverage is safe.
  24. Invocation to the Goddess:
    Hail to the God/ess, Great Mother Eternal,
    I salute you in your giving;
    I condole you in your grief.
  25. Pour libation into center bowl, then say:
  26. In gratitude, in love,
    I give thanks to you.
  27. Optionally light candle or incense, or raise hands heavenward:
  28. In your grief, in your sorrow,
    I give love to you.
    So mote it be!
  29. Invocation to the God:
    Hail to the God, Great Father Incarnate,
    I salute you for your sacrifices.
    I speak your praises when you are gone from us!
  30. Pour libation into center bowl:
  31. In your death, I speak my grief.
    In your memory, I bring you to life.
    So mote it be!
  32. Go to northern bowl or northernmost point:
    Hail to the north, where the dark of the earth lives.
    I pour this in gratitude for the food that springs from soil!
  33. Pour libation. Go to eastern bowl or easternmost point:
    Hail to the east, where sunrise brings the winds of the day,
    I pour this in gratitude for the movements of pollen!
  34. Pour libation. Go to southern bowl or southernmost point:
    Hail to the south, where noonday speeds all growth.
    I pour this in gratitude for the nourishment of sunlight!
  35. Pour libation. Go to western bowl or westernmost point:
    Hail to the west, where in twilight the green drinks deep.
    I pour this in gratitude for the wet that swells seed and bud!
  36. Pour libation.
  37. Return to center bowl and pour any remaining libation:
  38. Hail to the Goddess! Hail to the God!
    I give thanks for good harvest
    and thanks for all good.
    So mote it be!
  39. Meditation:
    Sit or stand. Imagine a seed in earth swelling until a green shoot bursts forth. See it expand into a plant. Note which plant you see to learn about over winter. Visualize it brightening, flowering, going to seed until brown and felled. Notice the seed blown by wind to new ground. Note which corner you "saw" the seed emerge; that quarter shows where the work of your next harvest lies.
  40. Make acknowledgments to deities and elements.
  41. To say farewell:
  42. With love, I depart from you, precious Goddess.
    With love, I depart from you, sacred God.
    I carry with me gratitude for the life on earth you have given.
  43. Opening the Circle:
    Walk counterclockwise with arm extended:
  44. Water flows back to the west.
    Southern fires flicker then rest.
    Eastern breezes cool the storm.
    North rests now in quiet calm.
    At equinox the light now fades,
    here is now more night than it is day.
    In gratitude I make my way
    with harvest at hand into winter's sway.
  45. Closing:
    Clean up your sacred space. If indoors, pour libations on ground outside or down sink drain. If outdoors, gather remaining materials from tray. After ritual, enjoy a hearty meal with grains, vegetables, and fruits representing the harvest.
