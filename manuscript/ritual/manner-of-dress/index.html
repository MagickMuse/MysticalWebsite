Ritual Grimoire

Manner of Dress

The Debate

Witches disagree over whether ritual work is best done nude or robed, and both camps consider their approach traditional.

The Case for Nudity

The usual argument is that clothing blocks the flow of witch power. That explanation doesn't hold up well, since power passes through walls and travels across distance without trouble, so a layer of fabric shouldn't stop it either. The real reason nudity helps is psychological: it supports release from tension, everyday concerns, and inhibition. If going without clothes puts you in the right headspace, that's reason enough to do it; just make sure you have privacy.

The Tabard Alternative

For colder climates or less enthusiastic practitioners, the tabard is the standard alternative: a long piece of heavy black fabric folded in half, with a head-hole cut poncho-style and the sides sewn up partway to leave arm openings, hanging to the ankles and belted with the Girdle Cord.

Many witches prefer more elaborate robes in colors like blue, violet, red, green, or white, sometimes with a hood or cowl for added anonymity during ritual. Sandals or bare feet are both acceptable.

Coven Uniformity

Covens often benefit from a shared dress code: many members keep one uniform robe for Sabbats and Esbats and a separate, more personal one for solo work. A witch name or personal signs can be embroidered on the hem or breast, though this is optional.

A Prop, Not a Requirement

Ultimately, the tabard (or nudity) works as a psychological aid rather than a magical necessity. Ordinary clothing serves fine for ritual as long as it doesn't pull your mind back to mundane concerns.
