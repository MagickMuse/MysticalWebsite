◇Directions
1. Servitors require ongoing energy to maintain their function. 2. How often you feed your servitor depends on how active it is. 3. A servitor working daily should be fed monthly. 4. A servitor with less frequent tasks can be fed less often but should be fed at least once a year. 5. Make a note in your calendar so feeding becomes part of your regular practice. 6. Gather the servitor's physical link if one exists. 7. This could be the paper with its name and sigil, or the object it is bound to. 8. If your servitor has no physical anchor, you will work directly with visualization. 9. Prepare your space. 10. You can make this as simple or elaborate as you wish. 11. Some practitioners simply sit quietly with the servitor's link. 12. Others light candles and incense matching the servitor's nature, or create full ritual space. 13. Choose what feels right for your practice and the servitor's function. 14. Ground and center yourself. 15. Take a few moments to connect with your own energy. 16. Breathe deeply and feel your connection to your power. 17. Call the servitor to attention. 18. If it has a physical link, hold that object. 19. Speak the servitor's name aloud or in your mind. 20. Visualize the servitor appearing before you or sense its presence in whatever way is natural to you. 21. The servitor may have developed preferences over time for how it likes to be fed. 22. Some servitors prefer specific forms of energy or particular visualizations. 23. Pay attention to these preferences. 24. Check in with the servitor. 25. Before feeding it, take a moment to assess how it is functioning. 26. Is it carrying out its task as intended? 27. Has anything changed? 28. Does its programming need adjustment? 29. This feeding time is your opportunity to monitor the servitor's work. 30. Generate energy to feed the servitor. 31. Visualize a ball of energy forming in your hands or within your body. 32. This can be white light, colored light matching the servitor's nature, or energy drawn from an elemental or planetary source. 33. The energy should feel substantial, like you are holding something real. 34. Offer the energy to the servitor. 35. See or sense the servitor taking the energy from you. 36. Some practitioners visualize this as the servitor consuming the energy ball. 37. Others see it as the energy flowing into the servitor like water filling a vessel. 38. Use whatever visualization feels right. 39. As you feed the servitor, reinforce its programming. 40. Speak its function aloud or repeat it mentally. 41. Remind the servitor of its task and its boundaries. 42. This strengthens the programming and keeps the servitor aligned with your original intention. 43. If the servitor's form or behavior has shifted in concerning ways, now is the time to address it. 44. Firmly restate the servitor's proper form and function. 45. Visualize it returning to its intended shape and behavior. 46. If the servitor has drifted significantly from your original programming despite regular feeding, consider whether it needs to be decommissioned. 47. Thank the servitor for its work. 48. Acknowledge what it has accomplished since the last feeding. 49. Gratitude strengthens the bond between you and your creation. 50. Release the servitor to continue its work. 51. See it depart or return to its physical anchor. 52. If you lit candles or incense, let them burn safely or extinguish them. 53. Note the date of this feeding and schedule your next maintenance session. 54. Over time, you may notice your servitor developing personality traits or preferences. 55. This is natural for long-term servitors. 56. As long as the servitor continues to function as intended and has not become erratic or harmful, this is not a problem. 57. However, any concerning changes in behavior or appearance should be addressed immediately through reinforced programming or decommissioning if necessary.
◇Description
A regular maintenance ritual to feed energy to an active servitor and reinforce its programming. Feeding sustains the servitor's function and strengthens the connection between practitioner and artificial spirit. This working should be performed at intervals determined during the servitor's creation, typically monthly or at least annually.

Regular feeding maintains your servitor's energy and reinforces its programming. This is not optional for long-term servitors. Neglected servitors may begin taking energy from other sources and drift from their original programming, as happened with Alexandra David Néel's tulpa that shifted from good-natured to sinister when it changed outside her control.

Frequency

How often you feed your servitor depends on its activity level. A servitor working daily needs monthly feeding. Less active servitors can be fed less frequently but should receive energy at least once annually. Make a note in your calendar to include feeding in your regular practice schedule.

Servitor Preferences

Over time, servitors may develop preferences for how they receive energy. Some want energy visualized as shiny objects (like coins or rings for raven-like servitors). Others prefer specific colors of energy, such as white light or energy from your heart center. Pay attention to these preferences during feeding sessions. Giving a servitor the kind of energy that resonates best with its nature helps it serve you better.

Checking Function

Feeding time is also check-in time. Before offering energy, assess how the servitor is functioning. Is it carrying out its task as intended? Has its form or behavior shifted? Does its programming need reinforcement? This regular monitoring prevents problems before they become serious.

When Things Go Wrong

If your servitor's form or behavior has shifted in concerning ways, address it immediately during feeding. Firmly restate the servitor's proper form and function. Visualize it returning to its intended shape and behavior. If the servitor has drifted significantly despite regular feeding, consider decommissioning it rather than trying to force it back into alignment.

Natural Development

Some personality development is normal for long-term servitors. As long as the servitor continues its intended function without becoming erratic or harmful, minor personality traits are not concerning. However, any significant deviation from programming requires immediate attention.
