A homemade oracle of cut poem lines, drawn like runes or tarot cards, that invites Brigid's guidance through the words of poetry.

Quantities

Favorite poems (cut into lines)
Basket, box, or jar

Directions

  1. Cut two or more favorite poems into individual lines with space around each, fold them, and mix them in a basket, box, or jar.
  2. Close your eyes, speak a triad asking Brigid for sense, understanding, and wisdom, then draw three slips and read them, noting first impressions before exploring deeper meaning.
  3. Sit quietly afterward and ask whether Brigid wishes to add anything, then return the slips to the jar.

As goddess of poetry and lore, Brigid may speak through divination that uses written or spoken words: bibliomancy, opening a book at random and reading where a finger lands, or audiomancy, noting the first words heard upon turning on a radio or television. This poetry oracle honors Brigid as goddess of wordsmithing, using fragments of poems the way runes or tarot cards are used.

You will need:

  • A basket, box, or jar that a hand can fit inside
  • Printouts of two or more poems, with space between the lines
  • Scissors
  1. Find at least two poems whose imagery and language feel appealing. Try not to assign divinatory meaning to the lines yet; it helps to work quickly and not dwell on the words.
  2. Print out the poems, or write them out by hand, leaving space between the lines.
  3. Cut the individual lines apart into slips of paper. If two lines insist on staying together, that is fine, but no more than that; the art of divination lies in working with symbols, not being handed the message spelled out literally.
  4. Fold the slips and mix the lines well by tossing them together in the chosen container.
  5. Close the eyes and settle into a receptive, calm state of mind. Offer this triad to Brigid:
Sense be in my thought,
Understanding be in my heart,
Wisdom be in my soul.
  1. When ready, draw three slips of paper and open them.
  2. Notice the very first thoughts that surface on reading the lines: this is what is sensed, the intuitive response before any filtering or censoring.
  3. Explore the words for deeper understanding. What specific associations do they carry? Does the order of the lines matter, or do they stand alone? Take the necessary time.
  4. Sit quietly for a few moments with eyes closed and ask Brigid whether there is any wisdom to add to the interpretation; that answer may not arrive until later. When finished, fold the lines and return them to the jar.

Add new lines to the oracle from time to time, or build an entirely new one. This is a wonderful oracle to make within a circle: have everyone bring a batch of lines, mix them together, then draw enough to fill individual containers, a poetry potluck. It also makes a meaningful gift: make a jar for a friend and ask her to make one in return. Whoever chose the lines, expect to be surprised. Brigid of the augury sends the right words at the right time, exactly what is needed to hear.
