🌾Moon Broom Dolly

A braided grain craft and dedication ritual rooted in European harvest tradition. Nine wheat stalks are soaked in Moon Water or jasmine tea, then braided with red ribbon, the color of life force, while holding intentions of gratitude, dedication, and connection to the spirits of the land. The finished broom-shaped corn dolly is blessed under the full moon and hung in the kitchen or over the door for abundance and protection. In some traditions, the previous year's dolly is burned at Imbolc and its ashes returned to the earth, completing the cycle of giving and receiving.

Quantities

  • 9 hollow grain stalks (such as wheat), grain heads intact
  • Moon Water or jasmine tea
  • 2 lengths of red ribbon

Directions

  1. Soak the nine grain stalks in Moon Water or jasmine tea for about 15 minutes, until they are soft and pliable.
  2. When ready, gather all stalks into a bundle and tie them together with one length of red ribbon right at the base of the grain heads, this is the top of your broom dolly.
  3. Gently divide the stalks into three equal bunches of three.
  4. Begin braiding slowly and deliberately, weaving the loose ends of the ribbon into the braid as you work.
  5. The red ribbon carries the energy of life force, let its presence remind you of the vitality being woven into this craft.
  6. As your hands move through the braid, turn your mind toward what you wish to dedicate yourself to: a new practice, a habit, an intention, or simply your lunar craft as a whole.
  7. Acknowledge the spirits of the land, those who tend the soil, hold the roots, and make the harvest possible.
  8. Let gratitude rise naturally as you work.
  9. Stop braiding before you reach the ends of the stalks, leaving the grain splayed outward in the shape of a broom.
  10. Tie the second ribbon around the stalks at the bottom of the braid to hold your work in place.
  11. If called to, add a bow with the remaining ribbon, or attach small charms that carry meaning for your dedication.
  12. Take the finished dolly outside, or to a window, and hold it in the light of the full moon.
  13. Offer a few words of blessing and gratitude, and feel the moonlight settle into the grain.
  14. Bring the dolly indoors and hang it in your kitchen, above your hearth, on your harvest altar, or over your front door to draw in abundance and protection.
  15. In some traditions, the corn dolly from the previous year is ceremonially burned at Imbolc, with its ashes scattered or returned to the earth to nourish the coming year's growth.
  16. You may honor this cycle by repeating the craft each year, burning the old dolly when the new one is complete.
