Troubleshooting Spellwork

If your spell did not produce the intended results, many factors could have contributed. Before declaring it null, wait for at least a full lunar cycle (twenty-eight days) for the intention to come to pass.

Doubt

Doubt is the killer of all magick. The fastest way to make sure your magick will not come to pass is to doubt that it will. The power comes from you, not the candles, incense, or chants. If you think you do not have the oomph needed, do not doubt. Work it out.

Lack of Power

When casting your intention and putting your spell out into the ether, you have to feel it in your bones. If it feels like you are reciting your phone number for a telemarketer, you are not going to have the desired results. Sound off like you mean it.

Speaking of meaning it, stop and take stock to make sure this is something you really want. It could be that the reason a workable amount of energy cannot be raised is that it is not a sincere desire.

Patience

It takes time for magick to work. Sometimes your working works right away, but that happens less often than you might think, and frequently only after a lot of practice. Magick is a skill, no matter how many witches you see in movies and television discovering they are magickal and knowing everything within two minutes.

Take a deep breath and know your intention is working.

Shoddy Scholarship

There is a reason that witches and even faerie tale wizards are associated with books. Knowledge is power, and without the know-how, your magick is not going to get any wind in its sails. Take the time to do the research needed to feel supported and fulfilled in your magickal endeavors. After all, you are building your magickal intent and your brain.

Lack of Focus

If your magick does not have a specific outcome, it scatters your attention, and therefore your intention. If your original plan is "Money for a new car" and mid-spell you start thinking how nice it would be to have some new clothes, but then you would have to work more hours, to pay for the clothes, and you really should get a new job because you are miserable here, and your boss is difficult... Do you see how that can take the fire out of the spell?

Concentrate while you are working and find a friend to vent to later. The same goes for trying to cast a spell in a house full of people, or in a messy room. All of these things can scatter your focus.

Too Many Cooks in the Kitchen

Working magick with friends and loved ones is fun. It can bring people closer together and is an excellent way to bond. However, bringing a friend, loved one, or partner into your magickal practice should be done carefully, if at all.

The idea of a coven sounds scintillating and neat. However, having a new practitioner who may or may not have a clear picture of what to do or how to do it can break down the building energy. Not to mention, if your spell is for a new car and your best friend thinks you need a sensible older model and to pay down your debt, it can break down the momentum you were gaining doing all the research.

If you want to practice with magickal friends, keep it simple and make sure everyone is on the same page.

Volatile Emotions

If you are too upset to drive, or crying too hard to talk, those emotions can hinder your magickal practice. This is not about mental health. Telling people they have to be 100 percent mentally healthy before someone will love them is harmful and ableist.

This is about giving yourself time to collect your thoughts and feelings, wipe the tears away, and ground yourself. This makes for a more focused intent. Trying to work magick when emotional can lead to overextended energy and unfocused, drained results.

By properly grounding before spell work, the practitioner ensures connection to the earth and a well of energy.

The Witches' Pyramid

To Know, To Will, To Dare, To Keep Silent.

These four pillars support effective magickal practice:

  • To Know: Do your research. Understand your correspondences, timing, and methods.
  • To Will: Commit fully. Your intention must be sincere and your focus unwavering.
  • To Dare: Take action. Magick requires courage to step into the unknown.
  • To Keep Silent: Protect your working. Sharing too soon can introduce doubt and scatter energy.

Why Do Some Spells Succeed but Others Fail?

Magick works. It can produce outcomes so unexpected and wonderful they might almost be called miracles. And yet, magick does not work every time. Even skilled practitioners face challenges and experience failure. Learning to work magick does not shield anyone from all disappointment.

A well-known herbalist, sought out by patients from around the world, once framed this plainly for her students: roughly a third of the remedies she prescribed failed outright. Another third produced only modest improvement. The final third produced extraordinary results. Her lesson was direct: as a healer, you must get over your fear of failure. If the first remedy does not work, try another, rather than deciding the situation is hopeless and giving up.

It Comes Down to Clarity

Successful magick does not require arcane techniques, expensive ingredients, or complicated rituals. Often, what separates a working that succeeds from one that fails is clarity of intention.

Consider a practitioner who had spent years searching for love without pausing to feel what their soul actually needed. Through meditation, they identified three specific qualities they truly wanted in a partner, not what seemed reasonable, not what they thought they deserved, but what they genuinely needed. They wrote those three things on a page torn from a book of love poetry and burned a candle over the petition. Simple, honest, and precise. Weeks later, they encountered the first partner who met all three criteria.

The spell worked not because of its complexity, but because of its honesty.

Asking for What You Actually Want

Knowing what you truly want is difficult. Asking for it can be even harder.

Kasia Urbaniak, a coach who draws on her background as a dominatrix to teach people about power dynamics in personal and professional life, has observed that many people, particularly women, avoid asking for what they want. Even when the desire is known, the request gets internally compromised before it is voiced. Instead of asking for what is truly wanted, people ask for what they think they deserve. The result: even when the request is granted, the outcome feels hollow, because it was not what was really wanted in the first place.

This applies directly to spellwork. A spell cast toward a compromised desire rather than the true desire will succeed in delivering something that still disappoints.

Close your eyes and take a breath. What do you really want?

Ancestral Trauma as a Block to Magick

The internal voice that forces self-compromise before a request is made is often a symptom of ancestral trauma. Someone in the ancestral line experienced pain, loss, or discrimination and developed a coping mechanism that helped them survive, but which now limits their descendants' ability to thrive. Sometimes this person was known in life; sometimes their name has been lost to living memory entirely.

One common pattern involves career and livelihood. Ancestors who survived under conditions of severe employment discrimination often passed down advice centered on keeping a low profile, not making waves, and never complaining. This wisdom served its original context. But in a professional environment where asking for what one deserves is standard practice, this inherited coping strategy can actively work against success. A practitioner might cast spell after spell for a promotion and see nothing manifest, not because the spell is weak, but because the ancestral pattern underneath it is running a counter-program.

The work in cases like this begins at the ancestor altar. Opening up to the ancestors honestly, naming the fear and the inherited pattern, and asking for their help in releasing it, can dissolve a block that no amount of spellwork alone would touch. When that inner preparation is complete, the spell that follows can manifest with unusual speed.

Close your eyes and take a breath. Who told you that you couldn't have what you really want?

The Real Magick Is in the Preparation

Sometimes the most important magick is the preparation done to become capable of receiving what is being asked for.

This can mean healing ancestral trauma. It can also mean developing the skills, experience, and readiness required to steward what is being sought. New jobs, new relationships, and increased abundance all require more of the person who receives them. Clearing the inner ground is not a detour from the work. It is often the work itself.
