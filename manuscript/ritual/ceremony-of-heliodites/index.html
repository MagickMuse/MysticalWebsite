☀️Ceremony of Heliodites

◇Tradition
Greek
◇Solitary or Group
Either
◇Form
Altar WorkingOffering Rite
◇Setting
Indoor
◇Method
InvocationPrayerDevotional WorkIncense MagickPetitionChant/MantraCleansing
◇Category
Ceremony
◇Difficulty
Advanced
◇Quantities
- Red wine for libations at every station - Olive oil for libation to Athena - Honey for libation to Helios - Frankincense incense (for Athena and Apollon) - Myrrh incense (for Athena) - Manna incense (frankincense, for Apollon and Helios) - Dried or fresh rose petals as incense for Helios - Fresh olive branch as offering to Athena - Red roses for Apollon and Helios - Fresh laurel leaf for Apollon - Music to accompany the Hymn to Apollon (live or recorded) - The first serving of the midday meal, set aside as offering to Helios
◇Directions
1. The Heliodites is timed to the daylight of Sunday, with the central libation to Helios falling at midday alongside the household lunch. 2. Begin earlier in the morning by bathing and dressing in clean clothes. 3. Set the altar with the wine vessel, the olive oil flask, the honey jar, the burner with charcoal, the frankincense, the myrrh, the dried rose for Helios' incense, the olive branch, the red roses, the laurel leaf, and a place set aside for the first serving of the midday meal. 4. If a different protector deity is appropriate to the region or household, substitute Their hymn for the Hymn to Athena. 5. Stand at the altar in stillness, then speak the opening: 6. Come O' Esteemed and Ancestral Gods Gods honoured in the homes of our forefathers. Come O' Immortal Gods, rulers of the holy days, that You may be venerated as is your due. Come with benevolence, as Friends and Protectors of our household, as You were to the line of our forefathers. 7. Libate red wine and set the offerings on the altar. 8. Follow with Plethon's (PLEH-thon) Prayer, the gratitude that anchors the rest of the rite: 9. Let me not cease, O' Blissful Gods to owe You gratitude, for all the good things I receive, and for that which I have received in the past under the patronage of Supreme Zeus (ZOOS). Let me not neglect, in accordance with my strength, that which is good for my nation. To serve willingly the common good, and to consider that as a great benefit to myself. Let me not be the cause for anything evil, of the type which befalls humans, but rather of the good, as much as I am able, so that I may become blissful, in Your likeness. 10. Libate red wine. 11. Move to the place of the household protector. 12. Lay the olive branch before the image, kindle frankincense and myrrh, and speak the hymn: 13. Most High Goddess Pallas (PAH-las), golden wreathed Athena (ah-THEE-nah), Great Goddess of the World; You who sprang fully armed from our Father's venerable head. Virgin goddess who awakens in us, the indestructible and illuminated Nous (NOOS, Mind). Shield-bearing, Immaculate, You who governs the Whole World. Untamed Metis (MEH-tees) who merges the opposites, thrice-born Maiden; bright flower. You have filled the mind of the Father with the laws of Universal Beauty. You are all which exists, all which is still to come and all that has been. Glorious Goddess, Bright-eyed Maiden, grant the strength and love to raise me from the embrace of Gaia (GUY-ah) to illuminated Olympos (oh-LEEM-pos). O' Goddess, offer to us your immaculate Wisdom and mental power, granting us the blessings of Olympos that uplift the soul, banishing the great illusions of the World. 14. Libate olive oil, then libate red wine. 15. Move to Apollon's (ah-POL-lon) shrine. 16. Set the red roses and the fresh laurel leaf before the image, kindle frankincense and manna, begin the music, and speak the hymn: 17. I remember and do not forget Apollon Hekateios (heh-kah-TAY-os). From years past, it is a sacred custom to invoke You. Come O' Blessed Paian (PY-an), Splendidly honoured Phoivos (FY-vos), Ie (EE-eh) Dispenser of Blessings, Spermatic, Pythian (PEE-thee-an), Delphic Diviner, Bright Daimon (DY-mon). You who sees the boundless aether and the joyful earth from above; Your eyes are the stars, dispelling the darkness. With your laurel branch, you shake the heavens, Apollon, You who does not appear to all but only to those who are good. Great is he who beholds You, insignificant are those who do not. We wish to be in Your presence, O' Hekatevolos (heh-kah-TEH-vo-los, Far-shooting), so that we may never be insignificant again. Thy hair drips healing aromatic oils onto the earth. Ie Paieon (py-EH-on), Ie Paieon, Ie Paieon. 18. Libate red wine during the final verse. 19. Move to Helios' (HEH-lee-os) place at the altar at midday, when the Sun is at its highest. 20. Set the red roses before His image, kindle the rose petals with manna, and set aside the first serving of the midday meal as offering. 21. Speak the first part of the hymn: 22. Come O' King of the noeric fire, Golden Titan. Come O' benefactor of light, who holds the key to the source, You preserve life, governing the material worlds from above with complete and absolute harmony. Come, You who holds the cardinal abode of the sky, and the central radiant circle of the World. You have filled everything with your providence; stimulating the mind. Most excellent of the Gods, Flame-crowned, You are the image of the God who created everything, You elevate the souls. 23. Libate honey. 24. Then speak the petition, repeating it three times in a steady, gathering voice: 25. Hear me and cleanse me Remove the stains which infect my soul. Keep me far from punishment, soothing the sharp gaze of justice which sees everything. To my body grant resilience and health. And drive away that which is sent against us from the ever changing weaves of the Moirae (MEE-rye), spun beneath the swirling stars, O' Great King, illuminate our minds and grant unimpaired light to our souls. 26. At the third repetition, libate red wine. 27. Return to the central altar to close the rite: 28. Khairete (KHY-reh-teh) O' Blessed Gods, Be with us always, Never leave, Cease all illness and drive away sorrow. 29. Libate red wine. 30. Seal the working with the closing word: 31. ΓΕΝΟΙΤΟ (yeh-NEE-toh, it is done). 32. Carry the rite into the rest of the day by eating the household midday meal in gratitude, since the first serving has already been offered to Helios. 33. Spent incense, ash, wilted petals, and the libated portion are returned to clean ground or running water afterwards so the offerings may pass back to the Earth. 34. The ceremony is meant to be kept unfailingly each Sunday as a steady weekly spiritual exercise; consistency over time is the heart of the practice.
◇Description
Modern Hellenic ceremony performed every Sunday, the first day of the solar week, also called Apollonia. Where the Noumenia honours the lunar rhythm, the Heliodites honours the solar rhythm and the Sun as the king of the visible world: a body whose light cleanses, uplifts the mind, supplies life and health, and teaches internal harmony as preparation for communion with the divine. The Sun is treated here not only on a physical level but on a noetic or mental level, as a first-class spiritual guide for the Apollonian path and a perceivable image of the Great Musician of the Universal World. Established as a steady weekly spiritual exercise to be kept unfailingly on a day that is already restful for most, the rite addresses Athena (or another local protector), Apollon as the perceivable source of the Sun King, and Helios himself.
