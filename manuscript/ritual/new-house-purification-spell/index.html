🛡️New House Purification Spell

A waning moon purification spell performed before moving into a new home. Herbs are ground and burned on charcoal in a handled cauldron while garlic cloves absorb negativity from each room. The space is censed widdershins, then vacated for thirteen minutes while the garlic draws out malefic energies. The garlic is disposed of without contact, and the candles burn for an additional hour before the remains are buried or discarded.

Quantities

  • 2 red candles
  • 1 mortar and pestle
  • Equal parts: bay leaf, yarrow flowers, rosemary leaves, St. John's Wort leaves, basil leaves, juniper berries, mullein leaves
  • 1 charcoal block
  • 1 handled cauldron
  • Peeled garlic cloves (1 per room)
  • 1 plastic baggie with twist tie

Directions

  1. Open all windows.
  2. Set the two red candles on either side of the cauldron on the kitchen counter and light them.
  3. Light a charcoal block and place it inside the cauldron.
  4. Grind equal parts bay leaf, yarrow flowers, rosemary leaves, St. John's Wort leaves, basil leaves, juniper berries, and mullein in the mortar.
  5. Add the ground herbs to the glowing charcoal and speak:
  6. I call upon ye herbs to drive out negativity and chaos, bringing thy blessings of peace and concord in their place.
  7. Peel the garlic cloves and place one in the center of each room.
  8. Speak:
  9. I call upon ye garlic to gather into yourselves the negativity and chaos that lingers, to draw into yourselves all malefic vapors and energies.
  10. Carry the cauldron from room to room, censing each one widdershins, then return the cauldron to the countertop between the candles.
  11. Vacate the house for thirteen minutes.
  12. Return and gather the garlic cloves into a plastic bag without touching them.
  13. Fasten the bag and place it outside in the trash.
  14. Close the windows.
  15. Let the candles burn for another hour, then snuff them.
  16. Scatter the cooled incense remains outdoors.
  17. Bury the candle remains or dispose of them in the trash.
