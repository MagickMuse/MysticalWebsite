A solitary devotional meditation on Hekate's nature as a primordial, unruled titan, performed with her sigil and a white candle.

Quantities

  • Clean sheet of paper
  • Pen
  • Bowl
  • Matches
  • White candle
  • Cinnamon, for dusting the hands

Directions

  1. Wash your hands three times, then dust them with cinnamon.
  2. At a liminal hour, sit alone somewhere private with pen, paper, a bowl, matches, and a white candle.
  3. Breathe deeply and let your mind settle.
  4. On the clean paper, draw Hekate's crossroads sigil, contemplating its meaning as you go, then write your initials on all four sides of it as before.
  5. Light the white candle and set it at the sigil's center.
  6. Read the hymn below silently to yourself while gazing into the candle's flame, reflecting on Hekate's nature as a fourth-generation titan: older than the gods, unruled by them, moving freely between worlds as the Liminal One who reconciles opposing forces.
  7. Close your eyes and picture yourself standing again at the misty crossroads at dusk, a ridge and starlit sea to your left, a dark wood to your right, two torches burning beside the forked path, and three thin masks hanging from a pole at its center. Glimpse the face of the first mask and let it affect you, knowing it to be one of Hekate's faces.
  8. Look down and see the sigil glowing in blue light beneath your feet, just as the candle rests on the paper sigil before you. Inhale and exhale three times, watching the glow brighten with each breath.
  9. On the third exhale, see the sigil rotate upright to align with your spine, its upper trident reaching to the heavens and its lower trident reaching to the underworld, both blazing with blue fire, and feel that same radiance fill you.
  10. Exhale slowly, open your eyes, and return your attention to the room and the candle's flame.
  11. Cover the sigil with both hands and speak the hymn aloud, twice, picturing the glowing trident now shining from within you with every word:
    O hundred-handed Hekate;
    O Serpent with a thousand scales;
    Far-Worker, Nothing-Shirker,
    Face hidden by a saffron veil;
    This much have you made known:
    You are neither goddess (though it pleases some to call you so)
    Nor beast (despite your scales and crescent horns)
    Nor woman (though you may take a maiden's form)
    Nor human (though we aspire to call you mother, lover, kin)
    Nor any combination thereof or therein.
    What then art thou?
    What, then?
    What?
    TITAN.
    First Force!
    Primal fire that formed
    Out of the void; from the Abyss
    Came they: old embodiments
    Of Universal Law: some of cold
    And some of flame; some of generation;
    Some of gravity, and sound, and speed;
    Some of degradation. But your lot, Hekate
    Was greater than these by far; for yours
    Was not just Earth, Sea, and Stars, but
    The borders that divide them; not places,
    But the paths between, which fork and branch
    And subdivide. O Queen of Subdivision,
    Of Void and Voices; Queen of Choices
    And of their consequence: your sacrament
    Is decision.
    O Titaness unchained! Release the one who knows
    Your name! She who stands before you and proclaims:
    Gaia is titan, is great, but is ruled by Demeter; Pontus is titan, is great, but is ruled by Poseidon; Helios is titan, is great, but is ruled by Phoibos. But Hekate, titan, greatest of the great, is alone unruled; her path unchecked; her orbit uncircumscribed; her jurisdiction unlimited by divine decree; and therefore is it said of her:
    Protou oloi oi theoi tremoun: "Before you, all gods tremble..."
    As do I.
    As do I.
    As do I.
  12. Close your eyes, exhale, and let the image of the glowing trident fade.
  13. With your eyes still closed, sense the candle flame before you without looking at it, then extend that same awareness to your left, your right, above you, and behind you, searching for another presence in the room. Let it remain, shift, or withdraw as it will.
  14. When that presence dissipates, take a deep breath, open your eyes, and blow out the candle.
  15. As before, kiss the sigil, burn it, and store the cooled ash safely.

Context

This is the second of nine devotional preparations performed across the eight nights leading up to a new moon and continuing through the night of the new moon itself, following Consecration of the Crossroads Sigil. This working centers on Hekate's identity as a fourth-generation titan who predates and stands outside the authority of the gods, moving freely between worlds as a liminal, reconciling force.

Recommended Reading

Proclus' Hymn to Hecate and Janus pairs well with this working and is best read just before sleep.
