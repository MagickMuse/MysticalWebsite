Juniper

A Buriat (indigenous Siberian) ritual for dedicating a living animal to a specific deity or spirit. The dedicated animal lives a charmed life, never ridden, worked, or eaten, as a gesture of sacrificial devotion. Originally used to honor the Ongon spirits, this practice has since been adopted by Mongolian Buddhists and can be adapted for any tradition.

Quantities

  • Juniper incense
  • Wine
  • Ribbons in colors associated with the chosen spirit

Directions

  1. Select an animal to be consecrated to a chosen deity or spirit.
  2. The most fitting choice is a creature already sacred to that spirit.
  3. Cleanse the animal by passing juniper incense smoke over its body.
  4. Anoint it lightly with wine.
  5. Adorn it with ribbons whose colors correspond to the spirit being honored.
  6. Once the ritual is complete, the animal returns to its home or flock.
  7. From that point on, the creature is never put to labor, ridden, or consumed.
  8. Instead it is treated with exceptional care, or at the very least allowed to live undisturbed, as an act of devotional offering.
  9. This privileged treatment may last the animal's entire natural life or for a set period of time, but the duration must be declared at the outset of the ritual.
