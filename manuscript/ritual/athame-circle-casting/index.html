Circle

A traditional athame-based circle casting and opening ritual from Wiccan practice, including the Cakes & Wine ceremony for grounding after ritual work.

Quantities

  • 1 athame (ritual blade)
  • Incense and censer
  • Libation bowl
  • Cakes or bread for offering
  • Wine or beverage for libation

Directions

  1. Light the incense and carry it around the circle area, walking clockwise (deosil) to purify the space with smoke.
  2. Take your athame and point it outward at the edge of where the circle will be.
  3. Walk clockwise around the perimeter, visualizing a line of blue-white energy flowing from the blade's tip and forming a protective sphere around you.
  4. As you walk, speak:
  5. I conjure thee, O Circle of Power,
    That thou beest a meeting place of love and joy and truth;
    A shield against all wickedness and evil;
    A boundary between men and the realms of the Mighty Ones;
    A rampart and protection that shall preserve and contain the power that we shall raise within thee.
    Wherefore do I bless thee and consecrate thee, in the names of the Goddess and the God.
  6. Return to where you began and seal the circle by bringing the athame point to meet the starting point of the energy line.
  7. After your ritual work is complete and before opening the circle, perform the Cakes & Wine ceremony to ground and center.
  8. Hold the athame over the chalice of wine and lower the blade into the liquid, saying:
  9. As the athame is to the male, so the cup is to the female, and conjoined they bring blessedness.
  10. Pour a small portion into the libation bowl as an offering to the Gods, then drink.
  11. Hold your hands over the cakes and bless them, then place a portion in the libation bowl before eating.
  12. When ready to close, take the athame and walk counterclockwise (widdershins) around the circle, drawing the energy back into the blade.
  13. Visualize the sphere dissolving back into the earth.
  14. Say:
  15. The circle is open, yet unbroken.
    May the peace of the Goddess and the God go in our hearts.
    Merry meet, and merry part, and merry meet again.
