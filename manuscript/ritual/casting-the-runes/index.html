🪵Casting the Runes

◇Description
A witch's method of geomantic divination using four dot-marked wooden rods to build a sixteen-figure chart, sharing its symbolic vocabulary with Cornelius Agrippa's fifteenth-century sand-tray geomancy.

Despite the name, this method has nothing to do with the Elder Futhark alphabet documented elsewhere in this Manuscript. It is a witch's form of geomancy, producing the same sixteen figures used in Cornelius Agrippa's fifteenth-century sand-tray system, but read from four cast rods instead of marks in sand. See Geomantic Figures for the figures themselves.

Preparing the Rods

Cut four flat rods from a fruit-bearing wood, such as apple, pear, cherry, plum, hazel, or rowan, about five inches long and a quarter to half an inch wide. Smooth every rough edge. Inside a cast circle, sprinkle the rods with salt water and pass them through Mercurial incense smoke. Paint a single, centered dot on one face of each rod. Once dry, turn each rod over and paint two dots on the other face, spaced so the rod reads as three equal segments. Carry the finished rods with you for some time before their first use, to charge them with your own power.

Setting Up

Gather clean paper, your pen and ink, Mercurial incense, your consecrated Square of Mercury, your thurible, and your cup of salt and water. Work at a flat table in a securely closed room, drapes drawn if there is any chance of being overlooked. Purify the space with fire and water as usual.

Attuning

Begin by working the Square of Mercury to bring your deep mind to the right pitch, then write your question on the paper.

Casting the Mothers

Take the rods in your right hand and cast them so they fall away from you in a rough line on the table. As you throw, say a version of:

In thy name, Herne, Lord of the Crossroads, I take up the rods; from word to word let me be led, from deed to another deed.

Repeat the cast three more times, four throws in total, recording the dot pattern each throw produces beneath your question, right to left. These four figures are your mothers.

Building the Daughters

Read off the top dot or dots of each mother in turn, right to left, to build a new figure, then the second row, then the third, then the bottom row. This produces four more figures, your daughters, taken from the mothers' top, second, third, and bottom rows in turn.

Building the Resultants

Lay all eight figures side by side, mothers first, then daughters. Add the dots of figures one and two together to make a ninth figure, then three and four for a tenth, five and six for an eleventh, and seven and eight for a twelfth. These four are your resultants. When adding dots, an odd total gives a single dot, an even total gives two.

Witnesses, Judge, and Reconciler

Add the ninth and tenth figures together for your first witness, figure thirteen, and the eleventh and twelfth for your second witness, figure fourteen. Add the two witnesses together for your judge, figure fifteen. Finally, add the judge to your very first mother to produce your reconciler, figure sixteen.

Reading the Chart

The two witnesses point to the two main factors or courses of action bearing on your question. The judge, as a synthesis of the whole, names the true nature of the problem. The reconciler shows the likely long-term outcome. Answers are only as precise as the question that produced them, so word your question with care before you begin.

Some diviners place the first twelve figures into the twelve houses of a horoscope chart for a fuller reading, interpreting each figure by that house's traditional rulership (house one for the querent, house two for money, house three for news and travel, and so on). This step is optional; not every practitioner finds it useful.
