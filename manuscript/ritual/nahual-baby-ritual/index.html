🌙Nahual Baby Ritual

A traditional Mexican ritual for discovering a child's animal protector spirit, or nahual. Ashes from sacred wood are used to capture the footprints of the first animal to approach, revealing the child's lifelong guardian. The ritual can also be adapted as a visualization to discover one's own animal allies at any stage of life.

Quantities

  • Sacred wood (enough to produce a generous pile of ash)
  • Placenta

Directions

  1. Preparations begin before the child arrives.
  2. The expectant couple selects a sacred wood and burns it completely to ash.
  3. A wood that produces white ash is traditionally preferred, since it makes footprints easier to identify, but any wood associated with magick (birch, ceiba, hazel, or rowan, for example) works well.
  4. These ashes are set aside until after the birth.
  5. Once the child is born, the placenta is taken to a meaningful outdoor location.
  6. Tradition calls for a mountain crossroads, though any wild area with animal activity will serve.
  7. The placenta is laid on the ground and the reserved ashes are scattered over and around it.
  8. Whatever creature first leaves tracks in the ash reveals the child's protector spirit, or at least the species it belongs to.
  9. Because this spell comes from a rural setting rich in wildlife, adjustments may be necessary for other environments.
  10. The working can also be performed purely as a visualization at any point in life, whether for a child or for yourself, as a method of discovering your own animal allies.
