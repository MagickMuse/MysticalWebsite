🪢The Love Knot

A two-phase knotted cord lust spell performed within a floor triangle consecrated to Cernunnos, using hair taken from the target's brush or comb braided into a cord worn on the body for 28 days. If no result occurs within that period, a second phase is performed: the cord is knotted nine times with a charm repeated at each knot, dipped in a chalice mixture of olive oil, red wine, honey, and the caster's own blood or urine, then burned on the thurible coals with a Latin incantation. The reverse form of the spell, the Denoument des noeuds (loosing of the marriage knots), was used by some practitioners to break existing bonds between lovers or spouses, a practice widely discussed in 16th-century theological writing on witchcraft.

Quantities

Phase 1: Three lengths of twine; hair from the target's head (taken from a brush or comb); red wine (a few drops in the chalice). Phase 2: The worked cord from Phase 1; chalice mixture of a few drops each of olive oil, red wine, honey, and the caster's own blood or urine; incense for the thurible (Cernunnos incense; recipe to be linked when available). Altar: pinecones, ivy, evergreens, horns, lamps of art.

Directions

Prepare the working space: mark out a large equilateral triangle on the floor with white tape, sized to contain yourself and the altar with room for movement. Set the altar with pinecones, ivy, evergreens, and animal horns. Pour a few drops of red wine into the chalice. Light the lamps of art and kindle the Cernunnos incense in the thurible.

Begin in the east. Chant the Invocation to the Horned One at each quarter, travelling clockwise. As you pass each side of the altar, cense and anoint it with a dab of wine from the chalice, consecrating the triangle to Cernunnos. Visualize yourself standing in a forest clearing: the canopy closes far above, and through the green twilight you hear the trampling and occasional cry of a great beast approaching, its goaty smell growing stronger with each quarter. By the time you complete the north and face east, see Cernunnos clearly in your mind's eye standing at the triangle's edge: the Horned One in his semianimal form, antlers rearing, eyes burning like coals. He may also be visualized as Pan, the Sabbatic Goat, or Robin Goodfellow.

INVOCATION TO THE HORNED ONE (chanted at each quarter):
Eko; Eko Azarak! Eko; Eko Zomelak!
Eko; Eko Cernunnos! Eko; Eko; Arada!
Bagabi lacha bachabe;
Lamac cahi achababa,
Karellyos!
Lamac Lamac Bachalyas;
Cabahagy Sabalyas,
Baryolas!
Lagoz atha Cabyolas;
Samahac atha femyolas,
Harrahya!

Return to the east. Facing west across the altar, take the three pieces of twine and consecrate them with incense and wine in the name of the Horned One. Summon the target's image vividly in the mind's eye. Braid the three lengths of twine together, binding the target's hair into the plait as you do. Repeat the charm continuously throughout the braiding:

Lord Cernunnos I ask of thee,
let [name] no pleasure, sleep, nor solace see,
till heart and loins be turned to me!

When the cord is fully woven, tie the free ends together forcefully, saying: As my will, so mote it be!

Fasten the finished cord around the upper thigh (for a woman) or around the genital area (for a man), tight enough to stay but not restrict circulation. Wear this cord for 28 days, removing it only for bathing.

PHASE 2 (if no result after 28 days): Prepare the working space as before with the large floor triangle, lamps, incense, and pinecones on the altar. Place in the chalice a few drops each of olive oil, red wine, honey, and your own blood or urine. Invoke Cernunnos at all four quarters with the invocation above.

Remove the cord. Tie nine knots along it: begin with one knot at each end, then work in pairs toward the center, placing the final (ninth) knot at the center. With each knot, repeat the charm: Lord Cernunnos I ask of thee, let [name] no pleasure, sleep, nor solace see, till heart and loins be turned to me! Finish the last knot with: As my will, so mote it be!

Dip the knotted cord briefly in the chalice so it picks up a few drops of the liquid. Cast it, together with a few grains of incense, onto the glowing thurible coals. As it burns, repeat the following with all the intensity and conviction you can summon:

Ure Spiritus Igne
Renes nostros et cor nostrum
Fiat, Fiat, Fiat!

The second phase reinforces the initial working at the level of the target's deep mind.

NOTE: The reverse form, the Denoument des noeuds (loosing of the marriage knots), uses the same working to break existing bonds between two persons. This is discussed in a separate chapter on baneful workings.
