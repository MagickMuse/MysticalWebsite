🌸Erotic Aromatherapy Spray

A reference guide to herbs associated with sexual desire, libido, and lust, with specific instructions for each herb's most effective application.

Quantities

One or more herbs from the list below, per your intention
Carrier oil, if using essential oils (coconut, sweet almond, or jojoba)

Directions

  1. Ground your intention clearly before beginning: name exactly what you want, with care for the will and wellbeing of all involved.
  2. Choose one or more herbs from the list below that match your intention.
  3. Prepare the herb using the method noted for it (tea, infusion, direct consumption, diluted essential oil, etc.).
  4. Use the prepared herb within your working, consuming it, bathing in it, or applying it as directed, while holding your intention.
  5. Never apply undiluted essential oils directly to the skin; dilute at a ratio of at least 1 drop essential oil per teaspoon of carrier oil.

Before You Begin

When working with herbs for sexual desire, specificity of intention is essential. There are well-known cautionary tales of practitioners who used lust herbs without precision, inviting not the desired partner, but an unwanted surplus of attention, or achieving the opposite of the intended effect. Because herbs are living beings, they amplify whatever energy they are given.

Herbs for Libido and Lust

Agave

The fermented juice intoxicates and stimulates erotic desire. The syrup may be diluted in water or stirred into a love potion.

Basil

Inspires confidence and a willingness to experiment sexually. The fresh leaves and flowers can be eaten or incorporated liberally into food.

Calamus Root

Used as an herbal bath to increase virility and sexual desire. Prepare as a strong infusion and add to bathwater.

Capsicum (Pepper)

A dash added to ripe pineapple and eaten together produces an aphrodisiac effect.

Celery

When eaten cooked or raw, strengthens the desire for sex and prepares the body for sexual activity.

Cinnamon

The diluted essential oil is used for sexual stimulation. Apply behind the ears, on the neck, and on the lips. Always dilute before skin contact.

Garlic

When pressed and combined with coriander seed, the mixture is used to reverse impotence caused by hexing. Ritually eaten in a meditative state to enhance its effectiveness as a counter-craft charm.

Ginger

Ginger's heat fans the flames of passion. Grate fresh root, infuse in hot water, and drink as a tea.

Ginkgo

Eating the toasted seeds is interpreted as an act of openness to sexual encounters. Improves sexual function.

Ginseng

Drunk as a tonic to increase the desire for sex.

Horseradish

Consumed to renew vigor after sexual intercourse.

Lovage

A tonic made from the root is traditionally said to inspire lust and awaken desire in those who are otherwise reticent.

Mustard

The seeds and greens are eaten to encourage virility.

Nutmeg

The essential oil inspires desire. Dilute and add to a spray to consecrate a sacred sexual space, or add a few drops to a sachet placed under a pillow.

Yarrow

Consumed as an infusion, yarrow was used as an aphrodisiac in Indigenous American practice, imbibed or chewed in the hours preceding intimacy.

Ylang Ylang

The essential oil diluted with coconut oil as a carrier makes a seductive massage oil.

Yohimbe

Dilates blood vessels, contributing to arousal and orgasm. Occasionally brewed as a tea, but consult a healthcare provider before use; yohimbe has significant physiological effects and can interact with medications.

Notes

When using herbs internally, research contraindications specific to your health situation before proceeding.
