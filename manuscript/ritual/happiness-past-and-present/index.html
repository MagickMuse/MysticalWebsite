A gratitude practice inspired by River Otter's ability to actively seek joy. By listing 30+ happy moments from the past year and keeping a daily gratitude journal, you train your mind to recognize joy and shift perspective from lack to abundance.

Quantities

Materials: Journal or calendar, pen

Directions

  1. Find a comfortable, seated position.
  2. Close your eyes and allow yourself to breathe deeply and naturally, without forcing anything.
  3. Visualize: Imagine a golden thread connecting your heart center to the heart of someone you love deeply (a family member, close friend, beloved pet, or anyone who evokes warm feelings).
  4. Feel that connection glowing with warmth and light.
  5. Notice how safe, loved, and peaceful this feels.
  6. Expand: Now imagine that golden thread extending from your heart to the hearts of everyone you know.
  7. One by one, picture each person's face and allow the warmth to flow between you.
  8. Don't rush.
  9. If someone is difficult, that's okay.
  10. Just acknowledge the thread and move on gently.
  11. Broaden further: Extend the thread to people you don't know well (acquaintances, coworkers, people you pass on the street).
  12. Feel the warmth flowing to and from them, recognizing that like you, they seek happiness and wish to avoid suffering.
  13. Envelope everyone: Now imagine golden threads radiating from your heart in every direction, connecting you to every living being on Earth (people, animals, plants, all life).
  14. Feel yourself as part of a vast, shimmering web of connection.
  15. Notice how everyone is linked, how suffering anywhere touches the whole web, and how compassion heals it.
  16. Rest in the feeling: Sit with this sensation of universal connection and compassion for as long as feels right.
  17. Let the golden light fill you completely.
  18. Return gently: When you're ready, bring your awareness back to your breath, wiggle your fingers and toes, and slowly open your eyes.
  19. Reflect: Take a moment to journal or simply sit with any feelings that arose.
  20. Compassion meditation is a practice, it deepens each time you return to it.
