Samhain

A profound ritual for couples to form or enhance psychic connection through eye gazing, synchronized breathing, and the Great Rite. Creates intimate bond by experiencing each other's inner selves during Samhain's powerful energy. Can be performed symbolically or physically.

Quantities

  • Tray (for altar)
  • Black candle
  • White candle
  • Athame
  • Cup or chalice
  • Wine or water
  • Blanket or rug
  • Pillows (1-2)
  • Massage oils (optional)
  • Private, uninterrupted space

Directions

  1. PURPOSE:
    This ritual forms or enhances a psychic connection between yourself and your partner. Aligning this with Samhain makes it especially powerful; the shared experience can create profound intimacy.
  2. SETTING:
    Indoors, preferably near a fireplace.
  3. PRE-RITUAL PREPARATIONS:
    This ritual involves the Great Rite. While many couples enjoy a physical coupling for this ritual, you can perform it symbolically if your health, morality, or age prevents you from full intercourse. Even if you do elect to perform this in a symbolic manner, take time weeks beforehand to reflect: do you trust your partner? Do you want to expose so much of your inner self to this person? Do you want to see what lives inside of this other being?
  4. Before you begin, lock up any pets and make sure the children can stay with a babysitter.
  5. Turn off any phone or device notifications.
  6. You may want to put a "do not disturb" sign on your door if you feel safe doing so.
  7. Both of you should bathe beforehand.
  8. Wear loose, comfortable clothing.
  9. Set out the blanket or rug.
  10. Set the tray that serves as an altar, with the candles on the outer edges and the athame and chalice between the candles in the center.
  11. Place the black candle on the left and the white candle on the right.
  12. You may want to lay down two to ensure comfort.
  13. Add a pillow or two if either of you experience discomfort sitting for long periods.
  14. Decide between you who should cast the circle, who should call the quarters, and whether one person should perform the invocation or if you should make the invitation together.
  15. THE RITUAL:
    The partner casting the circle should say:
  16. This is our time,
    this is our place
    at the edge of reality,
    where we both feel safe.
    The Lord and the Lady
    both walk here,
    beyond the veil,
    free of mortal fear.
    Above and below,
    within and without,
    surrounded we are
    in protective clouds.
  17. Next call the quarters.
  18. The person calling the quarters should face the east and say:
  19. Hail to the east, to the movement and knowing.
    We look to the direction that you are blowing.
  20. Turn to the south and say:
  21. Hail to the south, to the fires on the hill.
    We look to you to work our will!
  22. Turn to the west and say:
  23. Hail to the west, to the deep run wells.
    We look to you as we work our spells!
  24. Turn to the north and say:
  25. Hail to the north, to the darkened earth.
    We look to you in death and birth!
  26. Here one partner should light the candles as the other says the invocation/invitation, or both may speak together:
  27. Hail to the Lord and Lady!
    We wish you well in your descent,
    great mother!
    As you know your lover,
    shall we know our own!
  28. The person lighting the candles should pour a splash of wine into the cup as the other person performs the second invocation or say together:
  29. Hail to the Morrighan and to Dagda!
    We salute you in your coupling this night,
    as the confluence of two rivers,
    as life and death all come together.
    So mote it be!
  30. Have a moment of silence, to honor the presence of the gods before you begin.
  31. Sit cross-legged, face-to-face.
  32. Gaze into one another's eyes.
  33. Blink naturally and synchronize your breathing.
  34. You may wish to chant, hum, or sing together.
  35. You should both have moments of blurred vision.
  36. It is okay to blink, look away, and resume the gaze.
  37. One partner should say: "I open myself to you." The other should answer, "And I to you."
  38. When you both feel the connection, lay your palms flat against your partner's.
  39. Continue to synchronize your breathing and resume the eye gaze.
  40. Relax and focus on the moment, on the sound of your breathing, and on any sounds in the background.
  41. As you gaze, you may find yourself experiencing emotional responses, visions, or intuitions.
  42. Let yourself have them.
  43. If you wish to laugh or cry, go ahead.
  44. If your partner has this reaction to you, accept it and explore whether you can share in the emotion.
  45. Pay close attention to your physical responses as you touch and gaze.
  46. Some will be familiar, especially those related to sexual attraction.
  47. Others, however, may be new, those will give you deeper information about your partner.
  48. Once the response has passed, resume the gaze until you feel that there is no more to discover.
  49. When the physical sensations have stopped, move on to the Great Rite.
  50. If you choose to perform this symbolically, have one partner take the athame and the other take the cup.
  51. The one who takes the cup should pour a little wine into the chalice, and take a sip, saying:
  52. "This is the love and trust that flows between us."
  53. Offer it to your partner, saying "May you never thirst."
  54. The partner with the athame should accept the cup and, taking a sip, say: "May you never thirst," before handing it back.
  55. Then the cup-bearing partner should hold it up, as the athame bearer lowers the tip of the blade into the cup and says:
  56. As the God and the Goddess come together
    in the rite that makes all things,
    as the Morrighan and the Dagda came together
    for the confluence of life with death,
    so we come together in perfect love and perfect trust,
    in this time between time and this space between space.
  57. Remove the athame, wipe off the tip, and then pass the chalice between you until it is empty.
  58. As you pass the chalice between you, talk to each other about your experiences, gazing into one another's eyes.
  59. If either of you experience or see something difficult, make it part of the work of the new year to understand this difficulty and to heal or integrate it.
  60. Reserve a little wine until the end.
  61. Then, pour out a small amount and raise the glass in a salute.
  62. Say together:
  63. To Morrighan and Dagda, to the Lord and the Lady,
    with gratitude for your presence here,
    and with a loving farewell!
    May we never thirst for understanding between us!
  64. CLOSING:
    The person who called the quarters should in turn release them.
  65. First, turn to the north and say:
  66. Hail to the north, where the world lives,
    we release you with love! So mote it be!
  67. Turn to the west and say:
  68. Hail to the west, where the worlds meet,
    we release you with love! So mote it be!
  69. Turn to the south and say:
  70. Hail to the south, where the bale fires burn!
    We release you with love! So mote it be!
  71. Turn to the east and say:
  72. Hail to the east, where all knowledge lives!
    We release you with love! So mote it be!
  73. The person who cast the circle should then open it, saying:
  74. We open the circle now and here,
    to greet the year free from fear!
    So mote it be!
  75. After the ritual, spend some time planning a few festive activities for the near future.
  76. Pick activities where you can talk to one another and share your impressions.
  77. Taking a class where you both learn and share a skill will especially reinforce the link forged during this ritual.
