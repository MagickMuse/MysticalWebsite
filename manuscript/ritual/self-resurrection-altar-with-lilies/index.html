🔥Self-Resurrection Altar with Lilies

◇Solitary or Group
Solitary
◇Form
Altar Working
◇Setting
Indoor
◇Method
ManifestationDevotional Work
◇Category
Spell
◇Difficulty
Beginner
◇Quantities
A bouquet or a single stem of fresh, fragrant lily, white if the work leans toward resurrection or purity, yellow if it leans toward joy, orange or maroon if it leans toward fertility and abundance. A white candle, large enough to burn for the length of the working. A cup of clean water and a small dish of milk or cream. A piece of paper and a pen.
◇Directions
1. Clean the altar surface fully before beginning. 2. Place the lilies in a vase at the center, the candle to the right, and the cup of water and dish of milk to the left. 3. Write on the paper the name of what is being resurrected, whether a self that was lost, a project that was abandoned, a relationship with your own body, a creative life that withered, or a stream of money that dried up. Place the paper under the vase so the stems rest above it. 4. Light the candle and speak the name of what is being raised. 5. Pour a few drops of milk into the water cup, watch them swirl together, and name aloud the old hurt that needs to be transformed: the goddess's torn breast, the spilled milk, the loss that, in the telling, became flowers. Ask the lilies to do the same work for you, taking what was scattered and growing something fragrant out of it. 6. Sit with the altar as long as the candle burns or as long as you can, breathing the scent of the lilies deliberately. 7. When you are ready to close, pour the milk-water mixture out at the base of a living plant if you have one, or onto the earth outside. 8. Leave the lilies on the altar until they begin to wilt, then compost them with thanks. 9. Keep the paper folded somewhere safe until the working is complete in the world, then burn it.
◇Description
A simple altar working that uses fresh, fragrant lilies as the focal flower for any work of self-resurrection, rebirth, or abundance-after-loss. The rite leans on the double lineage the lily carries: the milk of Hera that made the first lilies on the ground, and the white lily of resurrection and purity that grew up around the Easter story. Either current is welcome, and the working bridges both, asking the flower to mark a return to life after whatever has cut you down.
