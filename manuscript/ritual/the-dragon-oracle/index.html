A two-person oracular rite in which a pair of Bacchae, embodying chthonic Zeus and Persephone, consecrate a braided leather cord by tying a knot around the brightest star in Draco, then breathe, spit, and bleed upon it to birth it as a serpent-avatar of Dionysus-Zagreus. Bound together at the wrists, the pair alternate single words to channel oracular answers through the spirit of the Black Bull.

Quantities

  • 1 yard or more of braided leather cord (the Serpent Cord; may be the Moon Girdle modified with snake skulls, hagstones, variolites, obsidian, or carved willow attached to its ends)\n- Wine\n- Honey\n- Hyacinth incense\n- Black candles (number unspecified; at least 3 for dog-skull holders)\n- 2 masks (one Zeus mask, one Persephone mask; may be elaborate or as simple as translucent black cloth)\n- 3 dog skulls\n- 2 sterile lancets

Directions

  1. Obtain the materials before the rite. Fashion the Serpent Cord: take a yard or more of braided leather and attach to each end objects redolent of serpent power, such as snake skulls, hagstones, variolites, obsidian, or carved willow. The Moon Girdle may be used if suitably modified.\n2. On the chosen night, both Bacchae purify themselves. Bring the Serpent Cord, wine, honey, hyacinth incense, black candles, and two masks to a deserted outdoor location, preferably woodland.\n3. Before entering the space, both Bacchae look up and locate the constellation Draco in the sky. One holds up the cord and, while both recite the Medea Chant, ties a knot in the cord aimed at the brightest star of Draco, imbuing it with the power of the Dragon/Serpent (Zeus and Persephone). The cord is now charged with stellar serpent energy.\n4. Find a secure, secluded spot within the space. Carve a circle in the earth with the Infernal Knife. Set black candles in three dog skulls to invoke Kerberos as guardian. Light the hyacinth incense.\n5. Both Bacchae sing the Consecration Song, then sit within the sacred circle facing each other.\n6. Together, both Bacchae intone the vowel pyramid of the Lyre of Apollon. Then pause.\n7. The first Bacchant (Zeus) raises both hands and recites:\nBehold, I am Zeus, I am Zeus, I am Chthonic Zeus;\nMy form is the dragon's form;\nThis hand, a claw;\nThis mouth, a maw;\nThis skin is scales,\nThe blades of my back, wings.\nBehold, for I am chthonic Zeus;\nFrom my breath all heroes spring.\n8. The first Bacchant leans toward the second and slowly exhales a breath into her mouth as if it were flame. She inhales, then raises her hands and recites:\nBehold, I am Persephone, Persephone, Chthonic Persephone;\nMy form is the serpent's form;\nI have no hands, no feet\nAnd yet I am complete;\nBehold my scales, my tail, my coils\nMy teeth are fangs, my venom, oil\nFrom which the god will rise.\n9. The second Bacchant (Persephone) leans forward and cobra-like spits into the first Bacchant's mouth as if her saliva were venom.\n10. Both Bacchae put on their masks: Zeus mask for the first, Persephone mask for the second.\n11. Each Bacchant pricks a finger with a separate sterile lancet. Together they say:\nLet our bloods mingle\nLet our bloods match\nAnd let, you gods,\nThere come to pass\nA Serpent-Bull, a Bull-Serpent\nFrom the sacred current\nWhich now between us flows.\n12. The Bacchae streak the cord with a drop of their blood. Then invoke Dionysus three times:\nBROMIOS! CHTHONIOS! ZAGREUS!\nBROMIOS! CHTHONIOS! ZAGREUS!\nBROMIOS! CHTHONIOS! ZAGREUS!\nO Serpent Who Becomes a Bull!\nO Bull Who Becomes a Serpent!\nDionysus * Dionysus * Dionysus\nBe born! Be born! Be born!\nSpeak now through your parents' mouths\nBreathe now through your parents' lungs\nO Bull with black horns,\nO Serpent with a forked tongue!\n13. The cord is now a spirit-serpent avatar of Dionysus. Each Bacchant loosely ties one end of the cord around the other's wrist. Facing each other and holding hands with palms lightly touching, both close their eyes and intone the Y vowel (of the Underworld) together until they achieve harmony and enter a light trance.\n14. One Bacchant asks a question aloud. The other begins the answer with its first word; they alternate one word each, channeling Dionysus, until the oracle is complete. The speaker of the final word says: I have spoken. Silence falls until another question is posed, or the god speaks without encouragement. When speaking, Bacchae stare into each other's eyes; when silent, eyes close.\n15. When all questions are answered, one Bacchant breaks eye contact and applies pressure to the other's hands to signal the oracle is finished.\n16. Both Bacchae remove their masks and thank Dionysus in their own words. Pour the wine on the ground as libation. Thank the cord, kiss it, touch it with a drop of honey and wine, and lay it gently to rest in its box. Henceforth the cord must always be treated with utmost respect: it is a spirit-serpent, avatar of Dionysus, and the Bacchae's child.\n17. Blow out the candles, formally dismissing Kerberos as each is extinguished. Leave the space.
