A Norse runic consecration rite for creating mobile sacred space (vé). The practitioner projects the complete Elder Futhark in a clockwise ring, visualizes a sphere of blue light with a red rune band as its equator, and signs the hammer (hamarsmark) in all six directions while intoning Old Norse galdrar. The rite shields consciousness for magickal or meditational work and can be adapted for active magickal purposes.

Quantities

  • Rune wand
  • Knife (optional, for releasing collected energy)

Directions

  1. Hold the rune wand in the right hand and face the North Star.
  2. Beginning with fehu in the north, project each rune of the Elder Futhark outward at solar plexus level, sending them clockwise in a ring that extends to the boundary of the working circle.
  3. The band should form a complete loop with othala arriving beside fehu in the north.
  4. Assume the cross stadha and visualize an equilateral cross lying horizontally in the plane of the rune ring, centered at the solar plexus.
  5. The arms of this cross extend outward to meet the rune band at its cardinal points.
  6. Around this cross, envision a surrounding sphere of shimmering blue light with the red rune band running along its equator.
  7. Then see the vertical axis descending through your full length from infinite space above and infinite space below.
  8. Feel the force flowing inward from all six directions toward your center, building there into a sphere of glowing red might.
  9. The color of this sphere may be changed to suit the ritual intention.
  10. Touch the base of the wand to the breast at the center of power, then thrust it forward, projecting force from that center to a point on the inner face of the outer sphere.
  11. Sign the hammer (hamarsmark) from the gathered magickal might while intoning:
  12. Hamarr í Nordhri helga vé thetta ok hald vördh!
    (Hammer in the North, hallow and hold this holy stead!)
  13. Turn ninety degrees to the right and sign another hammer while vibrating:
  14. Hamarr í Austri helga vé thetta ok hald vördh!
    (Hammer in the East, hallow and hold this holy stead!)
  15. Hamarr í Sudhri helga vé thetta ok hald vördh!
    (Hammer in the South, hallow and hold this holy stead!)
  16. Hamarr í Vestri helga vé thetta ok hald vördh!
    (Hammer in the West, hallow and hold this holy stead!)
  17. Return to face north and direct attention overhead.
  18. Sign the hamarsmark on the ceiling of the sphere, saying:
  19. Hamarr undir mér helgn vé thetta ok hald vördh!
    (Hammer over me, hallow and hold this holy stead!)
  20. Project the hammer sign downward to the floor of the sphere (not the physical ground) and intone:
  21. Hamarr undir mér helga vé thetta ok hald vördh!
    (Hammer under me, hallow and hold this holy stead!)
  22. Assume the cross stadha once more and sing:
  23. Hamarr helga vé thetta ok hald vördh!
    (Hammer, hallow and hold this holy stead!)
  24. Turn in the center of the vé and repeat this galdr once for each of the remaining four cardinal directions and once along the vertical axis.
  25. The visual result should be six axes of shining red hammers radiating from the personal center, all engulfed by a field of sparkling deep blue light and encircled by the bright red band of runes.
  26. Finally, fold the arms inward from the cross stadha, bringing the fingertips together at the solar plexus, and speak:
  27. Um mik ok í mér Ásgardhr ok Midhgardhr!
    (Around me and in me, Ásgardhr and Midhgardhr!)
  28. The rite may be performed again at the close of a working.
  29. The entire sphere can be drawn into the personal center, or the walls of the globe can be split with a knife to release the collected energy toward its intended goal.
  30. This rite is designed primarily to shield consciousness for magickal or meditational work.
  31. The form can be modified for active magickal use, and the runes on the face of the sphere can be drawn from or projected outward to create magickal effects.
  32. If a permanent sacred space is available, the rite need not be repeated each time.
  33. The working is best understood as a method for making sacred space highly portable.
