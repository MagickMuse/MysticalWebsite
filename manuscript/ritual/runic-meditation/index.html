🧘Runic Meditation

◇Tradition
Norse
◇Solitary or Group
Solitary
◇Form
Meditation/Visualization
◇Setting
Indoor
◇Method
MeditationVisualizationChant/MantraEnergy WorkProtection
◇Category
Meditation
◇Quantities
- Runic meditation card (one per rune, placed at eye level) - Paper and pen (for informal sessions) - Compass and protractor (optional, for informal geometric work)
◇Directions
1. Begin by performing one of the protective-invocatory rites while strongly visualizing the rune ring. 2. Assume a comfortable position, either sitting, standing, or in the stadha of the chosen rune. 3. Face north, east, or in the angle indicated by the rune's position within the ring. 4. Place a runic meditation card at eye level, either attached to the wall or set on a simple stand. 5. Fix the gaze firmly on the runic form shown on the card and softly sing the rune galdr, either aloud or inwardly. 6. If desired, introduce the esoteric meaning of the rune name on a secondary level of consciousness, building the center of concentration around the intended elements of the runic complex. 7. Slowly close the eyes while continuing the galdr and any contemplation of esoteric principle. 8. Visualize the form of the rune as it appeared on the card and in the mind's eye, striving to realize the oneness of the form-sound-idea complex. 9. In early practice it may be necessary to open the eyes briefly to reestablish the stave form, but with experience this step can be eliminated in favor of direct inward contemplation. 10. Maintain this state of inner concentration on the runic complex for at least several seconds, working gradually toward a span of five minutes. 11. Then lapse into inner silence, remaining totally attentive. 12. In this void the word of the rune will sound with a resounding peal, a word that cannot be expressed in any language but conveys the totality of the runic mystery in a single moment. 13. This is a holy experience in which the rune and the hugr of the vitki are momentarily unified. 14. Continue the meditation as long as a link with the runic force is felt. 15. In this state the vitki may be led along countless rune paths where secrets about the rune itself are revealed or relationships between runes become clear. 16. When the linkage dissipates or the vitki wishes to end the session, speak a formula such as "Now the work is wrought" and open the eyes. 17. Then ritually break the rune ring according to the Hammer Rite. 18. For informal sessions, sit at a writing desk surrounded by runic glyphs and cosmological diagrams. 19. Still the mind and turn it toward the rune world, allowing the hugr to wander until it settles on a seed concept. 20. Follow that concept relentlessly, drawing and noting revelations as they arise. 21. These notations serve as the basis for further work. 22. Informal sessions are best left unscheduled, undertaken whenever the spirit moves.
◇Description
A Norse meditational practice for developing a personal link with each rune of the Elder Futhark and the runic cosmology as a whole. The vitki concentrates on the threefold runic complex of form, sound (galdr), and root idea (name), stilling the hugr until the rune speaks directly to consciousness. The practice ranges from formal ceremonies within a protected vé to informal sessions at a writing desk, and opens a permanent stream of runic wisdom accessible in any spare moment.
