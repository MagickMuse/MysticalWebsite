🍀Twelve Grapes at Midnight

◇Solitary or Group
Either
◇Form
Food/Edible
◇Setting
Either
◇Method
ManifestationProtection
◇Category
Spell
◇Difficulty
Beginner
◇Quantities
Twelve ripe grapes per participant, washed, stemmed, and counted out into a small bowl or coupe. A glass of sparkling wine, sparkling cider, or grape juice for the toast that follows. A clock, bell, or recording capable of marking the twelve strokes of midnight clearly. Optional: a candle to light at the first stroke and let burn through the toast.
◇Directions
1. In the final hour before midnight on New Year's Eve, count out twelve grapes for each person at the gathering and arrange them in front of each place. If working alone, hold the bowl in your lap or set it on the altar where you can reach it easily. 2. Light the candle if you are using one, and steady yourself with a few slow breaths, bringing to mind the year that is closing and the year that is about to begin. 3. As the clock begins to strike midnight, eat one grape at each stroke, in pace with the bells. With each grape, name silently or aloud one of the twelve months of the coming year, asking that the month bring luck, abundance, blessings, and protection in the measure that is right for you. 4. If you fall behind the bells, finish the grapes as quickly as you can while the final chime still rings: the work is done when the twelfth grape is swallowed before the last note fades. 5. When the twelfth grape is eaten, raise the glass and toast aloud to the year ahead, drinking to seal the petition. 6. Save the stems and skins, if any, in a small dish; bury them in the garden or release them under a tree the next morning as thanks to the vine for its blessing.
◇Description
A Spanish New Year's tradition that has spread across the Spanish-speaking world: eating twelve grapes in time with the twelve strokes of midnight on New Year's Eve to seal the coming year with luck, abundance, and protection. Each grape stands for one of the twelve months ahead, and each swallow is a small act of magick aligning the eater with prosperity and warding off the misfortunes of the year to come.
