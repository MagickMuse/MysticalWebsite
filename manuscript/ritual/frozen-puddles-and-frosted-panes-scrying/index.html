Ice scrying divination using naturally formed ice patterns in frozen puddles, icicles, or frosted windowpanes. The crystalline structures reveal messages through their shapes, patterns, and the way light moves through them.

Quantities

  • Naturally frozen puddles, icicles, or frosted windows
  • Camera (optional, for recording patterns)
  • Journal and pen
  • Quiet observation time
  • Warm clothing for outdoor scrying

Directions

  1. To scry a frozen puddle, find one with clear ice rather than snow-covered ice.
  2. Observe during sunrise or sunset when light plays through the ice and note patterns: cracks, bubbles, inclusions, and color variations.
  3. Gaze softly at the surface, allowing images to emerge, then interpret shapes using traditional scrying symbolism and record your visions immediately.
  4. For frosted window scrying, allow windows to frost naturally overnight without artificial frost.
  5. Observe upon waking before the frost melts, looking for recognizable shapes such as feathers, ferns, faces, and symbols.
  6. Photograph patterns if desired for later contemplation and note which direction the window faces, as this adds directional symbolism.
  7. For icicle divination, observe icicle formation on your home or natural features; number and size indicate intensity of the message, direction of drip reveals where energy flows, clarity versus cloudiness shows how clear the path forward is, and broken or intact icicles indicate completion or interruption.
  8. Common pattern meanings: radiating lines suggest energy expanding outward and growth; circles and spirals point to cycles, returns, and completion; vertical lines relate to ascension and spiritual matters; horizontal lines indicate grounding and earthly concerns; stars and crosses mark significant events, crossroads, or divine intervention; organic shapes such as ferns and feathers carry messages of natural growth and nature spirits; geometric patterns call for order, structure, and mental clarity; and faces or figures represent ancestors, spirits, or aspects of self seeking attention.
  9. This practice is best observed during cold snaps when ice forms thickly and clearly, with dawn and dusk offering the most dramatic lighting.
