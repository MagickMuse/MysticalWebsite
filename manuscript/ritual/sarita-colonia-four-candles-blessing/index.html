A ritual burning four colored candles simultaneously to petition Sarita Colonia for health, love, work, and joy. Sarita is the patron of the marginalized and those struggling with life's difficulties.

Quantities

  • 1 white candle (for health)
  • 1 red candle (for love)
  • 1 green candle (for work)
  • 1 yellow candle (for joy)
  • Fresh roses or other flowers (optional)
  • Image of Sarita Colonia (optional)

Directions

  1. Prepare your altar or working space. If you have an image of Sarita Colonia, place it in the center. Arrange fresh roses or flowers around it if desired.
  2. Position the four candles. Arrange them in a square or cross pattern around the image:
  3. White candle for health
  4. Red candle for love
  5. Green candle for work
  6. Yellow candle for joy
  7. Light all four candles simultaneously. As you light each one, speak its purpose:
  8. "White for health"
  9. "Red for love"
  10. "Green for work"
  11. "Yellow for joy"
  12. Speak to Sarita. Tell her your needs. She is known for her boundless generosity and accepts everyone without judgment. Be honest about your struggles.
  13. Let the candles burn. Allow them to burn as long as safely possible. If you must extinguish them, snuff rather than blow.
  14. Leave offerings. Fresh flowers, especially roses, are traditional. Water that has held flowers at her shrine becomes holy water.

Background

Sarita Colonia was a young Peruvian woman who worked multiple jobs to support her family, sharing everything she had with others despite her own poverty. Since her death in 1940, she has emerged as a powerful saint for the marginalized, the poor, and anyone who feels excluded by conventional religion.

Best Timing

  • 1 March (Sarita's birthday)
  • 12 December (anniversary of her death)
  • Any time of desperate need

Who This Ritual Is For

Sarita accepts everyone. She is especially beloved by:

  • Those facing financial hardship
  • Immigrants and those separated from family
  • LGBTQ+ individuals
  • Anyone who feels judged or excluded
  • Those in dangerous professions or neighborhoods

The Power of Flowers

Flowers that have been in contact with Sarita's shrine absorb her power. If you visit her mausoleum in Callao, Peru, you may take flowers from the shrine for healing and spiritual cleansing—but bring fresh ones to replace them so others may benefit too.
