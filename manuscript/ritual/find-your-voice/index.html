◇Directions
1. When Gray Wolf is lost and lonely, he howls to find his pack. 2. When Wolf has a good hunt, he howls to share his joy. 3. When Wolf howls, he doesn't worry whether he is singing on key; he doesn't care if his voice warbles. 4. He simply expands and vibrates, opening his throat and, through it, his heart and mind. 5. Your throat connects your head with your heart. 6. Opening your throat will help you feel connected and whole. 7. Start by humming. 8. You can hum a tune or just one note. 9. Feel the air moving through your larynx, vibrating your being. 10. Now try humming with your mouth open. 11. Notice how this feels. 12. Do your teeth vibrate? 13. Next try toning: Create a single note, changing tone each time you take a new breath. 14. Feel into each note. 15. Notice if you feel it in your head or your throat or deep in your gut. 16. Are you scared to be heard? 17. Keep practicing opening your throat and perhaps one day you'll find yourself ready to howl!
◇Description
A throat-opening practice inspired by Gray Wolf's howl. Through humming, toning, and eventually howling, vibrate your being and connect head to heart. Wolf doesn't worry about singing on key, he simply opens his throat to share joy, call the pack, and feel whole.
