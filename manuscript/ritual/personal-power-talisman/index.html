A sewn and charged talisman that strengthens personal energy, confidence, and magickal growth.

Quantities

1 piece of cloth (favorite color and texture)
Needle and thread
1 piece of gold cord or ribbon, long enough to tie eight knots
8 small personal power charms (natural, meaningful, or symbolic)
Cinnamon powder, a cinnamon stick, or essential oil of cinnamon
1 pentacle or pentagram (any form; can be drawn)
Favorite incense
Candle (favorite color and scent)
Paint, markers, beads, or embroidery thread for decoration

Directions

  1. Sew a small charm bag from the cloth and thread.
  2. Decorate it with a symbol of personal power, such as an astrological sign or power animal.
  3. Fill the bag with eight personal charms that resonate with you, using natural items where possible, such as shed feathers, dried plants, small stones, or meaningful trinkets.
  4. Add cinnamon powder or a cinnamon stick; if using cinnamon essential oil, wait to anoint until the bag is tied.
  5. Tie the bag shut with the gold cord using eight knots.
  6. Set the talisman on a pentacle and let it charge in moonlight for one or more nights.
  7. At midnight, ideally on a Full Moon or your personal power day, create sacred space and place the pentacle on your altar with the bag, candle, and incense on it.
  8. Light the candle, then the incense.
  9. Raise power and hold the bag in the incense smoke with your projective hand, turning it slowly so all sides are smudged, as you say:
    "By moonlight and midnight,
    By starlight and insight,
    By smoke and candlelight,
    I accept my birthright."
  10. Beginning with North and moving clockwise, hold the talisman up and say:
    "Charged on this day, and at this hour,
    To help me grow and let me flower,
    By the powers of the Lord of each Watchtower,
    This talisman will stimulate my personal power.
    By the powers of the Lord of each Watchtower,
    This talisman will activate my personal power!"
  11. Return the talisman to the pentacle until the candle and incense burn out, focusing on filling it with energy.
  12. Ground the power and clean up.
  13. Keep the talisman with you as often as possible, recharging it as needed with incense smoke or moonlight.
  14. When it is no longer needed, bury, burn, or discard it respectfully, keeping any stones for future use after cleansing.

What is Personal Power?

Your personal power is the sum of your strengths, abilities, and untapped potential. It is the force that drives you to succeed with spells, live a magickal life, and achieve what you are capable of becoming. A Personal Power Talisman serves as a tool to help you overcome self-doubt, build confidence, and access your full potential whenever you need it.

The magick of this talisman begins the moment you start thinking about making it. The more slowly and deliberately you craft it, the more powerful it becomes. Deep thought, careful attention to detail, and genuine intention all contribute to its strength.

Ways to Use Your Talisman

Once charged, this talisman has many applications. Hold it before performing divination (tarot readings, I Ching castings, rune work). Place it on your altar while casting spells to amplify your working. Keep it nearby while studying or taking tests. Put it under your pillow to enhance dream work. Touch it whenever you need a magickal boost of confidence.

Selecting Your Charms

The eight charms you place inside should resonate personally with power for you. Consider including images or tokens of your power animal or totem animal, lucky stones or coins that have been with you for years, and small natural items you have collected over time.

When sourcing materials, favor items acquired without causing harm. Shed feathers are better than plucked ones. Fallen leaves and branches are preferable to those torn from living trees. Seeds and flowers should be gathered without preventing a plant from reproducing. Purchased items like spices work just as well as wildcrafted materials.

Herbs and plants are best used dried to prevent spoilage inside the mojo bag. Animal representations might include pictures, small statues, carvings, or if you have ethical access to shed fur or hair, those carry more power than images alone.
