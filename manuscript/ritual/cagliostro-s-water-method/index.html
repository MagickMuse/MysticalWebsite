An 18th-century adaptation of the Alexandrian child oracle method attributed to Count Alessandro di Cagliostro, in which a child scrys a pan of water surrounded by white candles and describes their visions. The querent records all observations for later interpretation. ⚠️ This is a historical method involving a child as oracle. It must only be undertaken with full voluntary consent, treated as play, and stopped immediately at the child's request. See the Children correspondence page for full ethical context. One of the Child Oracle methods.

Quantities

  • A pan of water
  • White candles (several)

Directions

  1. Arrange the ritual space in advance and perform all needed cleansings and protective workings before the child enters the room.
  2. The child may observe preparations but the querent alone performs all ritual actions.
  3. Set up a low table holding a pan of water and a number of white candles.
  4. Keep the atmosphere calm and neutral: the aim is neither to frighten the child nor to make the working feel artificial or performative.
  5. Have the child sit on the floor and gaze into the pan of water.
  6. Place your hand lightly on the child's head for a moment and, if not already done, briefly explain what you are asking them to do.
  7. Then step back.
  8. Maintain awareness of fire safety throughout.
  9. Invite the child to look into the water again and simply describe whatever they see.
  10. No trance, possession, or dramatic framing is required or desired.
  11. Write down everything the child says exactly as spoken, for later analysis.
  12. Do not lead, correct, or interpret the child's words during the working.
