🕯️Oedipus' Purification Rite to the Eumenides

A purification rite drawn from Sophocles' Oedipus at Colonus, performed when one has trespassed upon ground sacred to the Eumenides (the Kindly Ones, the Erinyes) and must make atonement. The cleansing is accomplished through libations of honeyed water poured from ritually pure hands, the laying of olive branches in nines, and a silent prayer spoken inaudibly to the goddesses, after which the worshipper departs without looking back.

Quantities

Drink-offerings drawn from an ever-flowing spring
Two bowls of skilled craftsmanship, each with crowned edges and double handles
Freshly-shorn wool from a ewe-lamb
Pure water
Honey (no wine)
Twenty-seven olive branches (three times nine)

Directions

  1. With ritually pure hands, gather the sacred drink-offerings from an ever-flowing spring and carry them to the place where the trespass occurred.
  2. Crown the edges and the two handles of the prepared bowls with the freshly-shorn wool of a ewe-lamb.
  3. Stand facing the dawn.
  4. Pour the offerings in three streams from the bowls.
  5. Fill the final vessel with water and honey, adding no wine.
  6. Once the dark earth beneath the shade has drunk the offering, lay down nine olive branches with both hands. Repeat this gesture three times in all, so that twenty-seven branches in total are laid upon the ground.
  7. Speak the prayer inaudibly, never raising the voice: "We call on the Eumenides, that with kindly and well-wishing hearts they may receive this suppliant as their saviours."
  8. When the prayer is finished, depart at once from the place, and do not look behind on the way out.

Drawn from Sophocles, Oedipus at Colonus.
