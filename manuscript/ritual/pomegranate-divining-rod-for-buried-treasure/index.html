Pomegranate

A folk dowsing method using a forked pomegranate branch to locate buried treasure, lost objects, or hidden springs.

Quantities

One forked branch of pomegranate, fresh or seasoned, roughly shoulder width
Clean cloth for wrapping the rod between uses
Small offering of pomegranate seeds or juice for the land
Optional: written description of what you are searching for

Directions

  1. Cut a forked branch from a living pomegranate tree with permission, leaving a respectful offering at its base.
  2. Strip the small leaves but keep the bark.
  3. Season the rod for at least a single moon cycle, wrapped in cloth in a dark place, naming aloud what you intend it to find each time you check it.
  4. To use the rod, hold the two short ends of the fork in your palms with the long single end pointed forward and slightly down, elbows soft at your sides.
  5. Walk slowly across the ground you suspect holds what you seek, speaking aloud what you are searching for.
  6. When the rod tips, twists, or dives toward the earth, mark the spot and dig.
  7. After a successful find, pour pomegranate juice on the ground as thanks.
  8. Wrap the rod in clean cloth between uses and keep it out of sunlight to preserve it for years of use.

This folk divining method from Italy uses a forked branch of pomegranate wood as a dowsing rod. The pomegranate's correspondence with hidden abundance, its seeds packed inside a hidden chamber, makes its wood unusually responsive to what is concealed below. The pomegranate is considered the fruit of the underworld and the keeper of hidden things.

Notes

The rod works for buried metal, lost objects, hidden springs, forgotten boundary stones, and old caches of coin. It can also be used metaphorically over a map for distance-dowsing, with the same offering rules. If the rod refuses to move for several attempts, leave a larger offering at the parent tree and try again on a different day. Do not use this method on land that is not yours or that you do not have permission to disturb.
