Kleidophorous Sigil

Meaning: Key-Bearer. A consecration rite that turns an ordinary key into a vessel for Hekate's power over thresholds, used for protection, to compel fortune, or to send the spirit-self to sacred places. Day 16 of the Hekate Devotee cycle.

Quantities

  • One or more keys
  • Spring water
  • Grave-earth
  • Myrrh, for fumes
  • A drop of the Devotee's blood

Directions

  1. Consecrate a key in spring water, then in grave-earth, then in the fumes of burning myrrh. This triple-consecration acknowledges Hekate's ouranian, einalian, and chthonian jurisdiction.
  2. Inscribe the key with the Kleidophorous sigil.
  3. Add a drop of the Devotee's blood to the key to feed its resident spirit, activating the item.
  4. Wear the consecrated key for protection, or press it into the earth teeth-first to compel the fortune of another, for better or worse.
  5. Set two consecrated keys into the earth on either side of a home's entrance, or hang them by nails from the door-frames, to provide protection.
  6. Leave a key bearing the Devotee's blood at a sacred or special place to which the Devotee hopes to send her spirit-self in dreams or visions.

Arcana

As Key-Bearer, Hekate decides who passes through the portals of place, space and time. The most significant portals for the Devotee are those which link the upper and lower worlds. As one who travels between worlds with impunity, Hekate has unfettered access to all gods, ghosts, spirits and daimons, and thus she is an invaluable ally of the sorceress, being uniquely suited to aid in her quest to access other realms, petition gods for favor, raise spirits, visit the Underworld, and speak to the dead.

Although Hekate is often portrayed as holding a key, she herself is the true key. She alone unlocks mysteries and unleashes the forces within us. Knowing this is the first and most important step toward piercing the veil of unknowing, and crossing the thresholds that stand between our will and its target.

One of Hekate's main functions is Tartarouchos (Guardian of Tartaros). Tartaros is the dark pit where the souls of the wicked reside. Supposedly it would take an anvil nine days to fall from Earth to Tartaros, which was said to be as far below the Earth as Olympos was high above it. Tartaros was said to contain all of the most rebellious titans. There in the dark they reside, imprisoned by an adamantine gate. Fierce, three-headed Kerberos guards it, and Hekate holds the only key. Thus, as Tartarouchos, Hekate is crucial to maintaining the regime of the gods and the safety of mortals. This has two applications for Devotees. The first is that Hekate, as Tartarouchos, is the one who can capture and contain malevolent spirits which would mischief the Devotee. The other is that she can free them. The Devotee will, in time, require both services.

Devotion is divine: it is the gods' due. But magic is titanic. To work magic the Devotee must draw upon ancient knowledge and call upon the faceless forces of the Earth, compelling them by the bonds that bind them, to do her will. Doing so requires a titanic intermediary - which is Hekate's role. As the Gate-Keeper (Propylaia) who bears the key to opening other realms, Hekate can be induced, under the proper circumstances, to unleash titanic forces which can bend the very skein of Fate.
