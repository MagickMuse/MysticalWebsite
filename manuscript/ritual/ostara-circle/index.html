🕯️Ostara Circle

◇Form
Circle RiteAltar Working
◇Method
InvocationElementalDirectional MagickMeditationDevotional Work
◇Category
Ceremony
◇Quantities
- Three altars: Elements and Deities, Balance, New Beginnings - Element representations: water in a clear glass container, earth (dirt, salt, or sand), air (burning incense or a feather), fire (candle or small torch) - Deity representations: gold item or candle for the God, silver item or candle for the Goddess - Purification setup at entry: two fireproof cauldrons with burning sage or charcoal tablets for oil drops - Offering items for the Lord and Lady (your choice, prepared in advance) - Optional: decorative items, participants’ lists of intended new beginnings, vision boards
◇Directions
1. Assign roles and prepare three altars: an Elements and Deities altar with close-to-true element representations; a Balance altar showing pairs or a weighing scale; and a New Beginnings altar with eggs, spring symbols, and participants’ intention lists or vision boards. 2. Space altars apart if outdoors, or place them at different directions if indoors. 3. Decorate collaboratively and prepare appropriate offerings. 4. Purify participants at the entrance according to group size. 5. For small groups, smudge or anoint each person with a purifying oil before entry. 6. For large groups, set two fireproof cauldrons at the entrance with burning sage or charcoal tablets; attendants add drops of oil as needed so everyone passes through the cleansing smoke. 7. Cast the circle around the gathered group. 8. If your tradition has a high priestess or high priest, they may lead; otherwise any designated facilitator may do so. 9. Call the elements with four different callers, one at each direction, each lifting the physical representation high so all can see. 10. Use the following invocations with line breaks preserved: 11. Sacred air, blessed by the Goddess and watchtowers of the east, join with us tonight on this sacred day of equal day and equal night. 12. Sacred fire, blessed by the Goddess and watchtowers of the south, join with us tonight on this sacred day of equal day and equal night. 13. Sacred water, blessed by the Goddess and watchtowers of the west, join with us tonight on this sacred day of equal day and equal night. 14. Sacred earth, blessed by the Goddess and watchtowers of the north, join with us tonight on this sacred day of equal day and equal night. 15. Invite the Goddess, lifting her representation or lighting her candle: Maiden Goddess, soon to be Mother, known by many names, we invoke your presence today, on this day of balance, of equal day and equal night, on this day of beginnings, to join with us in celebration and rejoicing of the conception of the son, the sun child who will grow stronger each day until his birth at Yule. Goddess of the earth, Goddess of all living things, Goddess of the spring, join with us now. 16. Invite the God, lifting his representation or lighting his candle: Robust Lord, soon to be Father, known by many names, we invoke your presence today, on this day of balance, of equal day and equal night, on this day of beginnings, to join with us in celebration and rejoicing of the conception of the son, your son, who shall become you upon Yule, his day of birth. God of the earth, God of all living things, God of spring, join with us now. 17. Offer the seasonal address while moving among the people, keeping it dynamic and engaging, with line breaks preserved where poetic: Today is the day of the Vernal Equinox. It is the day the earth awakens, with streams flowing once again. The sap courses through trees, bearing the announcement of new life. Snow melts away, the grass begins its greening, and soon flowers shall be fragrant on the breeze. The earth has come alive once more. Today is a day of balance and a day of new beginnings. The light and the dark are in balance. Today brings the dawning of a new spring. As the earth begins its cycle of new life, so must we begin a cycle of new life as well. As the earth sits in balance with the sun and the moon, with the day and the night, so must we sit in balance with the world, with our environment, with our deities, and within our own selves. 18. At the Balance altar, invite participants forward in small groups. Facilitators ask brief reflective questions about what is out of balance and which areas have become overemphasized. Close this working: Some changes must be made in order for us to bring our lives into balance as it should be. In order to restore this balance, this spring we have new beginnings we want to make, and we ask our Lord and Lady to guide us on our way. 19. At the New Beginnings altar, invite participants to focus on chosen changes and intentions. Use lists or vision boards as focal tools. Close this working: These changes brought forward in the minds of our people today are needed and wanted to restore full balance to our lives. We ask for strength and guidance as we travel the road laid out before us. Changes are not always easy, but can lead to bigger and better circumstances, so we will work hard to make these changes a part of our everyday lives to ensure balance is achieved and remains a constant in our lives. 20. Return to center and reaffirm resolve: New beginnings may be difficult, but we have the strength to see it through. The strength from the old year continues on, and enters into the new year. While our path may not always be clear, and at times our future may seem cloudy, we will continue down the road we know we are supposed to follow. We will work hard to obtain and accomplish our goals and always continue to set our sights higher and higher, never backing down or giving up. The springtime is the time to plant the seeds, and these seeds we plant within ourselves. The springtime is the time for hope, for desire, for new ideas and inspiration. We will walk hand in hand with the Lord and the Lady to follow our paths, and we will follow their guidance with grace and pleasure. 21. Present offerings to the deities, stating what they are before placing them back on the altar. Give thanks and bid farewell: To our Lord, we thank you for your presence and your love. In our devotion we bid you farewell. Depart from us at your will. To our Lady, we thank you for your presence and your love. In our devotion we bid you farewell. Depart from us at your will. 22. Release the quarters, each caller facing their direction: Watchtower of the east, blessed air, that which keeps us all alive, depart from us at your will. Watchtower of the south, blessed fire, that which brings heat to us all, depart from us at your will. Watchtower of the west, blessed water, source of life, in our devotion we bid you farewell. Depart from us at your will. Watchtower of the north, blessed earth, home of where all seeds grow, depart from us at your will. 23. All together close: The circle shall be open but unbroken.
◇Description
A participatory Ostara group ritual with three working altars that invokes Air, Fire, Water, Earth and welcomes the God and Goddess to help participants restore balance and initiate new beginnings. Designed for covens or public gatherings with delegated roles and interactive stations.
