A liminal-hour hymn that consecrates each of the five senses in turn, preparing the reader to perceive Hekate's approach. Rite 5 of Book I.

Quantities

1 pen, 1 sheet of paper, 1 bowl, matches, 3 white candles, myrrh incense with a censer or heat-safe holder, cinnamon for dusting the hands.

Directions

  1. As usual, wash your hands thrice, dust them with cinnamon, draw Hekate's sigil, and burn myrrh incense. This time light three white candles: one at the sigil's center, and one at the end of each trident.
  2. Settle your breath and close your eyes. Picture yourself again on the path before the crossroads, the sea to your left, the dark woods to your right. With three exhalations, feel the sigil rise upright beneath your feet, aligning with your spine and sending blue fire from your pelvis to the crown of your head.
  3. Over five breaths, watch the storm cell above you unfurl and dissipate, revealing the night sky. See the stars of Orion, and below them the hound Canis Major, whose brightest star, Sirius the Dog Star, flashes blue-green and is closely tied to Hekate.
  4. Watch five long beams of blue-green light stretch from that star to touch the earth around you, left, right, before, behind, and at your feet, converging into a single beam that strikes your eye. Inhale twice, and as you exhale, envision blue-green fire streaming from your mouth, splitting and curling back around you into a helix of woven light: a web of fire, recalling her epithet ARKUIA, the Netter. Exhale, and open your eyes.
  5. This hymn has five sections, one for each sense. As you begin each section, touch the named body part; at its end, as the closing epithet is spoken twice, hold both hands a few inches above the two trident flames, feeling their warmth as a blessing. Cover the sigil and read the full hymn aloud:
    "Before me are two pillars, one of ivory, one of bone; / Before me is a hole in the earth, leading to my home; / Before me is a curtain of stars, through which I pass alone; / Through which I pass, and pass, and pass, / Never to return.
    EYES, / I will see what the Sybil saw, and the Sybil / Saw four black bulls shimmering through the flames / Whose bellows rent the earth like a garment; / I will see what Medea saw, and Medea / Saw the crescent moon's tip / Drip blood like a sacrificial knife; / I will see what Solomon saw, and Solomon / Saw a woman uncoiling within a ring of chalk, / Six-armed like a scorpion, bearing two torches, two whips, two blades. / DADOUKHOS DADOUKHOS, / Bless these eyes that they may see you / All about, within, without, above and below, in all seasons.
    HANDS, / I will touch what Persephone touched, and Persephone / Touched a hand in the darkness / Which led her to the light; / I will touch what Clytius touched, and Clytius / Touched a flame of unendurable heat / Which burned away his impurities; / I will touch what Jason touched, and Jason / Touched dragon's teeth as smooth as seeds, / And like black seeds, he sowed them. / ATALOS ATALOS, / Bless these hands that they may feel you / All about, within, without, above and below, in all seasons.
    TONGUE, / I will taste what Dionysus tasted, and Dionysus / Sucked warm milk from a black she-goat / At dusk in a cave on Mt. Nysa; / I will taste what Ladon tasted, and Dragon Ladon / Licked poppy-broth from a juniper branch / That cooled his fiery fury; / I will taste what Odysseus tasted, and Odysseus / Swallowed honey, wine and barleymeal / Mixed with baneful herbs, yet lived and loved thereafter. / APOTROPAIA APOTROPAIA, / Bless this tongue that it may taste you / All about, within, without, above and below, in all seasons.
    NOSTRILS, / I will smell what the titans smell when the titans / Are raised from the earth, and what the titans smell / Is blood. / I will smell what the gods smell when the gods / Are pulled from their orbits, and what the gods smell / Is frankincense. / I will smell what the dead smell when the dead / Are pulled from their graves, and what the dead smell / Is myrrh. / ANGELOS ANGELOS, / Bless these nostrils that they may scent you / All about, within, without, above and below, in all seasons.
    EARS, / I will hear what Byzantium heard when Byzantium / Heard hounds barking deep within the earth, / Signaling the Macedonian approach. / I will hear what the Theban sorcerer heard when he, / Proclaiming himself a god, heard the hiss, pop and howl / That came from his own throat. / I will hear what Demeter heard when Demeter, / Wailing at her daughter's rape, heard from the cave / A voice saying, 'Mother, why do you weep?' / PSYCHOPOMP * PSYCHOPOMP, / Bless these ears that they may hear you / All about, within, without, above and below, in all seasons.
    Before me are two pillars, one of ivory, one of bone; / Before me is a hole in the earth, leading to my home; / Before me is a curtain of stars, through which I pass alone; / Through which I pass, and pass, and pass, / Never to return."
  6. When finished, close your eyes and extend your awareness to the three candles before you, sensing their heat, light, scent, and shape; then extend your senses outward in every direction for any other presence, listening especially for the barking of dogs. Abide with whatever you sense, or accept its absence, without concern.
  7. End the rite by blowing out the candles, then kiss and burn the sigil and store the ash.

Context

The fifth of nine nights leading to the new moon dedication, four days before it, in which the reader consecrates each of her five senses in turn so that Hekate's approach may be recognized however it manifests.

Recommended Reading

The Orphic Hymn to Hekate, a short and potent hymn dense with the epithets for which the Queen of the Crossroads was renowned.
