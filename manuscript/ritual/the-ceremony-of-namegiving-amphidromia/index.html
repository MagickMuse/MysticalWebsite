A Hellenic household ceremony in which a newborn is named and formally welcomed into the family and the worship of the Household Gods.

Quantities

Red wine, for libations after every section
Olybanum (frankincense) incense, for the Opening
Mixed perfume incense, for the Hymn to Hestia
Hydromel (honey-water), as offering during the Ode to the Household Gods
Storax incense, for the Hymn to Demetra Kourotrophos
Wholegrain bread, as offering during the Hymn to Demetra Kourotrophos
Fresh water, for the libation following the Hymn to Demetra Kourotrophos
A protective amulet, traditionally gold or silver jewelry, to hang around the child's neck
Mixed perfume incense, for the closing Prayer
A small bottle of perfume and a vessel of milk, as offerings during the Prayer
Sweets, to distribute to guests after the closing
Optional offerings appropriate to a chosen patron deity, if dedicating the child to a specific god or goddess
Optional gifts brought by guests, to be placed at the foot of the altar

Directions

  1. Gather the household at the hearth altar (or chosen outdoor location) with the wine, incense, hydromel, bread, water, perfume, milk, and amulet within reach.
  2. Recite the Opening invocation with olybanum incense, then libate red wine.
  3. Have the father approach the hearth fire and recite the Hymn to Hestia with mixed perfume incense, then libate red wine.
  4. Pour the hydromel offering and speak the Ode to the Household Gods; as the father names the generations, he lifts the child overhead and carries them toward the hearth and household altar.
  5. Have the mother hang the protective amulet on the child and recite the invocation to Demetra Kourotrophos with storax incense and a bread offering, then libate water.
  6. If dedicating the child to a patron deity, recite an appropriate hymn and make that deity's offerings.
  7. Circle the altar three times clockwise with the child, pronouncing the naming formula after each circle, then libate red wine after the third circle.
  8. Light mixed perfume incense; have the mother pray for the child's blissful life, then have the father conclude with a prayer to the Ancestors.
  9. Let guests approach to make a wish, greet the child, and place a gift at the foot of the altar.
  10. Close together with the closing invocation, libate red wine, and seal the working.
  11. Distribute sweets to the guests.

The Amphidromia (am-fee-dro-MEE-ah) is one of the most important moments in a child's life, marking the choosing of a name and introducing a new member of the family to the gods of the household. As Apollonios Tyaneus (Apollonius of Tyana) wrote, "It is shameful to have only someone's name without also having his qualities," so the parents are encouraged to choose a name everyone agrees upon, with friends contributing observations of the child's character. The preferred location is the hearth altar of the Oikos (household), but it may also be held outdoors at a place of special meaning to the family.

Ceremony Script

Gather the household at the hearth altar of the Oikos (EE-kos), or at the chosen outdoor location. Arrange the wine, incense, hydromel, bread, water, perfume, milk, and the protective amulet within easy reach of the altar. If outdoors, the father will need to be ready to light the fire at the hearth or altar.

Begin with the Opening, lighting the olybanum incense and addressing the Blissful Gods as great friends of mortals and pillars of the world:

Come, O' Blissful Gods,

great friends of mortals,

mighty pillars of the world.

Come, Immortal Gods,

by the grace of our libations, freely grant to us mortals,

untainted virtue, great happiness, and well-being.

Libate red wine. The father then approaches the hearth fire (or lights the fire if outdoors) and offers the Hymn to Hestia (HEH-stee-ah), changing the incense to a mix of perfumes:

Hestia (HEH-stee-ah), royal daughter of almighty Kronos (KROH-nos),

You who holds the central abode, the magnificent perpetual fire,

Elevate the respectful suppliants who worship You in their holy ceremonies,

Grant to them ever-flourishing happiness, glad and pure,

O' dwelling of the Blissful Gods, steady support of mortals,

Eternal, glow-formed, needed by all,

Come, smiling Blessed Goddess, accept these offerings with kind heart,

Inspire happiness and gentle health.

Libate red wine.

Next comes the Ode to the Household Gods, the moment of introduction. Pour the hydromel as offering and speak the address. As the father reaches the line about generations, he lifts the child over his head and carries them toward the hearth and the household altar:

Household Gods, Ancestral Deities and You, glorious Forefathers,

this is our child who shall continue our generations and our Household Traditions.

(The father lifts the child over his head and approaches the hearth and the household altar.)

Recognise us in him/her and endow him/her with a blissful life.

The mother now steps forward, hangs the protective amulet (traditionally gold or silver jewelry) around the child's neck, and invokes Demetra Kourotrophos (deh-MEH-trah koo-ro-TRO-fos, the child-nurturer). Light the storax incense and place the wholegrain bread on the altar:

O' Dio (DEE-oh), mother of all,

illustrious Goddess, venerable Demetra (deh-MEH-trah),

You who nurtures the children

and grants peace, wealth and happiness.

Much-desired beloved Goddess,

who takes care of all people.

Affectionate, child-nurturer;

You who holds the newborn children

in your arms; blow sweetly on my child,

granting the virtues of the soul to him/her

and the beauty of nature.

Libate water.

If the family wishes to dedicate the child to a specific god or goddess, or if the family holds a particular deity as patron, both parents now recite an appropriate hymn to that god or goddess and make the offerings proper to that deity.

Proceed to the Naming. Either the mother or the father takes the child in their arms and circles the altar three times in a clockwise direction. As each circle is completed, the parent holds the child above the altar and pronounces the formula:

ΕΣΤΩΣΑΝ ΟΙ ΘΕΟΙ ΠΡΟΣΤΑΤΑΙ της/του [child name]

(EH-sto-san ee theh-EE pro-STAH-teh tees/too [child name])

May the Gods ever Protect and Sustain [child name].

Libate red wine after the third circle is complete.

Light the mixed perfume incense for the closing Prayer. The mother takes the child and prays:

O' Immortal Gods, I call upon you to come with good disposition

and to grant to [child name] a blissful life,

a life of pleasure and a life of virtue guided by reason,

nature and ethos.

The father concludes:

Honoured Ancestors, help this child to continue your name

and your deeds.

Those present now draw near to make a wish and greet the child, placing a gift for the child at the foot of the altar. When everyone has done this, the parents lead the closing:

Khairete (KHY-reh-teh) O' Blessed Gods,

Be with us always,

Never leave,

Cease all illness

and drive away sorrow.

Libate red wine and seal the working with ΓΕΝΟΙΤΟ (yeh-NEE-toh, it is done).

Distribute sweets to the guests. A feast may follow.

Notes

The Amphidromia is one of the great rite-of-passage ceremonies of Hellenic household worship. The opening epigram is from Apollonios Tyaneus (Apollonius of Tyana). The amulet hung around the child's neck is traditionally gold or silver jewelry. Demetra Kourotrophos is invoked here in her role as nurturer of children, distinct from her broader agricultural and Eleusinian aspects. If the family holds a particular deity as patron, a hymn to that deity is added between the Demetra hymn and the Naming, with offerings appropriate to that god or goddess.
