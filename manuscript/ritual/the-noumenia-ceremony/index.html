Noumenia

Hellenic household ceremony for Noumenia, the first day of the lunar month, observed on the day after the astronomical New Moon when the first sliver of the new crescent appears. Noumenia is sacred to Apollon and is set aside to honour the cherished Gods, especially the Household Gods Hestia, Apollon, Zeus, Hermes, and Hekate. The work centres on tending the household statues and devotional items: cleansing them, crowning them, refreshing offerings on each altar and shrine, and addressing the divine company at large through the Hymn to Mousaios. A special hymn to Apollon and small odes to the rest of the Household Gods are then offered while their statues or symbols are cared for, asking that the new month begin under their goodwill.

Quantities

  • Red wine for libations at every station
  • Generic fragrance incense for Hestia (rose, jasmine, sandalwood, or any household fragrance the home keeps)
  • Olybanum (frankincense) incense for Zeus
  • Frankincense and manna incense for Apollon
  • Frankincense and styrax incense for Hermes
  • Red roses and fresh laurel leaves as offerings to Apollon
  • A kyrikeon (caduceus) image, a tongue-shaped token, rosemary, almond, and pine for the Hermes shrine
  • Music to accompany the Hymn to Apollon (live or recorded)
  • A clean cloth and fresh water for washing the statues, plus replacement greenery or garlands for crowning them

Directions

  1. Begin in the early part of the day after the new crescent has been sighted, with sufficient time set aside to care for every household statue and shrine.
  2. Bathe and dress in clean clothes, then gather wine, water, the cleansing cloth, the incense for each station, the fresh greenery, and the offerings appropriate to each God.
  3. Move from shrine to shrine, gently cleansing each statue or symbol, replacing wilted offerings, refreshing flowers and laurel, and crowning the figures with new garlands as the household tradition prescribes.
  4. When the household altars are tended and all is set in order, gather at the central altar to begin the ceremony.
  5. Open with the invocation to the Blissful Gods:
  6. Come, O' Blissful Gods,
    great friends of mortals,
    mighty pillars of the world.
    Come, Immortal Gods,
    by the grace of our libations, freely grant to us mortals,
    untainted virtue, great happiness, and well-being.
  7. Libate red wine.
  8. Move to Hestia's hearth or hearth-shrine.
  9. Kindle her fragrance incense and speak the hymn:
  10. Hestia (HEH-stee-ah), royal daughter of almighty Kronos (KROH-nos),
    You who holds the central abode, the magnificent perpetual fire,
    Elevate the respectful suppliants who worship You in their holy ceremonies,
    Grant to them ever-flourishing happiness, glad and pure,
    O' dwelling of the Blissful Gods, steady support of mortals,
    Eternal, glow-formed, needed by all,
    Come, smiling Blessed Goddess, accept these offerings with kind heart,
    Inspire happiness and gentle health.
  11. Libate red wine.
  12. Return to the central altar to call the whole company of Gods through the Prayer of Mousaios (moo-SY-os).
  13. Speak it slowly, allowing each name its honour:
  14. King Zeus (ZOOS) and Gaia (GUY-ah) and sacred heavenly flames of Helios (HEH-lee-os);
    holy light of Mene (MEH-neh, the Moon) and all the stars;
    and You, dark haired Poseidon (po-SY-don), earth-shaker;
    pure Persephone (per-SEH-fo-nee) and Demetra (deh-MEH-trah) of the bountiful fruit;
    Artemis (AR-teh-mees), the maiden archer and farshooting Phoibos (FY-bos),
    who dwells in the sacred valley of Delphi (DEL-fee),
    holding the highest honours amongst the Blessed; dancing Dionysos (dee-oh-NEE-sos),
    bold Ares (AH-rees) and powerful holy Ephaistos (eh-FYS-tos),
    Foam-born goddess, bearer of famous gifts,
    and You, King of the Underworld, great and venerable God;
    Hebe (HEH-beh) and Eileithia (eye-LEE-thee-ah) and brave Heracles (HEH-rah-klees),
    and the great providence of Dikaiosyne (dee-keh-OH-see-nee, Justice) and Eusevia (eff-SEH-vee-ah, Piety);
    I also call the splendid Nymphs and Pan (PAHN), the Great,
    and Hera (HEH-rah), vigorous wife of aegis-bearing Zeus;
    I invoke the lovely Mnemosyne (m'neh-MOH-see-nee) and the nine sublime Muses
    and the Kharites (KHAH-ree-tess) and the Horês (HOH-rehs) of the Eniautos (eh-nee-OW-tos, the annual cycle)
    Leto (LEH-toh) with the beautiful limbs, the modest Theio (THAY-oh) and Dione (dee-OH-neh),
    the Kouretes (koo-REH-tess), the armed Koryvantes (ko-ree-VAHN-tess), and the Kaveires (kah-VEE-ress),
    as well as our great Saviours, the indestructible children of Zeus,
    the great Idaian (ee-DAY-an) gods and the heavenly messenger,
    Hermes (HER-mees), the communicator, and Themis (THEH-mees), the diviner of men,
    I invite venerable Nyx (NEEKS) and brightly glowing Day,
    Pisti (PEE-stee) and Dike (DEE-keh) and the immaculate Thesmodoteira (thes-mo-do-TAY-rah, Lawgiver),
    Rhea (REH-ah) and Kronos and the dark-veiled Tethys (TEH-thees),
    Okeanos (oh-keh-AH-nos), the Great, and his daughters, the Okeanides (oh-keh-ah-NEE-thess),
    Atlas' (AT-las) and Aionos' (eye-OH-nos) superior power,
    and the eternal Time, and the splendid water of Styx (STEEKS),
    the soothing Gods and the benevolent Pronoia (proh-NEE-ah);
    the all-holy Daimona (DY-mo-nah) of the Gods and the malevolent of mortals,
    all heavenly, air and water Gods,
    all Gods of the earth, and of the fire, and of the underworld.
    Semele (SEH-meh-leh) and the company of Bakkhos (BAH-khos);
    Ino Leukothea (EE-no loo-koh-THEH-ah) and Palaimon (pa-LY-mon), the bliss-giver,
    Sweet tongued Nike (NEE-keh) and queen Adrastea (ah-drah-STEH-ah),
    and the great king, giver of tranquillity, Asclepios (ah-SKLEH-pee-os),
    Pallas (PAH-las), the fearsome maiden,
    and all the Winds and the Thunder,
    the steady foundation of the Kosmos (KOZ-mos),
    Mother of the Immortals, Attis (AH-tees) and Mena (MEH-nah), I call
    the Goddess Ourania (oo-RAH-nee-ah) together with her immortal pure Adonis (ah-DOH-nees),
    the Beginning and the End, the greatest of all,
    Come with benevolence and happy hearts,
    to this holy ceremony and sacred libation.
  15. Move to Zeus' shrine.
  16. Kindle olybanum (oh-LIB-ah-num) and speak the hymn:
  17. O' Zeus, Father Zeus, You are the power in the heavens,
    You who judges the deeds of men to be lawful or devious,
    and dispenses justice upon hubristic beasts.
    Come, O' Blessed Ktesios (k'TEH-see-os) and Erkeios (er-KAY-os), benevolent, comforting and bearing wealth with Agathodaimon (ah-gah-tho-DY-mon).
  18. Libate red wine.
  19. Move to Apollon's (ah-POL-lon) shrine and tend it with extra care, since this is His day.
  20. Set red roses and fresh laurel leaves before the image, kindle frankincense and manna, and let music accompany the hymn:
  21. I remember and do not forget Apollon Hekateios (heh-kah-TAY-os).
    From years past,
    it is a sacred custom to invoke You.
    Come O' Blessed Paian (PY-an), Splendidly honoured Phoibos,
    Ie (EE-eh) Dispenser of Blessings, Spermatic, Pythian (PEE-thee-an),
    Delphic Diviner, Bright Daimon (DY-mon).
    You who sees the boundless aether and the joyful earth from above;
    Your eyes are the stars, dispelling the darkness.
    With your laurel branch, you shake the heavens,
    Apollon, You who does not appear to all
    but only to those who are good.
    Great is he who beholds You,
    insignificant are those who have not.
    We wish to be in Your presence, O' Hekatevolos (heh-kah-TEH-vo-los, Far-shooting),
    so that we may never be insignificant again.
    Ie Noumenios (noo-MEH-nee-os), come with a good disposition, bearing good fortune and favourable outcomes to our endeavours in the month to come,
    Ie Paian, Ie Apollon, Ie Noumenios.
  22. Libate red wine.
  23. Move to Hermes' shrine, often kept at the threshold of the home or just outside the boundary.
  24. Set the kyrikeon (kee-ree-KAY-on) image and the tongue-shaped token before Him, lay rosemary, almond, and pine alongside, and kindle frankincense with styrax.
  25. Speak the ode:
  26. Heed my prayer,
    Hermes (HER-mees) of the roads, Zeus' Messenger,
    You who has an almighty heart, dear friend of mortals.
    With this modest libation,
    I evoke Thee to come as Protector,
    of this Oikos (EE-kos, Home) and our land,
    Swift-footed Messenger of the Will of the Gods!
  27. Libate red wine.
  28. If the household keeps a shrine to Hekate (heh-KAH-teh) at the crossroads or threshold, attend it now in the same manner: cleanse the image, refresh greenery, kindle the household incense, and speak any ode the household has set aside for Her, closing with a libation of red wine.
  29. Return to the central altar to close the rite:
  30. Khairete (KHY-reh-teh) O' Blessed Gods,
    Be with us always,
    Never leave,
    Cease all illness
    and drive away sorrow.
  31. Libate red wine.
  32. Seal the working with the closing word:
  33. ΓΕΝΟΙΤΟ (yeh-NEE-toh, it is done).
  34. Leave the offerings in place for the day.
  35. Wilted greenery, spent incense, and the libated portion are returned to clean ground or running water afterwards, while any shared cakes or bread are eaten at the household table to carry the blessing of the new month forward into the days that follow.
