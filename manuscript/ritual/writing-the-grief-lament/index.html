A nighttime candlelit writing practice that turns grief into a Celtic-style lament, rolled and knotted into a scroll to keep until the pain has eased.

Quantities

  • Paper (not your journal)
  • A pen
  • A candle
  • A ribbon

Directions

  1. Perform this writing at night, when the world is sleeping and the Otherworld feels close. Sit at a table in a darkened room with paper and a pen, setting your journal aside for this working.
  2. Light a single candle to illuminate your writing space, and let its circle of light become your sacred space.
  3. Before writing, press the palms of your hands over your eyes. In that deeper darkness, let your emotions rise to the surface in all their colors, and feel Brigid's presence close and warm.
  4. Begin writing when ready, drawing on thought-starters if helpful, such as "I am bitterly sad, for I have lost too much" or "The night is dark and a heavy grief comes over me." Write from the deepest place of your longing heart, without censorship or concern for craft, trusting Brigid to guide the pen.
  5. Continue across as many sheets as it takes, writing until exhausted, weeping, exalted, or at peace, without anticipating or striving for any particular outcome.
  6. When the writing feels complete for now, stack the papers and roll them into a scroll. Tie the scroll with the ribbon, knotting it three times, and put it away in a private place.
  7. Keep the scroll rather than destroying it. Return to it later, once Brigid's healing has softened the sharpest edge of the grief, if you wish to read the words again.
