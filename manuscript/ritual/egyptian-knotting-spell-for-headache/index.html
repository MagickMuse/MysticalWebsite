Egyptian healing spell using knotted cord to cure headache. Demonstrates that Seth had his uses in Egyptian magick despite associations with chaos.

Quantities

  • 1 length of cord (linen or cloth may also be used)

Directions

  1. The Egyptian word for knot, tjes, connects to a widespread practice: binding incantations into physical form.
  2. A magickian would tie knots in strips of linen (always pure, undyed linen) while reciting spells.
  3. Each knot 'locked' the words into the fabric.
  4. The knotted cloth became a wearable amulet, tied around the neck or applied to the affected body part.
  5. Materials matter: linen is plant-based, connected to agricultural cycles and purity, used in mummification and temple rites.
  6. Fresh, undyed linen is ideal.
  7. The magickian assumes the role of Seth, god of chaos and storms.
  8. The suffering person becomes Horus.
  9. This unusual pairing shows that even Seth, often cast as villain, could serve healing purposes.
  10. His raw power, properly channeled, could overcome the forces causing pain.
  11. Recite healing words while tying knots into a cord.
  12. Each knot locks the spoken words into the fabric, binding the magick into physical form.
  13. Once the knots are complete, place the knotted cord around the foot of the patient.
  14. The combination of written or spoken word, physical action (tying), and material object (cord) creates the three components Egyptian magick required: speech act, physical gesture, and material anchor working in harmony.
