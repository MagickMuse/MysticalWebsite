🌕Moon Water

A waning moon scrying ritual using dripped candle wax on water to receive psychic guidance, reflect on what to release, and illuminate lingering questions. As the moon retreats in light, its connection to the subconscious and inner knowing deepens, use that energy to seek wisdom and clarity on what lies ahead.

Quantities

  • Bowl
  • Water (moon-charged water works beautifully)
  • Candle
  • Matches or lighter
  • Natural quartz crystal point (optional)
  • Candleholder

Directions

  1. Place the bowl where moonlight can touch the water if possible, or in a quiet, darkened space if you prefer.
  2. Fill the bowl with water and rest your hands above the surface, setting your intention clearly in your mind: you are here for insight.
  3. Light the candle, pausing to feel your connection to the moon as the flame catches.
  4. Hold the candle upright and speak aloud:
  5. "As the moon's light wanes, this candle's light guides the way.
    Through the art of dripping wax, I receive guidance on the question I ask."
  6. State your question aloud.
  7. Then stir the water gently with a fingertip or the tip of a natural quartz point to set it in motion.
  8. With your question held in mind, tilt the candle horizontally over the bowl and begin dripping wax into the moving water.
  9. Watch without forcing, shapes, letters, and symbols may emerge and shift as the wax spreads and cools.
  10. Note everything you notice; each image may form part of the larger message.
  11. Continue until you feel the reading is complete, or until the wax slows and stills of its own accord.
  12. Set the candle safely in its holder nearby, letting its light fall across the bowl if needed.
  13. Soften your gaze and look into the water, observe what the candlelight or moonlight reveals in both the water's surface and the wax formations.
  14. Invite further impressions to arise without reaching for them.
  15. If clarity is elusive, breathe gently across the water to shift the wax with intention, or lift the largest wax form and examine its underside, where additional shapes often hide.
  16. Approach this practice with patience.
  17. Wax scrying, like reading tea leaves, is a deeply intuitive art, the less you strain to interpret, the more freely the images speak.
  18. Trust what you see, and release any expectation of perfection.
