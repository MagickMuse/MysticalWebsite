A comprehensive guide to creating custom essential oil blends for magickal purposes. This process teaches the technique of 'simpling' and provides an eight-step method for crafting intentional oil synergies from visualizing needs to blending and documenting results.

Quantities

  • 3-5 essential oils (chosen for your intention)
  • 2 ml bottles with dropper or orifice reducers
  • Index cards (for testing combinations)
  • Tissue (for testing blends)
  • Notebook or journal (for documentation)

Directions

  1. # Introduction to Simpling
  2. While a book of this size can be overwhelming, it should be stated that no one is expected to memorize this much material before experimenting with magickal aromatherapy.
  3. Each essential oil is structured to encourage a technique called simpling.
  4. Pick an essential oil or three, and learn its every use.
  5. Use the oils chosen for as many uses as possible, and really get to know them.
  6. Think of this book as a dinner party, and each of the oils as a guest.
  7. Having met Petitgrain once, it may be easy to remember that Petitgrain has two sisters, Bitter Orange and Neroli.
  8. However, if a few weeks pass without encountering Petitgrain, the names of her sisters may easily fall from the memory banks.
  9. Conversely, an encounter soon after meeting Petitgrain, three days later, for instance, will reinforce what you've learned about her.
  10. By learning as many uses as possible for each oil chosen before moving on to the next, your memory will retain much more than if you read this book straight through cover to cover and then place it on a shelf.
  11. Remember, we retain a much higher percentage of what we learn by doing, rather than listening and reading.
    ## Starting Your Practice
  12. Purchase the oils that smell the best to you (we are all pleasure-driven mammals, after all), and learn everything you can about them.
  13. The oils that smell the best will not only yield positive results but also a yearning to continue to learn more.
  14. There are pages worth of magickal uses for a single essential oil listed.
  15. Once working with the oils chosen feels comfortable, it can be time to start branching out and trying the recipes.
  16. No one is saying buy four hundred dollars worth of essential oils right off the bat.
  17. Start simple, and the lessons learned will stick.
  18. There are useful recipes contained within this book, including an index of needs for inspiration.
  19. In the event inspiration strikes, or need necessitates, the instructions below are the steps followed to create each essential oil blend contained within these pages.
  20. Think of creating a magickal blend as a Choose Your Own Adventure.
  21. Look down the line and see how things can play out before starting the blend to make sure it is exactly what is needed.
    ---
    # The Eight Steps
    ## Step 1: Visualize a Need
  22. Whether inspiration or necessity facilitates the creation of a magickal blend matters not.
  23. The first step is to decide what is needed, the desired end result.
  24. If the immediate need is money, the idea may need a bit more focus.
  25. How will that money be obtained?
  26. Money obtained painlessly is always the goal, but with rash actions there could be issues involved.
  27. If the goal is merely money, someone might convince themselves that a quick trip to the casino will solve the problem.
  28. The pain comes when all the money is gone and the problem is now worse than before.
  29. So a gambling oil is probably not the answer.
  30. However, if that someone has been loaning out money lately, it may be beneficial to call in some of that debt.
  31. Asking the friend to whom the money was lent is a direct way of handling it: "Hey, money is a little tight this month. Could you pay me back?" For some this could extend an uncomfortable situation.
  32. Consider the alternative of leaving the money for the universe to dispense painlessly.
  33. That way it is not profiting from the death of a favored aunt, or a settlement from a disfiguring car accident.
  34. Forewarned is forearmed.
  35. Merely saying "money" can be too broad.
  36. Saying "I need seventy-five dollars, painlessly, to pay a red-light camera ticket (or whatever bill you may need paid) by next Thursday" is specific enough to be beneficial without constraining where the money has to come from.
    ## Step 2: Make a List
  37. Make a list of five to ten essential oils that can get you closer to reaching your goal.
  38. If your goal is to get seventy-five dollars, you'd list all the prosperity oils, focusing on ones that bring good luck, attraction, drawing, blessing, manifesting, and removing blockages.
  39. The quick list will be citrus heavy, along with other scents that smell active, bright, and happy: angelica, ginger, melissa, neroli, orange, tangerine.
  40. Attempt to find a base note, a heart or middle note, and a top note that all fit the idea of prosperity, money, or abundance.
    ## Step 3: Sample a Few Combinations
  41. Once you've chosen three to five essential oils, take the lids off and place them on an index card in combinations of two or three.
  42. Smell different combinations to get an idea of how well the oils work together.
  43. If one of the oils stands out in a negative way, now is the time to replace it.
  44. Once oils start blending, there is no way to remove a discordant note, you can only attempt to drown it out with other oils.
  45. If the oils all blend well together, then it is time to proceed.
  46. In this example, May Chang, melissa, petitgrain, and vetiver were pulled from the shelf.
  47. Notice the strength of the vetiver oil in comparison to the other notes chosen.
    ## Step 4: Record Each Chosen Oil's Associations
  48. Once you've found a scent combination you like, write down the specific energies each oil brings to the synergy.
  49. For example, vetiver is a good luck oil, and a base note.
  50. It is derived from the root of the plant, so it will root the prosperity in the long term.
  51. Melissa (prosperity) and petitgrain (purification) are both warm, sweet, citrusy middle notes.
  52. They will lift the dark, earthy notes of vetiver.
  53. May Chang is used for strength, calm, and renewal, and has a sweet lemon scent that is brighter and sweeter than melissa.
    ## Step 5: Blend the Oils
  54. Every house needs a good foundation to stand on.
  55. The base note is the foundation for the oil and is the scent that will last the longest.
  56. Often base oils are heavier and thicker than their middle and top note counterparts.
  57. This will give the oil something to dissolve into later.
  58. A 2 ml bottle can hold about 45 drops, depending on the size of the dropper or orifice reducers used.
  59. When experimenting with blending, it is best to start with a small number of drops and adjust accordingly.
  60. If the final oils chosen smelled harmonious, start with equal drops of each.
  61. In this instance, with vetiver being a bit stronger, this blend started off with 3 drops of vetiver, 6 drops each of petitgrain and melissa, and 1 drop of May Chang.
  62. May Chang is a very strong, very bright top note, so it was important to make sure it did not overpower the blend.
  63. The bottle is capped, shaken, and allowed to rest for a few seconds before removing the lid and giving a test smell.
  64. A dot on a tissue may help to add oxygen to the blend to make sure it is performing as desired.
  65. If the blend is as intended, fill the bottle as much as is desired.
  66. With a rarely used need, it is less important to fill the container, especially if the blend contains expensive or hard-to-find materials.
  67. Once the bottle is filled to the desired level, make sure to replace the orifice reducer and cap to prevent future spills.
    ## Step 6: Name Your Synergy
  68. Now it is time to name your synergy.
  69. Fun names or self-explanatory, it's the dealer's choice.
  70. Just make sure to document everything.
  71. Recreating the blend is what takes magickal happenstance and turns it into repeatable results, just like any scientific method.
    ## Step 7: Review Your Work
  72. Anytime you blend or use an oil, review the process.
  73. If the oil sits for a day or so and the smell changes, make a note.
  74. Perhaps the base oil used had not yet dissolved into the solution.
  75. If your intention for the oil did not go as planned, repeat the experiment.
  76. Several things can come into play.
  77. The oil could have performed differently than expected.
  78. The timing for the working could have been off.
  79. Or the work might not have been successful because it is not the work needed.
  80. A fellow teacher and dear friend H. Byron Ballard had this to say about a working she did that was not successful: "It wasn't my work to do. That wasn't my job." Sometimes, it just is not your work to do, and that is okay.
  81. (Seek out her work for amazing folklore, herbalism, and more.)
    ## Step 8: Wash Your Hands
  82. We're all adults, I know.
  83. But throwing out, composting, washing up, and everything in between each project keep the risk of contamination down, as well as the risk of chemical burns and phototoxic exposure.
  84. It also keeps our sense of smell intact.
  85. Essential oils are volatile chemicals.
  86. Safety first.
  87. As anyone who has touched their eyes too soon after cutting chili peppers can tell you, we don't always wash our hands as well as we think we do.
  88. (If you just cringed reading that, you know exactly what I'm talking about.)
