🥁Finding the Rhythm

◇Tradition
Eclectic
◇Solitary or Group
Solitary
◇Form
Altar Working
◇Setting
Outdoor
◇Method
MeditationChant/Mantra
◇Category
Meditation
◇Difficulty
Beginner
◇Quantities
- Any percussive instrument or improvised noisemaker (drum, rattle, tambourine, sticks, spoons, beans in jar, etc.)
◇Directions
1. All the members of Katydid's family, from Cricket to Grasshopper, are said to be able to predict storms. 2. The syncopation of their song reflects changes in temperature, providing a rhythm appropriate for the season. 3. Katydid's song is actually percussive, coming not from his throat but from rubbing his forewings together. 4. He hears his rhythm with "ears" located on his front legs. 5. This nocturnal bard teaches us to find and celebrate the rhythms of the world around us. 6. The next time you hear the crickets and katydids playing their nightly tune, join them! 7. Grab anything percussive: a drum, rattle, or tambourine; two sticks or wooden spoons; beans in a mason jar... it doesn't matter what you use. 8. Listen for the rhythm and join in. Match the beat. 9. If you do this through the shifting seasons, you'll begin to notice changes in the nightly rhythm. 10. Do these changes correlate with weather changes? 11. With shifts in the moon's phase? 12. Notice if you feel any differently or if you notice things in a new way, now that you've joined the cosmic chorus.
◇Description
A seasonal percussion practice inspired by Katydid's nightly song. By joining the cosmic chorus of crickets and katydids with drums, rattles, or improvised instruments, attune to nature's rhythms and notice how they shift with weather, moon phases, and seasons.
