A solitary devotional working in which the practitioner draws, consecrates, and burns a sigil of Hekate to open a period of preparation before a direct encounter with her.

Quantities

  • Clean sheet of paper
  • New, never-used writing utensil
  • Fireproof bowl
  • Matches
  • Cinnamon, for dusting the hands

Directions

  1. Wash your hands three times, then dust them with cinnamon.
  2. At a liminal hour such as dawn, dusk, or midnight, sit down alone in a private space with your paper, an unused writing utensil, a fireproof bowl, and matches.
  3. Sit quietly for a few minutes to let your mind settle, then read the hymn below silently to yourself.
  4. On the paper, slowly draw the crossroads sigil, reflecting as you go: the paired tridents mark command over the upper and lower worlds, the connecting shaft shows Hekate's freedom to move between them, and the crossing X marks the meeting point where she manifests.
  5. Close your eyes, slow your breathing, and picture yourself walking a misty path toward a forked crossroads lit by two torches, where three mask-like shapes hang from a pole at its center.
  6. Open your eyes and reflect honestly on your own worth, considering what has brought you here and whether you are ready to continue.
  7. If you find yourself worthy and wish to continue, draw your initials or a personal sign to the left, right, above, and below the sigil, so it surrounds you as you surround it.
  8. Notice how looking at the completed sigil and your initials makes you feel, and register that feeling without judgment.
  9. Place both hands over the sigil, covering it completely, and speak the following hymn aloud in a clear voice, three times:
    See the Sigil of the Crossroads,
    Of the Forked Path, of divine divergence,
    And of that peerless She who guards them all
    And guides the traveler's choice:
    She whom we call Dragon-Voice, Terror,
    Torch-Bearer, Barking Bitch, Witch, Wonder,
    Ghost-Faced Moon, Nameless, Faceless, Fearless,
    Peerless Upriser, Deep Descender, World-Wrecker,
    Age-Ender, Eater of Filth, Cutter of Cords,
    Sewer of Souls: she whom it pleases to be called
    HEKATE!
    She who brings dole and delight as Patroness
    Of all progressions, successions, transgressions; she
    To whom the witches pray, who rules dawn, the day,
    And dusk, but deeply loves the night,
    And those who gather in it.
    Touch her sigil; know no limit.
  10. Kiss the sigil, then light a match and burn it above the bowl, letting the ash fall inside.
  11. Once the ash has cooled, store it in a safe place, since it will be used again later.

Context

This is the first of nine devotional preparations performed across the eight nights leading up to a new moon and continuing through the night of the new moon itself. Each night builds toward a personal encounter with Hekate, and this opening working centers on her sigil as a focus for early commitment and self-examination.

Recommended Reading

Hesiod's Theogony, lines 411 to 452, offers useful background on Hekate's nature and pairs well with this working.
