A nine-day or nine-night prayer ritual dedicated to a saint. Novenas are a Roman Catholic ritual, but the practice is easily adapted to any spiritual tradition. A novena may be done as part of requesting a favor from a saint or giving thanks for one received, or as a gesture of love with no expectation of personal benefit.

Quantities

  • Novena candle (nine-day candle in glass sheath) or 9 individual candles
  • Novena prayer (traditional or self-composed)
  • Optional: rosary
  • Optional: holy card or image of saint
  • Optional: glass paint for personalizing candle sleeve

Directions

  1. The word "novena" derives from a Latin root meaning "nine." However, the ritual may be adapted for a greater or lesser amount of time as desired.
  2. Choose a number of significance for you, your situation, or the saint.
  3. There are different ways of performing a novena:
  4. For nine consecutive days or nights
  5. For nine consecutive weeks (nine consecutive Tuesdays or other day of the week)
  6. The two standard components are prayer and candles.
  7. Novena Prayers
    Certain saints have specific novena prayers associated with them. Saints bearing reputations as great miracle workers (Saint Rita, for instance) may possess multiple standardized novena prayers, with different prayers for different kinds of miracles. Novena prayers are not generally secret: published books contain them, churches may provide this information, websites dedicated to specific saints share them, and holy cards frequently display them on the reverse side.
  8. However, ultimately, a novena prayer is a sincere prayer from the heart.
  9. If you cannot find an appropriate novena prayer, compose a new one.
  10. A new prayer may be an excellent offering for a saint.
  11. Example Prayer: Saint Dymphna
    Oh God, we beseech You
    Through your servant, Saint Dymphna,
    Who sealed with her blood the love she bore You,
    To grant relief to (name) from mental afflictions and nervous disorders.
    Oh, Saint Dymphna, helper of those who are mentally afflicted, please pray for (name).
  12. This style of prayer may be adapted as needed.
  13. The prayer or cycle of prayers is repeated on each day or night of the novena schedule.
  14. Timing
    Maintain the same time schedule for the entire novena period. If you begin at 4:00 in the afternoon on a Tuesday, the second day would begin either the following Tuesday at the same time, or the following day at that time.
  15. Candles
    Novena prayers may be accompanied by repetitions of a rosary. They are usually accompanied by candles. A fresh candle may be lit on each of the nine days or nights. There is also a mass-produced candle known as a "novena candle" designed to burn for a nine-day period. Traditionally, this candle is not extinguished but allowed to burn out in its own time.
  16. Botanicas and spiritual supply merchants sell novena candles in glass sheaths featuring images of specific saints.
  17. Sample prayers may be found on the back.
  18. Once the candle has burned down, the glass sleeve may be preserved and used again by inserting a new candle.
  19. For obscure saints, purchase loose nine-day candles in a multitude of colors.
  20. Coordinate the color with that associated with the saint if possible.
  21. If you cannot find a specific color or do not know what color suits a particular saint, use a white candle.
  22. Blank glass sleeves are also easily obtained: write the saint's name on the sleeve with glass paint, attach a picture or holy card, or decorate in whatever manner seems appropriate (just ensure it is fire-safe).
  23. Novenas may be public or private, performed at home on a personal altar either alone or in a group, or done in a church or shrine.
  24. Some churches allow you to sponsor novena prayers performed on your behalf.

Pagan Origins

The word novena derives from the Latin root for "nine," but the practice predates Christianity. Both Greek and Roman Pagan tradition observed nine days of mourning for the recently deceased, concluding with a communal feast on the ninth day after burial. The Romans also observed the Parentalia novendialia annually from February 13 to 22, a nine-day period dedicated to honoring all departed family members. The Parentalia ended with a ritual sacrifice and banquet on the ninth day.

This origin is why the novena retains a tone of mourning or yearning to this day, even when performed in celebration of a saint's feast. The emotional register of the practice carries the memory of its ancestry work roots.
