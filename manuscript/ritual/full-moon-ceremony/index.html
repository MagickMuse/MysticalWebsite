A modern Hellenic ceremony established by the Labrys community and performed on the night of the Full Moon, honouring the three goddesses most associated with the Moon's light: Selene, Artemis, and Hekate.

Ceremony Script

Wait until after sunset on the night of the Full Moon and arrange the working space so the Moon's light can reach the altar, either through a window or outdoors under open sky. Set the wine, water, incense, sweets, and candles within easy reach of the altar.

Begin with the opening invocation, addressing Ge (YEH) and the nocturnal Moon as the bright bearers of the gods' light to mortals, raising the voice of the household toward the heavens beside the princely throne. Speak the opening aloud:

O' Ge (YEH), nocturnal moon

Bright bearer of the God's light to mortals

We raise our voices in this ceremony

to the heavens beside the princely throne.

Libate red wine.

Light the incense of perfumes for Selene and recite the Hymn to Selene, addressing her by her many epithets including Mene (MEH-neh), the bull-horned night-roamer who waxes and wanes:

Hear us, O' Goddess, light-bearing Queen, divine Selene (seh-LEH-neh);

bull-horned Mene (MEH-neh), night-roaming, airborne,

nocturnal, torch-bearing, dark maiden; Mene,

who waxes and wanes, masculine and feminine;

illuminating, horse-loving, mother of time; fruit-bearing,

amber, atmospheric, all-shining, each night;

You, the all-seeing, vigilant, filled with beautiful stars,

joyful in the silence and the fortunate night;

bright, joy-bringing, full-cycled, jewel of the night;

O' star-queen, long-veiled, variable, all-wise maid,

approach with bliss and delight; rich with stars and shining

light three-fold; protect your new suppliants, O' Maid.

Libate water.

Light the frankincense manna incense and offer the Hymn to Artemis, calling on the daughter of Zeus by the names Diktina (deek-TEE-nah), Orthia (or-THEE-ah), Khthonia (kthoh-NEE-ah), and Cydonian (kee-DOH-nee-an):

Hear me, O' Queen, famous daughter of Zeus (ZOOS);

boisterous Titanis (tee-TAH-nees); illustrious archer; modest,

illuminating, torch-bearing Goddess; child-delivering Diktina (deek-TEE-nah),

giver of pain and deliverer from pain;

ungirdled, loving huntress, free of cares;

swift shooter of arrows, nature-loving, night-roaming;

enclosed deliverer; manlike

Orthia (or-THEE-ah, upstanding); giver of quick-birth; spirit of mortal nurture;

divine Khthonia (kthoh-NEE-ah), killer of beasts; blessed spirit,

who possesses the oak groves atop mountains, modest, deer-huntress;

taming queen of all; good youth; You are the eternal,

haunting of the woods; Cydonian (kee-DOH-nee-an), shape-shifting, protectoress of dogs;

Come O' saviour Goddess, friend to all initiates,

pleasantly bring the good fruits of the earth,

peace, love, beauty and health.

May you banish illness and pain to the mountain peaks.

Libate red wine.

Place the sweets on the altar, light the candles, and offer the Hymn to Hekate, addressing her as Enodia (eh-NO-dee-ah) of the crossroads and Perseia (per-SEH-ah), key-bearing queen of all the world:

I praise Hekate (heh-KAH-teh) of the crossroads; venerable Enodia (eh-NO-dee-ah),

heavenly, khthonian and of the sea; saffron-robed,

of the grave, entwined in Bakkhic (BAH-khik) revelries with the souls of the dead;

Perseia (per-SEH-ah), the lonely, rejoicing with the deer;

nocturnal, invincible queen, accompanied by dogs

and the shrieking of beasts; she of the irresistible countenance, armoured,

bull-drawn, key-bearing queen of all the world;

ruler, nymph, nurturer, you who frequent the mountains,

I plead with you, Maiden, attend this holy ceremony with joyful

disposition and good intent towards the worshippers.

Libate red wine.

Close the ceremony by saluting Selana (seh-LAH-nah) and the stars, asking them to follow the chariot of Night in silence:

Khaire (KHY-reh) Selana (seh-LAH-nah) of the shining throne,

Khaire to all the stars,

Be silent followers of the chariot of the Night.

Libate red wine and seal the working with ΓΕΝΟΙΤΟ (yeh-NEE-toh, it is done).

Notes

The footnote in the source identifies Selene as the daughter of Hyperion (hee-PEH-ree-on) and Theia (THAY-ah) per Hesiod's Theogony, sister of Io (EE-oh, Dawn) and Helios (HEH-lee-os). The opening lines are adapted from an excerpt in the Heracleids of Euripides.
