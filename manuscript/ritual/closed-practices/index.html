Ritual Grimoire

Closed Practices

This grimoire includes rituals, techniques, and traditions from many cultures. Some of these practices are considered closed, meaning they require cultural belonging, lineage, training, or initiation to practice respectfully.

Closed does not mean "secret," "elitist," or "rare."

It means these traditions belong to specific living communities and are not appropriate to use without membership or permission.

This grimoire includes closed practices because some users are practitioners within those traditions, and having this knowledge organized can support them.

However, inclusion is not permission.

Whenever a ritual, tool, plant, or technique belongs to a closed tradition, a Closed Practice Warning appears in the Notes & Warnings section of that entry.

If you notice a ritual from a closed tradition that is not marked, please leave a comment so it can be updated.

⭐ Examples of Closed and Restricted Practices

(This list is not exhaustive, but it reflects what culturally rooted communities define as closed.)

🔒 African Diaspora Religions (Initiatory)

These require lineage, initiation, or elder permission:

  • Haitian Vodou
  • Louisiana Vodou
  • Santería / Lucumí
  • Ifá (Yoruba divination system)
  • Palo Mayombe
  • Candomblé
  • Umbanda & Quimbanda (vary by lineage)
  • Hoodoo (African American folk magic; learn from Black practitioners and within the community)

Important:

Hoodoo is a closed practice rooted in African American culture and history. It is not open for outsiders to take up.

🔒 Indigenous Practices of the Americas

Most Indigenous ceremonies and sacred medicines are closed unless you are:

  • a member of the tribe
  • a descendant
  • a sanctioned student
  • formally adopted into the community

Examples:

  • Ceremonial smudging (varies by tribe)
  • Use of white sage in Indigenous rites
  • Sweat lodges
  • Vision quests
  • Sun Dance ceremonies
  • Ayahuasca ceremonies & dieta (Amazonian lineages)

Burning herbs in general (rosemary, lavender, mugwort, cedar, etc.) is not closed.

🔒 Romani Magick & Folk Traditions

Romani spiritual practices and protective workings are closed unless you are Romani or apprenticed directly.

🔒 Lineage-Based and Initiatory Traditions

These require training, oaths, or membership:

  • Gardnerian Wicca initiations
  • Alexandrian Wicca initiations
  • Golden Dawn rituals
  • O.T.O. workings
  • Freemasonry rituals
  • Rosicrucian order rites
  • Hereditary or family-based witchcraft rites

🔒 Other Culturally Restricted Workings

Depending on lineage, some of the following practices may be closed or require mentorship:

  • Specific Curanderismo rites (not all; some are family-held)
  • Specific Brujería traditions
  • Certain Indigenous folk-healing ceremonies
  • Siberian, Mongolian, or Andean shamanic initiations

The word "shaman" originally comes from the Tungusic peoples of Siberia; the role itself is cultural and not universal.

⭐ What Is NOT Closed (Common Misconception List)

These are open for everyone to practice, though they may be culturally rooted:

  • Kitchen witchery
  • Herbal magick (non-Indigenous, non-ceremonial)
  • Candle magick
  • Color correspondences
  • Cartomancy & tarot
  • Astrology
  • Florida Water
  • Numerology
  • Basic folk magick
  • Burning non-sacred herbs (for cleansing or scent)

⭐ If You're Unsure

If you're unsure whether a ritual is closed:

  • Research its cultural and historical context
  • Look for sources created by members of the tradition
  • Ask respectfully
  • Choose an open alternative that serves the same purpose

Respect is not optional.

This grimoire exists to inform, not to appropriate.

When in doubt, choose reverence, research, and cultural humility.
