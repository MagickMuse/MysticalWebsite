🌓Waning Half Moon Hermekate Drum Chant

A raucous drum-chant to invoke the wild abandon that is the gift of Hermekate, sung aloud to the beat of a goatskin drum at night during the waning half moon. The Hierophant and any companions dance with ecstatic abandon around an open fire or lit candles while repeatedly singing the chant, breathing deeply and surrendering to the rhythm. When exhausted, the Hierophant lies upon the ground, closes her eyes, and envisions a black goat with flaming eyes lifting her onto his back and leaping toward the moon.

Quantities

Black Candle: 1
White Candle: 1
Bowl: 1, filled with beer mixed with wine
Beer: mixed with wine in a bowl
Wine: mixed with beer in a bowl
Honey: mixed with blood in a cup
Blood: mixed with honey in a cup
Coins: 2, one placed face up and one face down
Drum: 1 goatskin drum

Directions

  1. Learn the Hermekate Drum Chant by heart before performing this rite.
  2. At night when the waning moon is half full, set out one black candle and one white candle.
  3. Between the candles, place: a bowl of beer mixed with wine, and a cup of honey mixed with blood. Also set down two coins, one facing up and one facing down.
  4. Light the candles. Build or light an open fire if outdoors; if indoors, the lit candles and offerings serve as the center.
  5. Begin beating the goatskin drum. The Hierophant and any companions begin singing the Hermekate Drum Chant repeatedly while dancing around the fire or candles.
  6. Chant (repeat in full, continuously): "My hand is on your skin; / Your skin is on the drum. / You with eyes like hourglasses -- / Come! Come! Come! (Repeat entire stanza thrice) / My eyes are on the moon / The Moon's between your horns. / The Moon is like a breast / That is pressed between two thorns. / As all milk comes from mothers, / As all the dead are dumb; / You, whose eyes are hourglasses -- / Come! Come! Come! / As all milk comes from mothers, / As all the dead are dumb: / You, whose eyes are hourglasses -- / Come! Come! Come! / My right foot is a right foot; / My left foot is a hoof. / We were standing on the ground, but now / We're standing on the roof. / O Goat with eyes of fire / Whose skin is on the drum; / Take me, take me higher, / And Come! Come! Come! / O Goat with eyes of fire / Whose skin is on the drum; / Take me, take me higher, / And Come! Come! Come! / My right tit's nicely covered; / My left tit's in your mouth. / The rest of me's in ecstasy / And every North is South. / Your heart is beating faster / Than my hand upon the drum; / Hail, my Lord and Master! / Come! Come! Come! / Your heart is beating faster / Than my hand upon the drum; / Hail, my Lord and Master! / Come! Come! Come! / My hand is on your skin; / Your skin is on the drum. / You, whose eyes are hourglasses -- / Come! Come! Come! (Repeat entire stanza thrice)"
  7. Dancing should mirror the abandon of Dionysus' Maenads: unpracticed, unselfconscious, combining quick and slow movements with gestures alternately sinuous and static. Breathe deeply throughout.
  8. Continue dancing and chanting until the Hierophant can dance no longer.
  9. Lie upon the ground, close the eyes, and envision a black goat with flaming eyes descending, nudging you onto his back, and leaping upward toward the moon and beyond.
  10. Whatever visions follow may be shared, recorded, or kept private, as the Hierophant sees fit.
