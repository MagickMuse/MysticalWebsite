A cursing-pole ritual for revenge and defense, drawing upon the forces of Hel through a carved and inscribed pole directed at the target. Performed only in response to genuine harm.

Quantities

A stick or pole, two feet or more in length
A sculpture of a horse's head, or a carved representation of the target
A knife or carving tool
Red pigment or blood, for coloring the Thurisaz and Isa staves

Directions

  1. Choose a location for the pole; it need not be near the target, but should ideally be within sight of their dwelling place.
  2. At midnight, go to the work-stead and perform a proper opening rite.
  3. Spend time in meditative silence, working up emotional power against the target.
  4. In this heightened state, compose a prosaic or poetic runic formula of niding.
  5. Transliterate the formali into runes and ritually carve them into the pole, coloring only the Thurisaz and Isa staves.
  6. Set the figurehead on the pole and sink it into the ground while repeating the niding formula, visualizing the forces of Hel destroying the target and returning them to her dark embrace.
  7. Close the ritual by breaking the sphere of working and projecting all residue through the figurehead to the target. Leave the pole standing until the desired result has been accomplished.

This working is called "revenge and defense" rather than merely "curse" because it should be performed only when the vitki has been genuinely harmed by the intended target, or when the target would have harmed the vitki or loved ones had the niding pole not been raised.

The Nidhstöng

The nidhstöng or cursing pole (literally "pole of insult or libel") is a long pole on which formulas of insult and curse are carved, then partially sunk into the ground facing toward the target's home. The head of the pole is furnished with a horse's head or a carved representation of the target. The sagas abound with accounts of these niding poles. In ancient times, skalds often composed niding poems and raised poles against enemies.

How It Works

The pole acts as a magnet for the deadly forces of Hel, which are drawn up through the pole from the subterranean streams and projected through the horse's head (or other representation) toward the target. The inscription on the pole forms the stream and gives it its mission.

Example Formula

Three thurses I threw to thee
(Thurisaz Thurisaz Thurisaz) and three
ices too (Isa Isa Isa),
All the wild wights and all the
fierce fetches worried and warted thy
sorry soul; Hel hast thee now
(target's name)!

Ethics and Discernment

In days of old, niding poles were usually raised against political or religious enemies rather than purely personal ones. The vitki must always consult the innermost self to determine whether the niding pole is the right solution to the situation.

Alternate Form

A curse may also be cast in a talismanic form that is given secretly to the target or placed somewhere in close proximity, in much the same way the love spell operates.
