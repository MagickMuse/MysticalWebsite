🌆Evening Garden

◇Tradition
Eclectic
◇Solitary or Group
Solitary
◇Form

◇Setting

◇Quantities
One candle, black, dark blue, dark plum, or dark gray, for each thing you want to end or release Sprigs of rosemary, as an optional charm Optional: an image of a sunset deity
◇Directions
1. Set up the garden facing West, where it bids farewell to the Sun each night. 2. Add a candle in black, dark blue, dark plum, or dark gray for each problem, hardship, illness, stress, bad habit, negative pattern, or toxic relationship you want to end. 3. Add candles for things that are naturally ending, such as life phases, relationships that have run their course, or situations reaching their conclusion, to encourage their satisfactory completion. 4. Add sprigs of rosemary and, if desired, an image of a sunset deity as charms. 5. Activate the garden in the evening, when its energy aligns with the setting sun.
◇Description
A candle garden facing West to bid farewell to the Sun, dedicated to endings, release, and things you want to go away.

An Evening Garden harnesses the energy of endings and release.

Appropriate sunset deities include Baal (Canaanite god associated with the setting sun), the Hesperides (Greek nymphs of the evening), Janus (Roman god of endings as well as beginnings), Nephthys (Egyptian goddess of twilight and the dead), Sekhmet (Egyptian goddess associated with the destructive heat of the sun), and the Zorya (Slavic goddesses, including the evening star).
