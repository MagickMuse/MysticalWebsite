A foundational breathwork practice for psychic protection, grounding, and creating an inner sanctuary. Adaptable for all neurotypes and experience levels.

Quantities

None required

Directions

  1. Lie down to start.
  2. Inhale deeply, pushing your belly toward the ceiling.
  3. Allow your chest to rise naturally as the diaphragm pulls air into your lungs.
  4. Notice how your lungs, belly, and torso feel.

The Foundation

Breath is ritual. In any moment, what you can do, change, and control is your breath. Everything else in magick, the stones, the tools, the words, builds from this foundation.

Finding Your Moment

Meditation doesn't require a stark room and perfect stillness. Find your moment:

  • While nursing a baby
  • After someone leaves your office
  • In your car when you get home
  • While doing dishes or pulling weeds

It doesn't need to be the same time daily, though consistency can help. The only thing that exists in that moment is your breath.

Approaches for Different Minds

🧠
For neurodivergent practitioners: There is no wrong way to meditate. Moving meditations, walking, gardening, washing dishes, are just as valid as sitting still with candles.

Gentle Attention

Invite yourself to notice the quality of your breath without harsh self-criticism. If focusing on physical sensations is uncomfortable, turn attention elsewhere, perhaps how your breath affects the room around you.

The Bubble Technique

For days when breath focus feels difficult: use a jar of bubbles. Breathe in gently through your nose, then exhale long and soft through your mouth with a bubble wand. It still counts. You're still breathing.

Guided Visualization

Guided meditations offer scenery to watch while you breathe. What you see (or don't see) matters less than the slow, even, deep breath that renews you.

Building an Inner Sanctuary

Create a protected space within your mind, a home wholly your own where you feel safe and well. This becomes a refuge you can return to.

For a neutral space, imagine a white room with acres of nothing, or any color that feels safe. The point is knowing nothing hides in the shadows of this meditative space.

Working with Monkey Mind

"Monkey mind" refers to the distracting thoughts, feelings, and fidgets that arise during meditation. Rather than fighting them:

  • Acknowledge thoughts as they surface
  • Release them to flow on their way
  • Redouble focus on your breath
  • Trust that the images in your mind will work themselves out
💫
Note: Some practitioners don't create visuals while meditating, and that's perfectly valid. The goal is giving yourself permission to release worry for a moment or two.

On Timing

Don't set timers. The bells and alarms of daily life are hard enough on your system.

  • Thirty seconds is valid if that's your capacity today
  • Five minutes one day and twenty-three the next, both valid
  • Every day calls for a different version of yourself

If you're stressed, tired, or emotional, don't be hard on yourself for having less to give your practice.

The only important part is continuing to breathe, because if you stop that, you stop everything.
