A seven-day working that invokes Obatala, the King of Peace, to create a charm bag promoting peace in every aspect of the self and every area of life, begun on a Saturday. ⚠️ King of Peace Seven-Day Spell invokes Obatala, an orisha of Yoruba-derived traditions. Working directly with the orishas through Lucumí is a closed initiatory practice meant only for those initiated within the tradition.

Quantities

2 blue or white candles
1 white cloth
8 sticks of patchouli incense
1 blue or white charm bag
1 small blue or white stone
7 pinches of dried rosemary
7 rose petals (preferably white)
7 clean, shiny dimes (or cowrie shells, silver coins, or small silver objects)

Directions

  1. Begin on a Saturday, at any time of day or night.
  2. Gather all ingredients, enter a magickal frame of mind, and create sacred space around the working area.
  3. Spread the white cloth on the altar or working surface, and set an image of Obatala nearby if one is available.
  4. Place one candle and one stick of incense on the cloth, reserving the other candle and the remaining incense for later days.
  5. Raise power, light the candle, and light the incense from its flame.
  6. Hold the stone and charge it with peace as you say:
    "Obatala, King of Peace,
    Chief of the White Cloth,
    Father-Mother of Humanity,
    Bringer of Peace and Harmony,
    I charge this stone with your positive energy.
    Obatala, by your power,
    This stone is charged with peace."
  7. Hold the stone in the incense smoke for about a minute while concentrating on peace and harmony, then place it in the charm bag.
  8. Hold each rose petal in the smoke for a moment, speak one blessing over it, and add it to the bag:
    "For peace with myself."
    "For peace with my past."
    "For peace with my present."
    "For peace with my future."
    "For peace with my karma."
    "For peace with those who love me."
    "For peace with those who do not love me."
  9. Repeat with the coins:
    "For peace of mind."
    "For peace of heart."
    "For peace of spirit."
    "For peace at home."
    "For peace at work (or school)."
    "For peace in the family."
    "For peace in the world."
  10. Repeat with each pinch of rosemary, speaking seven blessings of your own, each naming an area of life, an aspect of the self, or a person with whom you wish to be at peace.
  11. If the incense burns out before you finish, light another stick from the candle flame and continue.
  12. Close the bag tightly, tying seven knots in its drawstring if possible, and ground the power you raised.
  13. Leave the bag on the white cloth in a safe, undisturbed place for the week.
  14. On days two through six, burn a stick of incense on the altar and turn the bag in its smoke until it is fully smudged, concentrating on peace and saying:
    "By the power of the number seven,
    By the white clouds in the heavens,
    With blessed peace and harmony,
    This charm bag enchanted be!"
  15. On day seven, raise power, light the remaining candle and the last stick of incense, and smudge the bag as before.
  16. Drip seven splashes of candle wax over the knots that close the bag while concentrating on peace and repeating the same incantation.
  17. The spell is complete once the candle and incense have burned out. When the wax has hardened, place the bag where it will be kept and ground the power you raised.
  18. For a constant need for peace, wear the bag around the neck or carry it; for peace in one particular place, keep it there; for intermittent needs, store it safely and squeeze it a few times to reactivate it.
  19. Refresh the bag at any time by smudging it with patchouli incense.
  20. Once the need for peace has passed, bury, burn, discard, or store the bag; the stone and coins may be cleared and kept for future use.

Lore

Obatala, the Old Man of the Mountain, is an African deity regarded as a goddess in some traditions. In the New World Obatala is known by many names, including Oxala and Orisha Popo.
