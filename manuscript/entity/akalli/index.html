Magickal Properties

Akalli is Ariadne's older sister, the eldest of Pasiphae and Minos' children. While still a young teenager, she met the youthful Apollo and became his "first love." Despite various powerful deities pursuing her, her parents were always displeased with these relationships. Her name has two meanings: "without walls" (as in traditionally unwalled Minoan cities or a vulnerable woman) and "a narcissus flower."

Ritual Uses

Work with Akalli for maintaining autonomy in powerful relationships and resistance to unwanted influence. Her myth suggests she may be a very ancient and powerful pre-Hellenic goddess whose veneration was eventually challenged by incoming Olympian deities. She was venerated at Lera Cave, Crete.

Offerings

Narcissus flowers (especially wild narcissus) and honey. Present offerings that honor her connection to the narcissus flower and her ancient Cretan origins.

Favored People

Those seeking autonomy, mothers, those dealing with divine or powerful relationships, people maintaining independence from foreign spiritual influences, and practitioners honoring pre-Hellenic goddess traditions.

Petition For

Protection during pregnancy, strength in relationships with powerful figures, maternal guidance, autonomy and independence, and resistance to unwanted spiritual or social pressures.

Warnings

Her myth suggests resistance to foreign divine influence and represents pre-Hellenic goddess power. Her parents' dismay at divine suitors may represent attempts to maintain independence from foreign spiritual influences.

Additional Notes

Implicit in her myth is that none of the pursuing deities were worthy of her. Apollo is described as very young and a newcomer to the region. One myth suggests that while pregnant, she was banished to Libya, and the son born to her there is sometimes called "the first man," implying she is the primordial mother.
