Overview

Candarosana is a god of the Buddhist Mahayana and Vajrayana traditions, known chiefly in his extended form as Candamaharosana, meaning the great violent wrathful one. He is understood within these traditions as a form of the Buddha Aksobhya, the Imperturbable, one of the five buddhas of the mandala, and he embodies the wrathful expression of Aksobhya's mirrorlike wisdom that cuts through the obscuration of anger by taking anger's own energy as the path. He is central to the Candamaharosana Tantra, a Vajrayana text preserved in Sanskrit, Tibetan, and Newari translations, and he holds particular importance in the Newar Buddhist tradition of the Kathmandu valley of Nepal.

Classification

God. Yidam of the Buddhist Vajrayana traditions of Tibet, Nepal, and the wider Himalayan region. Wrathful form of the Buddha Aksobhya.

Domains

The wrathful side of the mirrorlike wisdom, the transformation of anger into cutting insight, protection of the practitioner against inner and outer obstacles to awakening, the tantric union of method and wisdom expressed through his coupling with the consort Prajnaparamita.

Iconography

Depicted in wrathful form, of yellow color in some cycles and of dark blue color in others, standing in the fierce posture with one leg extended. His central iconography shows him holding a sword raised above his head in his right hand and a noose in his left, standing upon a corpse in the wrathful stance, wearing the elephant skin or human skin as upper garment, and adorned with the ornaments of the wrathful deities.

Attributes

Sword raised in the right hand, noose held in the left, and skin (elephant or human) worn as garment across the shoulders and back. In fuller descriptions his lower hands may also hold a skull cup, and he is shown embraced by his consort Prajnaparamita in the yab-yum posture that expresses the tantric union of skillful means and wisdom.

Colors

Yellow in some cycles, dark blue in others.

Sacred Sites

Newar Buddhist temples of the Kathmandu valley, where his practice cycle has been continuously transmitted, and Tibetan monasteries preserving the Candamaharosana Tantra within the wider Aksobhya and wrathful deity practice traditions.

Ritual

His worship is undertaken through the sadhana practices of the Candamaharosana Tantra, restricted to initiated practitioners who have received empowerment from a qualified lama. In Newar Buddhist practice his cycle is one of the principal transmitted yidam practices of the Vajracarya priestly tradition. Outside these lineages he is not to be approached as a private tantric deity.

Lore

Candarosana embodies a central Vajrayana teaching on anger: the wrathful energy that the ordinary mind experiences as destructive is the very energy that, taken up under proper transmission, becomes the cutting insight of Aksobhya's mirrorlike wisdom. His wrathful form is therefore not the opposite of Aksobhya's serene one but its dynamic expression, and his tantra elaborates the practice by which the practitioner learns to recognize anger's arising as the arising of that wisdom in unrefined form. His preservation in the Newar Buddhist tradition of the Kathmandu valley makes him one of the important living links between the Indian Vajrayana of the medieval period and the continuous Buddhist practice of the Himalayan region.
