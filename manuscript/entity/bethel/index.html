🛡️Protection

Bethel, whose name means "house of god," is a local tutelary god of the Western Semitic and Phoenician world, probably of Aramaean or Syrian origin. He watches over cities, households, and the sacred stone or pillar in which the divine presence is understood to reside, functioning as both a patron of the community and as the numen of the sanctified place itself.

Domains

Guardianship of the sanctified place, protection of the household and city, and the indwelling of divinity within stone and dwelling.

Historical Context

Bethel is first attested in the fourteenth century BCE, appearing in a treaty between the Hittite king Suppiluliuma I and Niqmaddu II of Ugarit at Ras Shamra. He appears more regularly in inscriptions from the end of the seventh century BCE, and enjoyed considerable popularity during the Neo-Babylonian period. Some Israelite communities also acknowledged Bethel as a deity, a recognition that persisted even as the wider Hebrew tradition moved toward strict monotheism. There is no clear evidence linking the god Bethel to the historical Canaanite place names bearing the same designation.

Iconography

As a deity of the sacred stone, Bethel is closely associated with the standing pillar or betyl, a rough or shaped stone erected as the seat of the divine presence. Devotion is directed to the stone itself rather than to an anthropomorphic image.

Petition For

Protection of the home and city, guardianship of thresholds, and the recognition of the sacred within place and dwelling.
