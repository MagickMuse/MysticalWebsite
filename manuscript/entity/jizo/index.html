Jizo, the Japanese name for the bodhisattva Kṣitigarbha, is one of the most beloved figures in Japanese Buddhism. He is the patron of children, travelers, dream interpreters, fortunetellers, and the deceased, and protects the souls of departed children, guiding them safely through the afterlife. He is a compassionate guardian who vowed not to achieve enlightenment until all hells are emptied. In Japan, Jizo transcends orthodox Buddhism, functioning as both a Bosatsu and a folk deity (Kami).

Powers and Domains

Jizo is the great protector of children, especially deceased children and those who died before their parents (including miscarried, stillborn, and aborted children). He rescues the souls of children from Sai-no-Kawara, the riverbed of souls where children must stack stones to build merit.

Jizo is also invoked as:

  • Dream interpreter: He reveals the meaning of dreams and sends prophetic visions
  • Protector of travelers: He guards those journeying through dangerous places
  • Guardian of firefighters: He protects against fire
  • Rescuer from hell: He descends into the Buddhist hell realms to save suffering souls

Iconography

Jizo typically appears as a gentle-faced Buddhist monk with a shaved head, wearing simple robes. He often holds a staff with six rings (representing the six realms of existence) and a wish-fulfilling jewel. Statues of Jizo are ubiquitous throughout Japan, found along roadsides, in cemeteries, and at temples.

Small Jizo statues honoring deceased children are often dressed in red bibs and caps, placed by grieving parents.

Attributes

Monk's staff with six rings (shakujo), wish-fulfilling jewel (cintamani), simple monk's robes.

Colors

Red (especially for children's Jizo statues).

Offerings

Toys, candy, bibs, caps, flowers, incense.

Sacred Animals

Dogs, ravens.

Feast Day

  • 24th day of each month (particularly venerated)
  • Jizo Bon: July or August (during Obon season)
