Overview

Uzume is the kami of mirth, dance, and revelry, also known as Ame-no-Uzume.

The Dance That Lured Amaterasu

When the despairing sun goddess Amaterasu barricaded herself in a cave, the world was plunged into darkness. Try as they might, none of the other kami could persuade her to emerge; they sat outside the cave as despondent and despairing as Amaterasu within.

Finally, Uzume approached. She whispered to the smith kami and set him the task of creating a mirror and situating it right before Amaterasu's cave. Then she bound her head and sleeves with vines, overturned a barrel, and climbed upon it. Grasping leafy branches in her hands, she began to stomp rhythmically on the drum and dance.

The other kami's reactions are unclear at first, but eventually her joy proved infectious. They began to clap and drum too. The dance evolved into a striptease full of fun, defiance, and humor: Uzume exposed her breasts and flashed her vagina. All the other kami roared with laughter.

Amaterasu, locked within the cave, was puzzled. She heard laughter, not grieving. Why was no one mourning her absence? She called out to ask what was going on. Uzume called back, telling her they had a brand-new sun goddess, one more beautiful than ever, and beckoned her to come see. Amaterasu, unable to resist, poked her head out and, gazing directly at the mirror positioned before the cave entrance, saw her own radiant beauty. Before she could retreat, she was pulled from the cave, and the entrance was blocked. Dance and hearty laughter had restored order to the world.

Additional Lore

Uzume came to the rescue again later, alongside Sarutahiko. Despite her role as savior of the world, she is not envisioned as a young beauty or a standard sex goddess. Instead, she is pictured as a middle-aged woman who doesn't appear especially youthful for her age: her breasts and belly are a little saggy, she has some wrinkles, and her hair may be a little gray. She could probably stand to lose a little weight, but it doesn't matter. She is the spirit of joy and good humor that defies despair, and a primal spirit of shamanism, intoxication, sex, fertility, and reproduction. She is not mirthful because she is innocent or inexperienced; she has seen it all and still perceives the joy of life even in moments of absolute, deepest despair. Uzume is the prototype for Japanese shamanic dance.
