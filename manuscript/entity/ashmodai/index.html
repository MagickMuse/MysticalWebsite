Magickal Properties

Kingly spirit of wrath, lust, and revenge; patron of gambling luck; a loud, sensuous presence who can upend fortunes and marriages.

Ritual Uses

Command only with great caution; otherwise make respectful petitions for controlled wrath, protection from jealousy, and luck in games.

Offerings

Strong drink, frankincense, cards or casino chips; formal address as a king.

Favored People

Powerful sorcerers seeking mastery over wrath and lust.

Petition For

Gambling luck, control of wrath, protection from jealousy and infidelity.

Warnings

Address as a king; never pester, annoyance destroys luck.

Additional Notes

Rival and shadow to Solomon in legend; husband‑killing demon of apocryphal scripture; countered by Raphael.

Origins and Etymology

Asmodeus is the Greek version of the Hebrew name Ashmodai. Two theories exist regarding his origins:

Iranian Connection Theory: In dualist Iranian and Zoroastrian tradition, Aeshma Deva is an evil spirit who fights on the side of the Lord of Darkness. "Aeshma" means "Madness" and he is the third highest-ranking evil spirit. Jews may have encountered Aeshma Deva during their Babylonian exile and incorporated him into their mythology.

Independent Spirit Theory: Some occultists hold that Ashmodai is an independent Jewish spirit whose name merely sounds similar to the Iranian spirit, deriving instead from the Hebrew "Asamod," meaning "to destroy."

Roles and Titles

In Jewish demonology, Ashmodai serves multiple roles: the destroyer, a high-ranking avenging angel, and the Prince of the Revengers of Evil. He visits Heaven daily to learn the destined fate of human beings and receive his assigned orders. He is alternately identified as the King of Demons or King of Djinn.

Family and Relationships

According to Jewish tradition, Ashmodai is the son of Naamah, sister of Tubal-Cain (the first metalworker and descendant of Cain). Naamah herself is a formidable demon in Jewish folklore, serving as both ally and competitor to Lilith. Ashmodai is sometimes considered Lilith's husband.

The Solomon Rivalry

Ashmodai was King Solomon's primary competitor. According to legend, Solomon enslaved Ashmodai and forced him to help with building projects including the Jerusalem Temple. Ashmodai eventually paid him back by tricking Solomon into surrendering his magick ring. Once Ashmodai possessed the ring, he sent Solomon into exile, assumed Solomon's form, and ruled in his place, though Solomon eventually regained his throne.

Christian Demonology Form

In Christian demonology, Asmodeus is an extremely high-ranking demon or rebel angel. His name is also sometimes used as a synonym for Satan. He is traditionally envisioned as a horned, lame man or as a composite creature with three heads (bull, ram, and ogre), a snake's tail, and goose feet, riding on a dragon.

Goetic Specialties

As listed in the Medieval grimoire tradition: power and control, virtue, mathematics, and craftsmanship.
