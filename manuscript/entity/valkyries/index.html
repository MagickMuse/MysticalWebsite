Goddesses of Life, Death, Battle, and Magick

The Valkyries are Norse goddesses who serve as psychopomps, the divine escorts who guide souls to their next realm. The original Valkyries are daughters of Odin and Herta, but their ranks have swelled to include deified priestesses of Odin. Their leader is Freya, operating under the nom de guerre Valfreya. Individual Valkyries like Brunhilde, Hilda, and Sigrun achieved great renown and are sometimes venerated independently.

The Name

The Valkyries' name may derive from Valr ("fallen in battle") and Kyria ("she who chooses"). They are not exclusively battlefield goddesses: they serve as escorts for the dying in any context, though they are most famous for their role on the fields of war.

Role in Battle

The Valkyries travel to battlefields and determine the course of battle: who wins, who loses, who lives, and who dies. According to Norse cosmology, the souls of half the dead warriors journey to Freya's hall, and the other half to Valhalla, Odin's hall. The Valkyries select the warriors going to Valhalla and serve as their escort. When the souls arrive, the Valkyries greet them bearing drinking horns filled with Odin's best mead, supervise hospitality, and serve as hostesses.

The Weavers

The Norns are sometimes classified among the Valkyries and may perform double duty. Like the Norns, the Valkyries are weaving goddesses, though their materials are unique: arrows serve as shuttles for their looms, severed human heads serve as weights, and entrails form the warp and weft. The fabric is consistently crimson.

Bird Goddesses

The Valkyries are bird goddesses who manifest as birds and communicate as birds. Their origins may lie in ancient swan goddesses. They serve a similar function to other bird goddesses like the Sirens, the Vila, and possibly the Dakinis.

Erotic Shamanic Goddesses

The Valkyries are also erotic shamanic goddesses, and many may be deified shamans. They enter sacred marriages with shamans, warriors, and heroes, guiding, protecting, and sponsoring their human lovers. It is crucial to recall that Valkyries are not only involved in decisions regarding which soldiers die: they also help determine which survive.

Teachers and Patrons

The Valkyries teach shamanic skills, martial arts, battle spells, and rune lore to both women and men. As psychic, spiritual, and magickal warriors, they may be invoked to battle on behalf of a devotee in many contexts beyond actual warfare.

Manifestation

The Valkyries manifest as shield maidens, sometimes beautiful and sometimes not, as well as ravens and swans.

Attribute

A drinking horn

Mount

Despite romantic 19th-century illustrations, the Valkyries ride wolves, not horses. "Valkyrie horse" is a traditional Norse kenning for "wolf."

Creatures

Bees, swans, horses, wolves, crows, and ravens

Other Associations

The Valkyries are associated with the Aurora Borealis, the Northern Lights.

Alruna and the Sacred Mandrake

Alruna is the name of a Valkyrie to whom the mandrake is sacred. Like Atropos of the Greek Fates, Alruna chooses who lives and who dies. In German folklore the word alruna came to mean both mandrake and witch interchangeably, and the Alraun, a tiny domestic imp whose body is fashioned from a mandrake root and who lives inside a bottle, takes its name from her.

Messengers and Choosers

Bound up with death, the Valkyries were known as winged Angels of Death who watched over the battlefields and gathered up the souls of brave warriors slain in combat, guiding them to the banqueting table of the gods. They were also held to act as messengers for the gods, leading certain heroes to their deaths and stirring them to deeds of valor for the greater good.

Swan and Raven

The Valkyrie takes more than one form. Sometimes she wears the white feathers of swans, sometimes the black feathers of the raven, and in this guise she is also called Kraken, meaning crows. This links her to the great Celtic warrior-goddess Morrigan, who likewise appears on battlefields as a crow or raven. The Valkyries were said to take the shape of horses as well, and they share kinship with the shape-shifting Swedish witch known as the Volva.

Thirteen or Nine

The Valkyries are reckoned either thirteen in number, as in a coven, or nine, the number of the Muses. The comparison suggests that, like the Muses, they could grant a transcendent inspiration to the warriors in their keeping.
