A collective of seven Sufi saints whose tombs form a week-long pilgrimage circuit through Marrakesh, Morocco.

History of the Pilgrimage

Marrakesh, once Morocco's capital, hosts the tombs of approximately 200 holy people. In the seventeenth century, Sultan Moulay Ismail instituted an official pilgrimage visiting seven of these saints' graves. Beyond spiritual motivation, the sultan aimed to stimulate tourism revenues.

An official annual pilgrimage occurs each March, though the circuit may be completed at any time. The pilgrimage lasts one week, visiting one saint's tomb-shrine per day. The route proceeds counterclockwise, mirroring the circumambulation of the Kaaba in Mecca. Completing the full pilgrimage to all seven saints is considered spiritually equivalent to the Hajj.

The Seven Saints

Tuesday: Sidi Youssef ben Ali (died 1196/1197)

A leper who spent most of his life in a colony just outside the city walls. A Koranic scholar who devoted himself entirely to prayer and study, he epitomizes faith in God. He was buried in the cavern where he lived and is petitioned for relief from leprosy.

Wednesday: Caid Ayad (1083-1149)

A scholar and author who lived in Andalusia before relocating to Marrakesh.

Thursday: Sidi Bel Abbes

The most prominent of the Seven Saints and patron saint of Marrakesh.

Friday: Sidi Mohammed ben Slimane el Jazali (body moved to Marrakesh 1524)

A descendant of the Prophet Muhammad, he was expelled from the Moroccan city of Safi when the governor perceived him as a political threat. Though he neither lived nor died in Marrakesh, his body was moved multiple times before being laid to rest in a mausoleum near Sidi Bel Abbes.

Saturday: Sidi Abdel Aziz el Harrar (died 1508)

A disciple of Friday's saint and a renowned physician famous for curing syphilis. He possessed two huge terra-cotta basins covered with magical inscriptions in which he dipped patients, healing them. The basins were buried with him and allegedly still protect Marrakesh from venereal diseases. Known as "The Doctor without Fees" because he charged nothing for his services. Though venerated by the general population, prostitutes especially honor him as their protector.

Sunday: Sidi Moulay el Ksour (died 1528)

A Berber from the mountains who studied in Fez and Granada before becoming a disciple of Saturday's saint in Marrakesh. He led popular resistance against Portuguese invaders.

Monday: Sidi es Souheili (died c. 1185)

A great Islamic scholar from Malaga who came to Marrakesh around 1182 and died there approximately three years later. Also spelled Sidi es Soheyhi.

The Seven Little Saints

Near the shrine of Sidi Bel Abbes stands a row of seven small tombstones marking the graves of the Seven Little Saints. According to legend, they were seven brothers born to the same mother on the same day. Tradition holds that those unable to complete the full week-long pilgrimage may visit the Seven Little Saints as a substitute.

Access

Entry to the shrines is forbidden to non-Muslims, though many are situated to allow viewing from outside.
