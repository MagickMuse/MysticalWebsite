Classification

Roman Catholic saint.

Life and Legend

According to tradition, Colombe (c. 257-273 CE) came from a noble pagan family in Saragossa in what is now Spain. "Colombe" (meaning "dove") was her baptismal name; she may have been born Eporita. She crossed the Pyrenees into France accompanied by a large bear.

Settling in Colombiès in Aveyron, she was sustained by manna for twenty-eight days (the length of one lunar month or menstrual cycle). This manna arrived on a miraculous shield, echoing Grail legends.

Imperial Refusal

Emperor Aurelian wished to marry this mysterious, beautiful, miracle-working young woman to his son. When she refused, she was sent to a brothel. There her bear defended her honor.

Martyrdom

Authorities attempted to burn Colombe alive along with her bear. The bear escaped, and sudden torrential rains extinguished the flames. She was finally beheaded beside a healing fountain in Sens. Some versions say the fountain sprang forth in response to her execution.

During her beheading, a blind man in the crowd invoked her aid. His sight was instantly restored, and in gratitude he buried her. A chapel at her grave eventually grew into the Abbey at Sens.

Hidden Goddess?

There is no historical documentation for Colombe. Some scholars suspect a pagan bear goddess lurks beneath the saint's mask. Her cult centers in the eastern Pyrenees, an area linked to the Priory of Sion and various Christian heresies. She is closely associated with Black Madonnas.

Despite uncertain origins, she was once tremendously popular; numerous French locations bear her name.

Iconography

A young woman with a bear, or with a dog; being beheaded beside a fountain; or bound and standing on a funeral pyre with an angel.

Attributes

Book, peacock feather.

Sacred Animals

Dove (the meaning of her name), bear.

Sacred Sites

  • The Abbey of Saint Colombe in Sens (built 7th century over her tomb) was demolished during the French Revolution
  • She now has a chapel within the Cathedral of Saint Stephen of Sens
  • Saint Kolumba Church in Cologne, Germany (one of the city's oldest) was destroyed in WWII; its ruins are incorporated into the Kolumba Museum
