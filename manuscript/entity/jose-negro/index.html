José Negro (Black Joseph), a Cuban folk saint and Santería spirit, was a santero who achieved folk saint status after his death. He is particularly venerated by practitioners of Santería and Lukumí traditions in Cuba. He is not officially recognized by the Catholic Church.

Life and History

José Negro was a devoted santero who dedicated his spiritual practice to Elegua (Eleguá), the orisha of crossroads, beginnings, and opportunities. His deep devotion and spiritual power during his lifetime led to his posthumous veneration as a folk saint.

Connection to Elegua

José Negro's primary spiritual connection is to Elegua, one of the most important orishas in the Yoruba-derived religions of the Caribbean. Because of this connection:

  • He is petitioned for similar concerns as Elegua
  • His offerings mirror those given to Elegua
  • He serves as an intermediary between devotees and the orisha

Iconography

José Negro is depicted as an elderly man, often shown:

  • Smoking a cigar
  • With dark skin honoring his African heritage
  • In simple clothing befitting a humble santero

Powers and Domains

As a spirit associated with Elegua, José Negro is petitioned for:

  • Opening roads and removing obstacles
  • Guidance at crossroads and decision points
  • Spiritual protection
  • Communication with the orishas

Offerings

Traditional offerings include:

  • Cigars (his most characteristic offering)
  • Rum
  • Candy and sweets
  • Candles
  • Coconut
