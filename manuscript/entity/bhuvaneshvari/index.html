Bhuvaneshvari (She Who Rules the Universe) is one of the ten Mahavidyas, the great wisdom goddesses of the Hindu Tantric tradition. She is among the more benevolent of the ten forms, representing the Devi as cosmic nourisher and sustainer rather than as destroyer.

Iconography

Bhuvaneshvari nourishes the three worlds. She holds a piece of fruit in one of her four hands. Her breasts are large and ooze milk, signifying her role as the universal mother who feeds all existence. She has a bright, light complexion and smiles pleasantly, marking her as auspicious among the generally fearsome Mahavidyas. She holds a goad and a noose in her other hands, emblems of her authority over all living beings.

Nature and Significance

Her name means both queen and territory of the universe: the bhuvana is the world itself, and she is its ruler. This positions her as the earth and cosmos personified.

In Mahavidya theology, Bhuvaneshvari is identified with the space and matter of the universe itself. She is the world as a living goddess. Her pleasant expression and milk-oozing breasts within a group of otherwise terrifying forms underline that the Devi's power extends across the full spectrum from sustainer to destroyer.

As a Mahavidya

Bhuvaneshvari is one of the ten forms Sati assumes when she surrounds and terrifies Shiva to force his permission for her to attend Daksha's sacrifice. The Mahavidyas do not primarily defend cosmic order but assert the Devi's autonomy and supremacy. Bhuvaneshvari's contribution to the group is the terrible authority of the nourisher: the one who feeds all things also has the power to withhold that nourishment.

Aspects and Epithet

The name Bhuvaneshvari, meaning Lady of the Spheres, is not always confined to this particular Mahavidya. It is also used as an epithet applied to several other goddesses whose function is understood to encompass the whole of the created cosmos, so the title marks a certain kind of universal authority wherever it appears. Her recognized aspects include Siddharatri, the night of accomplishment, the perfected darkness in which the goddess's power is brought to fulfillment. Alongside the goad and noose already noted in her iconography, the epithet form of her name records these same attributes as her defining marks, so the hook and the noose remain the signature implements by which she is recognized in any of her guises.
