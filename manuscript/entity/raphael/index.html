Raphael is renowned for healing any illness or ailment, especially blindness and vision problems. As the Lord's Healer, he also soothes mental illness, banishes nightmares, and quells demons.

Classification

Venerated as a saint by most branches of Christianity. Like his fellow archangels Michael and Gabriel, Raphael holds the unusual distinction of being both archangel and saint, despite saints traditionally being born human. He is beloved across Judaism, Islam, and among angel devotees of all spiritual paths.

Epithet

The Lord's Healer (his name translates as "Healer of the Lord," "The Lord's Remedy," or "The Lord Heals")

History and Veneration

Veneration of Raphael predates Christianity. He was honored as a deity in ancient Mesopotamia and is recognized as an archangel by Judaism and Islam as well as Christianity.

Raphael does not appear by name in the Old or New Testaments, but he is the protagonist of the Book of Tobit, canonical for some Christians and apocryphal for Jews. In this text, he performs miraculous healings and teaches how to overcome powerful demons. The apocryphal Book of Enoch describes him binding the fallen angel Azazel. John Milton's Paradise Lost portrays Raphael as God's messenger sent to warn Adam.

Powers and Domains

Raphael, Regent of the Sun, presides over healing. He can reportedly cure any illness, ailment, or condition, though his traditional specialties include blindness and vision problems, whether literal or metaphorical. He soothes and heals mental illness and banishes nightmares. Beyond healing, Raphael embodies joy, love, and compassion, and he oversees personal guardian angels. He is also believed to be present, at least briefly, at every human birth.

Celestial Roles:

  • One of the seven angels surrounding the heavenly Throne of Glory
  • Guardian of the Tree of Life
  • Possibly the angel with the flaming sword guarding Eden's gates
  • Possibly the Guardian of the Holy Grail

Demon-Quelling: Raphael is a formidable adversary of demons. Possessing the power of the Creator's Ineffable Name, he commands, compels, and banishes even the most fearsome spirits. In the Book of Tobit, he ends the demon Asmodeus's reign of terror. Practitioners invoke him to expel wicked spirits and break curses and malignant spells.

Manifestation

Raphael may appear as a being of blinding light. However, in the Book of Tobit, he disguises himself so effectively that Tobias, the story's protagonist, remains unaware he is traveling with an angel despite close daily contact.

Iconography

Raphael is depicted as a winged angel or as a handsome, androgynous young man. Scenes from the Book of Tobit are popular subjects, especially images showing Raphael traveling alongside young Tobias. A Coptic legend describes Raphael saving an Egyptian church from a beached whale's thrashing tail; Ethiopian icons portray him spearing this whale. In his warrior aspect, he may carry a lance and shield.

Attributes

Fish, traveler's staff, flask, chalice.

Correspondences

  • Elements: Usually air; sometimes water
  • Direction: East or west (varies by tradition)
  • Colors: Typically blue and gold; some southern Italian traditions use pink and purple; some Orthodox traditions associate him with emerald green
  • Jewel: Emerald
  • Day: Tuesday (in Yezidi tradition)

Feast Days

  • Western Church: Originally 24 October; since 1969 Vatican reforms, shares 29 September with Gabriel and Michael
  • Eastern Church: 8 November
  • Ethiopian Church: The thirteenth day of each month

Sacred Sites

Church of the Archangel Raphael in Venice.
