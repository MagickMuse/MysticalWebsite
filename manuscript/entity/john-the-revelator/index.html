John the Revelator, venerated as a saint by most branches of Christianity, is the patron saint of alchemists, authors, booksellers, editors, oil blenders, publishers, and visionaries. He is traditionally identified as the author of the Book of Revelation (Apocalypse), the final book of the New Testament. Whether he is the same person as John the Apostle, one of Jesus' Twelve, remains debated by scholars.

The Beloved Disciple

If John the Revelator is identical with John the Apostle, then he was the "Beloved Disciple" who reclined next to Jesus at the Last Supper and to whom Christ entrusted his mother Mary at the Crucifixion. He would also be the author of the Gospel of John and three epistles.

Miraculous Survival

According to tradition, John was arrested in Rome during Emperor Domitian's persecution of Christians. He was thrown into a cauldron of boiling oil but emerged unharmed. Unable to kill him, the authorities exiled him to the island of Patmos in the Aegean Sea.

The Revelation

On Patmos, John received the apocalyptic visions recorded in the Book of Revelation. He describes being "in the Spirit" on the Lord's Day when Christ appeared to him with messages for seven churches in Asia Minor, followed by visions of the end times, the battle between good and evil, and the New Jerusalem.

Later Life

After Domitian's death, John was released from exile and returned to Ephesus, where he is believed to have lived to an advanced age. He is traditionally considered the only apostle to die of natural causes.

Powers and Domains

John the Revelator is invoked for prophetic insight, divine revelation, and protection from spiritual poison. As author of visionary texts, he is patron of those who seek to understand hidden mysteries.

Patronage

Patron saint of authors, publishers, theologians, alchemists (who see symbolic meaning in his imagery), and all who work with apocalyptic or prophetic literature.

Iconography

Depicted as an elderly man with an eagle (his evangelical symbol), often shown writing on Patmos or receiving visions. Sometimes shown emerging from a cauldron of oil.

Feast Days

  • Orthodox: May 8
  • Roman Catholic: December 27 (shared with John the Apostle)

Sacred Sites

  • Monastery of Saint John the Theologian, Patmos, Greece
  • Cave of the Apocalypse, Patmos, Greece (where he received his visions)
