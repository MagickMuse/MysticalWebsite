Goddess of Witchcraft and Venomous Creatures

Origin and Mythology

Malinalxochitl is a powerful Aztec goddess of witchcraft, sorcery, and magic. Her name means "Grass Flower" or "Malinalli Flower." She was the sister of Huitzilopochtli, the Aztec god of war and sun, who abandoned her during the Aztec migration because he feared her magical powers. In revenge, she founded the city of Malinalco, which became a center for sorcery and warrior training.

According to legend, during the Aztec migration from Aztlán, Huitzilopochtli ordered his people to abandon Malinalxochitl while she slept because he feared her growing magical power. When she awakened and found herself abandoned, she founded Malinalco in revenge. Her son Copil later tried to avenge his mother by attacking the Aztecs, but Huitzilopochtli killed him and threw his heart onto an island, where it became the foundation stone of Tenochtitlan.

Magical Powers

  • Command over venomous creatures (scorpions, snakes, spiders)
  • Powerful sorcery and hexing
  • Witchcraft and magical warfare
  • Transformation magic
  • Curse-casting

Manifestation

Malinalxochitl appears as a powerful sorceress-goddess, often associated with serpents, scorpions, spiders, and other venomous creatures that she commands. She radiates dangerous, primal magical power.

Sacred Sites & Attributes

Sacred city: Malinalco (which she founded). The city became famous for its temple carved into the mountainside and its elite warrior school. The site remains a powerful place of magic.

Sacred animals: Scorpions, serpents, spiders, venomous creatures

Modern Veneration

Malinalxochitl is honored by practitioners of Mexican witchcraft and brujería as a patron of magical power, particularly women's magical power and the reclaiming of indigenous magical traditions.

Connection to La Malinche

Some scholars suggest that the historical figure La Malinche (Cortés's interpreter) may have been considered an avatar or reincarnation of Malinalxochitl, continuing the goddess's complex and controversial legacy.
