Buddakepala is a deity of the Mahayana Buddhist tradition. He appears in tantric iconography as a wrathful form connected with the ritual skull cup, and he is attended by a retinue of minor goddesses including Bhima.

Domains

Wrathful transformation, the cutting through of ignorance, and the tantric integration of fearsome imagery into the path of awakening.

Iconography

Color: blue-black. His attributes are the club, the cup or skull cup, the drum, the image of Aksobhya set as a mark upon his crown or hair, and the knife. The bearing of the image of Aksobhya identifies him as an emanation of that dhyanibuddha and situates him within the eastern quadrant of the Vajrayana mandala.

Sakti

His Sakti is Citrasena, the feminine counterpart through whom his wrathful power is completed and through whom the tantric union at the heart of his practice is figured.

Encyclopedia Source Notes

Buddakepala, whose name is also written Buddhakapala and translates as Buddha's skullcap, is a significant emanation of Aksobhya, the imperturbable dhyanibuddha of the east. He is alternatively described as a form of Heruka, and in that reading he belongs to the wider family of wrathful tantric buddhas who transform the passions into the path of awakening.
