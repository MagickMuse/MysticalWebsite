Emerald

Hermes Trismegistos ("Thrice-Great Hermes") is a legendary figure who emerged from the fusion of Egyptian and Greek culture in Ptolemaic Alexandria. He represents the Egyptian god Thoth merged with the Greek Hermes, but conceived as an ancient human sage rather than a deity.

Origins

When Greeks encountered Egyptian religion, they identified Thoth, the ibis-headed god of writing, magic, and wisdom, with their own messenger god Hermes. Over time, the title "Trismegistos" (Thrice-Great) elevated this combined figure into something more than either original deity.

By the Roman period, Hermes Trismegistos was imagined as an ancient Egyptian priest-king who had lived in the remote past and written vast libraries of sacred wisdom. He was sometimes described as a contemporary of Moses or even older, predating all Greek philosophy.

The Hermetic Corpus

The Hermetic Corpus is a collection of Greek texts written between the 1st and 3rd centuries CE, attributed to Hermes Trismegistos. These texts discuss:

  • The nature of God and the cosmos
  • The ascent of the soul through celestial spheres
  • The relationship between mind, spirit, and matter
  • Magickal and astrological principles

The most famous text, the Poimandres, describes a visionary encounter with the Divine Mind. The Emerald Tablet (a later addition to the tradition) contains the famous phrase "As above, so below," which became a cornerstone of Western magickal thought.

Renaissance Revival

In 1463, Cosimo de' Medici ordered Marsilio Ficino to translate the Hermetic Corpus before completing his translation of Plato. Renaissance scholars believed these texts predated Greek philosophy and represented the oldest wisdom on Earth.

This gave Egyptian (or pseudo-Egyptian) wisdom supreme authority in European esoteric circles. The Hermetic tradition influenced:

  • Renaissance magick and natural philosophy
  • Alchemy and the search for the Philosopher's Stone
  • Rosicrucianism and Freemasonry
  • The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn
  • Modern ceremonial magick

Iconography

Hermes Trismegistos is typically depicted as:

  • An elderly sage in Egyptian or classical robes
  • Holding a staff or caduceus
  • Sometimes with an ibis (Thoth's bird) or other Egyptian imagery
  • Surrounded by alchemical or astrological symbols

Working with Hermes Trismegistos

Hermes Trismegistos is invoked for:

  • Alchemical operations (both physical and spiritual)
  • Study of sacred texts and hidden meanings
  • Astrological work
  • Ceremonial magick in the Western tradition
  • Seeking divine illumination and gnosis

His influence persists in the term "hermetically sealed" (alchemical vessels) and the concept of "hermetic" knowledge (secret wisdom passed through initiatory traditions).
