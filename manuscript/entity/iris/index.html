Rainbow

Iris is the Greek messenger goddess who travels between the worlds along the arc of the rainbow. She carries the words of the gods to mortals and the prayers of mortals back up to Olympus, and she walks the bridge between sea, earth, and sky.

Preferred Vocations

  • Messenger

Domains & Sacred Objects

  • Rainbow

The Rainbow Path

The rainbow itself is Iris. Where the sky touches the horizon and the colors bend down to earth, she crosses. The flower of the iris and the colored ring of the human eye are both named for her, each one a small inheritance of the goddess who keeps the bright bands of light in motion.

Guide of Souls

Iris is also a psychopomp for women. The Greeks placed irises on the tombs of women so that she would come and walk their souls along the rainbow road into the next world. The flower planted at the grave is the goddess's own signal that she will come for the one who lies beneath.

Working With Her

Invoke Iris for clear messages, true oaths, honest reconciliation, and safe crossings between any two states or worlds. Be precise and honest in your speech when calling her, for she is a literal messenger and ambiguity invites both miscommunication and insult.

The Female Hermes

Iris is in many ways the female counterpart of Hermes: a winged messenger capable of traveling freely between the worlds of the living and the dead. Like Hermes she has wings on her sandals as well as at her back, and she moves between the realms with the same ease. In her divine form she wears golden wings, a tunic, and a rainbow-hued veil or cloak that billows behind her in the breeze. When she descends to Earth from Olympus she must take on human form. She is the personal attendant and handmaiden of Hera, carrying her mistress's golden pitcher.

Service and Initiative

Iris is wholly dedicated to her duties and never shirks them. She shows practical initiative too: when the horses of the gods return to Olympus after bearing them across the sky, it is Iris who unharnesses them and feeds them with ambrosia and nectar.

The Threefold Flower

The three leaves of the iris flower are traditionally said to represent faith, wisdom, and valor. Burn dried iris root or place fresh blooms on the altar to invoke any of these three virtues together.
