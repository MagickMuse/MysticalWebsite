Saint Onofrio is the patron saint of weavers and textile workers.

Classification

Roman Catholic saint.

Life and History

Saint Onofrio (also known as Onuphrius, c. 320-400 CE) was an Egyptian hermit who lived in the Thebaid desert for approximately seventy years. According to tradition, he was the son of a Persian king who abandoned worldly life to pursue extreme asceticism. He lived in complete solitude, sustained only by dates from a palm tree and water from a spring that miraculously appeared near his cave.

The Hermit's Appearance

Onofrio's hair and beard grew so long they covered his entire body like a garment, a detail frequently depicted in his iconography. When the monk Paphnutius discovered him near death in the desert, Onofrio shared his life story before passing away. Paphnutius buried him and spread his story throughout the Christian world.

Powers and Domains

Saint Onofrio is the patron saint of weavers and those who work with textiles. His association with weaving may derive from his hair serving as his only garment—a natural weaving of sorts—or from the patience and solitary dedication required in both his hermit's life and the weaver's craft.

Iconography

Onofrio is depicted as an extremely thin, elderly man with long hair and beard covering his body. He may hold a rosary or be accompanied by a lion.

Attributes

Long hair and beard, palm tree, crown (referencing his royal birth), lion.

Feast Day

June 12
