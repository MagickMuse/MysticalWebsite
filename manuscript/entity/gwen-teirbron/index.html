Gwen Teirbron, known as The Triple-Breasted, is a Celtic saint venerated by most branches of Christianity.

Saint or Goddess?

Gwen Teirbron may be a saint. She may be a goddess. She may be both, like Brigid, her fellow Celt. Brigid is the presiding spirit of lactating ewes. Three-Breasted Gwen (the literal meaning of Gwen Teirbron) is the presiding spirit of lactating women.

Official Legend

According to her official legend, Gwen Teirbron was a Breton princess born c. 499 CE. She had three sons, allegedly the reason for her triple breasts. Twice kidnapped by Anglo-Saxon pirates and brought to England, Gwen walked home over the sea each time.

Less officially, many believe she is a Celtic fertility goddess who assumed the guise of a saint.

Powers and Domains

Gwen is counted among those saints credited with bringing Christianity to Brittany. Within folk religion, however, she is adored for her powers over fertility and lactation.

She is petitioned for fertility in general but is especially associated with abundant milk. Her other names (Blanche, Wite) are reminiscent of milk. Until the 19th century, she was the subject of intense veneration in Brittany.

Iconography

Legend and her name (Teirbron literally means "three breasts") has it that Gwen Teirbron had three breasts, and that's how she is portrayed. You can't see the breasts if she's completely clothed, so her images were revealing.

In the 1870s, local priests removed most of her images from churches and buried them, considering them obscene and a distraction.

Sacred Flower

Saint Candida's Eyes, the folk name for wild blue periwinkles.

Sacred Sites

  • Once had a shrine in the Chapel of Saint Venec, Brittany
  • Statue of her in Ploufragan, Brittany
  • Holy well named for her in Scaer, Brittany
  • The Church of Saint Candida and Holy Cross, Whitchurch Canonicorum in Dorset is unique: except for Westminster Abbey, it is the only church in England retaining its original medieval shrine and relics of its named saint. The church features a well renowned for healing miracles.
