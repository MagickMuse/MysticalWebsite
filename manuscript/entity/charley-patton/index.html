Charley Patton (c. 1891 to 28 April 1934) is an unofficial saint, a bluesman extraordinaire, singer, guitarist, and songwriter who stands among the architects of blues music. It is impossible to overemphasize his influence. He personally inspired virtually every Delta bluesman and epitomizes the soul, essence, and magick of the blues.

Domains

The blues is the threshold between traditional African music and later jazz, rock and roll, hip-hop, and countless other genres. At its finest expression, blues is a vehicle of healing and empowerment, and Charley is its best. For years, blues was considered disreputable, labeled the "devil's music" not only because it is gospel music's secular cousin. The best blues taps into a primal, primordial human energy that defies boundaries and restrictions.

Charley Patton is a magickal, revolutionary musician whose recordings resist successful transcription. For those who view blues not just as a musical genre but as a sacred code and conduit to the divine, Charley Patton is a special kind of hero, a messiah of the blues. He played ragtime and ballads too, but he is the foundation of modern blues, transmitted through his disciples Son House, John Lee Hooker, Robert Johnson, and Howlin' Wolf.

Life and History

Patton learned to play guitar on the Dockery Plantation outside Ruleville, Mississippi, his home for most of his life, though he was a traveling man. Howlin' Wolf, who lived nearby, took guitar lessons from Charley in his youth. During the 1920s, Patton played juke joints, dances, and logging camps, and busked on the streets. He first recorded on 14 June 1929, leaving a legacy of approximately sixty tracks. The exact number is unknown, as some masters disappeared when the recording company went out of business, allegedly sold as scrap metal and used to line chicken coops.

In a cutthroat world, he was generous with other musicians, providing Son House and Willie Brown with their first opportunities to record. He died of a heart ailment aggravated by hard living.

Iconography

Only one image of Charley Patton exists. He poses holding his guitar, looking suave in a bow tie and spats.

Sacred Sites

Charley Patton's grave in Holly Ridge, Mississippi, is Marker Number One on the official Mississippi Blues Trail.
