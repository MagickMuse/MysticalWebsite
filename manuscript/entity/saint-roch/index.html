Saint Roch is renowned for healing and preventing rabies, cholera, infectious diseases, epidemics, and skin ailments.

Roman Catholic saint, patron of plague victims and the falsely imprisoned.

Life and Legend

Roch was born in Montpellier, France, in 1293 with a striking birthmark: a red cross on his chest, reminiscent of the Knights Templar uniform. His father served as the city's governor. When Roch was twenty, both parents died and he embarked on a pilgrimage toward Rome, though he never reached his destination.

Traveling through northern Italy, Roch encountered plague victims in Aquapendente. While most travelers fled, Roch stayed to heal both people and animals through prayer and the sign of the cross. He continued his healing ministry through Mantua, Modena, and Parma until he reached Piacenza, where he himself contracted the plague.

Rather than seek treatment, Roch retreated into the forest to die. A dog discovered him there and returned daily with scraps from its master's table. Eventually the dog's master followed his hound, found Roch, and nursed him back to health.

When Roch finally returned to Montpellier, the gaunt, weathered pilgrim was unrecognizable as the governor's son. His own uncle, now governor, had him arrested as a spy. Roch never revealed his identity and spent his final five years in prison. Only when his body was prepared for burial was the distinctive red-cross birthmark discovered, and Roch was finally recognized. Miracles began occurring immediately after his death and continue to this day.

Domains

Roch is invoked for healing and preventing rabies, cholera, and other infectious diseases. He offers protection during epidemics and relief from skin ailments and knee injuries. Because of his own unjust imprisonment, he also intercedes for prisoners and those facing incarceration, particularly long-term confinement. Legend holds that he wishes no one else to die imprisoned as he did.

Iconography

Roch appears in traveler's garb: cloak, hat, boots, and staff. He is always accompanied by his faithful dog. Like the Fisher King of Grail legend, he points to a wound on his left upper thigh.

Attributes

Cross, traveler's staff, dog. His medals are frequently used as dog tags.

Colors

Yellow.

Sacred Plant

Grapevine.

Spiritual Allies

Roch is frequently venerated alongside Saint Sebastian and Saint Hubert. He is among the saints most often enshrined with Black Madonnas. In Meymac, home of a Black Madonna, his image appears with a lady in black rather than his usual canine companion.

Calabrian Feast Day Ritual

In Calabria, devotees celebrate his feast day by making panpepati, gingerbread ex-votos shaped like the body part requiring healing.

Sacred Sites

  • The Church of Saint Roch in Venice houses his relics.
  • The Shrine of Saint Roch in the Saint Roch cemetery, New Orleans, was dedicated in 1876 after Father Peter Thevis vowed to build it if his congregation survived the 1868 yellow fever epidemic.

Feast Day

16 August.
