Messengers Between Worlds

Belief in unseen beings who dwell in a spiritual realm and carry word between humankind and the divine seems to reach back as far as belief itself. The name comes from the Greek for messenger, while the Hebrew term carries the sense of both messenger and the shadow side of God. The idea is ancient, described long before the Old Testament was set down and well before the rise of Christianity. Figures resembling the angels known today appear among the ancient Akkadians around 2350 BC, and the concept was likely older still. Comparable beings recur across the world under other names: the Devas, Rakshasas, and Rishis of India, the Orishas of Africa and Brazil, and the Yokai and Oni of Japan, among dozens of further variations on the theme.

Wings and Transcendence

The defining mark of the angel is its wings, themselves a symbol of rising above the earthly plane. They grant the power of flight, and it is no accident that many of the earliest angel images are feathered figures, part bird and part human.

Hierarchies and Hosts

The history of these beings is hard to trace. The fullest early account came from an obscure sixth-century writer remembered as Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite, who set out the rankings of angels still widely accepted today. That account and scripture agree that the host of angels is vast beyond reckoning, the texts speaking of thousands upon thousands ministering to God while ten thousand times ten thousand stand before him. That angels move within a realm closed to human senses is captured in the old riddle of how many might dance upon the head of a pin despite being colossal, many-winged creatures.

Guardians and the Modern Cherub

These awe-inspiring biblical beings bear little likeness to the gentle figures that have become near-ubiquitous emblems of modern spiritual culture. The original angels stirred both wonder and dread, guarding and shielding humankind while bearing messages from the Divine. The notion that each person has a personal guardian angel is itself ancient and comforting, especially to children. Scripture does not name such a being directly, though it does hold that each of the faithful is aided by an angel who guides and protects them through life. The archetype is so potent that angels seem to live on in the collective imagination whether or not one believes in any God.
