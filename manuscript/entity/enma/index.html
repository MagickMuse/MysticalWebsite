Magickal Properties

Enma (Yama) is the Japanese Buddhist Lord of Hell who judges the dead, upholds karmic law, and maintains ethical order. He works with Oni as enforcers and stands opposite the compassionate mediator Jizō.

Ritual Uses

Name Enma in rites that strengthen moral conduct, remembrance of the dead, and right speech. For intercession, address Jizō as mediator while acknowledging Enma’s justice.

Offerings

Incense, sutra recitation, and small votives at memorial or afterlife shrines offered with cultural respect.

Favored People

Invoked in ethical and mortuary contexts; not a patron in the usual sense.

Petition For

Moral clarity, truthful remembrance, protection from harmful speech and deeds, orderly funerary matters.

Warnings

Do not trivialize hell imagery or attempt coercive work. Keep rites solemn and respectful of Buddhist context.

Additional Notes

Also called Emma‑Ō; related across Asian Yama traditions. Associated plant: red spider lily near grave paths.
