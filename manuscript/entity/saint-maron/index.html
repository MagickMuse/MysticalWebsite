Saint Maron, venerated by most branches of Christianity, was a fourth-century priest who became a hermit renowned for his extreme asceticism. Born circa 350, he established himself on a mountain near Antioch that had previously served as a sacred site for the West Semitic god Nabu. Maron took up residence in the ruins of Nabu's temple. His death is estimated to have occurred between 407 and 423 CE, and his feast day, February 9, is a national holiday in Lebanon.

Domains

Maron is considered among the fathers of monasticism. His relentless pursuit of spiritual discipline set him apart from other ascetics. He owned a tent but rarely used it, preferring to live exposed to the elements. He spent entire nights standing in prayer and fasted for extended periods. His reputation as a miracle worker spread, and he became famous for performing healing miracles.

Lore

Maron attracted numerous disciples during his lifetime. After his death, this community of followers evolved into what became the Maronite Church, an Eastern Catholic Church that remains one of twenty-two autonomous churches within the Catholic communion. Lebanon serves as the heartland of the Maronite tradition, though communities exist worldwide. Some prayers in the Maronite liturgy are still recited in Aramaic, the language spoken by Jesus.

Sacred Sites

Monastery of Saint Maron in Lebanon.
