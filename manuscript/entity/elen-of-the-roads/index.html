Elen of the Roads is a Celtic saint, Roman Catholic saint, and prehistoric goddess venerated under three potentially conflated identities.

Three Elens

Three figures may be conflated under this name:

  1. The Historical Woman: Wife of Roman general Magnus Maximus (c. 355-388), who ruled Britain, Gaul, Spain, and Roman Africa until his execution for treason
  2. The Prehistoric Goddess: An elusive deer goddess of sovereignty worshipped perhaps since the Paleolithic era
  3. Confusion with Saint Helena: Mother of Emperor Constantine, sometimes described as British

The Historical Elen

Welsh legends describe Elen as an indigenous British princess. Her father may have been a North Welsh chieftain, or she may have been the daughter of a Roman emperor and Celtic woman. Alternatively, she may be among the children of Coel Hen (Coel Godebog), also known as Old King Cole.

Magnus and Elen married in Britain and had seven children, five of whom are recognized as Celtic saints. She introduced Celtic monasticism from Gaul into Wales and encouraged her husband to build roads across Wales for military travel. Local men cooperated because Elen, their native princess and true ruler, had requested them.

The Problem

Many Welsh roads are named after Elen, but they predate this historical woman by centuries. The fourth-century princess cannot be responsible for building these roads.

The Primordial Goddess

Behind the saint may lurk an ancient goddess of sovereignty and Queen of the British Isles. This primordial deer goddess is associated with roads, crossroads, byways, springs, wells, waterfalls, ley lines, forests, and sacred groves. She may have been worshipped since the Paleolithic era. Though now most associated with Wales, her sacred territory once extended over all of Albion (Great Britain) and possibly beyond.

Some identify her with Nehalennia, whose shrine in the Netherlands was frequented by travelers, including British merchants.

The Dream of Maxen Wledig

In this medieval Welsh tale from the Mabinogion, Emperor Maxen dreams of traveling to the world's highest mountain and largest river, arriving at a castle where he sees a princess whose beauty shines like the sun. Obsessed, he sends men to find her. They do, but she says Maxen must come himself. He makes the journey, marries Elen, and as bride-price she requests three strongholds and roads connecting them.

Medieval Welsh manuscripts typically depict pre-Christian deities as royalty. Though described as a noblewoman, supernatural elements reveal Elen's true divine identity.

Dual Traditions

Elen serves different constituencies:

  • Christian devotees may deny goddess origins, insisting the fourth-century princess is the original and only Elen
  • Modern Pagan devotees may perceive her saint role as falsehood

Both traditions are valid. Pagan veneration of Elen is currently undergoing revival.

Manifestations

  • In the Dream of Maxen: fine robes and jewels
  • As ancient goddess: antlered lady of the woods
  • In most ancient form: possibly a tree goddess like Artemis or Asherah

Attributes

Fly agaric (Amanita muscaria), basket of apples, antlers.

Sacred Trees

Elder, elm, linden, apple.

Sacred Animals

Deer, reindeer, hounds (especially greyhounds and elkhounds).

Sacred Sites

  • Numerous Welsh roads and wells named in her honor
  • London may once have been her sacred territory; some theorize the city's name derives from hers
  • Salisbury Plain (where Stonehenge stands) was once called Ellendune

Feast Day

22 May (Roman Catholic calendar)
