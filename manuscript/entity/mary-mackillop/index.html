Mary MacKillop (1842-1909), the first Roman Catholic saint from Australia, was canonized on 17 October 2010 by Pope Benedict XVI. Born in Fitzroy, Melbourne, she was the eldest of eight children of struggling Scottish immigrants. Her father, though a poor businessman, was an excellent teacher who educated his children at home.

By age fourteen, Mary was working as a clerk. At eighteen, she moved to southern Australia as a governess and began teaching other children. She confided her desire to serve the poor to a local priest concerned about the lack of Catholic education in the region. In 1866, Mary and her sisters opened Saint Joseph's School in a stable, accepting anyone who wished to learn regardless of ability to pay.

Domains

Because of her bravery in exposing clerical abuse, Mary is considered a spiritual advocate for survivors of sexual abuse, especially by those in positions of authority, and for children in general. Her documented miracles involve cancer recovery.

Lore

In 1867, Mary became the first sister and Mother Superior of the Sisters of Saint Joseph of the Sacred Heart. By 1869, over seventy sisters had founded twenty-one schools throughout Australia, plus clinics and orphanages.

In 1870, Mary became aware of sexual abuse of children by a local priest. She reported the allegations to superiors, and the priest was sent back to Ireland. His friend, another priest, swore vengeance. On 22 September 1871, Mary was excommunicated for insubordination, turned into the streets with nothing. She lived with a Jewish family until Bishop Sheil lifted the excommunication five months later on his deathbed.

Her motto: "Never see a need without doing something about it."

Iconography

The most popular image is a photograph of her in her nun's habit. An Australian postage stamp commemorated her canonization. The Mary MacKillop Rose was bred and named in her honor.

Sacred Sites

The Mary MacKillop Memorial Chapel in North Sydney houses her tomb and museum. Other sites include the Mary MacKillop Centre in Penola, Australia; Mary MacKillop Cottage in Arrowtown, New Zealand; and Saint Margaret's Church in Roy Bridge, Scotland, her ancestral village.
