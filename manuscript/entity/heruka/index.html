Heruka is a wrathful male deity of the Buddhist Vajrayana and Tibetan tantric tradition. His name designates both a specific class of enlightened wrathful figures and, in the more restricted usage, a particular tantric buddha who embodies the fierce transformation of the passions into wisdom.

Domains

Wrathful compassion, the transmutation of desire and aggression into the path of awakening, the tantric union of method and wisdom, and the fierce protection of the dharma.

Iconography

Heruka is depicted as a naked wrathful figure with matted hair drawn up in a topknot, ornamented with garlands of severed heads and skull crowns. He stands or dances upon a corpse or a prostrate figure, and in most tantric forms he is joined in yab-yum union with his consort. His body is dark blue, blue-black, or red according to the specific cycle, and his multiple arms carry the ritual implements of the wrathful sadhana.

Attributes

Skull cup, vajra, curved knife, ritual staff, drum, and severed head garlands.

Relationships

Heruka is counted among the principal emanations of Aksobhya, the imperturbable dhyanibuddha of the east. Several distinct tantric buddhas of the Anuttarayoga class are also called Heruka or bear Heruka as a component of their names, including Chakrasamvara and Hevajra, and the wrathful god Buddhakapala is described as a form of Heruka in certain lineages.
