Saint Joseph is the patron saint of bakers, pastry chefs, carpenters, and workers.

Classification

Venerated as a saint by most branches of Christianity. Considered among the highest-ranking saints.

Life and History

Saint Joseph was a carpenter from Nazareth who became the husband of the Virgin Mary and the foster father of Jesus Christ. Though he speaks no recorded words in Scripture, his actions reveal a just and compassionate man.

The Righteous Man

When Joseph discovered Mary was pregnant before their marriage, he initially planned to divorce her quietly rather than expose her to public disgrace. An angel appeared to him in a dream, explaining that Mary's child was conceived by the Holy Spirit. Joseph trusted the divine message and took Mary as his wife.

Protector of the Holy Family

Joseph protected his family through dangerous times:

  • He traveled with pregnant Mary to Bethlehem for the census
  • He was present at Jesus' birth in the stable
  • Warned by an angel, he fled with Mary and Jesus to Egypt to escape King Herod's massacre
  • After Herod's death, he settled the family in Nazareth

Death

Joseph likely died before Jesus' public ministry began, as he is not mentioned during the Crucifixion. Tradition holds that he died peacefully in the arms of Jesus and Mary, making him the patron of a happy death.

Powers and Domains

Patronages

  • Carpenters and workers: As a craftsman himself
  • Fathers: As foster father of Jesus
  • Orphans: Protector of vulnerable children
  • Pastry chefs: Connected to Saint Joseph's Table tradition
  • The dying: Patron of a happy death
  • The universal Church: Declared by Pope Pius IX in 1870

House Selling

A popular folk practice involves burying a statue of Saint Joseph upside down in the yard of a house for sale. Once the house sells, the statue is dug up and given a place of honor in the new home. This practice, while not officially endorsed by the Church, remains widespread.

The Number 19

Saint Joseph is associated with the number 19, his feast day in March.

Saint Joseph's Table

On March 19, Italian communities prepare elaborate altars laden with food called Saint Joseph's Tables (Tavola di San Giuseppe). These feasts honor Joseph and feed the poor. Traditional foods include:

  • Bread in elaborate shapes
  • Pasta with sardines
  • Pastries and sweets (especially zeppole and sfinge)
  • Fava beans

Iconography

Depicted as a middle-aged or elderly man with:

  • Carpenter's tools
  • A lily (symbol of purity)
  • The Christ Child
  • A flowering staff

Feast Days

  • March 19: Solemnity of Saint Joseph (principal feast)
  • May 1: Saint Joseph the Worker (instituted by Pope Pius XII in 1955)

Sacred Sites

  • Saint Joseph's Oratory, Montreal, Canada (built by Saint André Bessette)
  • Church of Saint Joseph, Nazareth, Israel

Related Entities
