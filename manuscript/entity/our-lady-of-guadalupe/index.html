Overview

Our Lady of Guadalupe, a Roman Catholic saint, is a highly revered manifestation of the Virgin Mary and among the most famous of all Marian apparitions. She is also controversial:

  • Many are shocked at the suggestion she could be anyone other than Mary
  • Others are convinced she is an indigenous Mexican goddess who tolerates the guise of Mary

The Apparition

The apparition first appeared to Juan Diego as he passed Tepeyac Hill on 9 December 1531, twelve years following the Spanish conquest of Mexico. Speaking in Nahuatl, she told him to advise Church authorities she wanted an abbey built on the hill. After four appearances and Church skepticism, she told Juan Diego to gather flowers from the hillside.

Although it was winter, he discovered beautiful Castilian roses, completely out of season and out of place. When he presented them to the bishop in his agave-fiber tilma, the image of the Lady was miraculously emblazoned on it. The abbey was built. The Basilica of Guadalupe is the subject of the greatest pilgrimage in the Western Hemisphere.

The Miraculous Tilma

Juan Diego's original tilma, still emblazoned with the image, is housed in her Basilica. Scientific analysis indicates some later embellishment (golden rays, moon, angel), but the main portion cannot be explained. The blue pigment cannot be identified or reproduced. The tilma was woven from maguey fibers, which have a life expectancy of approximately ten years; its continued existence is a miracle in itself.

The Aztec Connection

Before Christianity's arrival, Tepeyac was the site of the Great Temple of the goddess Coatlique and already considered a place of immense sacred power. The apparition appeared a fifth time to Juan Diego's uncle, healing him of smallpox and telling him her "true name": Tecuauhtlacuepeuh. Church officials understood this as "Guadalupe," perhaps because many came from Spain's Extremadura, home of the Black Madonna of Guadalupe.

Nahuatl meanings of Guadalupe may include:

  • She Who Crushed the Snake's Head
  • She Who Comes Flying from the Region of Light Like a Fiery Eagle
  • She Who Banishes Those Who Ate Us
A bumper sticker popular in the southwestern United States reads, "In Guad we trust."

Iconography

The image is of a young brown-skinned Indian woman in Indian clothing. Her mantle is teal (blue-green), which in Aztec cosmology symbolizes abundance and prosperity, not the traditional European blue. She appears everywhere from sacred imagery to kitsch, murals to tattoos.

Feast Day

  • December 12 (also invoked on the first day of each month)

Sacred Sites

Her basilica on Tepeyac Hill. Replica shrines exist in Italy, Japan, and throughout the United States.
