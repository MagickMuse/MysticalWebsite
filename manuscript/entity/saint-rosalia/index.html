Saint Rosalia is renowned for protection from plague and performing healing miracles of any kind.

Roman Catholic saint and patron of Palermo, Sicily, renowned as a powerful miracle worker.

Life and Legend

Rosalia was born in 1130 to a noble Norman family in Sicily, her mother's line claiming descent from Charlemagne. Though she grew up pampered at the Norman court, Rosalia longed for religious life. She fled to live as a hermit on Mount Pellegrino, overlooking Palermo.

Her family sent men to bring her home by force, but according to legend, two angels guided Rosalia from cave to cave, always helping her evade capture. Finally, the angels led her to a cave where she inscribed on the wall: "I, Rosalia, daughter of Sinibald, Lord of Roses, and Quisquina, resolve to live in this cave for the love of my Lord, Jesus Christ." She died in that cave around 1166.

The Plague Miracle

Churches were dedicated to Rosalia as early as 1237, but her legend truly flourished in 1624, when bubonic plague devastated Palermo, killing a third of its inhabitants. Rosalia appeared in visions, first to a sick woman, then to a hunter. She revealed where her bones lay hidden in her mountain cave and commanded the hunter to carry her skeleton in procession through the city.

The hunter climbed Mount Pellegrino and found a woman's skeleton in a cave, exactly as described, with a rock nearby bearing the carved name "Rosalia." He followed her instructions. When the procession ended, so did the plague. Palermo claimed Rosalia as its patron saint.

Veneration

Devotees consider Rosalia a great miracle worker. Many insist that whatever is asked of her is granted.

Iconography

Rosalia wears a crown of roses.

Attributes

Book, cross, skull, hermit's staff.

Colors

Blue, white.

Sacred Site

The Sanctuary of Santa Rosalia in Palermo, built in 1625 in the cave where her bones were discovered. The climb to reach her small sanctuary is steep.

Festivals

  • 15 July: Annual reproduction of Rosalia's original processional, commemorating her arrival in Palermo
  • 4 September: Traditional barefoot pilgrimage from Palermo up Mount Pellegrino
  • September: Santa Rosalia Fisherman's Festival in Monterey, California

Feast Days

  • 4 September (primary feast)
  • 15 July (commemorates procession ending the plague)
