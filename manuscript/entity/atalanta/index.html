Atalanta

The Greek heroine Atalanta was the swiftest runner in all the land. Abandoned in the woods as a baby, she was raised first by bears and then by hunters, and grew into a fierce huntress who preferred a single life. She vowed to marry only the suitor who could outrun her in a footrace, with death as the price of losing. Several swift men still chose to risk it, certain of their own speed.

The Race for the Golden Apples

Hippomenes, sometimes called Melanion, was chosen to judge the race. He fell in love with Atalanta the moment he saw her and announced that he would run against her himself. Before the race, he prayed to Aphrodite, who had already kindled his love. The goddess slipped him three golden apples from her own hand with whispered instructions.

As Atalanta gained on him in the race, Hippomenes tossed an apple onto the path ahead. She stopped to pick it up, lost ground, then caught him again. He threw the second apple, and the third, and at the final moment Aphrodite herself urged Atalanta to stoop and claim the apple. Hippomenes reached the finish line first, and the two married.

The Transformation

On their wedding day, Atalanta and Hippomenes neglected to give thanks to Aphrodite. The angered goddess turned them both into lions and yoked them to her own golden chariot.

Plants

Apple is her sacred fruit, particularly the golden apple of magickal compulsion.
