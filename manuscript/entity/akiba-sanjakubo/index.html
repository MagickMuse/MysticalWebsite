Magickal Properties

Sanjakubo was born when his mother petitioned Kannon for fertility and may be an avatar of Kannon. As a tiny boy he demonstrated powerful spiritual gifts and inclinations, becoming a yamabushi (shamanic mountain priest) by age six or seven, studying esotericism, mysticism, and the secrets of fire. He appears as a beaked, winged Tengu haloed in fire with snakes entwining his body, mounted on a white fox.

Ritual Uses

Work with Akiba-Sanjakubo for fire prevention and control. After meditating for a week, Sanjakubo had a vision of a flame rising from incense smoke containing a Tengu with Fudo's attributes but mounted on a white fox, combining Buddhism and Shinto elements. Realizing this was his own reflection, he summoned the white fox and flew to Mount Akiba.

Offerings

Swords (especially from samurai), prayers, and incense. Traditionally offerings are given on the 23rd day of each month. During Japan's Warring Era, samurai offered their swords to his shrines in petition and gratitude.

Favored People

Yamabushi mountain priests, samurai, those seeking fire prevention, people working with fire safety, and practitioners combining Buddhist and Shinto elements.

Petition For

Fire prevention, weakening existing fires, traffic safety, longevity, protection from poverty, minimizing fire damage, and protection against general disaster.

Warnings

Originally a yamabushi who had a vision of a Tengu and realized it was his own reflection. Sacred day is December 23rd. His power combines Buddhist and Shinto elements in fire mastery.

Additional Notes

His attributes include sword of wisdom and the cord of rescue. His sacred day is December 23rd (anniversary of his death). Numbers 23 and 24 are sacred to him. He has shrines throughout Japan and established the shrine in Akiba-koen Park in Tochio, Nigata Prefecture, where annual festivals include fire-walking rituals.
