Classification

Roman Catholic saint. Also venerated as a Gnostic saint in Ecclesia Gnostica Catholica. Her historical existence is debated by scholars, though devotion to her is ancient and widespread.

Life and Legend

Saint Veronica is remembered for a single act of compassion during Christ's Passion. According to tradition, as Jesus carried his cross along the Via Dolorosa to Calvary, Veronica stepped forward from the crowd and offered him her veil to wipe his face. When he returned the cloth, his image was miraculously imprinted upon it.

The Name

The name "Veronica" may derive from the Latin vera icon, meaning "true image," referring to the miraculous imprint. Some scholars suggest she was originally called Berenice or Berenike, a Greek name meaning "bearer of victory."

Biblical Connection

Some traditions identify Veronica with the woman healed of a hemorrhage who touched Christ's garment (Matthew 9:20-22, Mark 5:25-34, Luke 8:43-48). This connection appears in early Christian writings but is not universally accepted.

The Veil of Veronica

The veil bearing Christ's image became one of Christianity's most significant relics. Known as the Sudarium or Vera Icon, it was venerated in Rome for centuries. The image it bore was called the Holy Face (Volto Santo) and became a major focus of devotion.

Relic History

The veil was kept in Saint Peter's Basilica in Rome, where it attracted countless pilgrims. Its current status is uncertain—some claim it was lost or destroyed, while others maintain it remains in the Vatican's possession.

Stations of the Cross

Veronica's encounter with Christ is commemorated as the Sixth Station of the Cross in traditional Catholic devotion, though it does not appear in the Gospel accounts.

Gnostic Veneration

Saint Veronica appears in the list of Gnostic saints honored in Liber XV, the Gnostic Mass of Ecclesia Gnostica Catholica. She is commemorated alongside other figures who preserved sacred images or relics.

Iconography

Veronica is depicted as a woman holding a cloth bearing the imprint of Christ's face. She may be shown:

  • Kneeling before Christ on the road to Calvary
  • Standing alone, displaying the veil
  • With the Holy Face clearly visible on the cloth

Attributes

The veil bearing Christ's image (Sudarium), sometimes shown with three faces to indicate the cloth was folded.

Shrove Tuesday

In addition to her feasts, Veronica is honored on Shrove Tuesday, the day before Ash Wednesday.

Sacred Sites

The Chapel of the Holy Face on the Via Dolorosa in Jerusalem, allegedly the site of the miracle and possibly also Veronica's home.

The Shrine of Saint Veronica in Saint Peter's Basilica in Rome.

Related Entities
