Saint Godelieve is renowned for healing throat ailments and eye problems.

Classification

Roman Catholic saint.

Life and History

Godelieve (1049-1070) was a Flemish noblewoman, daughter of Hemfried, Lord of Wierre-Effray. At age eighteen, she was given in marriage to another noble, Bertulf of Ghistelles, who abandoned her before the wedding feast was even over, allegedly at his mother's instigation.

The Abuse

Godelieve was left with her new in-laws, who abused her. She was locked in a tiny cell, starved, beaten, and subjected to verbal abuse. Eventually, she managed to escape and return home.

Failed Intervention

Godelieve's father was outraged and contacted secular and religious authorities. The bishop of Tournai and the Count of Flanders intervened, insisting that Bertulf reconcile with Godelieve, return her to his home, and treat her well. Bertulf acquiesced and seemed repentant. For a brief period, things seemed fine.

Murder

Bertulf soon became abusive and violent. On July 6, 1070, while Bertulf "happened" to be out of town, two hired assassins, coincidentally employees of his mother, first strangled Godelieve into unconsciousness and then drowned her in a pond or well.

Veneration

Local people perceived the murder site as sanctified and began making pilgrimages. Godelieve was soon credited with miracles. She restored the failing eyesight of her stepdaughter, Bertulf's daughter.

Like an Almita

Although a canonized Roman Catholic saint, Godelieve resembles a Latin American Almita in many ways. It is the tragic nature of her death in the prime of youth that caused her to be venerated and invoked.

Powers and Domains

  • Matron saint of those requiring restraining orders
  • Invoked against abusive relationships of all kinds
  • Traditionally invoked against difficult in-laws
  • Invoked for healing miracles, especially throat ailments

Iconography

  • A beautiful young woman with a rope around her neck
  • A pair of angels placing a crown on a beautiful young woman

Attributes

Crown, well.

Sacred Site

The Holy Well of Saint Godelieve in Gistel, Belgium. It is unclear whether these waters were considered sacred or therapeutic before her death.

Feast Day

6 July
