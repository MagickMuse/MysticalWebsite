Magickal Properties

Dryads are Greek tree nymphs, nature spirits intimately bonded to specific trees. Each Dryad's life force is tied to her tree. When the tree thrives, she thrives; if the tree is harmed, she suffers; if the tree dies, she dies. Hamadryads are Dryads specifically bound to oak trees, though the terms are often used interchangeably. Dryads are beautiful, shy female spirits who protect their trees and the surrounding forest. They can bestow blessings of nature, grant fertility, and teach herbal wisdom, but they fiercely punish those who harm their trees.

Dryads are ancient Greek female woodland spirits who serve as attendants of Artemis, guardians of trees, groves, and forests. The dryads live in trees but should not be confused with hamadryads, who are stationary spirits of individual trees that die when a tree is felled. Dryads, on the other hand, move around quite easily.

To see them was considered unlucky, although this may be because as vigilant guardian spirits they most frequently made themselves visible when displeased and intent on inflicting disciplinary action.

Ritual Uses

Dryads are invoked for forest protection, tree magick, herbal knowledge, and connection to nature spirits. Work with them by honoring their trees through respectful presence and offerings. They can grant blessings of fertility and abundance. Petition them when seeking to understand plant spirits or when needing forest protection. Never harm their tree if you wish to maintain relationship with them.

Offerings

Dryads are appeased and propitiated with offerings of milk, water, wine, oil, and honey, garlands of flowers placed on branches, songs and poetry in their honor, protection and care of their tree, eco-friendly actions that benefit forests.

Favored People

Those who protect trees and forests, herbalists and plant workers, nature lovers and conservationists, people seeking harmony with nature spirits, those honoring sacred groves.

Petition For

Forest and tree protection, herbal wisdom and plant knowledge, fertility and growth, connection to nature spirits, blessings of abundance, understanding of tree magick.

Warnings

Protect their tree and offer libations at the root. Never cut, damage, or transplant the tree because harming the tree harms the Dryad and invites her wrath and the wrath of other forest spirits. Those who harm Dryads' trees may face disease, misfortune, or death. Always ask permission before taking anything from their tree or the surrounding area.

Additional Notes

Dryads were particularly associated with oak trees but could inhabit other tree species. They were companions of Artemis and participated in her hunts and festivals. Ancient Greeks would not cut down certain trees believed to house Dryads without performing appropriate rituals and offerings. Dryads could leave their trees for short periods but could not stray far. They were known to fall in love with mortals and sometimes bore children. The punishment for harming a Dryad's tree could extend to an entire family line.
