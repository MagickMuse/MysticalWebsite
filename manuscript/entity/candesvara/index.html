Overview

Candesvara is a minor god of the Hindu tradition, understood in two closely related senses: as a benevolent aspect of Siva himself, and as an attendant on Siva who has been drawn so fully into the god's own being that his name carries the honor lord. In the attendant sense he was, according to tradition, a youthful cowherd of exceptional devotion whose worship of Siva at the cattle pastures earned him permanent place at the god's side. He is generally counted among the sixty-three Nayanars, the Saiva saints of the Tamil tradition, under the name Chandesa, and in the wider Puranic reckoning his cult belongs to the temple attendance and the honoring of offerings brought before the great god.

Classification

Minor god of the Hindu tradition. Benevolent aspect of Siva and honored attendant of the god, drawn from a Tamil Nayanar saint into the Puranic pantheon.

Domains

Guardianship of the Saiva shrine, protection and reception of offerings brought before Siva, blessing on the pastoral work of the cowherd, the honored place of the ordinary devotee raised by their devotion to a permanent office beside the god.

Iconography

He sits upon a lotus throne, in the posture appropriate to a divine attendant elevated to his own honored office. His many arms carry the standard emblems distributed across his attributes list, showing him at once as a servant of Siva and as a lord in his own right.

Attributes

Arrow, ax, bow, club, crown, hatchet, noose, rosary, snake, trident, and water jar. The trident and the snake belong to him as an aspect of Siva; the rosary and the water jar belong to him as a devotee raised to a divine attendant; and the crown belongs to him as the lord whom his name announces.

Sacred Sites

Saiva temples across India, particularly in Tamil Nadu, where he is honored as one of the sixty-three Nayanars and often has a small shrine of his own within the larger temple compound, placed to receive the leftovers and the transferred offerings that come to him from the main shrine of Siva.

Ritual

Honored within the ordinary rhythm of Saiva temple worship, particularly at the moment when offerings brought before Siva pass to him, a transfer often marked by a small ceremony at his shrine. Devotees may petition him as the friendly attendant at the temple door, and Nayanar traditions preserve his story in the hymns of the Tamil Saiva canon.

Lore

The story of Candesvara belongs to the wider Saiva pattern in which the intensity of a devotee's love for Siva raises them into a permanent office at the god's side. In the Tamil Nayanar tradition he is the young cowherd whose worship of Siva at the pasture was disturbed by his father's disapproval; when his father overturned the small offering the young devotee had made, the cowherd, without pausing in his prayer, struck his father down with the ax he carried for tending the cattle. Siva, moved by this absolute devotion, appeared and took the young cowherd to himself, raising him to his own attendance and giving him the office and title of Candesvara, lord of Canda. The story is a hard one and has always been read carefully within the tradition, but it belongs to the Saiva theology of devotion as the paramount value under which even the ordinary human bonds are subject to reordering.
