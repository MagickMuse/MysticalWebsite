Rasputin is renowned for healing bleeding disorders and blood-related ailments.

Grigori Rasputin (1869-1916) was a Russian healer and clairvoyant venerated as an unofficial saint by some and despised as a charlatan by others. Known as "the Demon Saint" or "the Mad Monk," he was actually a staretz (wandering pilgrim), never ordained as a monk. He remains one of the most divisive figures in Russian spiritual history.

Domains

Rasputin specialized in healing, particularly distance healing. He famously treated Tsarevitch Alexis's hemophilia when physicians had given up hope, stopping internal bleeding sometimes from considerable distances. Modern devotees credit him with continued healing miracles and protection from fatal traffic accidents.

Iconography

Rasputin appears as a man with long black hair and beard, and an intensely powerful gaze. His recognizable image, preserved in numerous photographs, appears on holy cards and icons. He may be depicted alone or with the imperial family, often placed behind Tsarevitch Alexis in a blessing pose. Some icons sanitize his image, softening his gaze, trimming his beard, and dressing him in spotless white.

Lore

Rasputin's spiritual awakening began at the monastery of Simeon Verkhoturye and culminated in an encounter with the Black Madonna of Kazan. He woke to find her icon weeping, and she told him: "Grigori, I am weeping for humanity's sins. Go wander and cleanse people of their sins." He spent years as a wandering holy fool, walking thousands of miles across Russia.

His death rivals the martyrdom legends of saints who supernaturally resisted execution. Conspirators lured him to Prince Yusupov's palace and served him cyanide-laced wine and cakes. He showed no ill effects. Shot in the heart, he was left for dead, but when Yusupov returned an hour later, Rasputin opened his eyes and struck him. Fleeing into the courtyard, Rasputin was shot in the back, clubbed, wrapped in a sheet, and dumped alive into the Neva River, where he finally drowned. Witnesses claimed his corpse levitated when later exhumed and burned.

Rasputin predicted his own death, settling his affairs weeks beforehand. He left a letter prophesying that if nobles killed him (as they did), Russian blood would flow for twenty-five years and there would be no Russian nobility, a foreshadowing of the revolution. For devotees, his passage through all four elements (water, earth, fire, air) exponentially increased his mystic powers.
