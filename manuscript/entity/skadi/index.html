Magical Properties

Frost Giantess and Death Goddess whose name means "Destruction." Skadi hunts bears and wolves in snow-covered mountains. Skiers and hunters who fail to return have fallen to Skadi. As a Frost Giant (Jotun), she is an eternal primordial force that will outlast the gods themselves.

Ritual Uses

Daughter of Frost Giant Thiazzi (who stole the immortality apples). After the gods killed him, Skadi marched to Asgard fully armed demanding revenge. She demanded a husband as wergild (blood price). Required to choose by feet alone, she selected the most beautiful feet thinking they belonged to Baldur—but got sea god Njord instead. The marriage failed: she hated sunshine and the seashore; he couldn't bear the cold of her ice-palace and the howling wolves. They now live apart.

When the gods bound Loki for Baldur's death, Skadi personally placed a poisonous snake over his face so its venom would drip onto him—her final revenge for her father's death.

Offerings

Venison and tokens of the hunt. Winter offerings. Prayers for safe passage through snow and ice. Honor her in mountain places.

Favored People

Hunters, skiers, those who live in winter lands, people who honor the wild and untamed nature.

Petition For

Skill in hunting and skiing, winter survival, revenge and justice, strength during winter months, safe passage through mountains and snow.

Warnings

Frost Giantess whose name means Destruction. Hunters and skiers who do not return from the mountains have fallen to Skadi. She is fierce and unforgiving, especially regarding matters of family honor and revenge. Her placement of the poisonous snake over Loki demonstrates her commitment to vengeance.

Additional Notes

Skadi represents the harsh but honest power of winter. Unlike many deities who were absorbed into pantheons peacefully, she arrived fully armed demanding justice. Her incompatibility with Njord speaks to the fundamental differences between mountain ice and ocean waves—two powerful forces that cannot be reconciled. As a Jotun (Frost Giant), she represents primordial forces older than the gods themselves.

Manifestation

Skadi appears as a tall, strong, beautiful, athletic woman who travels through the snow on skates, skis, or snowshoes, hunting with a bow and arrows. She may be accompanied by wolves.

Attributes

Ice skates, skis, snowshoes

Color

White

Abode

Skadi lives in Thrymheimr ("Noise Home"), her father's home, also described as being located among remote, high, snowy peaks.

The Rescuer

Despite her fearsome reputation, Skadi is also known as the Snowshoe Goddess and can be extremely helpful: she rescues winter travelers lost in storms and snow. Those stuck on a snowy slope or trapped by icebound weather should number among the spirits of first appeal.

Additional Lore

When the Aesir offered Skadi a husband as part of their reparations, they also had to make her laugh, a feat accomplished by Loki. After her failed marriage to Njord, she is said to have later found the god Holler (Ullr) more compatible. Skadi is said to have first taught men to hunt with bows and arrows; in remote regions a man was reportedly sacrificed to her annually, possibly as late as the seventeenth century. Scandinavia's name may echo hers as 'Skadi's Land.' She predates the Aesir and was one of the last Norse deities to be actively venerated after Christianization. Her name is sometimes translated as 'Shade,' possibly indicating the dim winter sun or the shades of the dead.
