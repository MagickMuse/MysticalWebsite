Saint Gerard Majella is renowned for healing and protection in all aspects of pregnancy and childbirth.

Classification

Roman Catholic saint.

Life and History

Gerard Majella (1725-1755) was rejected by the Capuchin Order because of his poor health. The Redemptorist missionaries accepted him as a lay brother, allowing him to serve as sacristan, doorkeeper, tailor, medic, and gardener.

Gerard became renowned for producing miracles and was reputedly capable of levitation, bilocation (being in two places at once), and clairvoyance.

The False Accusation

A pregnant woman accused Gerard of fathering her child. He did not respond or defend himself, instead retreating into silence. Eventually, the woman recanted her accusation. This is the basis for his patronage of all aspects of pregnancy and childbirth.

Patronage of the Unborn

Saint Gerard Majella is the patron saint of unborn children and is thus the favored saint of many anti-abortion organizations.

Vodou Syncretism

In Haitian Vodou traditions, the Gédé spirits of the dead, especially Gédé Nibo, are syncretized to Saint Gerard Majella, perhaps because of the human skull that is his traditional attribute.

Mortification

Saint Gerard was associated with fairly flamboyant mortifications of the flesh, including bloody, violent self-flagellation. He died at age twenty-nine of tuberculosis.

Iconography

Depicted contemplating a cross or in the act of praying while levitating.

Attributes

Cross, skull.
