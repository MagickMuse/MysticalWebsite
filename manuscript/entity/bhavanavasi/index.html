Bhavanavasi

◇Culture
JainIndian
◇Gender
Male
◇Type
Deity
≡Also called
The Dwellers in Places, Bhavanavāsī, Bhavanapati

Bhavanavasi, "the dwellers in places," is a generic name given to a class of gods in the Jain tradition. They are deities of youthful appearance, arranged in ten groups, each bearing the suffix -kumara, meaning "prince" or "youth," a reflection of their perpetual youthful form.

Domains

Guardianship of the elemental and natural forces that surround the ordered world, and the beneficent activity of youthful divinity within Jain cosmology.

The Ten Groups

The ten Bhavanavasi classes are:

  • Agni-kumara, the youthful gods of fire
  • Asura-kumara, the youthful gods of the asura order
  • Dik-kumara, the youthful gods of the directions
  • Dvipa-kumara, the youthful gods of the islands or continents
  • Naga-kumara, the youthful gods of the serpents
  • Stanita-kumara, the youthful gods of thunder
  • Suparna-kumara, the youthful gods of the great birds
  • Udadhi-kumara, the youthful gods of the oceans
  • Vayu-kumara, the youthful gods of the winds
  • Vidyut-kumara, the youthful gods of lightning

Each group governs the domain named in its title, and together they encompass the elemental and celestial forces that surround and support the Jain cosmos.

Iconography

Uniformly depicted as youthful princely figures, each group bearing the emblems appropriate to its domain.

Petition For

Blessing of the elemental spheres in which one lives and works, harmony with natural forces, and the fresh vitality proper to youthful divinity.
