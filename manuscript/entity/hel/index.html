Norse Death Goddess

Daughter of Loki and giantess Angrboða. Name means "to cover/conceal." Half alive, half dead, one side living flesh, other corpse-like. Odin threw her into Niflheim, giving authority over nine worlds to house those dying of sickness/old age (not warriors).

Balder Myth

Agreed to release Balder if every being wept. All wept except giantess Thokk (possibly Loki), so Balder remains until Ragnarok.

Domain

Éljúdnir hall with high walls, strong gates. River Gjöll crossed by bridge Gjallarbrú, guarded by Modgud. Everything named for suffering: dish (Hunger), knife (Starvation), servants (Lazy-Walker), threshold (Stumbling Block), bed (Sick-Bed), curtains (Gleaming Disaster).

Black Death

During Scandinavian plague, Hella traveled with rake/broom. Villages wiped out were swept by broom; survivors raked.

Classification as Giant (Jotun)

Nature of Giants

Primordial, permanent, eternal. First being was giant Ymir. Universe created from his corpse. Spirits surviving Ragnarok are sons of giantesses. Giants predate/outlast gods.

Giantesses

Manifest as fierce hags, warriors, mothers. Master shape-shifters called troll-hags and ogresses, words synonymous with "witch." Hel's dual living/dead form reflects giantesses' liminal transformative nature.

Hel's giant heritage explains cosmic power, eternal nature, role as fundamental force beyond gods. Not evil despite Christian demonization. Necessary cosmic force. Use funerary protocol at graves/crossroads/northern thresholds. Demands quiet dignity.
