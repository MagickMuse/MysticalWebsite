🌊Mari of the Euphrates

Overview

A mysterious water goddess worshipped in the ancient Amorite city-state of Mari, located on the right bank of the Euphrates in what is now Syria. It is now unknown whether the city was named after the goddess or vice versa.

Historical Context

The city of Mari was at the peak of its power circa 2900 to 2370 BCE. Hammurabi conquered it in 1759 BCE. The city was abandoned in the eighth century BCE.

Etymology and Connections

The Sumerian word mar may be interpreted as "womb" or "sea." Scholars speculate that the Amorites may have been the first to venerate Asherah, Lady of the Sea.

Possible Identities

Mari may or may not be the same goddess as Asherah. Alternatively, Mari may be:

  • The goddess of the star-gazing Nabataeans
  • The mysterious dolphin goddess whose image appears throughout the Middle East
  • A precursor or aspect of Asherah
  • The goddess of the same name who is currently venerated in the modern Feri tradition

Mystery

Much about this ancient goddess has been lost to time. Her exact nature, mythology, and worship practices remain largely unknown, though her importance to the ancient city of Mari is clear.

Important Note

This Mari is completely distinct from the famous Basque goddess Mari of the Pyrenees. They are two separate deities from different cultures and time periods who happen to share the same name.
