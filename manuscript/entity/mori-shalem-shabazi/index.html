Jewish saint, Kabalist, and the greatest of the Yemenite Jewish poets. 'Mori' is Aramaic for 'my teacher' or 'my master,' the Yemenite equivalent of rabbi.

Life and Legacy

Mori Shalem Shabazi (1619-1680) was a Kabalist, healer, miracle worker, astrologer, spiritual leader, and mystic poet. He is venerated by both Jews and Muslims and is invoked for blessings of healing and happiness.

Considered the preeminent Yemenite Jewish poet, Shabazi wrote in Arabic, Aramaic, and Hebrew. He composed nearly 1,500 poems (diwan in Arabic), of which only about 300 survive. He signed his work by embedding his name as an acrostic somewhere within each poem.

Im Nin'alu

Shabazi's most famous poem, "Im Nin'alu" ("If the Doors Are Locked"), gained international recognition when Israeli singer Ofra Haza recorded it in the 1980s, introducing his work to a global audience.

Dream Visitations

Mori Shabazi continues to appear in people's dreams, offering healing, warnings, and messages to those who seek his guidance.

Sacred Sites

  • His burial site near Taiz, Yemen
  • The Shabazi Synagogue in Netanya, Israel, which serves as a pilgrimage destination
