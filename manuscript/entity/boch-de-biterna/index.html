Boch de Biterna

Magickal Properties

Basque goat spirit whose name translates as "he goat." Term first emerged on French side of Pyrenees in reference to Basque witches and their nocturnal gatherings. Possibly references Akerbeltz or Mari, major Basque deities associated with goats and witchcraft. Connected to Pyrenean ibex, unique wild mountain goat indigenous only to Pyrenees.

Ritual Uses

Historically associated with nocturnal women's gatherings in deserted plains where goat was adored. Goats make frequent appearances in Basque magickal and spiritual traditions. Likely connected to broader Basque horned deity worship including Akerbeltz and Mari.

Offerings

No specific traditional offerings recorded. Likely similar to offerings made to Akerbeltz: bread, eggs, coins.

Favored People

Basque witches, particularly women who gathered by night for spiritual rites. Those working within traditional Basque spiritual frameworks.

Petition For

Likely similar petitions as made to Akerbeltz and Mari: protection, fertility, connection to ancient Basque traditions.

Warnings

Term emerged during Inquisition period when Basque spiritual practices were violently persecuted. Historical accounts colored by Inquisitorial bias against women's spiritual gatherings.

Additional Notes

Inquisition Documentation

In 1458, Inquisitor Alfonso de Spina deplored "perverse women who come together by night in some deserted plain to adore a goat commonly called the boch de Biterne." He claimed to have seen paintings in chambers of Toulouse Inquisition depicting these women adoring their boch. This represents Inquisitorial documentation of Basque women's spiritual practices.

Connection to Akerbeltz and Mari

Boch de Biterna may be another name for Akerbeltz, the black goat deity, or may reference Mari's goat associations. Pyrenean ibex is unique species of wild mountain goat found nowhere else, making goat symbolism particularly significant in Basque spiritual geography. Goats appear throughout Basque magickal and spiritual traditions as powerful symbols of connection to mountain spirits and ancient practices.
