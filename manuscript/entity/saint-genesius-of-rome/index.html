Saint Genesius of Rome is the patron saint of actors, comedians, performers, and entertainers.

Saint Genesius of Rome is renowned for healing epilepsy and seizure disorders.

Classification

Roman Catholic saint and martyr.

Life and Legend

Genesius was a popular Roman actor and comedian during the reign of Emperor Diocletian (284–305 CE). He led a troupe of performers who entertained the court with comedies and mimes, including satirical pieces mocking Christians and their rituals.

The Miraculous Conversion

One day, Genesius was performing a play that parodied Christian baptism for the emperor's amusement. In the scene, Genesius played a sick man requesting baptism. As part of the performance, other actors dressed as a priest and exorcist "baptized" him.

At the moment the water touched him, Genesius experienced a genuine conversion. He saw a vision of angels holding a book containing all his sins, which were washed away by the baptismal water. Rising from the mock ceremony, he declared to the audience and emperor that he had seen Christ and truly believed.

Martyrdom

Diocletian, initially believing this was part of the act, laughed. When Genesius insisted his conversion was real and refused to renounce Christ, the emperor flew into a rage. Genesius was tortured with iron hooks, burned with torches, and finally beheaded.

Powers and Domains

Genesius heals epilepsy and seizure disorders—conditions that in ancient times were sometimes associated with divine or demonic possession, fitting for one who experienced sudden spiritual transformation. He also protects actors and those in the performing arts.

Iconography

Depicted as a young man in Roman actor's costume, sometimes holding theatrical masks. May be shown at the moment of his conversion during the mock baptism, or during his martyrdom.

Attributes

Theatrical masks, scroll, palm of martyrdom, sword.

Sacred Sites

  • Church of San Ginesio dei Comici in Rome
  • Various churches dedicated to him throughout Italy and France

Feast Day

August 25

Related Entities

Saint Vitus, Saint Valentine, Saint Genesius of Arles, with whom he may be conflated
