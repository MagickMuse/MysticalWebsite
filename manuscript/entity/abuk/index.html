Magickal Properties

Abuk is the first woman in Dinka tradition, created alongside her male counterpart Garang. She is simultaneously an Eve-like figure and primordial ancestress, with dominion over the yin aspects of life: water and its distribution, women and anything to do with them, fertility and what grows, whether babies in the womb or fruits of the garden.

Ritual Uses

Work with Abuk for traditional women's activities including growing millet and brewing millet beer. Create rituals honoring the feminine principles of fertility, nourishment, and growth. Honor her through possession traditions and women's ceremonial practices.

Offerings

Home-brewed beer (ideally millet beer), millet, fruits, vegetables, and herbs from your garden. Avoid industrial alcohol or imported substitutes in favor of traditional, home-prepared offerings.

Favored People

Women, mothers, and those seeking fertility or nourishment. Dinka women and others engaged in brewing, gardening, and traditional women's activities. People working with agricultural abundance and traditional food preparation.

Petition For

Success in your endeavors, abundance, safety, good health, fertility, successful harvests, brewing mastery, and protection of women's traditional activities.

Warnings

Offer home-brewed beer, millet, fruits, and garden herbs. Avoid industrial alcohol or imported substitutes, and follow local Dinka protocols for possession and ritual because Abuk is the Dinka ancestral mother of millet, brewing, fertility, and women's rites.

Additional Notes

The Dinka are pastoralists from Southern Sudan with a monotheistic indigenous religion. Abuk presides over traditional Dinka women's activities and her son Deng is a sky deity with dominion over rain, storms, and fertility. Abuk remains the most popular name given to Dinka girls. She may manifest as small grey snakes.
