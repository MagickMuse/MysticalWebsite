Venerated as a Roman Catholic saint, Santiago Matamoros represents the militant aspect of Saint James the Greater.

Life and Legend

When remains discovered in a field at Compostela, Spain were identified as those of Saint James the Greater, King Alfonso II of Asturias (759-842) declared him patron saint of Spain and sacred leader of the war against the Moors. In Spanish, Saint James is known as San Diego, which evolved into Santiago. Santiago Matamoros literally translates as "Saint James, Killer of Moors."

At the Battle of Clavijo in La Rioja, Spain (834 CE), Christians were badly outnumbered by Moorish forces when Saint James the Greater suddenly appeared on the battlefield dressed in armor and riding a white horse, spurring the Christians to victory. This miraculous apparition established Santiago as a divine warrior saint.

Apparitions of Santiago were also recorded in South America as Spanish conquistadors battled indigenous forces. While Santiago generally assisted the Spanish, later reports describe him fighting on the Mexican side against Spain during Mexico's war for independence. Santiago Matamoros remains historically controversial due to his association with the Reconquista and the conquest of the Americas.

Magical Properties

Santiago is invoked to battle on your behalf in any situation. There is no problem for which he is not called upon. He is also petitioned for the fertility of mares.

Iconography

Santiago appears as a mounted warrior on a white horse, wielding a sword, often shown trampling enemies beneath the horse's hooves. This image is syncretized to the West African deity Ogun and the Andean spirit Illapa.
