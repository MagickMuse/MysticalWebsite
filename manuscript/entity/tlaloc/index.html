Lord of Rain

Tlaloc is among the most ancient figures in Aztec and broader Mesoamerican tradition, far predating the Aztecs themselves. As god of rain and water in all its forms, he governs not only rainfall but the mountains in which clouds gather, the springs that feed rivers, and the flow of water across the earth. He lives in mountain caves filled with treasure and rules Tlalocan, his own afterlife realm: a place of abundance and ease to which those who drowned or were struck by lightning were admitted.

The Four Jugs

Tlaloc was said to water the earth using four vast jugs of liquid, each one corresponding to a different face of the seasons and their effect on the harvest: growth and plenty, blight and disease, frost, and outright destruction. Through these four vessels he held the entire cycle of agricultural fortune in his hands.

Sacrifice

Tlaloc was the deity to whom the greatest number of sacrifices were made. Historical sources record that children were acquired specifically to be killed in his honour. The priests understood the children's weeping as a favourable omen: the more abundantly they wept, the more propitious the sacrifice was considered to be. These practices belong to the historical record and are presented here as such.

Manifestation

Tlaloc may manifest as an old man or as a child, with jaguar teeth or snake fangs.

Attributes

Color: green. Plant: chia. Creature: frog. Stone: jade.

Additional Notes

Tlaloc is said to have stolen corn from Quetzalcoatl.
