Jungle Goddess of the Tree of Life

Xtabay is a Mayan jungle goddess who may originally have been the goddess of the Tree of Life. She lives near lakes, rivers, and freshwater sources and is consistently portrayed as a lascivious goddess of love and sex. Her negative associations may derive from the sacred kapok tree (silk cotton tree) with which she is closely identified. The kapok was identified with the Tree of Life in Mayan cosmology, and as a spirit closely tied to traditional Mayan religion, she was vilified post-Christianity.

The Kapok Tree

The kapok is a massive tree with a buttressed root system potentially extending over thirty feet from the trunk. Its above-ground roots create the equivalent of caves. Xtabay sits among these roots singing and combing her gorgeous long hair using cactus spines as a comb.

The Lure

Xtabay's voice draws victims near. Men may approach assuming she is a woman of easy virtue, and they would be correct. She is so alluring that active seduction may not be required. She allegedly lures men into the jungle from which many never emerge, reputedly strangling them with her hair. Contact may result in insanity or illness. Victims allegedly die within a few days unless they receive immediate shamanic attention.

Caribbean Manifestation

Xtabay manifests in the Caribbean as well. On the night of Saint John (Midsummer Eve), she combs her hair with cactus spines, and men who fall asleep after sex with her wake to discover themselves seriously wounded. What seemed like a woman while making love is revealed to be a spiny cactus. The wounds become infected; the resulting fever is fatal if not treated shamanically and physically.

Manifestation

Xtabay manifests as a beautiful, seductive woman, a cactus, the Xtabentun flower, a kapok tree, and a green-and-yellow snake.

Sacred Plant

The kapok tree. Her associated flower is the Xtabentun, a white flower that grows wild among agave and the source of the Yucatecan Xtabentun liqueur.

Creatures

Quetzals and other tropical birds, snakes, jungle insects.
