Saint Mary of Egypt, venerated by virtually all branches of Christianity, lived from approximately 344 to 421 CE. She is often confused with Mary Magdalen. Often called a penitent prostitute, she was more accurately a sexual adventuress who ran away from home in Alexandria at age twelve. She claimed never to have accepted money for her favors, boasting instead of her depravity. For seventeen years she seduced young men and taught them the arts of love.

At twenty-nine, the age astrologers associate with the pivotal Saturn return, Mary encountered Christian pilgrims boarding a ship to Jerusalem. Amused by the idea of seducing holy men, she joined them and had relations with every pilgrim before the ship docked.

Domains

Mary guards against demons and temptation. She is petitioned for hair growth, a reference to her long hair that served as her only covering during decades of desert solitude.

Lore

In Jerusalem, Mary experienced unexpected self-loathing followed by spiritual epiphany. After Mass at the Church of the Holy Sepulchre, a disembodied voice told her she would find peace across the Jordan River. Taking John the Baptist as her model, she spent forty-seven years as a solitary desert hermit. Her clothes rotted away, leaving her naked and covered only by her extremely long hair. A lion became her sole companion.

The monk Zosimus encountered Mary in the desert and recorded her story. When he returned a year later, he found her dead. Too frail to dig a grave in the hard ground, Zosimus watched as a lion emerged from the wilderness, dug her grave, waited through the prayers, then vanished.

Iconography

Mary appears as either a sexy young naked woman or as a haggard, emaciated hermit. Her manifestation shows a deeply sunburned naked woman covered entirely by extremely long hair, with a lion as constant companion.

Sacred Sites

Mary's gravesite, now lost, was a popular pilgrimage destination in the sixth century. She has a chapel in Jerusalem's Church of the Holy Sepulchre, the site of her epiphany.
