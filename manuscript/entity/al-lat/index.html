Encyclopedia Source Notes (Allat)

Astral and tutelary goddess. Pre-Islamic northern and central Arabian. One of the three daughters of Allah. At Palmyra she was regularly invoked as a domestic guardian, either as Allat or as Astarte, with whom she is closely linked. At Ta'if she was symbolized in the form of a white granite stone. In Hellenic times she became syncretized with Athene or, according to Herodotus who called her Alilat, with Aphrodite. See also Atarsamain.

Western Semitic and Carthaginian Form (Allatu)

As Allatu, she was recognized as a chthonic underworld goddess of the western Semitic tradition, modeled on the Mesopotamian goddess Ereshkigal and possibly equating with Arsay in Canaanite mythology. The Carthaginians knew her as Allatu. In this aspect she is a tempestuous, volatile Queen of the World Below, who once threatened to raise an army of the dead to devour the living.

Mecca and the Satanic Verses

Al-Lat was once counted among the primary deities worshipped at Mecca before Islam. She is the goddess at the center of the so-called "satanic verses" episode referenced in connection with the Koran.
