Life and History

Longinus was a Christian saint and Roman centurion present at Christ's crucifixion. According to the Gospel of John, he pierced Christ's side with a lance to confirm death. Blood and water flowed from the wound onto Longinus, miraculously curing his failing eyesight. This immediate healing prompted his conversion: he is said to have declared, "Truly this man was the Son of God."

The Spear of Destiny

The lance Longinus used became known as the Spear of Destiny or the Holy Lance. It became one of the most sought-after Christian relics, believed to grant invincibility to whoever possesses it. Various objects have been claimed as the true spear throughout history, including one held in Vienna's Hofburg Palace.

The Blood of Christ

After the crucifixion, Longinus preserved the Blood of Christ that had flowed onto him. He eventually brought these relics to Mantua, Italy, where they remain housed in the Basilica of Saint Andrew.

Martyrdom

Longinus was eventually martyred for his faith. He is venerated as a saint in the Roman Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, and Armenian Apostolic churches.

Brazilian Folk Tradition

In Brazil, Longinus (São Longuinho) is invoked as a finder of lost objects. The distinctive petition ritual involves making three small jumps while asking for his help. This jumping motion is said to recall his hopping to see over the crowd at Golgotha due to his short stature or poor vision.

Iconography

Depicted as a Roman soldier, often holding the Holy Lance. May be shown at the foot of the cross, piercing Christ's side, or with blood/water flowing onto his face.

Attributes

Lance (spear), Roman military attire.

Sacred Sites

  • Basilica of Saint Andrew, Mantua, Italy (houses the Blood of Christ)
  • Saint Peter's Basilica, Rome (houses a monumental statue by Bernini)

Feast Day

March 15

Related Entities
