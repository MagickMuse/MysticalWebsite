Bhairava, whose name means "the terrible" or "the frightful," is a fierce form of the god Shiva in the Hindu Puranic and later tantric traditions. He is a guardian deity of doorways, thresholds, and sacred precincts, standing between the ordered world and the wild power beyond, and is classified among the ugra or wrathful aspects of the divine. Tradition holds that Bhairava was born from a drop of Shiva's blood, arising in a moment of divine anger to embody the destructive protective power of his source.

Domains

Guardianship of doorways and thresholds, the wrathful destruction of ignorance and pride, protection of sacred precincts, and the fierce grace that clears the path of practice.

Iconography

Bhairava is depicted in a style similar to Shiva but with up to five heads and as many as ten arms, bearing a fearsome countenance and holding a hook and a noose among his weapons. He is often shown accompanied by a dog, his sacred animal and constant companion.

Aspects and Epithets

Bhairava's principal aspects and epithets include Kalaratri, the black night; Ksetrapala, guardian of the sacred field; and Mahakala, the great time or great black one. He is also called Bhairon, a name particularly linked with the cult of dogs, whose devotees offer worship at doorways and crossroads.

Tantric Context

In tantric tradition, Bhairava is paired with the group of Mahavidyas, the ten wisdom goddesses who personify the Sakti or active power of Shiva. Each Mahavidya has her own corresponding Bhairava as consort and counterpart, and the pair together represent the union of fierce divine power in its masculine and feminine expressions.

Petition For

Protection of the home and its thresholds, guardianship during travel and passage, the wrathful clearing of obstacles, and the courage to face fearful circumstance.
