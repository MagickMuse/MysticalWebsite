Classification

Jewish saint and mystic.

Life and History

Widely recognized as the first woman before modern times to earn the title "rabbi," Asenath Barzani (1590-1670) was a poet, scholar, Kabbalist, yeshiva director, angelologist, and Baalat Shem (master of sacred names of power). She spent most of her life in Mosul in what was then Kurdistan (now Iraq).

Education and Marriage

Her father, Rabbi Samuel Barzani, stood as the leading scholar of Kurdish Jewry and a master of practical Kabbalah. He transmitted mystical secrets to his daughter and continued appearing in her dreams with guidance even after death. Both father and daughter reportedly possessed knowledge of God's Ineffable Name.

When Asenath married her cousin Jacob, her father insisted on an unusual wedding contract clause: Asenath must never be burdened with housework so she could dedicate herself fully to study.

Powers and Miracles

Contemporaries described her as both wise and beautiful. She performed fertility miracles and healed children through touch and blessing. Once, when a synagogue caught fire, she summoned angels to extinguish the flames.

A white dove nested outside her window and served as her guardian. When an intruder attempted to assault her one night, the dove alerted her. Rather than cry out, Asenath employed her mystical abilities: the attacker found himself suspended from her ceiling beams, trapped and exposed until she chose to release him.

Sacred Site

Her grave and her father's in Amadiyah (modern Iraq) drew pilgrims for centuries.
