The Historical Figure

Setne derives from Prince Khaemwaset, an actual son of Ramesses II who lived during the 13th century BCE. The historical prince earned a reputation as a scholar and restorer of ancient monuments. Centuries after his death, he became the subject of magical tales that transformed him into Egypt's archetypal sorcerer.

The Setne Tales

Demotic Egyptian stories, written centuries after the historical prince lived, depict Setne as a magician who raids tombs seeking powerful texts. In one tale, he steals a book of magic from the ghost of a prince named Naneferkaptah, whose family suffered terrible fates because of that same book. The ghost warns Setne, but he takes the text anyway and suffers consequences until he returns it.

In another story, Setne witnesses two funerals: a rich man carried to an elaborate tomb, and a poor man wrapped in a mat. When his son Sa-Osiris takes him to the Duat, Setne discovers their fates reversed. The rich man suffers eternally while the poor man stands blessed among the gods. Character during life matters more than burial wealth.

Role in Egyptian Magic

Setne represents the magician who transgresses boundaries in pursuit of power and knowledge. His stories acknowledge that Egyptian magic included dangerous practices: tomb-raiding for texts, necromancy, and dealings with the dead. They also warn about consequences.

Those who identify with Setne embrace the role of the boundary-crosser who seeks knowledge regardless of prohibition. His tales offer instruction through negative example as much as through technique.
