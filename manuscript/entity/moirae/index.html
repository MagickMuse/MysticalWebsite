The Three Fates

The Moirae (also the Moires) are the three Greek Fates who spin, measure, and cut the thread of life for all living beings. They represent the waxing, full, and waning moons—creation, existence, and destruction. The name "moira" means "portion," "lot," or "share," reflecting their power to determine each person's allotted fate.

The Three Sisters

  • Klotho the Spinner puts the wool around the spindle, beginning each life
  • Lachesis the Sustainer (also called Caster of Lots, Caster of Fate) spins the wool or measures the thread, determining the length and events of each life
  • Atropos the Cutter (also called "The Inevitable") snips the thread, ending each life

Supreme Power

The Moirae are eternal and were considered even more powerful than the gods themselves. No deity could override the fates they decreed. Their emblems include the spindle (for spinning life's thread), scroll (recording destinies), and scales (weighing fates).

Magical Properties

The Moirae represent the ultimate power over destiny and the inescapable nature of fate. They control the pattern of each life from birth to death, weaving individual destinies into the greater tapestry of existence.

Ritual Uses

The Moirae are honored rather than petitioned, as their decrees cannot be changed. Work with their energy to understand and accept fate, to divine the patterns of destiny, and to recognize the interconnected threads of all lives.

Offerings

Thread and wool, spinning tools, offerings at crossroads, libations.

Favored People

Those seeking to understand fate and destiny, people working with life transitions, practitioners of divination and fate-work.

Petition For

Understanding of fate rather than changing it, acceptance of destiny, divination guidance, wisdom about life's patterns and the inevitability of endings.
