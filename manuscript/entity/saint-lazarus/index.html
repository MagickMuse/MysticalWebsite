Saint Lazarus is renowned as a master healer, specializing in deadly infectious diseases and epidemics, including AIDS.

This Saint Lazarus is not the Lazarus resurrected by Christ, but rather the leper mentioned in the Gospel of Luke. In Jesus's parable, Lazarus lay suffering on a rich man's doorstep, covered with suppurating sores, with only dogs to lick his wounds. The wealthy man showed him no mercy. When Lazarus died, he went straight to Heaven and the bosom of Abraham, while the rich man was sent to a realm of punishment.

From the bosom of Abraham, Saint Lazarus is positioned as a powerful intercessor. Though comparatively obscure in mainstream Catholicism, his image is ubiquitous in spiritual supply stores because he represents Babalu Ayé, one of the most beloved spirits in Santeria. Babalu Ayé has dominion over smallpox and decides who will recover. As smallpox has become rare, both Babalu Ayé and Saint Lazarus are now associated with AIDS. In Haitian Vodou, his image sometimes represents Papa Legba, the spirit of the crossroads.

The original Catholic saint was also significant as patron of the Order of the Hospital of Saint Lazarus, founded in the 12th century when Templar and Hospitaller knights contracted leprosy. Templar rules required knights with leprosy to transfer to this order. All knights were lepers who carried out military duties while nursing their compatriots. The Order maintained a hospital for lepers beside Jerusalem's northern wall.

Domains

Saint Lazarus is renowned as a master healer, specializing in deadly infectious diseases and epidemics. He is also a guardian of dogs.

Colors

Purple, Brown

Iconography

A frail, ragged, minimally dressed beggar leaning on a walking stick, accompanied by two dogs. The image sometimes resembles a particularly decrepit Tarot Hermit who has wandered into the Tarot Moon landscape.

Ritual

Use an inexpensive, anatomically appropriate milagro to request his aid with healing. If he helps, offer a more expensive milagro in thanks. For truly miraculous healings, tradition calls for life-size milagros formed of precious materials like gold.

Sacred Sites

The Shrine of Saint Lazarus in El Rincón, near Havana, Cuba, has been visited by leaders as diverse as Pope John Paul II and Fidel Castro.
