✨Saint Benedict's Healing Spell

Saint Benedict is renowned for healing animals and protecting against poison, kidney stones, and contagious illness.

Classification

Venerated as a saint by most branches of Christianity.

Life and History

Benedict and his twin sister Scholastica were born around 480 CE to a wealthy, noble family in Norcia, now in modern Italy. Their mother died giving birth to them. At an early age, Benedict traveled to Rome to pursue his studies. Disgusted with the religious quarrels he witnessed and the dissolute ways of his fellow students, he abandoned Rome and moved to a cave, later known as the Holy Cave, where he lived for three years. A monk brought him food and gave him a monk's habit to wear.

Paranormal Abilities

Benedict acquired a reputation as a holy man through his religious devotion and the paranormal abilities he manifested, including telepathy and clairvoyance. He was invited to lead a monastery, but his austerities proved too severe for the other monks, who attempted to poison him. When Benedict made the sign of the cross over the poisoned cup, it instantly shattered. A second assassination attempt was foiled when a crow snatched away the poisoned bread.

Founding the Benedictines

Benedict returned to his cave seeking solitude but was inundated with visitors seeking him as their mentor. Twelve small monasteries soon sprang up in the hills near his cave, each with twelve monks reporting to him. Sometime between 525 and 529, Benedict moved to Monte Cassino, then in the midst of a Pagan revival. Temples dedicated to Jupiter and Apollo stood on the hill. Benedict cut down the sacred groves and reconsecrated the temples to Saint Martin. He smashed the statue of Apollo and destroyed Apollo's altar at the summit, then built a chapel there and dedicated it to John the Baptist.

In 529, Benedict founded the Monastery of Monte Casino and the Benedictines, the monastic order bearing his name. He composed the Rule that became the foundation for all Western monastic orders, earning him recognition as the founder of Western monasticism. He achieved renown as an exorcist, able to drive away even the fiercest, most malevolent demons. Benedict died on 21 March 547 while engaged in prayer.

When Cardinal Joseph Ratzinger became pope in April 2005, he adopted the papal name Benedict XVI in honor of this saint, whom he described as "the great patriarch of Western monasticism."

Powers and Domains

Benedict is the patron saint of exorcism. He provides protection against poison, poisoners, and kidney stones. He is the patron of happy death and protects against witches, witchcraft, and malevolent spells. He is invoked for assistance by servants who have broken their employers' possessions and provides healing for animals.

Iconography

A bearded man wearing the cowl of the Benedictines holds the Benedictine rule or the rod of discipline.

Attributes

Broken cup, broken sieve, scourge, a cup with two snakes, pastoral staff, book, raven carrying bread in its beak.

The Saint Benedict Medal

The Saint Benedict medal, sometimes called an "anti-Satan" medal, allegedly wards off evil. This double-sided round medal traditionally depicts Saint Benedict holding a cross in his right hand and the book or scroll of his Rule in his left. His attributes, the shattered chalice and crow, also appear, along with Latin inscriptions meaning "The cross of our blessed Father Benedict" and "May we be strengthened by his presence at the hour of our death." Letters on the back deliver injunctions: "Get back, Satan!" and "The drink you offer is evil; drink that poison yourself."

The medal's precise origins remain unknown. It was formally approved by Pope Benedict XIV in 1741, but documentation of its existence dates to the 17th century. It may be based on a 1415 manuscript housed in the Bavarian Abbey of Metten featuring Saint Benedict holding a staff and scroll with the full words corresponding to the medal's initials.

The medal may be worn on a chain as a medallion, attached to rosary beads, or carried in a pocket or charm bag. It may also be placed in a car or buried in a building's foundation to ward off malevolent spirits. It reputedly protects against poison, contagious illness, and tempestuous weather. Brought into the delivery room, it provides for safe childbirth.

Sacred Day

Sacred Animal

Sacred Plants

Saint Benedict's herb, also known as herb bennet (Geum urbanum), credited with the power to ward off malevolent spirits and sometimes worn as an amulet. Also hemlock.

Constellation

Ophiuchus has been interpreted as depicting Benedict amid thornbushes.

Sacred Sites

  • The Abbey of Monte Casino in Italy where Benedict established his first monastery in approximately 529
  • Saint Benedict Abbey in Saint-Benoit-sur-Loire, France, where in 660 the body of Saint Benedict was moved to protect it from attacks on Monte Casino
