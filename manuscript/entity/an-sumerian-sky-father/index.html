An (Sumerian Sky Father)

An is the supreme creator god of the Mesopotamian Sumerian tradition, his name meaning sky. His worship spans roughly 3500 BC to 2000 BC and continues as the Babylonian creator god, called Anu in Akkadian, until 100 BC or later. His center of cult is Unug, modern Warka.

Sources

No definite art references survive, although An is probably represented symbolically on seals and seal impressions from the third millennium onward. The literary sources include cuneiform texts among them Sumerian creation accounts and the Babylonian epic Enuma Elis.

Cosmic Role

In Sumerian creation mythology An is the supreme being. With his chthonic female counterpart Ki he is the founder of the cosmos. Some texts identify him instead as the son of Ansar and Kisar. He stands at the head of the older generation of gods. He is believed to have formed the basis for the calendar.

Bovine Origins

An is arguably first represented in bovine form, a shape derived from the old herders' pantheon. Several texts name him the bull of heaven.

Separation of Heaven and Earth

Legend tells that heaven and earth were once inseparable until An and Ki bore a son, Enlil, god of the air, who cleaved heaven and earth in two. An carried away heaven. Ki, in company with Enlil, took the earth.

Other Pairings and Offspring

An is also paired with the goddess Nammu, by whom he fathered Enki. As patron god of Unug, called Erech in the Vetus Testamentum, An remains a remote shadowy figure. He occasionally lends a hand to tilt the balance of fate but otherwise tends to be out of touch with the day-to-day affairs of heaven and earth. His main sanctuary is the Eanna temple.

Decline

After the Semitic takeover of Sumer by Sargon the Great around 2500 BC, Enlil supersedes An as the supreme national god of the Sumerian city states.
