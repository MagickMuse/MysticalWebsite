While the Sun is frequently depicted as a male deity across many traditions, Amaterasu is a Sun Goddess, making her a notable exception and one of Japan's most important deities.

Source-Text Additions

Cult Center

Her principal sanctuary is the Ise Naiku shrine, visited by about five million devotees each year, and Amaterasu takes pride of place in every family shrine. Her shrines are sometimes placed adjacent to those of Susano-Wo. For many years her residence was maintained in the palace of the Emperor, but her presence there created political tension: the priestesses who served her could, by virtue of their proximity to the divine, challenge the Emperor's authority. This classic clash between sacred and secular power eventually led to her permanent installation at Ise. The shrine has been rebuilt many times, yet always as an exact replica of what came before, making it a living example of ancient architecture.

The shrine is home to flocks of roosters, the symbol of the Sun not only because they are among the first creatures to greet it at dawn but because of their rainbow-colored cockscomb.

Literary Sources

Her mythology is preserved in the Nihongi and Kojiki, the Japanese sacred texts. Her known period of worship runs from circa 600 CE or earlier until the present.

Lineage and Cosmic Origin

She is one of the daughters of the primordial god Izanagi and is said to be his favorite offspring, born from his left eye. She is the sibling of Susano-Wo, the storm god. According to mythology, she and Susano-Wo are obliged to join each other in order to survive. The birth of the two deities is considered to mark the transition between cosmic and material genesis.

The Cave Myth

Susano-Wo ascends with her to heaven but is thrown out after trying to enter her house and committing various excesses. Amaterasu refuses to be sullied and obstinately hides herself away in a cave. It requires the combined diplomacy and craft of many other deities, including Ama-Tsu-Mara, to persuade her to come out. The lure is the sacred octagonal mirror in which she sees her reflection. It is believed that the spirit of Amaterasu enters this mirror in order to communicate with humankind; the mirror itself is enshrined at Ise.

Position in the Pantheon

She is the central figure of Shintoism and the ancestral deity of the imperial house, also serving as the tutelary goddess of the emperor. Hers tends to be a monotheistic cult in which all other deities take a subservient place. Though powerful she does not always succeed and is often subject to attack.

Buddhist Identification

She has been arguably identified with the god Vairocana in Buddhist religion, the Japanese form of whom is Dainichi Nyorai.
