Venerated as an unofficial saint, Sarah is the subject of the largest annual Romani pilgrimage and has developed a following among goddess devotees and conspiracy theorists alike.

Life and Legend

Although called Saint Sarah, she is not a canonized saint. She is venerated alongside the Marys of the Sea, official saints with whom she shares an annual festival. In Roman Catholic belief, Sarah is subordinate to the two Saint Marys. For the Romani, however, Sarah is the holiest and most significant of the three, with the Marys subordinate to her. Although they share a shrine, Sarah's remains rest in the crypt rather than the church itself. For centuries, only the Romani were permitted to venerate Saint Sarah.

Her true identity remains bitterly debated:

  • An Egyptian servant who accompanied the three Marys (Magdalen, Jacobe, and Salome) to France
  • A Romani priestess who greeted them upon arrival in Provence
  • A priestess of Ishtar who welcomed the Marys of the Sea to France
  • The daughter of Mary Magdalen and Jesus Christ
  • A disguised form of continued veneration for the goddess Isis
  • The goddess Kali, who accompanied the Romani from India
  • The Romani goddess of fate

According to local legend, the Marys of the Sea escaped Judea in a small boat with no oars or sails. Their party may have included Mary Magdalen, Mary of Bethany, Lazarus, Martha, and Joseph of Arimathea. Sarah may have been aboard or met them upon landing. Those who consider Sarah the Divine Child, the sang real who is the true Holy Grail, believe smuggling her to safety was the journey's purpose.

King Rene of Provence (1409-1480) ordered excavations in the region. Remains discovered beneath a church in Ratis were identified as Mary Jacobe and Mary Salome; the town was renamed Les-Saintes-Maries-de-la-Mer. Additional remains found in a bronze chest were identified as Sarah. Major Romani pilgrimages began in the 15th century, coinciding with their migrations through western Europe.

Magical Properties

Saint Sarah may be petitioned for anything, but her specialties are healing and fertility. Touching her clothing or statue can lead to miracles. Pilgrims bring clothing from loved ones too sick to attend and touch these to Sarah's statue, transmitting healing long-distance. As the fabric absorbs Sarah's power, healing reaches any petitioner.

Iconography

Sarah's statue is carved from dark brown wood with long black hair, a crown, and powerfully expressive eyes, resembling a Black Madonna. Replicas are not readily available; a black queen chess piece may represent her on home altars. In Romani fortunetelling, the Justice tarot card indicates Sarah's presence, influence, or intervention.

Sacred Sites

Her shrine in Les-Saintes-Maries-de-la-Mer, France.

Festival

Romani from around the world converge on Les-Saintes-Maries-de-la-Mer annually during May. The pilgrimage dates are 24 and 25 May, though crowds gather earlier. During the festival, Sarah's statue is carried in procession to the sea, immersed and bathed, then returned to her shrine and dressed in finery.
