Korrigans

Corrigans

The Korrigans (also spelled Corrigans) allegedly did not begin existence as spirit beings; once upon a time they were Pagan Breton princesses who opposed Christianity and were transformed into fairies. Whether this transformation was punishment (the "official" story) or reward is subject to interpretation. Another explanation suggests that they were Druidesses, although theoretically there's no reason they couldn't be both.

Manifestations

As spirits, Korrigans now manifest in multiple forms:

  • Mermaids: Water-dwelling forms
  • Land-living fairies: Beautiful, long-haired women wearing flowing white garments
  • Fierce hags: Especially when encountered in daylight

Behavior and Habitat

The Korrigans are most frequently encountered in the forest or by streams. They travel in packs and like to have fun, dancing among stone circles and menhirs. They lead travelers astray with their beautiful voices and mysterious fairy torchlight. Allegedly, they especially enjoy playing tricks on priests.

Connection to Ys

The Korrigans helped Princess Dahut build the magical crystal city, Ys. This connection to one of Brittany's most famous legends demonstrates their power and involvement in great magical works.

Ritual Uses

Work with Korrigans for connection to ancient Breton magic and understanding of fairy realms. They possess knowledge of building magical structures and working with sacred stone sites. However, they are tricksters who must be approached with caution and respect.

Offerings

Respectful distance, offerings at stone circles and menhirs, acknowledgment of their mischievous nature and power.

Warnings

Korrigans are among those spirits accused of stealing human babies and leaving changelings behind. They are tricksters who especially enjoy misleading priests and Christians. Their beautiful voices and fairy lights can lead travelers hopelessly astray. Approach with appropriate caution and never underestimate their capacity for mischief.
