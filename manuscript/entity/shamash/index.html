The name Shamash means "sun," and it belongs to the Akkadian solar deity who was arguably the most important God in the pantheon. Shamash is described as the child of the Moon God, Nannar, also known as Sin. That the Moon should be seen as the parent of the Sun reflects the prominence of lunar reckoning in nomadic societies, where the constantly shifting phases of the Moon were far more informative about time and place than the steady regularity of the Sun. It was only once people had settled into a more agrarian way of life that the Sun rose to prominence.

Mythology and Imagery

Shamash makes his appearance each morning after the giant scorpion-men guarding the mountains of the East open the great folding door through which he enters. He then ascends to the mountaintop where Bunene, his coachman, waits with a chariot to carry him across the skies. He exits in the West through another pair of gates and descends into the depths of the Earth.

Shamash is most celebrated for justice, as well as for his courage and vigor. Just as the Sun brings physical light to the world, Shamash chases away the shadows of injustice and makes crime impossible by clarifying truth. He also had the power to dispel demons, which took the form of illness or misfortune, just as he dispelled the darkness. He was known by the epithet "Judge of the Heavens and the Earth" and often appears seated, always the posture of a very important God, holding a scepter and a ring.

Divination

Shamash was also a God of divination, capable of revealing future events through a soothsayer who would make sacrifices to him and then read the signs, whether by analyzing entrails or examining the patterns made by oil poured on water.

Religious Associations

  • The Hebrew word for sun is shemesh. The central candle of the Menorah, used to light all the others, is called the Shemesh candle.
