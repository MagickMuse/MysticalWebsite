The Lotus of Enlightenment

In Buddhist teaching the Lotus represents enlightenment. The plant grows in mud yet purifies the water around it while bringing forth great beauty and nourishment.

Magickal Properties

The Buddha represents the potential for enlightenment within all beings. As both historical figure and spiritual principle, he embodies the awakened mind that has overcome suffering and achieved perfect wisdom and compassion.

Ritual Uses

Practice meditation, follow the Noble Eightfold Path, and cultivate mindfulness rather than performing traditional petitioning rituals.

Warnings

Approach the Buddha with reverence and sustained practice rather than transactional petitions; do not substitute ethical practice or professional help with ritual requests.

Additional Notes

The Buddha is both a historical figure (Siddhartha Gautama) and a spiritual principle representing enlightened consciousness. In spiritual practice, connection comes through ethical conduct, meditation, and wisdom rather than offerings or petitions.

Encyclopedia Source Notes

The Buddha is treated in the received tradition as a historical figure of northern India. He was born at Kapilavastu near Gorakhpur and died at Kusinagara circa 486 BCE. His father was Suddhodana of the Sakya clan, his mother was Maya, and his wife was Yasodhara. His known period of worship extends from circa 500 BCE to the present day, with centers of cult pan-Asiatic in scope. He is preserved in metal and stone sculptures and in paintings across the whole Buddhist world.

In certain respects the Buddha is regarded as the equal of the Hindu god Vishnu, and in some Hindu accounts he is numbered among the avataras of Vishnu.

He is generally depicted with shaven or cropped head and may be crowned; the hair may also be shown tightly curled in the rows of small snail-curls that mark him in South and East Asian sculpture. His color attribute is gold.

By tradition, the Buddha preached his first sermon at Mrgadava in Sarnath near Varanasi. The site remains a major pilgrimage destination for Buddhists across Asia, and an enclosure of gazelles was later erected there in commemoration of the sermon.
