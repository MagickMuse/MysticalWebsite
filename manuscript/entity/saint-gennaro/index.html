Saint Gennaro is renowned for healing blood illnesses including leukemia, hemophilia, and AIDS. He is also invoked for safe blood transfusions.

Classification

Roman Catholic saint.

The Saint Who Never Says No

Saint Gennaro is considered an exceptionally responsive, generous saint, described as "the saint who never says no." Ask him for what you need.

Life and History

Saint Gennaro (died c. 305) was reputedly the bishop of Benevento, martyred in Pozzuoli near Naples. After he was beheaded, his family's slave Eusebia, once Gennaro's wet nurse, gathered two vials of his blood, which dried.

The Miracle of the Blood

When Gennaro's relics were eventually transferred to Naples, the blood spontaneously liquefied. This miracle, described as the blood "boiling," has repeated three times a year, on schedule, ever since:

  • 19 September (his feast day)
  • 16 December
  • First Saturday in May

The first historical reference to this miracle occurs in a document from 1389.

The Aunts of San Gennaro

The Aunts of San Gennaro are a women's hereditary religious sisterhood in Naples dedicated to the saint. Members claim descent from Eusebia, the woman who preserved his blood.

Guardian of Vesuvius

Saint Gennaro protects against volcanic eruptions, both literal and metaphoric. He allegedly keeps a watchful eye on Mount Vesuvius. The dates his blood boils correspond to anniversaries when he successfully soothed the volcano.

Years when his blood fails to liquefy coincide with volcanic eruptions. Disaster and misfortune are anticipated in these years. Shortly after the blood failed to liquefy in 1980, almost 3000 people were killed in an earthquake in nearby Irpinia.

Powers and Domains

  • Patron saint of blood banks
  • Invoked for illnesses associated with blood (leukemia, hemophilia, AIDS)
  • Invoked by those needing transfusions
  • Protection against the Evil Eye

Color

Red.

Sacred Sites

  • Cathedral of Naples: Houses his relics, including a vial of blood. Reliquaries are exposed for public veneration three times annually.
  • Chapel of the Treasure of San Gennaro: Within the cathedral, built as an ex-voto for rescuing the city from plague. One of the greatest examples of Neapolitan Baroque.
  • Museum of San Gennaro's Treasury: Houses precious ex-voto gifts amassed over five centuries (opened 2004).
  • Parish church in Pozzuoli: Possesses the stone on which he was beheaded. Bloodstains reputedly liquefy simultaneously with the Naples vial.
  • Most Precious Blood Church, Mulberry Street, New York City: Over one million attend the annual Feast of San Gennaro, New York's oldest and largest religious festival. Legend says women who participate improve their marital prospects.

Feast Day

19 September
