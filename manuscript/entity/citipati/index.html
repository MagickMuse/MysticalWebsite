Magickal Properties

The Citipati were two ascetics who meditated so deeply they were unaware of being beheaded by robbers. After death, they emerged as wrathful deities invoked as fierce but benevolent protectors. They are companions of Yama, Lord of Death, representing the eternal dance of death and embodying undying life-force.

Ritual Uses

Post their image for protection and to drive off evil spirits. The Citipati represent the transformation of death into protective power. Their ritual dance is commemorated twice annually in Tibet, celebrating the continuity of life through death.

Offerings

Images of the dancing skeletons for protective purposes, offerings that honor both death and life energies, items acknowledging the erotic and protective aspects of death spirits.

Favored People

Those seeking fierce protection from evil spirits, tantric practitioners working with death energies, people needing to understand the transformative power of death.

Petition For

Protection from evil spirits and malevolent forces, understanding of death as transformation rather than ending, fertility and life-force despite death's presence, courage in facing mortality.

Warnings

They are wrathful deities who provide fierce protection. Their erotic nature as lords of sex and death should be understood within tantric context. Approach with respect for their protective but powerful energy.

Additional Notes

Iconography shows two grinning, dancing skeletons with intertwined bodies dancing ecstatically. They wear headdresses of five jeweled skulls with heads resembling a large skull topped by tiny ones. They are erotic spirits like the Barons, embodying the connection between sexuality and death in tantric understanding.
