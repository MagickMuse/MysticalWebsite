Korravai is an ancient South Indian goddess of war, victory, and the hunt, counted among the earliest and most widely worshipped deities of the Dravidian peoples.

Overview

Korravai is a fierce, bloodthirsty, and wild goddess of southern India. She is thought to have been one of the earliest and most widely worshipped deities of the ancient Dravidian peoples.

Goddess of War and the Hunt

She was above all a goddess of the semi-nomadic hunting tribes of South India, who called on her for success in cattle raids and appeased her with bloody sacrifices. In the old Tamil heroic poetry she appears with little emphasis on fertility, standing instead as a goddess of war and victory.

Connection to Chinnamasta

Because of her fierce, wild, and bloodthirsty character, Korravai has been suggested as another possible forerunner of Chinnamasta, and she is close in both name and nature to the battlefield goddess Kotavi. The resemblance is real but limited: Korravai is a warrior goddess who receives blood sacrifices, while Chinnamasta is not a warrior and instead gives her own blood to her devotees rather than demanding theirs.

In Magick

Korravai is called on for victory, courage in conflict, and success in bold or risky undertakings such as the hunt or the raid. Her energy is fierce and martial, suited to workings for strength, triumph over rivals, and the will to prevail.
