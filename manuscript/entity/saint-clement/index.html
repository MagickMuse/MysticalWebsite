Classification

Venerated as a saint by most branches of Christianity.

Life and Legend

Clement (died c. 99 CE) was an early pope, possibly the second or third after Saint Peter. Banished from Rome by authorities, he was sent to labor in a stone quarry. Finding his fellow prisoners suffering terrible thirst, Clement prayed, then drove his pickaxe into the ground. Sweet water gushed forth.

He made many converts and performed numerous miracles before being martyred: authorities threw him into the Black Sea with an anchor tied around his neck.

The Historical Clement

The actual Clement was likely a Roman nobleman who converted to Christianity early on. However, legends describe him as the first blacksmith, the first to refine iron ore, or the first to shoe a horse. These claims are obviously false historically, but scholars believe they indicate syncretism with Wayland the Smith, a mysterious spirit once venerated throughout Germanic, Nordic, and British lands.

The Clem Feast

Clement's feast day was the traditional festival of smiths, featuring torch-lit processions. The saint was represented by an effigy or a smith chosen to portray him. Anvils in smithies were ceremonially fired by exploding gunpowder on them.

"Clementing" meant going house to house begging food, drinks, and money in exchange for songs, similar to wassailing or Halloween trick-or-treating.

Iconography

Portrayed as a pope holding an anchor, sometimes standing beside springs.

Attribute

Anchor, or the Anchored Cross (also called Saint Clement's Cross or the Mariner's Cross).

Botanical

Convex wood sorrel (Oxalis convexula).

Sacred Sites

  • Bones said to be Clement's are housed in Rome's Basilica of Saint Clement
  • His head is reputedly in the Monastery of the Caves, Kiev
  • The Church of Saint Clement Danes in Westminster, London (designed by Christopher Wren, 1682), serves as the Central Church of Britain's Royal Air Force

Altar

Home altars for Saint Clement may be constructed on an anvil.

Feast Days

  • 23 November (Western Church)
  • 24 or 25 November (Eastern Churches)
  • 8 December (Coptic Church)
