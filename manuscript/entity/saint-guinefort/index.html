Saint Guinefort is renowned for performing healing miracles, especially for children.

Classification

Unofficial saint.

The Question

Must a saint be human, or can a dog be a saint? Saint Guinefort has inspired passionate debate since the 13th century.

The Legend

A baby in France was left in the care of the family greyhound while his parents ran errands. A snake (possibly Satan in disguise) menaced the baby. Guinefort killed the snake, splashing the baby with snake blood.

When the parents arrived home, they saw their bloody baby and blood on the dog's mouth. They jumped to conclusions. Their first impulse was to punish the dog; they threw him down a well, where he died.

Only then did they examine their baby, who was unharmed and perfectly fine. The snake cadaver was discovered. Guilt-stricken, they drew the dog from the well and gave him a proper burial. A shrine was created by planting a grove around the well.

Veneration

Local peasants perceived that the noble guardian dog had been unjustly killed, essentially martyred like a saint. They began venerating him, calling him Saint Guinefort and visiting his grave. The loyal dog began performing miracles. Devotion spread throughout France.

Hidden Spirit?

This legend may camouflage a pre-Christian spirit associated with a healing well. Alternatively, it may derive from the ancient tradition of venerating sacred hounds, once prevalent in western Europe and Britain.

Church Suppression

The cult was controversial from the start. The Church actively discouraged it, to no avail. Shortly before 1261, Inquisitor Étienne de Bourbon disinterred Saint Guinefort, burned his skeleton, and cut down the grove.

But the trees grew back and pilgrimages continued, as did miracles. As centuries passed, people assumed Guinefort was officially canonized. (Not so. The Vatican has never canonized him and has actively attempted to suppress his veneration.)

Powers and Domains

Guinefort is invoked for miracles of healing and to guard children.

In Film

His legend is featured in the 1987 French movie The Sorceress.

Sacred Sites

Guinefort's grove was visited until 1940. Ruins of a shrine dedicated to him are found in Trévron, Brittany.

Feast Day

22 August
