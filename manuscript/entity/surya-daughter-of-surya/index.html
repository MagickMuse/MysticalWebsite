Surya, the daughter of the sun god of the same name (sometimes named instead as Savitr), is a minor goddess of the Hindu Vedic tradition. The hymns of the Rg-veda preserve very little of her independent character; the bulk of what survives is contained in the tradition of her marriage.

The wedding story

All of the gods desire the young goddess, but her father intends to give her to Soma. The matter is settled by a contest: the first to reach the sun will take her as a bride. The twin gods, the Asvins, win the race and the bride. After their victory the hymns describe them as having attained everything they wished.

Other passages complicate the picture. One verse gives her to the god Pusan, and elsewhere Soma is said to court her. Most other references in the Rg-veda picture her riding in the chariot of her twin husbands, the Asvins.

In the marriage hymn

The long marriage hymn of the Rg-veda offers an unusually dense and somewhat obscure portrait of the goddess. In it she appears to be likened to the sacrifice itself and is said to pervade the cosmos. Her husband in this hymn is Soma, and the marriage may be read as a metaphor for the interdependence of Soma and the sacrifice, the latter personified in the figure of Surya. The Asvins, who elsewhere are her husbands, appear here in the unusual role of groomsmen on behalf of her father.

Disambiguation

This page concerns the daughter of the sun god, sometimes called the bride or the daughter of the sun. She should not be confused with the male solar deity Surya, who is her father in the standard genealogy.

Cited hymns

Key Rg-veda references: 6.58.4 (given to Pusan), 8.8.10 (the Asvins attain all after winning her), 10.85 (the long marriage hymn), 10.85.9 (wooed by Soma).
