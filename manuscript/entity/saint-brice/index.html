Saint Brice is renowned for healing and preventing stomach disorders.

Classification

Roman Catholic saint.

Life and History

Born around 370 CE, Brice was a French orphan raised as protégé of Martin of Tours. Rather than showing gratitude and obedience, he proved wild and defiant. His behavior was so obstinate that some suspected demonic possession. Advisors urged Martin to cast him out, but the saint replied that if Jesus could tolerate Judas, he could tolerate Brice.

Struggles for the Bishopric

Brice entered the priesthood and was designated Martin's successor as Bishop of Tours. However, when Martin died in 397, the local populace rebelled, selecting a different bishop and violently driving Brice from town. He traveled elsewhere but faced repeated accusations of immorality, though always acquitted.

When Martin's successor died, Brice tried to claim the position but was again expelled by a mob. The third vacancy proved different: in 437, he returned to find the people had mellowed and his reputation improved. They welcomed him, and he served as bishop until dying of natural causes in 444.

Powers and Domains

Brice heals and prevents stomach ailments. Those facing false accusations may also seek his intercession, as he was repeatedly vindicated.

Iconography

Depicted in the bishop's vestments he waited so long to legitimately wear.

Botanical
