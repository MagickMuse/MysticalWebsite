Elcmar is a minor local deity of the Irish Celtic tradition, remembered in the mythological cycle for his marriage to Boann and for the deception through which the Dagda cuckolded him to father Angus mac Og. In some traditions Elcmar, rather than Nechtan, is named as the husband of Boann and the keeper of the Sidhe of the Boyne. The two traditions coexist, and older Irish texts move between them without reconciliation.

The Cuckolding of Elcmar

The central story preserved of Elcmar tells how the Dagda desired Boann, Elcmar's wife, and sent Elcmar away from the Sidhe on an errand that was made by magick to occupy a single day in the ordinary reckoning of time. Under this enchantment the sun was held still, so that Elcmar's absence lasted only from morning to evening as far as he could tell, while for the Dagda and Boann the time stretched to nine full months. In that hidden interval Angus mac Og was conceived, carried, and born, and when Elcmar returned no time was seen to have passed, and he suspected nothing.

Later Fate

When Angus mac Og came of age he was instructed to ask Elcmar for the loan of the Sidhe of the Boyne for a day and a night. Elcmar granted the request, but under the older Irish idiom of a day and a night, meaning without any bound of time, and Angus kept the Sidhe as his own, dispossessing Elcmar of the mound. In this way Elcmar's role in the mythology is set: the wronged husband whose home passes into the keeping of the son not born to him.

Relation to Nechtan

Separate strands of the tradition give the same role to Nechtan, keeper of the Well of Wisdom. Elcmar and Nechtan appear as parallel names for Boann's husband in different sources, and the two figures may reflect distinct local memories preserved side by side in the surviving corpus.
