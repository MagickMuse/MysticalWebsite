Saint Mark is renowned for healing ailments of the ears. He also protects against sudden death.

Venerated as a saint by most branches of Christianity, Mark was an evangelist, Gospel author, and the first bishop of Alexandria.

Life and Ministry

Saint Mark died 25 April 68 CE. Born to a Jewish Levite family in Cyrene (modern Libya), he may have been the cousin of Saint Barnabas. Mark traveled with Barnabas and Saints Peter and Paul during the formative years of the Christian Church.

Mark served as Peter's secretary and is credited as the author of the oldest official Gospel, which he began writing while in Rome with Peter. Some believe the Gospel of Mark is based on Peter's memoirs.

Alexandria

After Peter's execution, Mark fled to Alexandria with both Peter's writings and his own. He established the first official church there, though the city was already a volatile center of Gnostic traditions. Mark died during violence between Christians and Pagans.

The Secret Gospel

Mark allegedly wrote a second, secret gospel that he entrusted to the church in Alexandria. In 195 CE, Clement, bishop of Alexandria, described this secret gospel as being read only to those initiated into the great mysteries. Clement complained that the Carpocratians, Gnostics perceived as heretical, had obtained a copy. The whereabouts of this gospel and Clement's letter remain unknown.

Transfer to Venice

To encourage Christian veneration, the feast day of the Alexandrian deity Serapis and many functions associated with him were reassigned to Mark. In 828, Venetian merchants took Mark's body from Egypt, then under Muslim rule. Mark became patron of Venice while remaining patron of Egypt. A relic was returned to the Coptic Church in 1968.

Domains

Mark heals and causes ailments of the ears. He protects against sudden, unexpected death and liberates prisoners and slaves.

Iconography

Pen and book. His emblem is the winged lion.

Sacred Animals

Pigeon and lion.

Botanical

Clarimond tulip.

Sacred Sites

Saint Mark's Basilica and Saint Mark's Square in Venice. Saint Mark's Basilica in Rome. Saint Mark's Coptic Cathedral in Cairo, the seat of the Coptic pope. Saint Mark's-in-the-Bowery in New York City, the oldest site of continuous worship in the city.

Feast Day

25 April.

Lore

The name marzipan may derive from Marci panis, meaning Mark's bread.
