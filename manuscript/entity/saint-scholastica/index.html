Saint Scholastica (c. 480 to 10 February 547) is venerated by most branches of Christianity. She is the twin sister of Saint Benedict, and their mother died giving birth to them.

Life and History

Scholastica (c. 480-543 CE) was the twin sister of Saint Benedict of Nursia. Born into a noble family in Norcia, Italy, she dedicated herself to religious life from an early age.

Founding of Convent

Scholastica established a convent at Plombariola, about five miles from her brother's monastery at Monte Cassino. She led a community of nuns following the Rule that her brother had written.

Annual Meetings

The siblings would meet once a year at a house midway between their two communities to discuss spiritual matters. Their deep bond reflected their shared devotion to God.

The Miraculous Storm

The most famous story about Scholastica concerns their final meeting. When Benedict rose to leave, Scholastica asked him to stay longer so they could continue their spiritual conversation. When he refused, she bowed her head in prayer. Immediately, a violent storm arose, making it impossible for Benedict to travel. He exclaimed, "God forgive you, sister! What have you done?" She replied, "I asked you and you would not listen, so I asked my Lord and He did listen." Three days later, Scholastica died. Benedict saw her soul ascending to heaven in the form of a dove.

Feast Day

  • February 10

Domains

Scholastica is invoked for protection from storms and rain. Her famous miracle demonstrates that divine love is stronger than human rules.

Iconography

Scholastica is portrayed in a nun's habit. Sometimes a white dove flies from her mouth, representing her soul's ascent to heaven. She carries a crozier and crucifix as her attributes.

Sacred Sites

Scholastica and Benedict rest together for eternity beneath the altar of the church at Montecassino.

Lore

Three days after the miraculous storm, Benedict had a vision of a dove flying toward Heaven and realized his sister had died. He sent for her body and had it placed in the grave he had intended for himself. Shortly after, Benedict received a vision of his own death and had his tomb reopened and ready six days before he died.
