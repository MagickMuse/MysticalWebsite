Lucifer Calaritanus (died 20 May circa 370), the bishop of Cagliari, Sardinia, is venerated as a saint in Sardinia and wherever Sardinians have immigrated. His status as a saint is subject to dispute, and not just because of his name.

Lucifer was a fiery and entrenched opponent of Arianism, an early branch of Christianity that disagreed with the Vatican on the interpretation of the Trinity and the true nature of Jesus Christ. He was an argumentative, uncompromising man, and he may have been excommunicated. Saint Jerome condemned his followers, known as the Luciferians.

Perhaps coincidentally, Jerome is responsible for translating the Hebrew words Helel ben Shachar ("Shining One, Son of the Morning") from Isaiah 14:12 as "Lucifer," beginning the association of that name with Satan.

On the Name

Lucifer may be translated as "Light Bringer" and is now most associated with the fallen angel. In Saint Lucifer's time, however, it was a popular first name among the general Pagan populace, associated with various deities, such as Diana Lucifera, the childbirth-assisting aspect of Diana who encourages babies to emerge from the darkness of the womb into the light.

There is disagreement as to whether the Lucifer venerated as a saint in Cagliari is really this historical Bishop Lucifer or possibly another man with the same name, perhaps an early martyr.

Sacred Sites

  • The Church of Saint Lucifer in Cagliari
  • A chapel dedicated to him in Cagliari's cathedral reputedly contains his tomb
