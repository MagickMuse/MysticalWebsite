Cihuacoatl

Magickal Properties

  • Aztec female warrior spirits, souls of women who died in childbirth
  • Considered equivalent to dying valiantly in battle
  • Given glorious role of escorting the sun on its downward passage through the sky
  • When not busy with celestial chores, they haunt crossroads
  • Bodies of women who died in childbirth were considered very valuable; soldiers fought over their remains as talismans for courage and victory

Ritual Uses

  • Invoke for warrior courage and strength
  • Petition for escort of the sun during its downward passage
  • Call upon for protection at crossroads
  • Seek their warrior energy and fierce protection

Offerings

Stone sculptures and shrines at crossroads

Favored People

Souls of women who died in childbirth, warriors who died valiantly in battle

Petition For

Escort for the sun's downward passage, warrior courage and strength, protection at crossroads, fierce protective energy

Warnings

They are accused of stealing children, seducing and harming gullible men, and causing seizures and madness at crossroads. Approach with respect for their warrior nature and the tragedy of their deaths.

Additional Notes

Aztec stone sculptures portray them as kneeling women with skeletal faces and taloned hands. Shrines for the Cihuateteo are placed at crossroads.

The Aztec afterlife was fairly dismal for most dead souls, but the Cihuateteo were given the glorious role of escorting the sun, honoring their death as warriors who fell in the battle of childbirth.
