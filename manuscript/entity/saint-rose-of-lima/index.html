Saint Rose of Lima (20 April 1586 to 30 August 1617) was the first person born in the Americas to be canonized as a Roman Catholic saint. Born Isabel Flores de Oliva in Lima, Peru, she was nicknamed Rose as a baby because of her beauty, a trait that would become her lifelong spiritual burden.

Domains

Rose is invoked for virtually everything, but her specialty is healing. She protects against earthquakes, having claimed to save Lima from devastation through her prayers. Those ridiculed for spiritual devotions find particular kinship with her.

Iconography

Rose is depicted with an anchor and a crown of roses. The rose crown conceals the silver crown with ninety-nine spike thorns that she wore in imitation of Christ's suffering.

Attributes

Rose received visions of Jesus as a child, who instructed her to consecrate all her love to him. When seeking guidance about her spiritual path, a black-and-white butterfly visited her daily, which she interpreted as a sign to join the Third Order of Dominican nuns rather than enter a convent.

She modeled herself on Catherine of Siena and practiced extreme austerities for fourteen years: a silver crown with ninety-nine thorns hidden beneath roses, a hair shirt, an iron chain around her waist, gloves stuffed with stinging nettles, prolonged fasting, self-flagellation three times daily, and sleeping on a bed of broken glass, thorns, potsherds, and stones.

Colors

Pink

Sacred Sites

Rose is enshrined in the Church of Santo Domingo, Lima. Her adobe hut and surrounding garden in her family's property is now a shrine. Rose's well, where she dropped the key from her cilice, remains a site where pilgrims throw flowers and notes.

Ritual

On 30 April, pilgrims cast letters detailing their petitions to Rose into the well where she dropped the key from her cilice.

Lore

Rose rejected many offers of marriage, wishing only to be the bride of Christ. When her mother gave her a wreath of flowers to wear, Rose fastened it by driving a pin so deeply into her head that they had trouble removing it. Told her hair was beautiful, she chopped it off. Told her face was beautiful, she disfigured it with pepper and lye.

Rose experienced intense visionary experiences and spiritual ecstasy, though she also suffered periods of extreme self-doubt. Her last years were spent in virtually constant ecstasy. In 1614, as the Feast of Saint Bartholomew approached, Rose joyfully announced she would die on Saint Bart's Day, which she did at age thirty-one.

Miracles attributed to Rose include teaching mosquitoes to buzz hymns instead of humming, tossing roses into the air that formed a floating cross, and saving Lima from earthquakes through prayer. Crowds lined the streets for her funeral procession and immediately began invoking her intercession.
