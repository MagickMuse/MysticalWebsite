Magickal Properties

Agni literally means "fire," and the name is etymologically related to "ignite." He is fire and the spirit of fire simultaneously. When you gaze at a flame, you gaze at Agni. He was critically important with over 200 hymns in the Rig Veda dedicated to him. He is an equal-opportunity spirit who loves all people regardless of wealth, power, or influence.

Ritual Uses

Agni serves as mediator between people and other spirits, accepting and delegating burned offerings. An offering that fails to ignite has been rejected by Agni. He protects people both before and after life and serves as a psychopomp, guiding souls to other realms.

Offerings

Ghee (clarified butter) - place a cotton wick in ghee and burn it like a candle. Any offering must be burned for Agni to accept and transmit it to other spirits.

Favored People

Everyone regardless of wealth or status, those seeking to enhance virility, lovers seeking passion, and anyone who maintains a hearth or cooking fire. He lives in any home with a hearth, no matter how humble.

Petition For

Protection for fetuses from danger, immortality, cleansing of sins at death, guidance to other realms, enhancement of erotic fire and virility, and mediation with other spirits.

Warnings

Fire deity with whom people have daily contact. Equal-opportunity spirit who loves all people. An offering that fails to ignite has been rejected by Agni. Handle fire safely when working with his energy.

Additional Notes

He manifests as fire and lightning, or as a three-legged, two-headed man with seven tongues of flame, red faces, black eyes and wild hair, with butter smeared on his shining face. His consort is Svaha, a solar deity. His sacred animals include parrots, rams, and horses. His attributes include flaming spear, torch, axe, and prayer beads.

Lineage

According to one tradition Agni is the son of Kasyapa and Aditi, and according to another he is the son of Dyaus and Prthivi. His consort is Svaha, and according to some texts he is the father of the god Skanda.

Identification with Siva

In his destructive capacity Agni is seen as an aspect of the god Siva. The same fire that lights the offering can also burn the world, and that wider face of his nature is read as part of Siva's domain.

Dikpala of the Southeast

Agni is also a guardian or dikpala of the southeastern quarter. As one of the directional guardians he holds the southeast in the great compass of the Hindu cosmos.

Born in Wood

In ancient hymns Agni is said to have been born in wood as the embryo life force of all trees and plants. He emerges when wood is rubbed together, the spark of fire-by-friction read as the moment of his appearance in the world.

Vehicle and Form

His vehicles are a she-goat, or a chariot drawn by red horses or parrots. His color is red. He is sometimes depicted with seven arms and the head of a goat, carrying a wide variety of objects.

Period of Worship

Agni's known period of worship runs from circa 1500 BCE onward, and he is still recognized in present Hindu practice.
