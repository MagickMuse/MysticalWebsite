Magickal Properties

  • Roman Goddess of War and Conquest; rallies armies and sanctifies foreign campaigns
  • Embodies victorious ferocity and the fire of battle

Ritual Uses

  • Petition for disciplined courage, strategic ferocity, and protection of troops
  • Convene war councils and oaths of service under her sight

Offerings

  • Torch and flame, scourge and whip tokens, clean steel; vows kept after victory

Favored People

Soldiers and those who must fight

Petition For

Courage, victory, protection in battle, and righteous ferocity when required

Warnings

Call her with respect and clear necessity. She is beautiful but deadly; do not invoke for cruelty.

Additional Notes

  • Feasts and councils for foreign wars were held in her Capitoline temple
  • Attributes: Scourge/whip, torch; sacred plant: belladonna

Mother Goddess Aspect

Beyond her martial character, Bellona also carries a mother goddess role inherited through her syncretism with the Cappadocian mother goddess Ma. The composite Ma-Bellona unites the fierce protector of Rome with the great mother of central Anatolia, expanding her domain to include the ecstatic and generative aspects of the feminine divine.

Historical Context

The first known temple dedicated to Ma-Bellona by the Romans is dated to 296 BCE. Bellona was attended by Asiatic priests who performed frenzied dances and ritually cut themselves with swords, offering the blood on the goddess's altars. Because of the intensity of these rites, Rome refused to officially recognize the cult until the third century CE, though private and unofficial devotion persisted throughout the intervening centuries.
