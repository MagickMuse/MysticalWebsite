Saint Bona of Pisa is the patron saint of adventurers, travelers, couriers, tour guides, and pilgrimage leaders.

Saint Bona of Pisa is renowned for healing headaches and migraines.

Classification

Roman Catholic saint.

Early Life

Bona (1156-1207) showed mystical tendencies from childhood. Once, while contemplating the crucifix in Pisa's Church of the Holy Sepulcher, she witnessed Christ turn to look at her and extend his hand. She also received a defining vision of Saint James the Greater, who became the focus of her lifelong devotion.

Born out of wedlock, Bona lived with her mother in Pisa until age ten, when she moved alone into a tiny cell beside the Church of San Martin. For three years she occupied herself with prayer and knitting, fasting on minimal bread and water three days each week.

Rejection and Captivity

Bona's father, a prominent Crusader, had powerful legitimate sons: one was Patriarch of Jerusalem, another Master of the Templars, and the third a Knight Hospitaller. When Bona's mother sent her to Palestine to meet her father, he dispatched soldiers to intercept her, ashamed of his illegitimate daughter.

Bona and her escorts hid with a hermit before attempting to return to Pisa. Saracen pirates captured their ship. Bona spent years in an African prison, nearly dying before being ransomed at age eighteen.

The Pilgrim Guide

Back in her cell, Bona soon embarked on a new calling: leading pilgrimages to Santiago de Compostela under the auspices of the Knights of Saint John. Despite perpetually fragile health, she guided nine groups along the arduous route, earning renown as a holy woman whose touch could heal.

Her final journey proved too much. Collapsing shortly after departure, she was carried home to Pisa, where she died. The Church canonized her in 1962.

Powers and Domains

Bona heals headaches and migraines. She especially favors those leading spiritual tours and anyone journeying to Compostela.

Attribute

The scallop shell of Saint James.

Sacred Site

The Church of San Martino in Pisa houses her relics.

Feast Day

29 May
