Ben Temalion is a spirit or demon from Jewish tradition, not a saint but deeply intertwined with saintly legends.

Overview

Ben Temalion occupies an unusual position in Jewish spiritual tradition. Though classified as a demon, his most famous story portrays him as helpful rather than harmful. In traditional Jewish angelology, some angels may be evil while some demons may occasionally be beneficial.

The Talmudic Legend

The story of Ben Temalion is found in the Talmud. Simon bar Yohai, the renowned first-century spiritual leader, traveled to Rome with a delegation of Jewish leaders hoping to obtain revocation of edicts harmful to Jews. Upon arriving in Rome, they found all official doors of authority closed to them and despaired of accomplishing their mission.

The demon Ben Temalion then appeared, offering his services. He proposed to possess a girl belonging to the emperor's family and remain there, resisting all other exorcism attempts, until Simon Bar Yohai was summoned to perform the exorcism.

Simon objected, convinced the plan would fail. He was unknown in Rome, belonged to a disfavored religion, and the city had many skilled exorcists. Why would anyone summon him?

Ben Temalion promised to make the possessed girl herself call out for Simon Bar Yohai. He also promised that when Simon whispered his name into the girl's ear, the demon would immediately depart, shattering all the glass in the palace as proof of his departure.

The Talmud records that the sages did not wish to accept this demon's services but could think of no other effective way to obtain the revocation. Ben Temalion proved honorable. Everything unfolded exactly as he suggested. Following the successful exorcism, Simon was offered any reward he wished from the imperial treasury. He declined all wealth, instead requesting only the harmful edicts, which he promptly destroyed.

Implications for Exorcism

The story of Ben Temalion confirms suspicions held by some throughout history that exorcists may sometimes be in collusion with the demons they expel, that exorcism can be a cooperative effort between practitioner and spirit.

A similar story is told about Saint Bartholomew in India, though Bartholomew refused any reward and ended up tricking and destroying his demon rather than working cooperatively with it.

Connection to Saint Bartholomew

The similarity between Ben Temalion's legend and that of Saint Bartholomew may not be coincidental. The name Bartholomew derives from the Hebrew Bar Tolomei. Bar means "son," and while the meaning of Tolomei remains unknown, it is similar to Temalion. The Ben in the demon's name is not a shortened form of Benjamin but the Hebrew word for "son."

Saint Bartholomew is among those saints who do not behave in consistently conventional manner and display magical aspects. Some perceive that the demon Ben Temalion may masquerade under certain guises of the saint.

Later Identification

An alternative theory suggests that following the Diaspora, Ben Temalion immigrated to France, where French Jews identified him as a lutin, a helpful household spirit.

Manifestation

Ben Temalion is described as resembling a small, mischievous boy.
