Magickal Properties

Coyolxauhqui is the Aztec goddess of the moon, her name referring to the golden bells adorning her face. Her myth represents the conflict between older lunar-based spiritual traditions and the rising solar religion of the Aztecs.

Ritual Uses

Work with Coyolxauhqui for understanding lunar cycles and connecting to older spiritual traditions. She is especially present when the moon is red-hued and full, and is associated with the Milky Way. Her energy can be invoked for moon magick and honoring ancient lunar wisdom.

Offerings

Aztec marigolds (Tagetes spp.), moonflowers (Ipomoeia alba and Datura inoxia), gold and copper items, offerings made during red full moons when her presence is strongest.

Favored People

Those working with lunar cycles and moon magick, people seeking to honor older spiritual traditions that predate solar religions, practitioners connecting to ancient lunar wisdom.

Petition For

Understanding of lunar cycles and their spiritual significance, connection to ancient lunar-based traditions, guidance in moon magick and nighttime spiritual work, honoring the wisdom that comes before dawn.

Warnings

Her story involves violent family conflict and dismemberment. She represents traditions that were violently suppressed by newer religious and political systems. Approach with understanding of her role as a displaced but persistent lunar power.

Additional Notes

Her destruction indicates the rise of solar religion and Aztec political force in the region. Four hundred star brothers accompany her severed head through the sky (representing the multitude of stars). On February 21, 1978, her massive stone image was discovered during construction in Mexico City, showing her with severed head and limbs. She is sometimes depicted weeping and may be represented solely by a dead, severed head.

The Myth

Upon hearing that their mother was pregnant, Coyolxauhqui led her brothers, the four hundred gods of the southern stars, in a rebellion against her. Before they could succeed, their brother Huitzilopochtli burst forth from their mother fully grown and fully armed, immediately beheading Coyolxauhqui and defeating the star brothers. The moon is understood to be her severed head rolling through the sky each night. Though dismembered, her power persists.
