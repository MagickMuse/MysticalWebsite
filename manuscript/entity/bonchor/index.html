Bonchor is a tutelary god of the pre-Islamic Berber tradition of Tunisia, in North Africa. He is preserved in the surviving record as a local guardian and, in the reading most often given him, as a creator figure whose office lay at the origin of the world and its ordered life. The record is thin, and his character must be assembled from scattered epigraphic and later ethnographic references rather than from any sustained mythological narrative.

Domains

Tutelary guardianship of the Berber communities of Tunisia, and, in the most widely accepted reading, the creator office through which the world and its peoples were first brought into being.

Historical Context

Bonchor's cult belongs to the religious life of the Berber peoples of North Africa before the arrival of Islam in the region. Like many pre-Islamic Berber deities his figure survives chiefly through inscriptions, later Roman and Punic-period records, and traces preserved within Berber oral tradition. The identification of him as a creator god is a scholarly reading drawn from the character of his cult rather than from a preserved cosmogonic narrative in his name.

Precautions

Bonchor belongs to a Berber tradition that continues in living form among the Amazigh peoples of North Africa. Approach his figure with the awareness that his religious context belongs to a specific cultural inheritance and that the surviving record of his cult is fragmentary.
