Lunar Deities

To our earliest ancestors, the moon was a god. The moon defined the passage of time as it changed phases throughout the lunar month, lighting the nighttime sky and then disappearing. Despite the shift from lunar- to solar-dominant worship with the start of more permanent settlements, many moon deities are still honored and worshipped to this day. While many associate the moon with the perception of feminine energy, moon deities have appeared across the gender spectrum throughout human history and in some cases, individual moon gods have changed gender over time.

The variations on mythology surrounding the moon are truly endless. Some ancient cultures adapted and adopted moon deities from other societies, while others worshipped different deities for varying aspects of the moon. Some moon gods were considered personifications of the moon itself, others were deities of the moon, and still more were simply associated with it.

Below you will find descriptions of some of the lunar deities commonly referenced in modern witchcraft. The full list of moon deities one can connect with is far longer, exemplifying the endless potential of the moon's power.

Lunar Deities in the Entity Grimoire

  • Arianrhode: Celtic/Welsh, silver wheel goddess of naming and fate
  • Artemis: Greek, mistress of wild nature, moon goddess and twin of Apollo
  • Chang'O: Chinese, lunar goddess who ascended to the moon
  • Chandra: Hindu, deity of the moon and timekeeping
  • Coyolxauhqui: Aztec, moon goddess whose severed head rolls through the sky
  • Kwan Yin: East Asian, bodhisattva of compassion often depicted with moon
  • Luna: Roman, spirit of the moon
  • Mama Quilla: Incan, Mother Moon, defender of women and marriage
  • Mani: Norse, personification of the moon
  • Nanna: Sumerian/Mesopotamian, ancient moon god and keeper of time
  • Selene: Greek, ancient moon goddess and matron of magickians
  • Hekate: Greek, goddess of witchcraft and the dark/waning moon

The Triple Goddess

Trinities have long been considered sacred across cultures and traditions. Many deities embody divine triplicity: the Tridevi, the Morrígan, the three Fates, and the Christian trinity among them. The Triple Goddess as understood in modern Neopaganism and Wicca is not a single deity but an archetype: a symbolic framework associated with the moon's phases and the many expressions of feminine divine power.

The Triple Goddess is represented by the triple moon symbol, a full moon flanked by two crescents, and is broadly associated with the waxing, full, and waning or dark moon. Some practitioners associate the archetype with the trio of Artemis, Selene, and Hekate.

For a time, the three phases were mapped onto perceived stages of a woman's life: maiden (waxing), mother (full), and crone (waning/dark). Many modern witches have found these labels limiting. The archetype can hold much more:

  • The underworld, the earth, and the heavens
  • The three selves: id, ego, and superego
  • The three souls of witchcraft
  • The three phases of life: youth, adulthood, and old age

The meaning you bring to it is the meaning it holds. Choose whichever representation resonates.

⚠️
A note on Hekate: Hekate is called a triple goddess because of her dominion over the sky, land, and underworld, not because she represents the maiden/mother/crone framework. Her triple nature is one of cosmic realm and liminal power, not a life-stage archetype. See her page for full context.
