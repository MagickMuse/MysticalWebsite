Dracula is the archetypal vampire of the Gothic tradition, a noble of the Carpathian mountains who survives by draining the life of the living. He is named here only as the figure against whom the garlic wreath, the sealed window, and the rubbed threshold are deployed.

Classification

Legendary figure of the Romanian and wider Eastern European Gothic tradition. Stands within the broader category of the undead. See Vampires.

Warning

This figure is never petitioned, never invoked, never offered to. He is the test case for whether your wards hold. Any working involving Dracula is protective, banishing, or instructional.

Nature of the Figure

A pale predator with a courtly bearing. Travels by night. Crosses water by uneasy passage. Recoils from garlic, from sunlight, from the rooster's crow, from the sacred sign. Capable of glamour, of slow seduction, of the long siege against a single sleeper.

Method of Attack

Approaches at the window. Returns night after night. Drains slowly, leaving small twin wounds at the throat and a deepening pallor in the victim. The attack is gradual. The victim grows fond of the visitor before understanding what has been lost.

Counter-Method

The ward is garlic at every opening. Sash, jamb, fireplace, and a wreath at the throat of the threatened sleeper. The window must not be opened. The wreath must not be removed. Silence and obedience are part of the rite. See Vampires for the broader catalogue of folkloric countermeasures.

Iconography

A tall, pale man in dark formal dress, sometimes shown as a wolf, a bat, or a column of mist. The mouth shows fangs in moments of feeding.

Lore

The story preserved in the Gothic tradition is of a young woman attended at her family home by physicians who do not understand what is happening to her. A learned Dutch professor arrives with garlic from a friend's glasshouses, seals her room, and weaves the wreath. For as long as the ward holds, she sleeps in peace. When the ward fails, she does not.
