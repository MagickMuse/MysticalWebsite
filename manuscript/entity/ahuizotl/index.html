⚠️ The Ahuizotl is rooted in Aztec and Mesoamerican Indigenous traditions. These are closed practices and should only be approached by those within the originating culture or with explicit cultural permission.

Magickal Properties

The Ahuizotl resembles a cross between a monkey and dog with pointy ears and a hand on the end of its tail. It is a guardian spirit of freshwater fish but has developed a taste for human flesh. It lives at the bottom of deep lakes and pools of freshwater, using trickery to lure victims.

Ritual Uses

The Ahuizotl cannot be worked with safely. It is a malevolent guardian spirit. Those seeking protection from it should work with other, more benevolent water spirits rather than attempting to appease the Ahuizotl directly.

Offerings

None, this spirit cannot be propitiated and any attempt to do so would be dangerous. Instead, make protective offerings to benevolent water spirits.

Favored People

None, it is antagonistic to fishing people and dangerous to all humans. It has become malevolent toward humans despite its original role as fish guardian.

Petition For

None, this spirit is to be avoided entirely. For protection from water dangers, petition other water spirits or protective deities.

Warnings

Lives at bottom of freshwater lakes/pools. Cries like baby to lure victims, then drowns them using the hand on its tail. Corpses surface missing eyes, teeth, and nails. Guardian of freshwater fish but enemy of fishers and killer of anyone who approaches.

Additional Notes

Some theorize it may be a cryptid (undiscovered animal), perhaps a type of killer otter. An Aztec ruler who reigned before Moctezuma II was named Ahuizotl, suggesting this creature may have been his nahual (sacred soul animal). It can come onto land but doesn't travel far from water's edge.
