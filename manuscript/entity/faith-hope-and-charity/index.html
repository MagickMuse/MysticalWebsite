Faith, Hope, and Charity are saints venerated by most branches of Christianity and syncretized in Haitian Vodou.

Life and Martyrdom

Faith, Hope, and Charity were three Christian sisters, named for virtues, who were martyred during Emperor Hadrian's reign in the second century. Faith was twelve, Hope was ten, and Charity (sometimes called Love) was nine. According to legend, they were arrested for proselytizing, then tortured and killed one by one.

The Martyrdoms

Faith was placed in a cauldron of boiling tar. Suddenly, a rose tree emerged from the tar. Flame-colored roses instantly bloomed and petals fell, completely filling the cauldron. Faith was removed and beheaded.

Hope was thrown onto a bed of burning coals, but remained unharmed as the coals transformed into a field of lilies. She too was beheaded.

Charity (or Love) was stuffed inside a hot stove and left to die. When her tormentors opened the door expecting a charred body, they caught a glimpse of a beautiful paradise garden. Charity emerged singing, and her head was also cut off.

The sisters' heads were placed on display.

Historical Questions

No historical evidence of these sisters exists. Their mother is called Saint Sophia (Blessed Wisdom), so some suspect their legend was originally allegorical. However, others argue that early Christians frequently took virtue names as baptismal names.

Vodou Syncretism

In Haitian Vodou, Faith, Hope, and Charity are called the Three Twins and syncretized to the Marassa, the sibling spirits.

Powers and Domains

Saints Faith, Hope, and Charity are invoked for healing and for all matters pertaining to children.

Iconography

Chromolithographs depict the girls' heads and shoulders floating amid clouds. They are also posed in formal family portraits with their mother, who shares their feast day.

Attributes

A cross, anchor, and heart symbolize each sister respectively.

Feast Days

  • 1 August (Western Church)
  • 17 September (Eastern Church)
