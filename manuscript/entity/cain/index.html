Magickal Properties

Cain is the biblical figure cursed by God to a life of permanent wandering after killing his brother Abel. The Bible describes God placing a magickally protective mark on Cain to prevent others from killing him during his wandering, though no description of this mark is provided. Many traditions believe this protective mark was a set of horns.

According to legend, Cain was eventually slain by his blind descendant Lamech, whose son Tubal-Cain saw Cain from afar and mistook him for a horned animal, advising Lamech to shoot an arrow that met its mark. Tubal-Cain is identified in the Bible as Earth's first metalworker, establishing Cain as the ancestor of smiths.

In medieval Europe, Cain was among those believed to lead the Wild Hunt, sometimes called Cain's Hunt. Because he was identified as the ancestor of smiths, he was simultaneously associated with witchcraft, shamanism, and sorcery.

Ritual Uses

Cain may be invoked in metalworking traditions and shamanic practices. His association with the Wild Hunt connects him to liminal spaces and eternal wandering. Work with his energy for understanding exile, protection during wandering, and the magickal arts of smithcraft.

Offerings

No traditional offerings are recorded in the source material.

Favored People

Metalworkers, smiths, those connected to shamanic and sorcerous traditions, wanderers.

Petition For

Protection during exile or wandering, metalworking arts, shamanic wisdom, sorcerous knowledge.

Warnings

Multiple contradictory legends exist about Cain's parentage. A Jewish legend suggests Cain was Lilith's son by Adam, not Eve's son. A Christian legend suggests Cain was Eve's son but his father was Samael (the old snake), equated in Christian folklore with Satan.

Additional Notes

Cain's mark of horns connects him to other horned deities and spirits. His role as ancestor of metalworkers links smithcraft to magickal and shamanic traditions. The Wild Hunt association places him among liminal spirits who traverse between worlds.
