🌕Khonsu's Banishing Spell

Khonsu is an ancient Egyptian god of the moon, time, and healing. His name means 'traveler,' referring to the moon's nightly journey across the sky. He rules the passage of time and the human life span, weaving the moon's rhythms into the very cycles of life.

His appearance shifted throughout Egyptian history. He is most often depicted as a child mummy or with the head of a falcon (similar to Horus), and commonly shown wearing a crescent moon cradling a full moon disc on his head. Earlier traditions gave him a more fearsome, cannibalistic aspect. He was later established as the son of Amun and Mut, forming the Theban Triad with them in the role of divine child, though his parentage varies across sources.

Khonsu is associated with baboons and shares correspondences with Thoth. He governs lunar cycles and is particularly known for his healing powers and ability to drive away evil spirits.

Ritual Uses

Invoke Khonsu for healing work, especially for serious or chronic illnesses. Petition him for safe travel at night, for marking and honoring the passage of time, and for lunar magick. His energy is especially strong during the waxing crescent moon, when he was most often petitioned to help women and livestock conceive. His power also supports safe pregnancy and childbirth.

Warnings

Khonsu can be both benevolent healer and dangerous enforcer. In some myths, he is depicted as bloodthirsty. Approach with respect and clear intent, especially when requesting healing. Use moonlit timing and purification when invoking him. Do not perform careless night rites or try to wield lunar power without proper ritual boundaries.
