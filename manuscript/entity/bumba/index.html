Bumba is the creator god of the Boshongo, a Bantu people of the central African region historically settled along the Kasai river system. He stands at the head of Boshongo cosmogony as the primordial being from whom the world, the sky-lights, and the living kinds all issued forth, and he belongs to the wider family of Bantu creator figures whose acts precede and shape the world of humankind.

Domains

Creation, cosmogony, the ordering of chaos into world, the origin of the heavenly bodies, and the origin of every living kind, including humanity.

Iconography and Attributes

Bumba is envisaged as an immense male being of the primordial period, alone with the dark and formless waters of the beginning. His body is understood as the source-substance of the world; from within him the elements of the cosmos and its creatures issue outward. He is not associated with a fixed cult image in the same manner as later, more localised powers, but is remembered above all through the story of the emergence.

Lore

At the beginning there was only Bumba and the primordial dark water, and no world of any kind. Bumba then suffered a great pain in the belly, and out of that pain he vomited forth the sun, whose light dried a portion of the waters and revealed dry land. He vomited forth the moon, and after the moon the stars, and then he vomited forth the first living kinds: the leopard, the eagle, the crocodile, the small fish, the tortoise, the white heron, the black-and-white kite, the beetle, the goat, and, at the last, the first human beings. From this line of first-born creatures every kind that walks, swims, flies, or crawls upon the earth is descended. The story places Bumba as a solitary and originating source rather than a maker who works upon pre-existing matter, and the world stands, in Boshongo memory, as the emitted substance of his own body.
