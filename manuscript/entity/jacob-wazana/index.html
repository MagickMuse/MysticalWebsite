Rabbi Jacob Wazana (c. 1902 to 1952), perhaps the most extraordinary, mysterious, and unconventional Jewish saint, is the great-grandson of Rabbi Abraham Wazana. He is renowned for healing infertility, impotence, chronic miscarriage, and spirit-caused illnesses, and also heals ailing children and breaks hexes and curses. A renowned shaman, scholar, and miracle worker, he healed Muslims and Jews alike, located stolen goods for French and Arab authorities, and served governors and sheikhs, sometimes willingly but often conscripted into service.

Born in Assaraz in Morocco's western High Atlas Mountains, Jacob Wazana studied with Jewish Kabbalists and local Muslim healers. He apprenticed with the famed magicians of the Souss, a region renowned for its wonder workers and occult traditions.

The Spirit Marriage

Wazana's incredible knowledge and power allegedly stemmed not from human sources but from his sacred marriage to a spirit identified as a demon, djinn, or afarit (a particularly fearsome type of djinn). According to some accounts, his bride was an afarit of the sea. They had several spirit children.

Wherever he traveled, Wazana insisted on a private room so his spirit family could join him. He was overheard talking to spirits all night. This sacred marriage gave him miraculous powers of healing and exorcism, but may also have prevented human marriage and damaged his reputation. Lifelong bachelors are viewed with suspicion in traditional Jewish communities. His associations with Muslim sorcerers and forthright practice of magick lent him an air of disrepute that lingers over fifty years after his death.

Powers and Methods

As a djinn-master, the djinn cooperated with Wazana, helping him heal and perform exorcisms. He allegedly made lost objects materialize out of thin air. He possessed a magic mirror to locate missing persons. He brought those hovering near death back to life. Like many shamanic healers, he was adept at both sleight of hand and genuine magick.

Appearance

No photographs of Jacob Wazana survive; they all vanished after his death. Those who knew him describe a tall, handsome, jovial man who was scrupulously clean. He dressed distinctively in white, except for his signature red tarboosh (similar to a fez) covered with amulets. He sometimes wore a black cloak.

Death

At age fifty, Wazana died eight days after performing an exorcism the djinn had expressly forbidden. A sheikh's daughter had accidentally killed some baby snakes that were really baby djinn. The enraged djinn parents attacked her. Because their babies died, the djinn demanded the girl's life. The sheikh demanded Wazana heal his daughter. The djinn warned that if he did, he would die in her place. If he refused the sheikh's summons, he risked arrest and danger to his family.

Wazana defied the djinn and performed the exorcism alone, without their cooperation. His last days were spent in tremendous pain, visibly fearful. Just before death, he gave three blood-curdling shrieks. He was found on the floor with blood spurting from his throat. Local belief holds the expelled djinn entered Wazana's body instead.

After his death, neighbors raided his room. All his belongings vanished: his books, magic mirror, amulets, staff, hat, and the copper ring he used to command djinn. Only his mother's treasured robe was returned after he appeared in a looter's dream, threatening grievous injury if she failed to surrender it.

Continuing Miracles

Rabbi Jacob Wazana continues to appear in dreams and work wonders. He heals infertility, impotence, and chronic miscarriage. He protects those anticipating difficult childbirths and heals ailing children. He heals spirit-caused illnesses and breaks hexes, curses, and spells. He identifies thieves and locates lost and stolen objects.

Sacred Site

His grave in the cemetery at Agouim, Morocco, not far from Rabbi David U-Moshe, to whom he was devoted. However, when his biographer visited in 1993, the cemetery was ruined and his grave could not be identified.

Feast Day

27th of Tammuz
