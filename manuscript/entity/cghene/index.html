Also known as

Oghene, Oghenekaro, The High God of the Isoko

Overview

Cghene is the creator god of the Isoko people of the Niger Delta region of southern Nigeria in West Africa. He is an abstract being, conceived as the supreme and original power behind the created world, but not approached directly in worship. Instead of receiving worship through temples, priests, or direct petitions, Cghene is present in the world through a mediator in the form of the Oyise, a sacred wooden totem through which the community relates to the distant and abstract power of the creator. The god has no temples and no priests, a pattern characteristic of some West African high-god traditions, in which the supreme creator is considered too remote or too abstract to receive ordinary cult.

Classification

Creator god of the Isoko people, Niger Delta, southern Nigeria. Abstract supreme being, present in the world through the Oyise mediator totem.

Domains

The original creative power behind the world, the absolute and abstract divine from which the world has come, the mediated relationship between the community and the supreme being through the sacred totem, the distant sovereignty of the creator who does not interfere in daily life but whose power underlies it.

Iconography

Cghene himself has no iconography, being an abstract being without fixed form or image. His presence in the community is embodied by the Oyise, the sacred wooden totem that stands as his mediator and that is the physical focus of what religious engagement with Cghene there is.

Attributes

The Oyise, the sacred wooden totem. No other attributes are assigned to the god himself.

Sacred Sites

Cghene has no temples. The Oyise totem is the nearest thing to a sacred site that his worship possesses, and the community's relationship with the divine at this level is held through that object rather than through any built structure.

Ritual

Cghene is not approached directly. There are no priests whose office is specifically to serve Cghene, and no temples dedicated to him. The pattern of this high-god tradition is one in which the supreme creator is too remote to receive ordinary cult, and the practical religious life of the Isoko community is conducted through the lesser spirits and through the mediation of the Oyise rather than through direct address to the creator.

Lore

The pattern represented by Cghene, in which the supreme creator god is abstract, remote, and without direct cult, is one of the recurring forms of West African high-god religion. Across the Niger Delta and the wider West African region, many peoples honor a supreme being understood as the original creator and source of all existence but not involved in the daily life of the community in the way that the spirit powers, the ancestors, and the lesser gods are involved. In the Isoko tradition this remote creator is Cghene, and the community's religious practice maintains its connection to him through the Oyise mediator, preserving the relationship with the creator without requiring the impossible intimacy of direct petitioning of an abstract cosmic being.
