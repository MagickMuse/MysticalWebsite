Saint Zita (1218-27 April 1272) was an Italian domestic servant whose extraordinary piety and miraculous experiences while serving one family for forty-eight years led to her veneration as patron of household workers.nn## DomainsnnDomestic service, finding lost objects (especially keys), baking, protection of servants, angelic intervention in daily tasks. Petition Saint Zita to heal household troubles, recover lost items, and protect those in service.nn## Life and HistorynnBorn near Lucca, Italy, Zita was a devout child who sought only to serve God. Her family's poverty sent her into domestic service at age twelve. Hired by a wealthy family, she saw her position as an opportunity for charitable deeds rather than personal advancement.nnZita constantly gave away food and clothing to anyone needy who came to the door. As word spread, many came seeking her generosity. She frustrated her employers immensely, once giving away the master's good coat to a beggar. Yet the very first miracle associated with her was that she kept her job, remaining with the same family until her death.nnHer employers recognized her holiness and perhaps saw retaining her as their own act of charity. Angels reportedly baked bread for her when her duties overwhelmed her charitable work. When a fellow employee attempted to rape her, she fought him off successfully. His visible scratches led their employer to question them both. Zita told the truth, and he promoted her to head housekeeper with care of the family children.nn## IconographynnA woman in servant's dress giving bread to a beggar. Often shown with keys or surrounded by loaves of bread.nn## Sacred SitesnnThe Basilica of San Frediano in Lucca contains a shrine dedicated to Saint Zita, where her mummified body is displayed for public veneration.nn## RitualnnIt is traditional to commemorate Zita's feast by baking loaves of bread.nn### Finding Lost KeysnnNo elaborate offerings or complex rituals are required. Simply chant her rhyme:nnZita, Zita, I've lost my key!nPlease look around and find it for me!nn## LorennConsidered a saint while alive, Zita was venerated as an unofficial saint from the day she died, though official canonization waited until 1696. Over 150 miracles are attributed to her intercession."
