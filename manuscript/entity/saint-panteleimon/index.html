Saint Panteleimon is renowned for healing boils and soothing colicky babies.

Saint Panteleimon ranks among Christianity's most powerful healer saints, recognized across both Eastern and Western traditions as a master of miraculous cures. He is known by the epithets "The All Merciful" and "The Wonder Worker."

Life and History

Born around 275 CE in Nicomedia, Pantaleimon grew up between two worlds. His mother practiced Christianity while his father followed traditional Roman religion. He pursued medicine and achieved considerable success, eventually treating patients from the highest social circles, including Emperor Maximian himself.

His medical practice evolved to incorporate spiritual healing rooted in his Christian faith. This openness proved dangerous during an era of religious persecution. Authorities pressured him to make public offerings to Rome's official deities. His refusal constituted treason under Roman law, leading to his arrest.

His followers later modified his birth name from Pantaleon to Pantaleimon, creating a meaningful wordplay. The altered name translates to "all compassionate," reflecting his character. Eastern Orthodox communities typically use Pantaleimon, while Western churches often retain Pantaleon.

Lore

His execution became an extended ordeal as each method failed to kill him. Torturers stretched him on a rack, burned him with candles, submerged him in boiling lead, threw him into flames, and cast him to starving beasts. He survived everything. Roman officials, unable to explain his endurance, concluded he practiced forbidden magic.

They bound him to a boulder and hurled both into the river. The stone floated, carrying him safely to shore. Finally, his persecutors tied him to an olive tree and severed his head. Witnesses claimed milk rather than blood poured from the wound.

Constantinople soon built a church in his honor. His remains now rest in locations across Europe, including Cologne and several Italian cities. Churches in Rome, Naples, and Ravelli preserve vials of his blood that reportedly turn liquid each year on his feast day.

Though he forgave his executioners before death, his posthumous nature carries an edge. Those who offend him may find themselves covered in painful boils. Conversely, he heals those same afflictions for his faithful.

Domains

Panteleimon answers prayers for miraculous intervention of all kinds. Parents with fussy infants seek his help calming their children. Gamblers consider him their patron, and he reportedly delivers winning lottery numbers through dreams. His refusal to charge patients during his lifetime earned him membership among the Silverless Physicians, and he also belongs to the Fourteen Holy Helpers.

Iconography

Depictions show him carrying medical implements or a physician's bag, marking his healing vocation.

Sacred Sites

Churches dedicated to him include Saint Pantaleimon Monastery on Mount Athos in Greece, Saint Pantaleimon in Saint Petersburg, San Pantalon in Venice, and San Panteleone in Rome.
