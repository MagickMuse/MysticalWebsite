Also known as

Chandarupa

Overview

Candarupa is a goddess of the Hindu tradition, a distinct form of Durga and one of the nine navadurgas, the nine forms of Durga worshipped through the nine nights of Navaratri. Her name means fierce form, from canda meaning violent or burning and rupa meaning form or appearance, so that her name announces her as the embodied fierce shape of the great goddess. She belongs to the wider family of goddesses whose names sound the canda root, including Canda, Candanayika, Candavati, Candogra, and the compound name Camunda, all of them expressions of the wrathful edge of Durga.

Classification

Goddess of the Hindu Sakta tradition. Distinct form of Durga, one of the navadurgas.

Domains

The fierce embodied form of the great goddess, the ninefold turning of Durga through the nights of Navaratri, the wrathful shape by which the goddess is recognized among her nine navadurga aspects.

Iconography

Depicted in the manner of the fierce forms of Durga, multi-armed and richly armed, mounted upon the lion of Durga, adorned with the ornaments of the wrathful goddesses. Her image belongs to the navadurga cycle rather than to a separate independent iconography, and she is generally shown as the standard fierce Durga figure with the emblems of her office.

Attributes

Sword, trident, bow, arrow, discus, mace, noose, and severed head carried across her multiple arms, together with the emblems of Durga's sovereignty.

Sacred Sites

Durga temples across India that observe the navadurga cycle during the nine nights of Navaratri, particularly in Bengal, Odisha, and the northern Indian regions where the Navaratri observance is strongest.

Ritual

Invoked as one of the nine navadurgas during Navaratri, taking her turn on the nightly worship. She receives offerings appropriate to the fierce forms of Durga: red flowers, red cloth, and the recitation of the Devi Mahatmya and related Sakta hymns.

Lore

Candarupa belongs to the extended family of Durga forms whose names carry the canda root, and her particular note within that family is the naming of her as the fierce form itself. Where Canda is the violent one, Candanayika is the mistress of the fierce, and Candogra is the fierce and terrible, Candarupa is the fierce as embodied shape, the sensible appearance of the wrathful goddess. Her worship in the navadurga cycle brings that embodied fierce form forward on her allotted night of Navaratri, one of the nine faces through which Durga turns across the festival.
