Aksobhya, "the imperturbable," is the second dhyanibuddha or meditation buddha of Buddhist tradition originating in India. He is one of five mystic spiritual counterparts of a human buddha in Vajrayana Buddhism, a product of the Adibuddha who represents the branch of the cosmos concerned with consciousness.

Origins and Realm

He originates from the blue mantra HUM and lives in the eastern paradise Abhirati. His Sakti is Locana, and he is normally accompanied by two elephants.

Iconography

Color: blue. Attributes include bell, three monkish robes, and staff, also jewel, lotus, prayer wheel, and sword.

Lamaism

Aksobhya may also be a tutelary deity in Lamaism (Tibet), in which case his attributes are similar.

Emanations and Companions

Emanations include Heruka, Manjusri, Vajrapani, and a large number of minor names. See also Amitabha, Amoghasiddhi, Ratnasambhava, and Vairocana.

Period and Sources

His known period of worship runs from circa 500 BC to the present, with pan-Asiatic centers of cult. Art references include metal and stone sculptures and paintings. Literary sources include the Sadhanamala and Tantric ritual texts.
