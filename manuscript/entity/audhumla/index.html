Audhumla is the primordial cow of Nordic cosmogony, one of the earliest living beings to arise within the misty void of Ginnungagap. According to the account preserved by Snorri Sturluson in the Prose Edda, Audhumla came into being alongside the primal giant Ymir at the meeting of the fires of Muspellsheimr and the rime of Niflheimr. From her four udders flowed four great rivers of milk, and by this milk she nourished the giant Ymir, making possible the survival of the first living body in the created cosmos.

The Salt Licker

Audhumla fed herself by licking the salty blocks of primordial ice that filled the void. Over the course of three days her tongue drew a shape out of the ice: on the first day the hair of a head, on the second day the head itself, and on the third day a complete being. The being she released from the ice was Buri, the ancestor of the gods. From Buri came Bor, and from Bor came the Aesir gods Odin, Vili, and Ve.

Domains

The primordial nourishment out of which the first living beings drew their sustenance, the maternal cow-power that sustains life at the beginning of things, and the slow shaping work by which a hidden being is drawn out of the substance of the world.

Iconography

Audhumla is depicted as a great cow of vast proportions, her udders flowing with rivers of milk. In some medieval and later illustrations she is shown licking a figure out of the ice while Ymir sleeps at her side.
