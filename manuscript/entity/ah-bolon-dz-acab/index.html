Ah Bolon Dz'acab

◇Culture
MayanMayaMexican
◇Gender
Male
◇Type
Deity
≡Also called
Many Generations, God K

Ah Bolon Dz'acab is a chthonic fertility god of the classical Mesoamerican Mayan tradition of Mexico. His name is given the meaning many generations, marking him as a deity whose work runs across long stretches of time and across the renewing cycles of the land.

Rain, Thunder, and Young Crops

Ah Bolon Dz'acab is identified with rain and thunder, and he is strongly linked with agriculture and young crops. His domain is the early life of the field, the moment when seed becomes shoot under the wet weather he brings down from the sky.

Possible Avatara of Itzam Na

He is possibly a vegetation avatara of the iguana god Itzam Na. Read in this way, Ah Bolon Dz'acab is the green-growing face of the older iguana deity, the form that Itzam Na takes when the land turns toward agriculture and the young crops in the milpa.

Attributes

His attributes include a leaflike ornament worn in the nose, a piece of regalia that signals his fertility and vegetation aspect. He is also known in the Mayan codices as God K.

⚠️ Closed Practice

Ah Bolon Dz'acab belongs to the Mayan religious tradition of Mexico, a closed practice rooted in specific cultural and initiatory context. Approach this entry as reference only. Do not adapt or perform Mayan rites outside of the lineages and communities to which they belong.
