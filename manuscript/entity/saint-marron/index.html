Saint Marron is an unofficial saint venerated in New Orleans, with Marie Laveau counted among his devotees. His name carries layered meaning: 'marron' means brown or chestnut in French, while its similarity to the Spanish 'cimarron' (wild one) connects him to the Maroon communities of escaped slaves who established free settlements in harsh terrain throughout the Caribbean and Louisiana swamplands.

Saint Marron may be understood as the patron saint of runaways. He embodies defiance of unjust laws and remains relevant long after the abolition of slavery. Petitioners invoke him when they believe laws are unfair, whether or not others agree with that assessment.

Domains

Marron is identified with Saint Raymond Nonnatus, the canonized patron of slaves. His spiritual jurisdiction extends to all who wish to remain hidden from authority or evade what they perceive as unjust persecution.

Iconography

No official iconography exists for Saint Marron. Devotees use images of Saint Raymond Nonnatus as a stand-in, typically sprinkling them with Holy Water before placement in locations where the law's reach must be averted.

Colors

Brown.

Sacred Sites

Saint Raymond's Chapel in the Central City section of New Orleans served devotees until its demolition in September 2008, following flood damage from Hurricane Katrina.
