Magickal Properties

  • Germanic goddess of abundance from Bavaria, allegedly returns whatever you give her many times over
  • Rules transit area for souls, caring for and guarding those who died as babies until they reincarnate
  • Was immensely popular goddess but post-Christianity aggressively demonized as Queen of Witches
  • Among leaders of Wild Hunt, usually leading parade of unbaptized babies

Ritual Uses

  • Petition to soothe and remove destructive ghosts, escorting them to her realm where they'll be happier
  • Request abundance and protection, lay offerings on Epiphany Eve
  • Honor during entire Yule season for blessings of prosperity

Offerings

Traditional Yule Offerings: Hearty meal of herring and dumplings; schnapps or other alcoholic beverages left on rooftops during Yule

Twelfth Night (Epiphany Eve): Pancakes must be left for her on Twelfth Night (January 5th) or dire consequences are threatened

Favored People

Those grieving lost babies, mothers, children, souls without proper burial or people to remember them

Petition For

Abundance returned many-fold, removal of troubling ghosts, protection of children, proper escort of restless souls

Warnings

Church demonized her as bogeywoman to make children behave. People told to baptize babies or they'd end up in her realm not Heaven. She evolved from beloved goddess to threat figure.

Additional Notes

  • Manifestations: Beautiful woman with pearls in gold hair, white veil, silk dress OR old decrepit hag with wild grey hair
  • Attributes: Keys to happiness and mayflowers (as beauty) or distaff (as hag)
  • Sacred time: Entire Yule season, Berchtentag (Epiphany, January 6th)
  • Realm: Subterranean palace with fabulous garden for children's souls
  • Sacred creatures: Crickets, swans, geese; may be original Mother Goose

Multiple Paths of Perchta

Perchta possesses various distinct paths or manifestations:

Butzenbercht: She comes bearing gifts to deserving households.

Spinn Stubenfrau (Spinning Room Woman): She visits homes at night to oversee domestic spinning work.

Stomach Slasher: Inflicts severe punishment on women who do not leave her traditional Yule offerings of pancakes, dumplings, and herring. She rips open their stomachs, removes what she wants, and roughly sews them up again.

The Perchten and Wild Hunt

Perchta travels with a retinue of spirits known as the Perchten. According to Christian legend, the devil rides in their midst, although this may merely indicate the existence of a male deity who once accompanied her. Modern Perchten processions are characterized by grotesque masks.

Spinning Goddess of Twelfth Night

Perchta is intimately connected to spinning and textile work. As Spinn Stubenfrau (Spinning Room Woman), she visits homes at night to oversee domestic spinning. Twelfth Night is her sacred day, when women are supposed to stop working in her honor.

According to one legend, a hard-working woman kept working on Twelfth Night. Perchta looked in the window, saw her, gave her two gold bobbins and told her to fill them within the hour. The woman did the best she could, then threw the bobbins into a running stream in order to reach Perchta. This tale demonstrates both Perchta's connection to spinning and her demand for proper observance of rest days sacred to spinning goddesses.

Sacred Time

With Christianity's arrival, she became the personification of the night preceding Epiphany (January 6th). In German tradition, this is known as Perchtennacht. Epiphany is also called Perchtentag, Perchta's Day.

Modern Devotion

Vestiges of devotion to Perchta survive. In some Alpine villages it is still customary to place offerings of food for her on rooftops during the Yule season.
