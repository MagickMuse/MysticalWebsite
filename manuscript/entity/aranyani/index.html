Aranyani is a minor goddess of woodlands in the Hindu Vedic tradition. She possibly evolved from a primitive animistic guardian spirit of animals.

Character

Aranyani is an elusive, rarely seen deity who is recognized in the sounds of the trees, particularly at dusk. She is a benign figure, sweet-scented and unwilling to destroy unless severely provoked.

She avoids the villages and is more often heard than seen, speaking through the rustle of the forest itself or through what the hymn describes as the faint sound of her tinkling bells. Her presence is felt most strongly in the evening, and those who spend the night in the forest sometimes report hearing her scream. She is named as the mother of all things that grow and live in the woods, and she is said to provide ample food without the labor of cultivation.

Archaic resonances

Two strands of older imagery surface in this single hymn. The figure of a forest goddess who shelters and feeds the creatures of her domain recalls the very old type known as the mistress of animals, although the Rg-veda does not specifically show her guarding the herds or providing them for human hunters. She also closely resembles the Yaksis of later Indian tradition, the female spirits of the forest who are worshiped away from the village and who keep an uncanny edge despite their generally benign nature. This late hymn of the Rg-veda may stand as one of the earliest literary references to a Yaksi, or at least to a goddess modeled on the indigenous spirits of the Indian forests.

Cited hymns

Key Rg-veda reference: 10.146 (the single hymn dedicated to Aranyani).
