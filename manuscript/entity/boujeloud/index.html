Magickal Properties

Moroccan horned male fertility spirit celebrated in annual festival in Jajouka village in foothills of Morocco's Rif Mountains. Festival similar to ancient Roman Lupercalia. Spirit manifests through ritual dancer sewn naked into skin of freshly slaughtered goat. Original source of music for renowned Master Musicians of Jajouka. Like Pan, serves as music teacher and fertility spirit.

Ritual Uses

Annual festival where spirit's dancer, sewn into goat skin, flails women with oleander switches. Women struck by switches anticipate pregnancy within the year. Festival combines fertility magick with musical tradition, honoring Boujeloud as both fertility bringer and music teacher.

Offerings

No specific traditional offerings recorded. Festival itself serves as primary form of honoring the spirit.

Favored People

Women seeking pregnancy, musicians and music students, those honoring traditional Moroccan fertility practices, practitioners interested in connections between music and fertility magick.

Petition For

Fertility and pregnancy, musical instruction and inspiration, creative vitality, connection to ancient fertility traditions.

Warnings

Festival involves intense physical ritual with oleander switches. Oleander is toxic plant. Approach with respect for traditional Moroccan cultural practices.

Additional Notes

The Jajouka Festival

Annual festival occurs in Jajouka village in foothills of Morocco's Rif Mountains, continuing ancient fertility traditions similar to Roman Lupercalia. Central ritual involves dancer sewn naked into skin of freshly slaughtered goat, embodying Boujeloud. Dancer flails women with oleander switches, conveying fertility blessing. Women struck anticipate pregnancy within year.

Music Teacher Spirit

According to Master Musicians of Jajouka, Boujeloud was original source of their music. Like Greek god Pan, Boujeloud serves as music teacher, connecting musical creativity with fertility and vital life force. Master Musicians have preserved this tradition for generations.

Modern Documentation

Various recordings of Master Musicians of Jajouka exist, most famously Brian Jones Presents: The Pipes of Pan at Jajouka, recorded in 1968 by Rolling Stones' Brian Jones. These recordings preserve musical tradition attributed to Boujeloud's original teaching.
