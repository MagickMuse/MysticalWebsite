Mari

Overview

Mari, great goddess of the Pyrenees and eldest of the Basque deities, dwells within deep caverns. She controls storms and is intensely identified with lightning. Emerging from her caves, she flies through the air encompassed in flames. Her caves are filled with enchanted gold and gems which, if stolen, transform into coal.

Position

An all-powerful goddess, she is head of the Pagan Basque pantheon. The meaning of her name is unknown although many interpret it to mean "lady."

Moral Code

Devotees must uphold her moral code: Mari despises lying, thieving, and bragging. She is a queen and must always be treated with due respect. Removing anything from her sacred sites is forbidden and incurs swift punishment. It is considered good manners to request her permission before entering any place associated with her. Do not sit in her presence.

Global Traveler

Mari is a global traveler with an impeccable sense of direction. Allegedly if you call her name aloud three times when lost, she will appear overhead to provide directions. (Remember, she's a shape-shifter!)

Powers

Mari provides accurate oracles. She can produce miracles of healing, fertility, rescue, and protection. Traditionally Mari heals via herbs and the water dripping from stalactites.

Post-Christian Era

Post-Christianity, Mari was redefined as a Queen of Witches. Her devotees were arrested by the Inquisition and accused of practicing witchcraft. Even so, her veneration survived. Mari remains an extremely popular goddess. She is as hostile toward Christianity as it has historically been toward her. Although she will share devotees with other Pagan deities, she does not tolerate anything redolent of Christianity.

Manifestation

Mari has a massive repertoire of forms including:

  • A woman
  • A woman with bird feet
  • A tree
  • A tree radiating flames
  • A woman radiating flames
  • Gusts of wind
  • Lightning
  • Rainbows
  • A cloud
  • Birds

She sits, spinning or combing her hair. She may sit on a golden throne or at the entrance to a cave.

Iconography

She is portrayed as a woman with the full moon behind her head.

Attributes

  • Attributes: Comb, distaff, sickle
  • Consort: Maju, also known as Sugaar, the Dragon Lord of Thunder
  • Day: Friday
  • Planet: Moon
  • Mount: Horses pull her chariot; she rides a lightning bolt or travels in the form of a fireball. Sometimes she rides a sheep or goat.
  • Animals: Snake, goat
  • Birds: Crow, vulture
  • Elements: Earth, fire, water
  • Color: Red

Sacred Sites

Numerous places in Basque territory are named in Mari's honor. She is venerated in caves, within stone circles, and atop mountains. Mari inhabits the highest peaks of Pyrenean Mountains like Aizkorri, Amboto, and Muru.

Spinning Tradition

Mari is often encountered spinning in the moonlight, typically outside the mountain caves she inhabits. The Basque witches who adored her would bring their spinning tools outside to work under the moon alongside their goddess. Spinning at night under moonlight, especially by a well, sacred tree, or at a crossroads, was considered an unmistakable sign of witchcraft in the Basque regions.
