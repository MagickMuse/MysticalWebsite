Indrani is a minor goddess of the Hindu Vedic tradition, the consort of the great storm god Indra. She is mentioned more often than any of the other goddesses formed by attaching a feminine suffix to a god's name, although she still stands well in the shadow of her husband.

In Vedic literature

The Rg-veda describes Indrani as beautiful and gives her one of the few vivid scenes among the consort goddesses. In one hymn she is shown as jealous of her rivals for Indra's favor. She is also known by the name Saci, a word that denotes power and that has often been read as an early hint of the much later concept of sakti, the personified might of a god expressed through his female partner.

In another hymn, Indrani-Saci boasts of having won her husband by conquering him and claims that he is submissive to her will. In the same hymn, however, she goes on to petition the gods to rid her of rivals for his attention, and elsewhere she is said to stay at home. The portrait that emerges is uneven: despite her flashes of bold speech and despite the resonance of her secondary name with later theology, the Rg-veda still presents her as a minor figure of relatively little independent power.

Cited hymns

Key Rg-veda references: 3.53.6 (said to stay at home), 10.86 (jealous of rivals), 10.159 (Indrani-Saci boasts of conquering her husband and asks the gods to remove her rivals).
