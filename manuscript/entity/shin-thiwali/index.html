Venerated as a Burmese Buddhist saint, Shin Thiwali is an arhat (comparable to an Apostle in Buddhist tradition) and magical adept associated with extraordinary good fortune and safe travel.

Life and Legend

Shin Thiwali was born to royalty during the time of Siddhartha Gautama, the historical Buddha. From the moment of his conception, his mother's kingdom was showered with blessings and abundant harvests. However, her pregnancy lasted seven years, and labor continued for seven days with no progress.

Near death, the queen asked to see the Buddha for a final blessing. After the Buddha arrived and blessed her, she immediately gave birth to a healthy boy. Both mother and child survived, to everyone's amazement.

The prince, named Sivali, was devout from his first breath. By age seven, he had become a mendicant monk. Wherever he traveled, luck and good fortune followed. Those in his company, eventually a following of 500 monks, shared in this bounty. No matter how remote or deserted their location, those traveling with Shin Thiwali always had food, even if fairies had to bring it.

According to Burmese folk belief, Shin Thiwali still lives in the forest as a monk and magical adept. He is not written about in the past tense because tradition holds he has not died.

Magical Properties

Shin Thiwali's luck is contagious. Travelers carry tiny images of him so he accompanies them on journeys. He is considered the patron saint of travel, with his image worn or carried in the same manner as that of Saint Christopher. Those who honor him before embarking on journeys receive his protection and good fortune.

Images kept in the home protect against fires and theft. Those who revere him never lack food, prosperity, happiness, and success.

Iconography

Shin Thiwali appears as a serene traveling monk with a gentle expression and half-smile, his head shaved in monastic fashion. He carries prayer beads and a begging bowl, leaning on a walking staff. For traveler's amulets, the favored image shows him holding his fan in one hand and staff in the other, as if ready to begin a journey.

Attributes

Walking staff, fan, Buddhist rosary beads, begging bowl.
