Jack of the Beanstalk

Favored People
Children, only sons, the impoverished and underestimated, traders who suspect they are being cheated, climbers, dreamers who plant what no one else will plant
Precautions
Do not invoke for revenge or for theft from anyone who has not first stolen from you. The giant in his story is the older order; remember that you must come down the stalk as well as climb it.
Culture
English
Petition for
Sudden reversal of fortune, the courage to climb past a familiar ceiling, taking back what a giant has taken, recovering what a household has lost, the patience to let a foolish bargain prove itself wise
Gender
Male
Offerings
A handful of dried beans on the windowsill, a small heap of coins, a verse spoken at the foot of a climbing vine, a song to a beanstalk
Type
Legendary Figure
Plants1
Beans

Overview

Jack is the only son of an impoverished widow in an English folk tale that has been told and retold for at least two hundred years. Sent to market to sell the family's last cow, he traded the cow for a handful of beans. His mother despaired. Jack planted the beans anyway. By morning the beans had risen into a stalk that climbed past the clouds, and Jack, who could not resist a stalk that high, climbed it, found a kingdom in the sky belonging to a giant, and took back a bag of gold, a goose that laid golden eggs, and a singing harp. He came down the stalk, his mother set up for life, the giant gone. The story is told in more than one version, and in some, the man on the road who sells Jack the beans is a wizard in disguise; in others, a butcher tricking a foolish boy. Either way, the beans are real, and the climb is real.

Magickal Properties

Jack stands for the reversal of fortune that comes through what looks at first like a bad bargain. He is the patron of children whose parents have called them foolish, of households reduced to one last asset, and of anyone who has been told that the thing they are about to do makes no sense. He also stands for the climb itself: the willingness to leave the ground when a way up appears, even an absurd one, and the wits required to take only what the household needs and come down again before the stalk gives way.

Iconography and Attributes

Jack is shown as a young boy, often barefoot, holding either a handful of beans or the foot of an enormous climbing vine. The bean, the stalk, the cow, the harp, the goose, and the bag of gold are all his attributes. The axe with which he cuts down the stalk at the end of his story is also his. He is sometimes paired with the giant, who carries Jack's shadow alongside him.

Colors

The red of scarlet runner bean flowers, the green of a fresh stalk, and the gold of what he brought down.

Sacred Sites

Any garden bed where pole beans have been planted in poor soil and grown anyway. The threshold of a small home with little to its name. The base of a tall climbing vine.

Ritual

When a household is at its last asset and the future is unclear, plant a handful of pole beans in good soil with Jack's name spoken over them. Speak aloud what the household needs, plainly, no more than three things. Tend the vine. When it is climbing well, climb a ladder once, just a few rungs, in honor of the boy who climbed past the cloud line. Speak thanks when the beans come in. Do not ask Jack for what is not yours to take.

Lore

The scarlet runner bean, the most likely candidate for Jack's magickal beans, was first domesticated in the highlands of central Mexico thousands of years ago and reached Europe in the early seventeenth century by way of a botanist gardening for an English queen. By the time the fairy tale of Jack reached print, the scarlet runner was a familiar cottage-garden climber, fast-growing, red-flowered, and tall enough to be plausibly mistaken for a stalk that could pierce the sky. One version of the tale specifically calls the magic beans scarlet runners, the boy himself muttering, as he sows them in the morning soil, that they are nothing but common beans. The fairy tale knows what no adult in the story knew. The common bean was the magic bean all along.
