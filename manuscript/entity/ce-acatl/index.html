Quetzalcoatl

Also known as

Ce-Acatl, Acatl, One Reed, Topiltzin Ce Acatl Quetzalcoatl

Overview

Ce Acatl is a minor creator god of the classical Aztec religion of the central Mexican plateau, and his name means One Reed, from ce meaning one and acatl meaning reed, the name of the day-sign that is the thirteenth of the twenty day-signs of the tonalpohualli, the Aztec sacred calendar. He is one of the deities collectively classed as the Quetzalcoatl complex, in which the great feathered-serpent god Quetzalcoatl is understood not as a single figure but as a family of related divine and semi-divine beings gathered around the central Quetzalcoatl name. Ce Acatl is the calendrical form of the god, the aspect of Quetzalcoatl whose office is to hold the day-sign One Reed at the point at which it turns within the sacred year.

Classification

Minor creator god of the classical Aztec religion. Calendrical aspect of the Quetzalcoatl complex.

Domains

The day-sign One Reed within the tonalpohualli, the calendrical initiation into the Aztec sacred year, the reed as tool of writing and cutting, the sovereign creator power of Quetzalcoatl gathered under the sign of the reed, the passing of the years within the wider fifty-two-year cycle of the Aztec calendar.

Iconography

Depicted in the manner of Quetzalcoatl, wearing the pointed cap of the wind god, adorned with the conch-shell wind jewel across his chest, and carrying the emblems of the god of the day-sign. His distinguishing mark is the reed itself, held or worn as the sign of his office within the tonalpohualli. In some traditions he takes the human form of the historical or legendary Topiltzin Ce Acatl Quetzalcoatl, the priest-king of ancient Tollan whose name conflates the divine and the human within the wider Quetzalcoatl complex.

Attributes

Reed, conch-shell wind jewel, pointed cap of Quetzalcoatl, day-sign glyph, ritual staff.

Sacred Sites

Temples of the Quetzalcoatl cult across the central Mexican plateau, particularly at Tula in what is now the state of Hidalgo, and at other ancient centers where the calendrical aspects of the Quetzalcoatl complex were honored. His cult belongs to the wider religious system of the classical Aztec period, which continued the older Toltec religious tradition centered on Quetzalcoatl.

Ritual

Honored on the day of One Reed within the tonalpohualli, and honored in the wider ceremonies of the Quetzalcoatl complex that mark the turning of the sacred year. Aztec calendrical religion assigned each of the twenty day-signs to a divine patron, and Ce Acatl's ritual office belonged to that fine-grained pattern of daily worship woven through the whole life of the community.

Lore

The Quetzalcoatl complex is one of the richest and most complicated bodies of Aztec religious tradition, and Ce Acatl holds a distinctive place within it as the calendrical form of the god, tied to the day-sign One Reed. This day-sign carries special weight in the wider history of the Aztec religion because the legendary Toltec priest-king Topiltzin Ce Acatl Quetzalcoatl, whose reign at Tula in the tenth or eleventh century is remembered in the Aztec historical tradition, bore the same day-sign name and became so entangled with the god Quetzalcoatl that the Aztec religion could not always distinguish where the human king ended and the divine feathered serpent began. This entanglement gives Ce Acatl his particular character as the point at which calendar, kingship, and divinity meet within the Aztec religious imagination.
