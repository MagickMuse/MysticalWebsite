Magickal Properties

The Mamayutas are Andean Corn Mothers and Spirits of Fertility who transmit generative powers to women, their descendants. They are perceived as the ultimate female ancestors, the first, primordial female ancestor, founder of the female (or matrilineal) line. As Corn Mothers, they embody both the agricultural abundance of maize cultivation and the ancestral power flowing through the female line.

Ritual Uses

The Mamayutas were honored through rituals that acknowledged both the sorrows and blessings of fertility. Descriptions of rituals conducted for the Mamayutas may be found in witchcraft and idolatry trial transcripts of the Spanish Inquisition, operating in the Department of Arequipa, now Peru. These rituals honored the full cycle of fertility, including losses and grief.

Offerings

Historically, women presented the Mamayutas with offerings of their aborted fetuses and stillbirths. Inquisitors were horrified by what they labeled witchcraft, unable or unwilling to comprehend the emotional resonance of this spiritual transaction. This practice represented a profound connection between fertility, loss, and the ancestral line.

Favored People

Women, descendants seeking generative powers, those honoring female ancestral lines, practitioners working with Andean spiritual traditions.

Petition For

Fertility and generative powers, connection to primordial female ancestors, matrilineal blessings, healing grief from pregnancy loss, honoring the complete cycle of fertility including loss.

Warnings

Approach with deep respect for Andean cultural traditions. The historical ritual practices were deeply meaningful within their cultural context and should not be appropriated or judged through colonial Christian frameworks. The Spanish Inquisition's interpretation as "witchcraft" reflects colonial misunderstanding rather than the spiritual truth of these practices.

Additional Notes

The Mamayutas represent the intersection of agricultural fertility (as Corn Mothers) and human fertility (as ancestral spirits). Their veneration demonstrates the profound connection between the fertility of the land and the fertility of women's bodies. The offerings of pregnancy losses to the Mamayutas reflect a worldview in which all aspects of the fertility cycle, including grief and loss, are sacred and worthy of ritual acknowledgment.
