San Martín Caballero, literally Saint Martin the Horseman, is a magical Latin American manifestation of Martin of Tours. He serves as the patron of small businesses and is invoked for success and prosperity. His name is pronounced cab-ah-yair-oh.

Domains

Martín Caballero bestows luck and prosperity through his images. Shrines dedicated to him are maintained in stores and restaurants throughout Latin America. Establishments serving liquor traditionally keep small altars hidden behind the bar or in discreet locations, believed to encourage customers to drink more.

He also governs domestic happiness, though his blessings require regular offerings to his horse.

Lore

Tradition holds that a business's first dollar or currency earned should be offered as tribute, either framed, placed on the altar, or tucked beneath his statue. This money must never be spent or lost. Bars, taverns, cafés, and restaurants keep water and grass on the altar for his horse. Legend states that as long as the horse remains fed, the kitchen will never run out of food or beverages.

For domestic blessings, devotees must provide grass weekly and fresh water daily for the horse. A glass of wine for the rider is also appreciated.

San Martín de Loba (Martin of the She-Wolf) represents another path of Martin of Tours, though this aspect likely conceals the Roman god Mars. Wolfish Martin patronizes wheelers, dealers, and hard bargainers.

Iconography

The classic image of Martin of Tours on horseback, giving half his cloak to a beggar, represents Martín Caballero.
