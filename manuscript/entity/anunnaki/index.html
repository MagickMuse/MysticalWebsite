The Anunnaki are the children and courtiers of the god of heaven in the Mesopotamian tradition, both Sumerian and Babylonian-Akkadian. They are known from at least 2500 BCE until circa 200 BCE, the latest evidence preserved in Babylon.

Origin as Fertility Powers

The Anunnaki originate as chthonic fertility deities, the powers of the earth's productive depths whose work feeds the surface world. This earlier face shows them as a generative collective, gods of the underground from whom the fertility of the land arises.

The Seven Judges of the Underworld

In the later strand of the tradition, the Anunnaki feature as the seven fearsome judges of the underworld. They answer to Kur and to Ereskigal, and they are responsible for passing sentence of death. Among the sentences attributed to them is the death imposed on the goddess Inanna-Ishtar during her descent.

Identification with the Igigi

The Anunnaki are often closely identified with the Igigi, the two collectives blurring at the edges in the surviving record. Where the Igigi are sometimes placed in heaven and the Anunnaki on or beneath the earth, the boundary between the two groups is not always firm.
