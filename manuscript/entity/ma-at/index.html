The Triple Nature of Ma'at

Ma'at is a word, a concept, and a goddess:

Ma'at as Word: Ma'at is a word meaning "true," "genuine," "the real thing." Thus khesbet ma'at means "real lapis lazuli" as opposed to blue paste.

Ma'at as Concept: Ma'at, the concept, is a state of sacred harmony linking the mundane world with the divine. Ma'at, consisting of truth, justice, and balance is a state of grace, which can be damaged by human actions and negative emotions like greed. An Egyptian network of temples and divine rituals existed to maintain and preserve the state of Ma'at.

Ma'at as Goddess: Ma'at is the ancient, predynastic goddess who rules this state of grace. She is a goddess of truth, order, and natural ethical and moral law, the spirit of what is right versus what is merely legal or lawful.

The Weighing of the Heart

Ma'at is now most famous for the part she plays in ancient Egyptian after-life trials. At the final judgment, the dead individual's heart is weighed against the feather of Ma'at. The heart must equal or weigh less than Ma'at's feather. If the heart's weight tips the scales, that person is denied eternal life.

This judgment scene represents the ultimate test of a life lived in accordance with Ma'at: truth, justice, and balance. A heart heavy with sin, greed, cruelty, or dishonesty will tip the scales and fail the test.

Personal Goddess of Righteousness

Ma'at is not only a goddess of the afterlife; she has historically been venerated as a personal goddess and held up as an example of that righteous moral code, which ensures connection with the divine and the soul's survival.

Sacred Partnership with Thoth

Ma'at is very closely associated with Lord Thoth. They stand together in Ra's barque and are sometimes envisioned as married. This partnership represents the union of truth (Ma'at) and wisdom (Thoth), the perfect balance of ethical law and divine knowledge.

Manifestation

Ma'at is a beautiful woman who may have wings. She may wear her signature feather on her head.

Iconography

She is sometimes portrayed as a woman with an ostrich feather for a head, demonstrating how completely she embodies the principle of truth and balance.

Magickal Properties

Ma'at represents the cosmic order that maintains the universe. She is not simply a goddess of human morality but the personification of the divine structure that keeps chaos at bay. When Ma'at is honored and upheld, the world functions in harmony. When Ma'at is violated, disorder and chaos threaten to consume creation.

Ritual Uses

Invoke Ma'at for:

  • Establishing and maintaining truth and justice
  • Ethical decision-making and moral clarity
  • Preparation for death and the afterlife journey
  • Restoring balance and harmony after disruption
  • Legal matters where true justice is sought (not merely legal victory)
  • Understanding the difference between what is right and what is merely lawful
  • Connecting with the divine through righteous living

Lore

Ma'at's feather is not merely a symbol but a measure of a soul's worth. In the weighing of the heart ceremony, presided over by Anubis and recorded by Thoth, the deceased's heart is placed on one side of the scales and Ma'at's feather on the other. A heart lighter than or equal to the feather passes into eternal life. A heart heavy with wrongdoing is devoured by Ammit, the devourer of the dead.

The concept of Ma'at influenced ancient Egyptian society at every level, from the pharaoh's duty to uphold cosmic order to individual citizens' responsibility to live truthfully. Pharaohs claimed to rule by Ma'at, and temple rituals daily renewed the cosmic balance she represents.

Related deities who embody similar principles of justice and truth include Metis (Greek) and Archangel Michael (who also weighs souls).
