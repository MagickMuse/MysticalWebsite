Magickal Properties

Datsueba is a gateway goddess whose name relates to undressing, theorized to originally be a goddess of birth and death who presents each birth with its skin and requires its return at death. In Japanese Buddhism, she is now a gatekeeper of Hell who strips clothing from sinners arriving at the River of Three Roads.

Ritual Uses

Datsueba represents the fierce aspect of death transition and the removal of worldly attachments. She can be understood as representing the necessary stripping away of illusions and material concerns during major life transitions.

Offerings

Offerings appropriate for fierce gateway deities, items that acknowledge the difficult but necessary aspects of transition and transformation.

Favored People

Those seeking to understand death processes and major transitions, people needing fierce protection during vulnerable times, those working with concepts of death and rebirth.

Petition For

Understanding of death and transition processes, fierce protection during vulnerable periods, assistance with letting go of worldly attachments, strength during major life changes.

Warnings

She is not kind or gentle but fierce and implacable. May strip off skin if someone arrives without clothes. Makes no exceptions for children or babies. She breaks fingers of thieves and doles out punishment as deemed appropriate.

Additional Notes

She stays at the gate with male consort Kenne-o (she strips clothes, he hangs them on trees). Some statues are credited with healing powers. Women sometimes invoke her aid with lactation or child protection. Iconography shows her seated on ground with one knee up, breasts partially exposed. Vestiges of her role as birth/death goddess sometimes emerge.
