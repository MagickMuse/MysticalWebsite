Magickal Properties

Al-Buraq is the miraculous winged steed who carried the Prophet Muhammad on the Night Journey from Mecca to Jerusalem and then ascended with him through the seven heavens. Her name derives from the Arabic word for lightning, reflecting her incredible speed and luminous nature. Buraq is described as a creature between a mule and a donkey in size, with wings and a human face. She is a celestial being of extraordinary swiftness who can travel vast distances in an instant. Buraq represents the vehicle of spiritual ascension and divine transportation, embodying the bridge between earthly and heavenly realms.

Ritual Uses

Buraq is honored as part of the sacred narrative of the Night Journey and should be referenced with reverence within that context. She symbolizes spiritual elevation, swift divine assistance, and the possibility of transcendent experience. Her story inspires meditation on spiritual ascension and the journey toward divine understanding.

Offerings

Buraq is not petitioned in the traditional sense but is honored through respectful remembrance of the sacred narrative. Appropriate devotional practices include prayer, study of the Night Journey accounts, and reflection on themes of spiritual transcendence.

Favored People

Muslims reflecting on the Prophet's journey, those seeking understanding of spiritual ascension, and people meditating on the bridge between material and spiritual realms.

Petition For

While not petitioned directly, Buraq's story inspires prayers for spiritual elevation, swift divine guidance, transcendent understanding, and safe passage through spiritual journeys.

Warnings

Reference and depict the Buraq with reverence and avoid irreverent or commercialized portrayals. Do not appropriate sacred narratives or use them disrespectfully. This is a figure from Islamic sacred history and should be approached with proper understanding and respect.

Additional Notes

Buraq appears in Islamic art and literature as a beautiful winged creature with a woman's face. She is said to have been the steed of previous prophets including Abraham. The Western Wall in Jerusalem is traditionally called the Buraq Wall because it is believed to be where Buraq was tethered during the Night Journey. Her depiction varies across Islamic cultures but consistently emphasizes her luminous, swift, and celestial nature.
