Legend

La Ghriba is a Jewish saint. A strange, beautiful young girl appeared mysteriously on Djerba Island off the coast of Tunisia. She lived quietly in solitude and poverty. People tolerated her but never really welcomed her. One day, her house burned down. Her body was found among the ashes: dead, but completely intact, with not a burn mark on her. This was perceived as a miraculous mark of sanctity. People flocked to pay homage and request her intercession. Miracles occurred in response to petitions. A synagogue was erected over the site of her burned-down house.

"Ghriba" means "strange," "marvelous," or "wondrous" in Arabic. At least six African synagogues have historically been called Ghriba, but the most famous is the one on Djerba.

Alternative Legend

La Ghriba was a young Jewish refugee fleeing the fall of Jerusalem in the first century CE. She carried stones from the Jerusalem Temple with her. When she reached Djerba, she collapsed and died of exhaustion. The synagogue was erected on the site where she died.

Temple Relics

El Ghriba Synagogue is the most famous of Djerba's many synagogues, not just because of its mysterious saint, but because it is believed to possess relics from Solomon's Temple, possibly brought by fleeing priests. Sometimes the marvelous girl is credited with bringing the stones. Sometimes they allegedly flew to Djerba by themselves or were carried by angels.

La Ghriba Ritual

This ritual must be done at her shrine, though she may also be invoked at home:

  1. Inscribe your name on an egg. Focus on your petition while holding the egg.
  2. Carry it into the tunnel constructed where La Ghriba's body was discovered.
  3. Leave the egg beside the candles lit for La Ghriba. The heat will cook the eggs.
  4. Eat the egg and receive La Ghriba's blessing. (You'll know your own egg because it has your name on it.)

During the annual Lag B'Omer festival, names of unmarried girls are inscribed onto eggs, which must be brought into the tunnel by someone else. After the eggs cook, they are given to the girls to eat. Allegedly, their wishes will come true.

Tragic History

Ghriba was once considered a kind of Jewish Lourdes where Christians and Muslims, as well as Jews, sought blessings. In recent years, it has been the target of Islamist hostilities:

  • In 1985, a local policeman hired to provide security opened fire on a festival crowd, killing three including a child
  • On 11 April 2002, a suicide bomber drove a gas truck close to the temple, causing twenty-one deaths

Festival

Coincides with the Jewish holiday of Lag B'Omer.

Sacred Site

La Ghriba Synagogue on Djerba Island, also known as the Delet or "Door," has periodically been destroyed and rebuilt. The present building probably dates from the 19th century. The niche below the ark is called the Magrath Sibiya (Cave of the Girl).
