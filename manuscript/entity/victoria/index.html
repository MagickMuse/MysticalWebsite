Overview

Victoria is the Roman goddess of victory, identified with the Greek Nike; it can be extremely difficult to disentangle the two goddesses. Victoria is venerated alongside Jupiter and Mars rather than Minerva, since Nike is intensely associated with Athena, to whom Minerva is identified. Victoria's more intensely exclusive association with military victory than Nike's may be a reflection of her devotees: Roman soldiers carried her veneration throughout the Empire.

Magickal Properties

Victoria was a major goddess possessing a vast official cult that existed for centuries. The Romans had a hard time denying or giving her up: hers was among the last cults abandoned with the rise of Christianity. Her image appeared on Roman coins until the third century CE.

Manifestation

Victoria is envisioned as a winged woman carrying trophies and spoils from defeated enemies; she may stand with one foot resting on a globe.

Iconography

Her image is often incorporated into reliefs carved on Roman triumphal arches, including the Arches of Augustus, Constantine, and Septimius Severus.

Sacred Sites

Victoria had multiple temples, including one on the Palatine Hill dedicated in 294 BCE. An altar was also dedicated to her in Rome's Senate house.
