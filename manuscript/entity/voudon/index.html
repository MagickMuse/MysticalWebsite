Voudon

Voudon is a tradition rooted in the beliefs of the Dahomey peoples of West Africa, particularly from the region of present-day Benin. Voudon recognizes many different lwa (also spelled loa), spirits that have attained a God-like status. These lwa are able to possess the human body, which is the aim of many of the trance-inducing ceremonies of the faith.

The Lwa

The spirits split into two broad families:

  • Rada: protective and guiding spirits, generally benevolent in character.
  • Petro: less forgiving and more associated with the darker currents of Voudon magick. Each lwa holds aspects of both in its personality.

Mawu-Lisa and the Four Days of Creation

Mawu-Lisa is the primary deity of the Dahomey tradition and an androgynous figure. According to one creation account, on the first day the world was made; on the second, all plant and animal life came into being; on the third, humans were given intellect and languages; and on the fourth, the far-sighted God created technology.

Religious Associations

  • Voudon is an amalgam of West African beliefs with Christianity. One of its creation myths draws comparison to the Abrahamic creation narrative; whereas the Abrahamic God is said to have needed six days to create the world, Mawu-Lisa needed only four.
