Lady of Summer

Walpurga is the ancient goddess of beauty, warmth, fertility, renewal of life, and grain. Rituals in her honor were once intended to defeat the forces of winter and permit the emergence of summer. For nine days before May Day, the Wild Hunt pursues her; if they capture her, they can prolong winter and prevent summer, and so they ride all night, intensifying their pursuit in a last-ditch attempt to maintain their power. On the run, Walpurga seeks refuge among villagers, who leave their doors and windows open so that she may find safety from the frost and her pursuers.

According to myth, Walpurga once begged a farmer to hide her in a stack of grain. He did so, acting from compassion and not realizing her true identity. In the morning, she had vanished, leaving grains of gold sprinkled amongst his rye crop.

Manifestation

Walpurga the goddess is a beautiful, crowned woman with long, flowing hair, wearing fiery shoes.

Attributes

Ears of grain, a spindle, and a triangular mirror revealing the future.

Correspondences

Tree: alder. Planet: sun. Element: fire.

Under Christian Influence

Under Christian influence, Walpurgis Night was transformed into a time to banish the forces of Paganism rather than the forces of winter, remade as a holiday honoring a saint instead of a goddess. It remains, however, a night for witches to revel. Vestiges of the goddess survive in the proliferation of spindles and thread used in divination and love spells on this night.

The Saint

Saint Walpurga (c. 710-25 February 799), an English abbess born in Wessex to a devoutly Christian family, traveled to Germany (then still largely Pagan) as a missionary. She founded religious houses and eventually presided over a community of nuns in Heidenham. Though comparatively little is known of her life, she was renowned for extreme fasting.

After her death, her body was laid in a hollow stone niche that soon began exuding aromatic, miracle-producing oil. A church was built over her grave. She was canonized in 870 and her relics distributed throughout Europe. Walpurgis Oil has reputedly produced healing miracles for over 1,000 years. The oil continues to flow, especially between October and February.

Saint Walpurga assumed many functions of the Teutonic grain goddess of the same name. The goddess inspired Walpurgis Night (May Eve), a night of sacred revelry for witches. May 1st is also a feast day for the saint. The original Walpurga may have been an aspect of the earth goddess Herta or her daughter Freya. The name may derive from "vala," the Norse term for a sybil or prophetess.

Powers and Domains

Saint Walpurga is renowned for healing anorexia and eating disorders.

  • Defense against magic and witchcraft
  • Protection from plague, famine, and crop failures
  • Protection from rabid dog bites
  • Healing eating disorders, including severe anorexia

Iconography

Portrayed as a nun holding a crozier in one hand and a flask or ewer in the other.

Attributes

Sheaf of grain, three ears of grain, phial of oil.

Place

Matron saint of Antwerp.

Sacred Site

Her shrine in Eichstatt, Germany.
