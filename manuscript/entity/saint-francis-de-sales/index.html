Saint Francis de Sales is the patron saint of authors, writers, editors, journalists, and self-publishers.

Saint Francis de Sales is renowned for healing hearing disorders and is the official patron of the deaf and hearing impaired.

Classification

Anglican and Roman Catholic saint.

Life and History

Francis was born on August 21, 1567 at the castle of Sales in Annecy, France. Given a strict Catholic education emphasizing mortification, he became a priest and served as spiritual counselor to many noblewomen.

He was elected bishop of Geneva in 1602, but since Geneva was vehemently Calvinist, Francis stayed in Annecy. In 1610, he cofounded the Order of the Visitation with Sister Jeanne de Chantal.

The First Self-Publisher

Francis was author of the most popular spiritual book of the 17th century, Introduction of the Devout Life. Concerned that his messages weren't reaching people, he had them printed onto sheets of paper that he personally slipped under doors. He is the patron of writers in general, but especially those who self-publish.

Sign Language Pioneer

At a time when the deaf were routinely dismissed as mentally deficient and denied spiritual training, Francis developed a method of teaching catechism via his own system of symbols and sign language. He is now the official patron of the hearing impaired.

Death and Canonization

Francis died of a stroke on December 27, 1622. He was canonized in 1665 and proclaimed a Doctor of the Church in 1887.

Attribute

The sacred heart surrounded by a crown of thorns.

Sacred Site

The Basilica of the Visitation in Annecy, France contains his tomb and is reputedly the site of many miracles.
