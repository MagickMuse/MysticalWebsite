Ancient Goddess of the Moon

Selene is an ancient Greek goddess of the moon, daughter of the Titans Hyperion and Theia. Her siblings are Helios, god of the sun, and Eos, goddess of the dawn. While many Greek goddesses hold lunar associations, Selene and her Roman counterpart Luna are considered true personifications of the moon itself. Comparatively little information regarding Selene's veneration and rituals survives, though she is described as "great in magic" and was apparently invoked in spells. Many of her functions were later transferred to Artemis, as Helios' were to Apollo.

The Nightly Journey

Selene bathes in the ocean before riding up to the sky each night in a chariot pulled by a pair of winged steeds — though she is also depicted riding sidesaddle on a white horse. Sometimes she must hide from a dragon, which accounts for a lunar eclipse or absent moon. She is an amorous goddess with many lovers including Pan and Zeus. Her most famous love affair is with the young shepherd Endymion. His attentions so distracted her from her duties that Zeus intervened, offering Endymion a choice: eternal youth in a permanent slumber, or death. He chose the eternal, youthful sleep. Each dark and new moon, Selene descended to the Anatolian cave where Endymion lay sleeping, and together they produced many daughters.

Shedding Light

Selene's name is etymologically related to a word meaning "light." In her guise as the full moon, Selene sheds light on problems and mysteries.

Manifestation

Selene is the goddess of the moon, but she also is the moon. Selene may manifest as a woman or a cow, but you can also gaze at the moon and see her. As a woman, she is described as being very beautiful. She has wings and wears a diadem.

Iconography

Early depictions of Selene envisioned her as a cow. Her horns are the crescent moon.

Crystals

Moonstones and Selenite (literally "moon rock"); Selenite is often found in the form of wands and is used to heal sleep disorders, among other uses.

The Three Faces

Selene is among the goddesses most often cited in discussions of the Triple Goddess, the ancient concept of the threefold divine feminine. Her three aspects correspond to the phases of the Moon: waxing, full, and waning. The waxing and waning phases are not simple mirror images of each other: the waxing counts as its own distinct aspect because it represents active, visible growth, in contrast to the apparent stillness at either extreme of the cycle. It is precisely this ceaselessly shifting quality that has led the Moon to be understood as a fundamentally feminine energy across so many traditions.

Roman Luna

In Roman tradition, Luna was the direct personification of the moon itself—simpler and more elemental than the Greek myths surrounding Selene. Luna's original Roman worship was likely more direct, without the elaborate mythology. The Romans celebrated Luna with festivals on 31 March, 24 August, and 29 August. She had temples on the Aventine Hill and on the Palatine, the latter dedicated to Luna Noctiluca ("Luna Who Shines By Night"). Luna's powers particularly affect women, fertility, and the sea. She may be invoked to intensify or ameliorate lunar forces or petitioned for favor when astrological lunar phases are inauspicious.
