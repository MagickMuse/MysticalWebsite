Paschal Baylon is a Roman Catholic saint and the patron of chefs, pastry chefs, candy makers, and Eucharistic devotion.

Life and History

Paschal Baylon (1540–1592) was a Spanish Franciscan friar known for his intense devotion to the Eucharist and his humble service in monastery kitchens. Born to a poor family in Aragon, he worked as a shepherd before joining the Franciscans as a lay brother. His duties included cooking and serving food, which led to his patronage of chefs and those who work in kitchens.

Eucharistic Devotion

Paschal spent hours in prayer before the Blessed Sacrament. His ecstatic devotion became legendary, and he was known to levitate during prayer. He is particularly associated with Eucharistic adoration and is considered the patron of Eucharistic congresses.

Powers and Domains

As patron of chefs, pastry chefs, and candy makers, Paschal is invoked by those who prepare food, especially in professional kitchens and bakeries. His humble service in monastery kitchens made him a natural patron for culinary workers.

Feast Day

17 May
