Lazarus is renowned for healing AIDS and conditions involving death and resurrection of hope.

Lazarus and his sisters, Mary and Martha, lived in Bethany near Jerusalem and were close friends of Jesus Christ. Those who consider Mary of Bethany to be the same person as Mary Magdalen also consider Lazarus to be the Magdalen's brother.

Lazarus is the subject of a famous miracle recounted in the Gospel of John. Martha came to Jesus in distress over the death of her beloved brother. Though Lazarus had been dead for four days, Jesus resurrected him. Many consider this miracle among the pivotal moments of Christ's life, possibly the event that precipitated Jesus's arrest and crucifixion due to the incredible acclaim it brought him.

Later Life

Various traditions describe what happened to Lazarus following the Crucifixion:

  • He may have traveled to France on the same boat as his sisters
  • A Roman Catholic legend suggests he became the first bishop of Marseille
  • An Eastern Orthodox legend holds that he traveled to Cyprus and became the first bishop of Larnaca

Conflation with the Beggar Saint

In folk religion and magic, Lazarus the Beggar (from Luke's parable) is more significant and beloved than Lazarus of Bethany. The two saints are frequently confused and conflated. Iconography associated with Lazarus the Beggar is often applied to Lazarus of Bethany. Many churches bless dogs, the constant companions of the beggar Lazarus, on the feast day of Lazarus of Bethany.

Lore

"Lazarus phenomenon" or "Lazarus syndrome" is a medical term for someone who, having been declared dead and failing to respond to resuscitation, spontaneously and mysteriously returns to life.

Iconography

The raising of Lazarus has long been a favorite subject of artists including Caravaggio, Van Gogh, and Rembrandt. He may be portrayed as an apostle, a bishop, or wearing a shroud or winding sheet.

Ritual

In Russian tradition, it is customary to eat caviar on Lazarus Saturday.

Feast Days

  • December 17 (Roman Catholic)
  • Lazarus Saturday, the day before Palm Sunday (Orthodox)
  • July 29, celebrated with his sisters Martha and Mary (Lutheran)

Sacred Sites

  • Bethany (Al-Eizariyah): The Arabic name derives from Lazarus. The Tomb of Lazarus, traditionally believed to be the site of his resurrection, has long been a pilgrimage site for Christians and Muslims. A church was established there by the late fourth century; the site is now occupied by the Mosque of Al-Uzair, with Roman Catholic and Greek Orthodox churches nearby.
  • Church of Saint Lazarus, Larnaca, Cyprus: Reputedly built over the saint's tomb
  • Cathedral of Saint Lazare, Autun, France: Said to house his relics
