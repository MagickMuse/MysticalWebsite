Saint Mammes is renowned for healing broken bones.

Legends regarding Saint Mammes conflict with each other and may not all reference the same person. According to his most famous legend, his parents, Theodotus and Rufina, were Christians living in Caesarea (now Kayseri in modern Turkey). They were jailed as Christians during the third century while his mother was pregnant with Mammes, who was born in jail. Orphaned when his parents were executed, Mammes was raised by a wealthy widow who died when he was fifteen.

After her death, he lost protection and came to the attention of anti-Christian authorities. Mammes was first tortured by the governor of Caesarea and then again by order of Roman emperor Aurelian. He was thrown to lions, but they became docile in his presence. He was rescued by an angel who ordered the boy to hide in the nearby mountains. (An alternative legend suggests the lions were not so docile and that Mammes was devoured by one.)

Mammes preached to wild animals. A lion was his constant companion. He visited a local duke who condemned him to death. Fatally struck in the stomach with a trident, he dragged himself close to a theater where he succumbed to his injuries.

The Cypriot Legend

According to Cypriot legend, Mammes was a cave-dwelling hermit who resisted attempts by authorities to tax him. He was arrested, but while guarded by armed soldiers, he saw a lion attack a lamb. He broke free, grabbed the lamb, jumped on the lion's back, and rode it into town, where authorities decided to free him and exempt him from taxation.

Domains

Mammes sounds like "mammary," and Saint Mammes is invoked for milk by nursing mothers. He may also be helpful to those who cannot pay their taxes or are morally opposed to taxation.

Iconography

Mammes is envisioned as a young boy holding a lamb and riding on a lion.

Sacred Sites

  • The 12th-century Langres Cathedral (Cathedrale Sainte-Mammes) in Langres, France, houses his relics including his head
  • An alternative legend suggests his head is housed in the Church of Santa Maria Magdalena in Zaragoza, Spain
  • Many churches are dedicated to Saint Mammes in Cyprus and Lebanon, including the Church of Mar Mema in Edde, Lebanon
