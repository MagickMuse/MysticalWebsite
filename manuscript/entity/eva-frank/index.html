Classification

Frankist saint.

Life and History

Eva Frank (1754-1816) was the daughter of Jacob Frank (1726-1791), the controversial Jewish messianic leader who claimed to be the reincarnation of King David and Sabbatai Zevi. Eva's mother, Hannah Frank, was declared the living incarnation of the Shekhina, the indwelling spirit of God or the female aspect of God in exile on earth.

When Hannah died in 1770, sixteen-year-old Eva assumed that role and was addressed as Matronita, a Kabbalistic honorific usually reserved for the Shekhina.

The Female Messiah

Eva Frank was venerated as the female messiah. By Jewish standards, this was exceptionally subversive. Jacob Frank eventually evolved a whole new religion, incorporating elements of Judaism and Christianity.

Titles

Eva was addressed by multiple titles:

  • Gevirah: Hebrew for "the Lady" or "the Mistress" (all mystic overtones intended)
  • Panna: The Polish equivalent
  • Ha Betulah Ha-Kedushah: Hebrew for "the Holy Virgin"

The "Holy Virgin"

Eva never married. Whether she was literally virginal is unclear, and whether the title was ever intended literally is uncertain. Her mother was also called Holy Virgin even after giving birth to Eva and several sons.

Controversy

Constant rumors circulated regarding the Frankists' unorthodox sexual practices. Eva was accused of assuming her mother's role in the bedroom as well as spiritually. At some point, Jacob Frank claimed Eva was really an illegitimate Russian princess, secret daughter of Catherine the Great, and that he was only her guardian, not her father. This may have been to avoid anti-Semitism or evoke glamour rather than to facilitate incest.

The Talismanic Portraits

In 1777, Jacob Frank commissioned three portraits of Eva. Copies were highly prized talismans. The Kabbalist and historian Gershom Scholem wrote that, as late as 1930, cousins of US Supreme Court Justice Louis Brandeis treasured Eva Frank's portrait, considering her a saint. Justice Brandeis allegedly kept her portrait on his desk. The parents of Justice Felix Frankfurter were also reputedly devotees.

Later Life and Death

After Jacob Frank's death, Eva commanded the Frankists. Thousands made pilgrimage to receive her blessings. Her fortunes dwindled and she reportedly died in poverty in Offenbach, Germany in 1816.

Immortality

Devotees declared her immortal and only hiding, not dead. They claimed that, like Jesus, Eva Frank would return one day for her second coming.
