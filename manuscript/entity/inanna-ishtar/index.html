Supreme Mesopotamian fertility/war goddess. Inanna (Sumerian) and Ishtar (Semitic) interchangeable, though Ishtar manifests more intensely, more sexual, violent, volatile. Controls love, war, fertility, childbirth, healing, disease, STIs. Can kill or bless with glance.

Queen of Heaven Tradition

Biblical sources (Jeremiah 7:18, 44:16-19): "children gather wood, fathers kindle fire, women knead dough to make cakes to Queen of Heaven." When rebuked, exiled Jewish women insisted: "when we offered to Queen of Heaven, we had plenty...since we left off...we have wanted all things."

Inanna-Ishtar and Tammuz: Goddess-Consort Pattern

Exemplify ancient hierarchy where powerful goddesses had younger male consorts whom they initiated, tutored, and sometimes killed. Goddess eternal; consorts replaceable.

Tammuz (Dumuzi) serves as consort and sacrificial king. After Inanna's underworld descent, she condemns unmoved Tammuz to replace her there half the year. Goddess controlled life, death, resurrection. Power dynamic Christianity inverted by making male figures dominant.
