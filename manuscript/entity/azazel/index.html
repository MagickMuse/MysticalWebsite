Desert Spirit and Ruler of Goat-Spirits

Azazel is a desert spirit worshipped by ancient Semites. He rules over a band of goat-spirits known as the Se'irim, commanding these powerful desert entities from his wilderness domain.

The Original Scapegoat

Azazel is famous for his role in the Jewish Day of Atonement (Yom Kippur) rituals. As per instructions in Leviticus 16:21-28, two goats were brought into the Jerusalem Temple. One was sacrificed to the Creator; the other, dedicated to Azazel, is the original scapegoat. The sins of the people were ritually transferred to this goat, which was then brought to the desert and set free as an offering to Azazel. Although this became a Jewish custom, its roots are believed to be pre-Judaic.

Teacher of Forbidden Arts

As one of the rebel angels, Azazel taught the daughters of man two significant crafts: the art of metalworking and the sacred art of cosmetics. These teachings represent the transmission of practical skills alongside beautification knowledge.

Satan Associations

In Christian demonology, Azazel is considered a fallen angel and counted among Satan's host. Perhaps because of his strong associations with goats, as ruler of goat-spirits and recipient of the scapegoat, his name is also sometimes used as a synonym for Satan himself.
