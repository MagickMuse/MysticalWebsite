Earth's First Woman

According to Jewish mystical traditions, Lilith existed prior to Eve as Earth's first woman and first witch. She was created not from Adam's rib but from Earth simultaneously with him, making them equals. Genesis contains two versions of human creation, which some interpret as reflecting this dual tradition.

The Divorce from Paradise

Adam and Lilith's relationship quickly became contentious. She refused to be subordinate, specifically refusing to always lie beneath him during sex. Lilith demanded equality, basing her claim on their common origin. When Adam attempted to force her, Lilith uttered the secret, ineffable name of the Creator (a power shared only with Isis), giving her the ability to fly away. She initiated Earth's first divorce, preferring to run away and dance all night with demons in the most desolate regions of Earth rather than living in Paradise with someone who wished to dominate her. Upon first leaving Eden, Lilith yearned for the company of the "little faces," mysterious winged baby-like angels, but when she attempted to fly up among them, the Creator thrust her forcibly back down to Earth.

The Negotiation with Angels

Adam appealed to YHWH to send his woman back. Three angels were dispatched to fetch her, but through the power acquired from knowledge of the Creator's name, Lilith was able to defy them. The angels threatened her but Lilith threatened them right back, warning that she intended to spend eternity killing human babies and striking adults infertile. Instead of dragging her back to Eden, the angels ended up negotiating: Lilith agreed not to harm babies in homes where her name is posted. Significantly, her name does not appear in the Bible (the one possible reference in Isaiah is encoded and may refer literally to screech owls), yet her name is ubiquitous in Jewish amulets.

Anti-Lilith Amulets

Lilith's anger and her own fertility frustrations are blamed for a reputation as a baby snatcher, held responsible for miscarriage, especially chronic miscarriage, stillbirth, and sudden infant death alike. Despite this fearsome reputation, amulets invoking her name were reportedly revealed by Lilith herself, so using them does not offend or further enrage her. Posting her name is instead a form of respect: unlike the Bible, where she remains nameless, most anti-Lilith amulets demand that her name be displayed so that all present recognize her power.

Lilith is also said to have once encountered the Prophet Elijah, himself a childbirth guardian, while traveling down a road. Elijah recognized her immediately and demanded new, improved protective methods; in response, Lilith revealed several of her lesser-known names to him and promised to cause no harm anywhere those names were seen or heard. Reportedly thirteen or seventeen names were given, though more variations exist today, perhaps owing to the traditional lack of written vowels in Semitic languages.

Origins and Ancient Forms

Lilith's earliest incarnation seems to have been as a wind spirit from Mesopotamia associated with childbirth. The Burney Plaque (c.2300 BCE) is believed to portray Lilith: it depicts a beautiful winged woman flanked by large owls, naked but for her horned cap. She stands atop two lions on her bird's feet, holding the ring and rod of power. The plaque was discovered in what appeared to be a personal shrine.

The Huluppu Tree

Lilith may have early roots as a tree spirit. In the Sumerian myth The Huluppu Tree, "Dark Maid Lilith" lives in the sacred tree together with a snake and a sacred bird. When the formerly wild goddess Inanna makes the transition to an urban, settled, agricultural environment, she chops down the sacred tree Lilith calls home. Lilith flees, remaining a spirit of the wilderness. In Jewish folklore, she is still found living within trees.

Many Forms

According to her numerous legends, Lilith possesses many forms. She appears as an old hag or a beautiful young woman. Sometimes she is beautiful from head to waist with pure flame underneath. Sometimes she is a snake from the waist down. Often dressed in red, Lilith's long hair is alternately described as black or red: distinctive because it is beautiful, wildly disheveled, or both. In cultures where women bind or hide their hair, Lilith's is defiantly loose.

She manifests in animal form as a large black cat, an owl, or snake. Even in apparently human form, Lilith often displays aspects of a bird: feet, claws, or wings.

Avatars and Manifestations

Helen of Troy and the Queen of Sheba are reputedly among her avatars. Among Sephardic Jews, La Broosha ("the witch") is a euphemism for Lilith. Here she usually manifests as a large, black cat, which may account for the irrational fear that cats will suck a baby's breath out of its body.

The Children of Lilith

According to some legends, Lilith is infertile herself and burns with rage, hence her resentment of human mothers. In other legends, Lilith is the mother of a host of demons, the Lilin or Daughters of Lilith. Their father may be the fallen angel Samael or the dangerous spirit Asmodeus, both eventually identified with the devil. Alternatively, their father may be Adam, who allegedly reunited with Lilith after his expulsion from Eden. Another legend suggests Lilith is the original succubus: she secretly seduces men while they sleep, and their nocturnal secretions become her children.

The Snake in Eden

Christian legend would ultimately claim that the snake in the Garden of Eden was Lilith in disguise, spitefully returning to take revenge upon Adam and her replacement. Some now feel that perhaps she was attempting to share her knowledge with her sister.

Sacred Associations

Sacred Creatures: All wild animals but especially jackals, hyenas, wild cats, ostriches, snakes, and unicorns. The owl is her sacred bird and messenger.

Element: Lilith has all the bases covered, having associations with air, water, earth, and fire.

Planet: The Moon. Her strength increases with the waning of the moon.

Time: Solstices, equinoxes, and during the astrological sign of Scorpio.

Cultural Significance

Although known throughout Semitic areas, it is in Jewish folklore that Lilith survives strongest. She is a major player in Jewish fairy tales where she retains her goddess-like features, although whether she is a dangerous benefactor or a sometimes-benevolent demon is subject to interpretation.

Lilith continues to weave her spell: she is invoked in modern works of fiction more than any other witch-spirit, from Victorian novels to comic books like Vampirella. Feminists have adopted her as a role model, as have witches, Neo-Pagans, and Jewish-Pagans.

Name and Meaning

Her Hebrew name means "screech owl," which is cognate with strix or strega. "Lilith" is also related to the Semitic root word for "night."

Spirit Nature

No spirit possesses a more fabulous history than Lilith. She remains very vital and alive, feared and adored. Spirit of darkness, night, and feminine independence, Lilith is associated with witches, angels, djinn, demons, and vampires. Lilith is a bird woman with powerful associations with cats. In any form, she cannot be tamed.
