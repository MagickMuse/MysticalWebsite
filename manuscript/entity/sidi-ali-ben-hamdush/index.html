Venerated as a Sufi saint, Sidi Ali ben Hamdush is among the most renowned of Moroccan saints and patron of the Hamadsha Brotherhood. He is a miracle worker so powerful that he can command the sun itself.

Life and Legend

An 18th-century ascetic and scholar, Sidi Ali spent ten years in a corner of Qarwiyyin University in Fez, praying all night and fasting all day. He never moved. He never spoke to anyone. He repeated the Islamic profession of faith 18,000 times daily.

He later moved to the village of Beni Rachid on the southern face of Mount Zerhoun, the gateway to the Rif Mountains. There he developed a reputation as a miracle worker, drawing pilgrims from across North Africa.

A protocol governed interactions with the saint. Pilgrims kept a respectful distance, never sitting next to him or speaking directly to him. They simply stayed nearby while Sidi Ali listened to men discuss the Prophet. Eventually the spirit would move him to rise and begin dancing the hadra, a healing ecstatic dance that preceded his miracle working.

The Summoning of Aisha Qandisha

Sidi Ali directed his servant Sidi Ahmed Dghughi to fetch the powerful djinn Aisha Qandisha from the Sudan. He told Sidi Ahmed he would find a bundle of branches tied with rope. Sidi Ahmed closed his eyes, and when he opened them, he stood in the Sudan before that bundle of branches. He tugged it and Lalla Aisha appeared.

When Sidi Ahmed explained his urgency, saying his brother was dying, Lalla Aisha told him to climb on her back. Instantly they returned to Mount Zerhoun. Just as they climbed the steps to his cave, Sidi Ali died. Sidi Ahmed prepared his body for burial. Mourners and devotees founded lodges dedicated to Sidi Ali throughout North Africa.

The Miracle of the Sun

Sultan Moulay Ismail (reigned 1672-1727), nicknamed "the bloodthirsty," threatened to behead his barren wife if she did not conceive the very next day. That night, Sidi Ali appeared in her dreams. She visited his shrine with her husband's permission, though he threatened to behead her if she had not returned by sunset.

Sidi Ali performed two miracles for the queen: she conceived, and he stopped the sun from setting until she reached home safely.

The Hamadsha Brotherhood

The Hamadsha are a mystical healing spiritual brotherhood famed for bringing relief to those possessed by djinn or suffering from illness. They maintain close associations with the djinn Aisha Qandisha and Aisha Sudaniyya. Their rituals are considered extreme by most other Muslim groups. Hamadsha rites include drinking boiling water, eating spiny cactus, slashing their own heads with knives or razors during trance dances, and handling enormous snakes.

Sacred Sites

Sidi Ali is buried on Mount Zerhoun, sixteen miles from Meknes. His mausoleum is among the largest in Morocco. A spring flowing from the cliffs below his tomb possesses curative powers. Pilgrims drink from it, apply the water to their bodies, and bring some home.
