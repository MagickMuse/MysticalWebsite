The Avenger

Horus wears the falcon head and embodies kingship, sky, and justice. His mythology centers on vengeance: Set murdered his father Osiris, and Horus reclaimed the throne. Living pharaohs incarnated Horus; dead pharaohs became Osiris.

Hidden in the Marshes

Set killed Osiris and took power. Isis escaped while pregnant. She concealed herself in the papyrus swamps of the Nile Delta and gave birth to Horus there. Years passed. She raised him in hiding, deploying every magical technique she knew against Set's agents and marsh dangers: scorpions, serpents, crocodiles, fevers.

Egyptian protective magick references this narrative constantly. Healing spells invoke the image of the divine mother shielding her vulnerable child. The logic holds: magick sufficient to protect a god's son from the lord of chaos can protect anyone from anything.

The Contendings of Horus and Set

Adult Horus challenged Set. Their conflict extended eighty years and involved combat, competitions, and deception. Neither achieved decisive victory.

Set ripped out one of Horus's eyes during battle. Thoth (or Hathor, depending on the account) repaired it. The restored eye became the wedjat, among Egyptian magick's most potent protective symbols. It signifies healing, completeness, and the defeat of disorder.

Resolution came through divine tribunal. Neith delivered the verdict: Horus received Egypt's throne; Set received expanded territory and two goddess wives, Anat and Astarte. Ra had initially supported Set as the more seasoned candidate. The ruling stood regardless.

The Living Pharaoh

Horus's triumph created the template for Egyptian rulership. Each reigning pharaoh embodied Horus, the legitimate sovereign maintaining maat against chaotic forces. Royal authority derived from divine right established through victory over Set.

Role in Egyptian Magick

What Horus represents:

  • Legitimate inheritance and proper succession
  • Order prevailing over disorder
  • Defense of those who cannot defend themselves
  • Restoration and healing (symbolized by the wedjat)
  • Duty to family and justice for wrongs

Invoking Horus means invoking rightful sovereignty, the power that restores balance after disruption. The wedjat remains widely used on amulets, burial objects, and magickal implements.

Iconography

Horus takes falcon form, human form with falcon head, or child form (Harpocrates) nursing at Isis's breast. The double crown of unified Egypt adorns his brow, marking dominion over Upper and Lower kingdoms.
