The Holy Myrrh Bearers, venerated as saints by most branches of Christianity, are the patron saints of aromatherapists and those who prepare bodies for burial.

Overview

The Holy Myrrh Bearers are the women who, as described in the Gospel of Matthew 28:5–6, brought myrrh and other materials to the Garden of Gethsemane with the intention of anointing the dead body of Jesus. When they arrived, an angel told them the tomb was empty. The Myrrh Bearers are the subject of miraculous icons. Although each is also venerated separately, they are invoked as a group for blessings, protection, and spiritual guidance.

The Seven Myrrh Bearers

Although the Gospel of Matthew identifies the Myrrh Bearers only as Mary Magdalen and "the other Mary," the group is usually envisioned as larger. The seven women traditionally considered Holy Myrrh Bearers include:

  • Mary Magdalen
  • Mary Jacobe
  • Mary Salomé
  • Mary of Bethany
  • Martha of Bethany
  • Saint Susanna
  • Saint Joanna

Joseph of Arimathea and Nicodemus are also sometimes considered Myrrh Bearers.

Powers and Domains

The Myrrh Bearers represent devotion, courage, and faithful service. They are honored for their faithfulness and courage in approaching Christ's tomb when the male disciples had fled in fear. They are invoked by those who minister to the dying and dead, and by those who prepare bodies for burial.

Iconography

Three or more Myrrh Bearers walk or stand closely together. At least one carries an unguent pot. Sometimes Mary Magdalen is portrayed alone as the Myrrh Bearer.

Sacred Sites

Cathedral of the Holy Myrrh Bearers in Baku, Azerbaijan, but many other churches are also dedicated to them.

Feast Days

11 May. In the Eastern Orthodox and Greek Catholic Churches, the second Sunday after Easter is the Sunday of the Myrrh Bearers. Each Myrrh Bearer may also be venerated alone on her individual feast.
