The Sphinx God

Horemakhet ("Horus-in-the-Horizon") is the deity embodied by the Great Sphinx of Giza. A solar god combining aspects of Horus and Ra, he represents the sun at its rising on the eastern horizon.

The Dream Stela of Thutmose IV

The most famous account of Horemakhet comes from the Dream Stela, a 3.6-metre (12-foot) high granite monument erected between the paws of the Sphinx.

As a young prince, Thutmose fell asleep at midday in the shadow of the Sphinx, which by that time was buried to its shoulders in sand. In his dream, Horemakhet spoke to him:

The god promised Thutmose the throne of Egypt if he would clear away the sand that had drifted over his leonine body. Thutmose fulfilled this request and became pharaoh Thutmose IV (c. 1400 to 1390 BCE).

Significance for Dream Magick

This royal dream demonstrates the Egyptian belief that sleep was a state of consciousness in which meeting with a god bearing a positive or helpful message was possible. You didn't need to be royal to receive such messages, but the Thutmose episode shows how seriously the Egyptians took prophetic dreams.

Solar Symbolism

As a form of the rising sun, Horemakhet connects the earthly Sphinx, facing east toward the sunrise, with the celestial journey of Ra. The Sphinx's lion body evokes solar power and royal authority.
