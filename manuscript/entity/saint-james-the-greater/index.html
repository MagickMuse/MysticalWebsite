Classification

Venerated as a saint by most branches of Christianity.

Saint James the Greater is renowned for healing arthritis, rheumatism, and joint disorders.

Life

The Apostle James the Greater was the son of Mary Salome and the fisherman Zebedee. If Mary Salome is the daughter of Saint Anne, as many believe, then James and his brother John were Jesus' first cousins. Jesus nicknamed James and his brother the Sons of Thunder. He is a powerful, forceful saint invoked for protection and healing.

The Saragossa Apparition

Legends suggest that following the crucifixion of Jesus in 33 CE, James left Judea to travel. In 40 CE, he was in Saragossa, now part of modern Spain, where he witnessed an apparition of Mary being transported from Jerusalem on a throne carried by angels. James prostrated himself before the vision. Mary gave him a jasper column and a small statue of herself, saying: "This place is to be my home." In the context of the first century, the pillar and statue of a female divinity is far more reminiscent of Pagan practices, possibly Phoenician.

Martyrdom and Journey to Spain

After building a chapel in Saragossa as directed, James returned to Jerusalem where, in 44 CE, he became the first martyred Apostle, executed by King Herod Agrippa. His disciples placed his body aboard a ship with no sails or rudder that eventually reached Galicia, Spain. Wild bulls were delegated to select his burial place. They pulled for ten miles and then stopped in a field. James was buried there in a marble sarcophagus.

Eight centuries later, a hermit followed unusual stars to this field and discovered his grave. A shrine built on the site was named Compostela, short for Campo de Estrellas (Field of Stars).

The Scallop Shell

When Saint James miraculously saved a knight and his horse from drowning, the two emerged from the water covered in scallop shells. The grooves on a scallop shell are also symbolic of the roads leading to Compostela. Yet another theory suggests this emblem may predate Saint James: scallop shells have historically been emblems of water spirits, most famously Aphrodite. The region may have been dedicated to a Celtic water spirit before becoming a Christian pilgrimage site.

The Paths of Saint James

  • Saint James the Warrior/Peacemaker - doesn't negotiate; he makes and enforces peace. Invoked for protection, victory, and justice, especially for those who have been lynched or hung.
  • Saint James on Horseback - evolved into Santiago Matamoros. In Vodou and Santeria, syncretized to Ogun, the spirit of iron.
  • Saint James the Traveler/Pilgrim - invoked for healing, especially joint disorders like arthritis and rheumatism, and any condition obstructing mobility.

In any guise, he is invoked for a bountiful apple harvest and on behalf of apple trees.

Iconography

  • Warrior: knight in armor
  • Horseback: military garb, riding white horse, armed with lance or sword, trampling enemies
  • Traveler: dressed like a pilgrim with broad-brimmed hat (like Odin, Mercury, or Hermes) embellished with a scallop shell

Sacred Site

Cathedral of Saint James of Compostela in Galicia, northwestern Spain. In the medieval era, it was the third most popular Christian pilgrimage after Rome and Jerusalem. Currently over 100,000 visitors annually; tens of thousands walk the pilgrimage route.

The Way of Saint James

The most popular Western European pilgrimage route since the Middle Ages. Traditionally made on foot but may also be done by bicycle, horseback, or donkey. The route was once a Roman trade route nicknamed the Milky Way. The pilgrimage may take weeks or months depending on route and schedule.
