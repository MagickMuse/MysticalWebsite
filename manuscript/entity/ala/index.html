⚠️ Ala is venerated within Igbo and West African Indigenous traditions. These are closed practices and should only be approached by those within the originating culture or with explicit cultural permission.

Magickal Properties

Ala is "The Queen of Life and Death," daughter of Chi (the Supreme Creator) and the most significant Ibo spirit. She is mother and guardian of the Ibo people, their sacred custodian and a spirit of ethics and morality. Ala formed the Earth and provides all that sustains life. She is the Mistress of Life and Death who places babies into human wombs and receives the dead back into Earth, her womb.

Ritual Uses

Work with Ala for fertility, agricultural abundance, and ethical guidance. She places babies into human wombs during conception, and when people die, they return to Earth, her womb. She guards and rules buried ancestors. Create rituals honoring her role as both life-giver and receiver of the dead.

Offerings

Yams and brass items. As a spirit closely associated with Earth and agriculture, she appreciates offerings from the harvest, particularly yams which are sacred to her.

Favored People

Those seeking fertility, agricultural abundance, and ethical guidance. She is the sacred custodian of the Ibo people and favors those who live peacefully and morally.

Petition For

Human fertility and agricultural abundance, protection of buried ancestors, ethical guidance, bountiful harvests from both human wombs and Earth.

Warnings

She is the most significant Ibo spirit and sacred custodian. If her children are peaceful and well behaved, she provides bountiful harvests. Her favor depends on ethical and moral behavior.

Temple and Iconography

Her temple is the Mbari, which contains a cult statue depicting her seated with a child in her arms and adorned with the crescent moon. She is flanked by attendant deities. Painted clay representations traditionally depict her with a child seated on her knee, emphasizing her roles as mother and child-bringer.

Worship

She enjoys a profusion of local shrines among the Ibo communities of eastern Nigeria, well supplied with votive offerings. An annual yam festival is celebrated in her honor. Serious crimes including murder are considered offenses against her.

Additional Notes

She is associated with yam knives and brass leg bracelets. Earth is her element, and the hornbill is her sacred bird. When Earth first emerged from primeval chaos, it was Ala who decreed that the dead should be buried in Earth.
