Thérèse of Lisieux is renowned for healing AIDS, tuberculosis, and addiction. She also breaks curses and hexes.

Classification

Roman Catholic saint. Doctor of the Church.

Life and Legend

Thérèse (2 January 1873 to 30 September 1897) desperately wished to become a missionary in the Far East. Instead, she developed tuberculosis and never left her convent. She came from a devout, middle-class French family. Her mother died of breast cancer when Thérèse was four. As the youngest of the family, she was coddled by her father and four sisters. Her constitution was always delicate, leading her to call herself "the little flower."

At fifteen, she followed two older sisters into the Carmelite Order but proved a disastrous novice, lacking practical skills. She struggled with simple household chores and fell asleep during meditation. She was publicly called a disgrace before the entire congregation. Little sympathy was shown even after she developed tuberculosis.

Eventually, Thérèse found her calling. By age twenty-two, she was the convent's novice-mistress. She vowed to dedicate herself to love. Her sister suggested she write an autobiography, not realizing Thérèse only had three more years to live. Her autobiography, The Story of a Soul, became a bestselling sensation translated into thirty-eight languages.

She is the saint of small things who recommended the path of spiritual childhood (absolute surrender and trust). Her spiritual path is called "the Little Way." Thérèse wrote that it was not necessary for everyone to accomplish heroic feats to attain holiness; it was sufficient only to love.

Death and Legacy

Thérèse died of tuberculosis at age twenty-five. Miracles were almost immediately attributed to her intercession. She was held up as a role model for young women. Although she had tuberculosis, she slept in a freezing cold cell without ever complaining or requesting warmer coverings. She was canonized in 1925.

Iconic French singer Edith Piaf (1915–1963) credited Saint Thérèse of Lisieux with miraculously healing her complete blindness following a pilgrimage to her grave.

Domains

The Little Flower is a powerful saint credited with many miracles:

  • Protectress and matron of those with AIDS and tuberculosis, petitioned for healing, alleviation, and prevention
  • Invoked for healing anything related to addiction, including drugs and alcohol
  • Petitioned in magickal spells for true love and romantic assistance
  • Called upon for protection against vicious, wicked, malicious people
  • Breaks curses, hexes, and malevolent spells

The Rose Promise

Thérèse promised: "I will spend my Heaven doing good on earth." She vowed to send a rose to those who invoke or petition her as a sign that she has heard their plea and that their prayers will be answered.

Iconography

The Little Flower holds a bouquet of roses.

Spiritual Allies

Canonization is in process for Thérèse's parents, Louis and Zèlie Martin, and her sisters.

Sacred Sites

Numerous pilgrimage sites are associated with Thérèse:

  • The entire town of Lisieux in northwestern France has evolved into a pilgrimage site. Her relics are carried in an annual procession through town on the last Sunday in September.
  • Her relics are housed in the chapel of the Carmelite Convent in Lisieux, the same chapel where Thérèse herself prayed.
  • The Basilica of Saint Thérèse in Lisieux, where her parents are buried in the crypt.
  • Les Buissonets, the house where Thérèse and her family lived for eleven years following her mother's death, is now open to the public.

Related Entities
