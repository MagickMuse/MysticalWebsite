Sodasi (the girl of sixteen) is one of the ten Mahavidyas and is identified in many lists with Tripura Sundari (the beautiful one of the three worlds), also known as Lalita. She represents the Devi as supreme beauty and the highest expression of Shakta wisdom.

Iconography

Sodasi is a girl of sixteen with a red complexion. She is shown astride the prone body of Shiva, with whom she is having intercourse. They rest on a pedestal composed of the gods Brahma, Vishnu, Rudra, and Indra, the four major deities of the Hindu pantheon serving as a literal platform beneath them. This iconography is a direct statement of Shakta theology: the goddess and her consort rest upon the male divine, which serves as her foundation and support.

Nature and Significance

The number sixteen carries multiple layers of meaning. It is the age of maximum beauty and sexual vitality, and it is also the number of syllables in the sri-vidya mantra, the supreme Tantric mantra of the Shakta tradition. In many Shakta lineages Sodasi or Tripura Sundari is regarded as the highest form of the Mahavidyas, the one in whom beauty, power, desire, and liberation converge.

The posture astride Shiva is shared by several fierce goddesses and represents the female deity as the active, dominant principle. Shiva beneath her is inert without her energy. She supplies the sakti that animates him.

The pedestal of four gods asserts the Mahadevi's supremacy not only over Shiva but over all the major male deities simultaneously.

As a Mahavidya

Sodasi is one of the ten forms Sati assumes to surround and terrify Shiva in the Mahavidya origin myth. The Mahavidyas assert the Devi's autonomous power and independence. Sodasi contributes the overwhelming authority of beauty and desire wielded as sovereign force.
