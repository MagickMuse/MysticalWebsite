Magickal Properties

  • Great Paralyzer who stills tongues, stops slander, and halts hostile speech and action
  • Grants verbal power, eloquence, and word‑based protection and counter‑cursing

Ritual Uses

  • Petition to stop gossip, legal calumny, and harmful speech
  • Workings for eloquence, courtroom speech, debate, and mantra potency
  • Defensive sorcery to arrest an aggressor’s actions or words

Offerings

  • Yellow flowers, incense, candles; milagros depicting mouths/tongues; miniature hammers and wooden pegs

Favored People

Witches and sorcerers specializing in word‑magick and mantra; those beset by libel or hostile speech

Petition For

Silencing of slander, protection from verbal harm, domination over hostile speech, eloquence, health, prosperity, courage

Warnings

Treat her with strict protocol and respect; she can be irritable and foul‑tempered. Undertake domination or vengeance only with robust protections and clear ethical responsibility.

As a Mahavidya

Bagalamukhi is listed among the ten Mahavidyas as Bagala. The standard Mahavidya description: she has the head of a crane, is seated on a throne of jewels, is yellow, holds a club with which she beats an enemy, and with another hand pulls her enemy's tongue. This concise iconography concentrates her essential power: absolute silencing and physical paralysis of opponents.

Within the Mahavidya origin myth she is one of the ten forms Sati assumes to overwhelm Shiva and force his permission for her to attend Daksha's sacrifice. The Mahavidyas do not primarily defend cosmic order; they assert the Devi's autonomous supremacy. Bagala contributes the power of paralysis and domination: stambhana (causing paralysis), uccatana (causing a person to cease their actions), and mohana (causing infatuation and confusion) are the specific Mahavidya ritual powers that map most directly onto her domain.

Additional Notes

  • Often depicted grasping the enemy's tongue and striking with a mallet
  • Lunar and yellow currents; rites commonly kept at night in disciplined silence
