Magickal Properties

Chandra, Lord of the Moon, is the divine ruler of lunar energies and fertility who controls the moonbeams that possess magickal power to stimulate pregnancy. He governs the astrological sign Cancer and rules over the twenty-seven mansions of the moon in Vedic astrology. His mythic cycle of waxing joyful as he approaches his beloved Rohini and waning when forced to leave her reflects the eternal lunar rhythm.

Ritual Uses

Petition Chandra for assistance with fertility and conception, as his moonbeams stimulate pregnancy. Astrologers and students of astrology invoke him for guidance in learning the lunar arts. He is particularly helpful for understanding the mansions of the moon and their influence on human destiny. Lunar practitioners work with him during proper moon hours for enhanced magickal power.

Offerings

Club and lotus flowers representing his divine attributes. Make offerings during proper lunar hours when his power is strongest. Honor the Star Maidens who are his wives with acknowledgment of their celestial roles. Present items that reflect lunar energy and the beauty of the night sky.

Favored People

Astrologers and those learning the astrological arts, people seeking fertility assistance especially women hoping to conceive, those born under the sign of Cancer, lunar practitioners and moon magic workers, students of Vedic wisdom.

Petition For

Fertility assistance especially stimulating pregnancy through lunar power, astrological guidance and instruction in reading celestial signs, understanding of cosmic timing and fate, enhanced lunar magickal abilities, harmony in relationships despite competing obligations.

Warnings

Respect the cosmic order of his monthly visits to each Star Maiden wife. Do not attempt to force or coerce emotional or fertility outcomes. Work within natural lunar cycles rather than demanding immediate results. Honor his primary devotion to Rohini while acknowledging his obligations to all twenty-seven wives.

Additional Notes

Chandra manifests as a youthful, fair, and very handsome man. He rides an antelope or drives a chariot pulled by ten white horses, with rabbit and antelope as his sacred animals. His primary consorts are Rohini (identified with the star Aldebaran) and Tara, though he wed all twenty-seven Star Sisters who represent the mansions of the moon. His story reflects the eternal tension between cosmic duty and personal desire.

Chandra as Dikpala of the North

Chandra holds an office within the dikpala system, the guardians of the directions of the Hindu cosmos, as the guardian of the north. In this role he stands alongside Indra of the east, Yama of the south, Varuna of the west, and their fellow dikpalas of the intermediate quarters, sharing the northern direction with Kubera in some enumerations and holding it in his own right in others. His whiteness, his sacred rope, and his prayer wheel belong to this directional office as much as to his lunar office proper.

Consorts and Family

Alongside Rohini and Tara, Chandra's consorts include Kaumundi, and he is understood as the husband of the twenty-seven naksatras, the astral goddesses of the lunar mansions, taken collectively. His son by Tara is Budha, the planet-god Mercury, and Budha's own names Candraja and Candrasuta preserve this lineage as the child of the moon.

Attributes and the Soma Cup

His attributes include the club, the lotus, the sacred rope, and the prayer wheel. The term candra itself often refers to the cup that holds the sacrificial yellow beverage soma, and in this usage the name of the god and the name of the ritual cup become one and the same, so that soma the drink, soma the deity, and candra the moon-cup are all facets of a single reality. Chandra is at once the pale yellow disc of the moon in the sky and the pale yellow soma in the sacrificial vessel below.

Chandra in Buddhist Tradition

Chandra is also honored as a planet-god within the Buddhist traditions of India and the Himalayan region, where he is depicted as white in color, attended by a goose, and holding as his chief attribute the moon disc set upon a lotus. In this Buddhist form he is one of the navagraha, the nine planetary powers, taken over from the Hindu astrological cosmos into the wider Buddhist reckoning of the heavens.
