Overview

Cautha is a sun god of the Etruscan religion, the pre-Roman religion of ancient Etruria in what is now central Italy. His iconography shows him at the moment of the sunrise, rising from the sea, wearing the sun disc as his crown and holding fire in each of his hands, so that the god appears at once as the sun itself and as the bearer of the sun's fire. He belongs to the Etruscan pantheon alongside Tinia the sky father, Uni the sovereign mother, and the wider company of Etruscan gods, and his cult is one of the older solar cults of the Italian peninsula, older than the Roman religion that later spread across the same land.

Classification

Sun god of the Etruscan religion. Pre-Roman Italic solar deity.

Domains

The rising sun, the dawn coming out of the sea, the sacred fire carried in the hands of the god, the daily passage from night into day, the illumination of the world by the returning light, the mystery of the sun that dies at evening and rises again at morning.

Iconography

Depicted at the moment of the sunrise, rising from the sea, with a sun disc crown upon his head and fire held in each of his two hands. His image is preserved on Etruscan bronze mirrors and on other objects of Etruscan ritual art, and the rising-from-the-sea posture is his most distinctive iconographic feature, marking him as a god whose proper moment is not the noon of the sun's strength but the dawn of its return.

Attributes

Sun disc crown, fire held in each hand, the sea from which he rises, occasionally a torch or a solar chariot in later syncretic forms.

Sacred Sites

Etruscan sanctuaries across the ancient territory of Etruria, particularly along the Tyrrhenian coast where the sunrise-from-the-sea imagery held its most vivid meaning. Etruscan mirrors bearing his image have been found across the wider region of Etruscan settlement, indicating a cult that extended beyond the coast into the inland Etruscan territories.

Ritual

Honored in the dawn rites of the Etruscan religion, when the god's proper moment of rising was marked by the appropriate offerings and prayers. The Etruscans held a highly developed religious system with detailed rites for the different moments of the day and the seasons, and Cautha's morning office belonged to that framework alongside the offices of the other solar and celestial gods.

Lore

The Etruscan religion is known to modern practitioners chiefly through its iconography and its epigraphic traces, since the surviving Etruscan literature is very limited. Cautha's image, preserved on the bronze mirrors that were among the most important ritual objects of Etruscan domestic religion, gives one of the clearest pictures of the pre-Roman Italian understanding of the sun: a young god rising fresh from the sea each morning, carrying fire in his hands, wearing the sun disc as his crown, and moving into the day with the sacred light. This image belongs to the wider Mediterranean pattern of the rising sun god, but it holds its own distinctive Etruscan form, marked by the two hands of fire and by the coastal Etruscan feeling for the sea as the place from which the light returns.
