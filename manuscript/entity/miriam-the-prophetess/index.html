Classification

Jewish saint.

Life and History

Miriam was a prophetess, the eldest of the three children of Amram and Jochebed, and the sister of Moses and Aaron. She played a crucial role in saving her baby brother Moses when he was placed in a basket on the Nile to escape Pharaoh's decree. She watched over him and, when Pharaoh's daughter discovered him, cleverly suggested their own mother as a wet nurse.

The Song at the Sea

After the Israelites crossed the Red Sea, Miriam led the women in song and dance, celebrating their deliverance. The "Song of Miriam" (Exodus 15:21) is considered one of the oldest passages in the Hebrew Bible:

"Sing to the Lord, for He has triumphed gloriously; horse and rider He has thrown into the sea."

Miriam's Well

According to Jewish tradition, a miraculous well accompanied the Israelites throughout their forty years in the wilderness, providing water in the desert. This well existed in Miriam's merit, and when she died, the well disappeared, causing a water crisis. The well is said to still exist, hidden in the Sea of Galilee, and those who drink from it are healed.

Death and Ascension

Miriam entered Heaven alive. According to tradition, the Shekhina (Divine Presence) transported her with a kiss, one of the most peaceful deaths described in Jewish tradition. She died at Kadesh and was buried there.

The Name

Her name, Miriam, has been interpreted variously as meaning "mistress of the sea," "drop of the sea," or "myrrh of the sea." The name Mary derives from Miriam.

Connection to Alchemy

Miriam the Prophetess is identified with Maria Prophetissa (Maria the Jewess), a legendary alchemist from the Hellenistic period. She is credited with inventing several alchemical apparatus, including the bain marie (water bath), still used in cooking and chemistry today and named in her honor.

Sacred Site

Her traditional burial place at Kadesh.
