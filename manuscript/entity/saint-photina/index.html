Saint Photina earned her place among Christian saints through a remarkable conversion that began with a shipwreck and an encounter with a hermit determined to avoid all contact with women.

Life and History

Almost nothing survives about her origins. She was likely young, perhaps still a teenager, when she boarded a vessel that would meet disaster somewhere along the Turkish coastline. Her destination and companions remain lost to history. When the ship broke apart, she clung to floating debris until currents deposited her on a tiny rock jutting from the sea.

The Encounter with Martinian

This barren outcrop had one inhabitant: a Christian hermit named Martinian, who had chosen this extreme isolation specifically to escape temptation. Women represented his greatest spiritual challenge, so finding a beautiful young survivor on his rock presented an unwelcome test.

Despite his dismay, compassion guided his actions. He calculated that rescue ships passed the area every two months and gave Photina his entire store of bread and fresh water, enough to sustain her until help arrived. His parting advice was to remain calm. Then he walked deliberately into the waves, choosing potential death over remaining alone with her. Dolphins carried him safely to the mainland.

Conversion and Sainthood

Martinian's willingness to die rather than compromise his vows made a profound impression on Photina. She devoted the rest of her days to prayer and spiritual contemplation. The Church honors her alongside Martinian on their shared feast day.
