Unofficial saint venerated in Argentina for her role as an advocate for justice.

Life and Death

María Soledad Morales was a seventeen-year-old schoolgirl from Catamarca, Argentina who dreamed of becoming a model. She was born 19 September 1973 and died 9 September 1990. In September 1990, she attended a party where she was drugged, assaulted, and murdered by two young men from privileged backgrounds. Her killers mutilated her face in an attempt to prevent identification before abandoning her body by the roadside.

The Fight for Justice

One of the perpetrators was revealed to be the son of a prominent national congressman. A cover-up was attempted, and arrests only occurred after massive social protests erupted. The principal of María's high school organized silent Thursday evening marches, establishing a new form of protest in Argentina. The men were finally brought to trial in 1996, but a mistrial was declared. Conviction and incarceration came in 1998 after years of sustained public pressure.

Veneration

Years of protests combined with national television coverage kept María Soledad in the public consciousness. She emerged as an unofficial saint invoked for justice, particularly by those facing powerful adversaries. She is considered an extremely persistent advocate who does not abandon those who seek her help.

Iconography

A school photograph serves as her most beloved image.

Sacred Site

Her grave shrine in Catamarca, Argentina.
