Overview

Caelestis, also called Dea Caelestis or Juno Caelestis, is a Roman sky and mother goddess of the North African provinces, honored above all in the ancient city of Carthage and its surrounding territory. She is the Roman inheritor and adaptation of the Punic goddess Tanit, and after the Roman conquest of Carthage her worship spread through the empire and eventually became one of the most important cults of Roman Africa. In the imperial period she was recognized as a supreme feminine sky power, associated with sovereignty, fertility, and the celestial vault itself.

Syncretism

Caelestis was identified with a range of local and imperial goddesses across the empire. In North Africa she carried forward the theology and iconography of Tanit, the celestial queen of Carthage. In Britain she was joined to the tutelary goddess of the Brigantes, giving the composite name Caelestis Brigantia attested on an altar at Corbridge in Northumberland. She was also brought under the umbrella of Juno as Juno Caelestis, and further identified with Cybele and with the Virgo of the astronomical zodiac. Elsewhere in the empire she was drawn into the cult of Aphrodite-Venus, extending her celestial mother-goddess character across additional provincial devotion and folding her into the wider Mediterranean family of great love and sky goddesses.

Rome and the Tarpeian Hill

Caelestis was brought to Rome in the form of an abstract block of stone, in the same manner that Kybele had been carried from Pessinus into the city, and her cult became popular there during the early part of the third century AD. Annual games were held in her honor. In this Roman guise she was known as the mighty protectress of the Tarpeian hill, joining the tutelary powers of the Capitoline landscape to her older celestial and maternal office. The choice of a plain stone as her cult image links her presentation directly to the aniconic tradition of the Anatolian mountain mother, so that when Romans looked upon her image they saw the same kind of sacred stone that had once travelled from Phrygia.

Domains

Caelestis presides over the sovereignty of the sky, over the ancestral mother of Carthage and its diaspora, over the syncretic recognition that a great goddess of one people may be met and honored in the great goddess of another, and over the celestial powers that watch above the empire and its provinces.
