Magickal Properties

Bosatsu is the Japanese variant of the Sanskrit Bodhisattva, meaning "Enlightened One" or "Enlightened Being." Prominent Bosatsu include Amida, Jizo, and Kannon. From the orthodox religious perspective, Bosatsu refers specifically to enlightened beings, but in popular folk religion, the definition becomes more nebulous. Distinctions between Bosatsu and Kami sometimes blur, with spirits like Jizo and Kannon (technically Bosatsu) often venerated and treated like Kami, while conversely the Kami Hachiman is sometimes envisioned in Buddhist monk garb and treated like a Bosatsu.

Ritual Uses

Bosatsu may be male or female and play prominent roles in Tibetan Buddhism, where images of the sacred feminine are ubiquitous in contrast to other Buddhist cultures. They are approached for spiritual guidance and assistance with both spiritual and worldly matters.

Offerings

Rice, fruit juices, white and yellow flowers.

Favored People

Those seeking enlightenment and spiritual guidance in Japanese folk religion, particularly practitioners who blend Buddhist and Shinto traditions.

Petition For

Spiritual guidance, enlightenment, and assistance with worldly concerns when orthodox Buddhist practice becomes more flexible in folk religious contexts.

Warnings

Distinctions between Bosatsu and Kami sometimes blur in popular folk religion, which may not align with orthodox Buddhist or academic perspectives.

Additional Notes

In folk religious practice, the definition of Bosatsu may be more nebulous than the orthodox Buddhist understanding. The blending of Buddhist Bosatsu with Shinto Kami reflects the syncretic nature of Japanese folk religion.
