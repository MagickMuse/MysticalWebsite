Jewish saint and staple of Jewish folklore, one of the rare figures who ascended to Heaven without dying. Her name is pronounced ser-akh, with a hard guttural concluding "h" (like the German "ach").

Jacob's Blessing

When the biblical Joseph was reunited with the brothers who had sold him into slavery, he sent them to bring their elderly father Jacob to Egypt. The brothers faced a dilemma: none wanted to deliver the shocking news that Joseph was alive. Their solution was to delegate the task to Asher's young daughter Serah. When the child told Jacob that Joseph lived, the patriarch blessed her: "May death never rule over you, for you brought my spirit back to life."

Jacob's blessing proved extraordinarily potent. Serah outlived Methuselah. According to Jewish folklore, she remained on earth until at least the ninth century and is among the select few who never died but entered Heaven alive. Other members of this elite group include Enoch, Miriam, Moses, and Elijah.

Biblical Significance

Three biblical references listing Asher's sons explicitly name Serah as their sister, making her the only female included in that genealogical chart. These references, found in Genesis 46:17, Numbers 26:46, and I Chronicles 7:30, mark her as uniquely significant, since virtually all other biblical sisters and daughters remain anonymous.

Witness to History

Serah's extraordinary lifespan made her a living witness to the entire arc of Israelite history. She accompanied Jacob to Egypt for the reunion with Joseph, then crossed the Red Sea during the Exodus. Between these events, she worked as a midwife and was enslaved alongside the children of Israel, forced to labor in a mill. Her presence at key moments proved invaluable:

  • Having witnessed the hiding of her Uncle Joseph's coffin, she later revealed its location to Moses
  • According to legend, when the Creator personally parted the Red Sea, Serah and Moses were among the very few to gaze upon the divine presence and survive
  • She revealed Jerusalem's Foundation Stone to King David
  • She helped Jeremiah conceal the Temple vessels after the fall of the First Temple
  • When the Tribes of Israel were dispersed, she went into exile with the tribe of Asher

Heavenly Role

In Heaven, Serah presides over the Hall of Caregivers, a magnificent palace where she welcomes the souls of women who devoted their earthly lives to caring for the elderly and infirm.

Departure from Earth

Serah's last known address was Isfahan, Persia, where she gained renown as a miracle worker and healer. Two versions describe her departure from the earthly plane:

The Fiery Chariot: In the ninth century, Serah was inside Isfahan's synagogue when a fiery chariot descended from Heaven to retrieve her. Witnesses saw the synagogue wreathed in flames that vanished without causing any damage. Only Serah was missing. The synagogue was renamed in her honor.

The Fire: The Jews of Isfahan claimed Serah died in a twelfth-century fire. Until the end of the nineteenth century, her mausoleum was a popular pilgrimage destination.

Sacred Site

Her grave is believed to lie in the Serah bat Asher cemetery in the Iranian town of Kukuli, between Isfahan and Shiraz.
