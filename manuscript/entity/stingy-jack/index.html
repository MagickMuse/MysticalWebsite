The Stingy Wanderer

Stingy Jack was a man who hated to pay for anything, though he had plenty of money. He would not even spend coin on a horse or a cart, and walked the road instead. His meanness gave him his name, but it was his cleverness that gave him his fate.

The First Trick

Walking the road one day, Stingy Jack met the Devil coming the other way. Jack invited him for a drink and, knowing the Devil meant him harm, set out to beat him at his own game. He goaded the Devil into proving his shapeshifting by turning into a tree, then a rabbit, then a gold coin. The moment the Devil became the coin, Jack pocketed him beside a silver cross, which kept him from changing back. Jack drank on credit at the pub with the Devil burning in his pocket, and freed him only after the Devil agreed to leave him alone for a year and to forfeit all claim on his soul.

The Second Trick

A year and an hour later, the Devil came knocking. Jack said he was just heading to the orchard to pick fruit, and led him to the finest tree, where the biggest apple grew high in the branches. When the Devil sprang up to fetch it, Jack carved a cross into the trunk and trapped him there. He let the Devil down only after another bargain: ten more years of peace, and no claim on his soul at the end.

Wandering With a Coal

Before the ten years were up, Jack died. God would not have him in heaven for cavorting with the Devil. The Devil, bound by his promise, refused him in hell. Jack was sent out to wander in eternal darkness with only a single burning coal for light, carried inside a carved turnip. People who saw him passing in the dark would say, "There goes old Jack of the lantern."

The Jack-o'-Lantern

Stingy Jack is the origin of the jack-o'-lantern. Long before pumpkins were brought into Ireland, turnips were hollowed and lit and set in doorways and windowsills on Samhain night to ward off Jack himself, the Devil, and the other roaming spirits that walk when the veil is thin. See Turnip for the lantern tradition and the older Samhain practice of carrying blessed coals from the bonfire to the hearth.
