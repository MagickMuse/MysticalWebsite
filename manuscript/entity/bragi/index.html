Magickal Properties

Bragi is the Norse Lord of Poetry, renowned for his wisdom and eloquence. He always knows the right words and the right way to express them. Bragi is apparently Odin's son, though his mother is unknown. She may be Gunlod, guardian of the mead of poetry who was seduced by Odin. Bragi possesses exceptional oracular abilities and can provide guidance through his mastery of language and expression.

Ritual Uses

Bragi may be invoked for advice and assistance, particularly when seeking wisdom or the right words for important communications. His expertise in poetry and eloquent expression makes him valuable for those who need to communicate effectively.

Offerings

Mead, poetry, stories and beautiful writing.

Favored People

Those seeking wisdom, eloquence, and advice, particularly poets, writers, and anyone who needs to communicate effectively.

Petition For

Wisdom, eloquence, knowing the right words and right way to express them in any situation.

Warnings

Bragi and Loki are enemies.

Additional Notes

Bragi's consort is Idunn, the goddess who keeps the apples of immortality for the gods of Asgard. His parentage connects him to Odin and possibly to Gunlod, the guardian of the mead of poetry, linking him directly to the sources of poetic inspiration in Norse mythology.

Encyclopedia Source Notes

Bragi is a Nordic god of poetry whose name is translated as poet or leader, and whose recorded worship belongs to the Viking period, circa the seventh century onward, continuing until the Christianization of the Nordic lands around the eleventh century. He is known chiefly from the Icelandic codices and from the Prose Edda of Snorri Sturluson. He carries the epithet of the long bearded one, and no dedicated cult center is preserved for him.

In Snorri's account he is a son of Odin and the consort of Idunn, the goddess who keeps the apples of immortality. Some scholars have suggested that Bragi may also serve as a pseudonym for Odin himself in his aspect as sovereign of the poetic art. He is often found in the company of Aegir, the great sea-host, at the hall where the gods gathered to drink mead.

The Cup of Bragi

The cup over which oaths were sworn in the Nordic world was known as the cup of Bragi, and its name preserved his standing as the god who ratified the binding word. In the hall of the slain, Valhalla, Bragi was seen as the poet and orator, the speaker who set the terms of the ceremony and gave voice to the honor of the fallen warriors gathered there.

No carvings of Bragi survive under his name, though it is likely that he is the subject of some anonymous poetic carvings whose inscriptions have been lost or worn away.
