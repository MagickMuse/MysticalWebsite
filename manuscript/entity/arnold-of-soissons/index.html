Arnold of Soissons is the patron saint of brewers and hop cultivators.

Classification

Roman Catholic saint.

Life and History

Arnold, born in approximately 1040 near Brabant, Flanders, was a soldier before joining the Benedictine Abbey at Soissons, France. For three years, he lived as a hermit but was eventually appointed abbot. He tried to avoid this honor by fleeing, but a wolf forced him to return and accept.

In 1080, he became bishop of Soissons, again against his own desires. When a rival occupied his see, Arnold took the opportunity to retire. He founded the Abbey of Saint Peter in Oudenberg, West Flanders, later destroyed during the French Revolution.

Brewing Innovations

There, he began to brew beer, encouraging local peasants to drink it rather than water, which was at that time potentially a health hazard.

When an abbey brewery's roof collapsed, Arnold asked God to multiply the stores of beer left for the monks' consumption. His prayers were answered, earning him a reputation as a miracle worker while still alive.

While weaving bee skeps for the abbey's apiary, Arnold realized that they could be used as a filter to further clarify beer.
