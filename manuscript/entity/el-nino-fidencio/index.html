El Niño Fidencio is an unofficial saint and curandero renowned for healing cancer, tuberculosis, leprosy, mental illness, intestinal disorders, blindness, and performing surgery without anesthesia. Fidencismo is officially recognized as a religion in Mexico.

Life and History

José Fidencio Sintora Constantino (1898-1938) was born in Guanajuato, Mexico to a large, poor family. He is known as El Niño ("the boy") for three reasons:

  • His healing skills manifested in childhood; the first person he cured was his mother
  • Many devotees perceive him as an incarnation of Jesus Christ (manifestations of the Christ Child are called El Niño in Spanish)
  • He maintained a childlike persona and appearance his whole life, with a high voice, no facial hair, and reportedly the genitalia of a prepubescent boy

The Vision

In 1927, around age twenty-nine, El Niño experienced a profound mystic vision while weeping beneath a pepper tree in Espinazo, Nuevo León. A sacred being (possibly Christ or an angel) appeared, calling him to service and blessing him with extraordinary healing powers.

Healing Methods

El Niño was credited with healing cancer, tuberculosis, leprosy, mental illness, intestinal disorders, and blindness. He delivered babies and performed successful surgery without anesthesia.

His methods included:

  • Traditional medicinals and folk cures
  • A garden of over 200 herbs
  • Herbal baths and nutritional therapy
  • Laughter, singing, and dancing
  • Rolling people in dirt
  • Sitting the mentally ill on children's swings
  • Massage with saliva or a paste of honey, herbs, soot, tar, and soap
  • "Shock therapy" (a deaf-mute was cured by an encounter with his pet cougar)

Beyond Healing

El Niño helped the lovelorn and mediated family, legal, and real estate disputes. With no church or priest in Espinazo, he administered baptism, confirmation, communion, marriages, and last rites, preaching that anyone can baptize a child if done in God's name.

He practiced astral projection, yoga, and meditation. He guarded his virginity zealously, believing its loss would rob him of healing powers. He sometimes disguised himself as a woman to escape overwhelming demands.

Fame and Persecution

In February 1928, Mexico's president visited him. One month later, 30,000 people camped in Espinazo seeking his attention.

This attracted negative attention. In 1929, Nuevo León began legal proceedings against him for practicing medicine without a license. He was arrested twice. In 1936, the archbishop of Monterrey demanded he stop administering sacraments; he agreed but kept doing it anyway.

Mysterious Death

By 1935, his health was failing (possibly pernicious anemia or psychic exhaustion). Seeking to heal and recharge, he entered a three-day trance. Express orders were given not to disturb his body.

Someone either panicked or, as devotees believe, jealous physicians seized their opportunity. An autopsy or embalming was begun and his throat was slit. When cut, fresh blood spurted out, the blood of a living person.

Continuing Ministry

A curandero in life, he continues healing in death. He communicates via dreams, visions, and clairaudience (hearing his voice). He speaks only Spanish but works with interpreters. He has manifested to perform healings even for those unfamiliar with him.

Color

Purple.

Sacred Site

His shrine in Espinazo, Mexico.

Spiritual Ally

El Niño Fidencio was a great devotee of Guadalupe. They may be venerated together.

Immigration and Legal Matters

El Niño Fidencio has become a specialist in immigration matters, reportedly speeding the processing of official paperwork and helping keep unwanted scrutiny from authorities at a distance.

How to Work with El Niño Fidencio

Display his image in your working space. Offer fresh flowers. He loved flowers and used them in his cures. Light a white candle or a commercially produced El Niño Fidencio devotional candle. Speak from the heart and tell him exactly what you need. Make the customary vow: that when you are able, you will provide assistance to someone else who faces the same circumstances you are now in. For very large favors, a vow to make a pilgrimage to his shrine in Espinazo, Mexico may be appropriate.

Feast Days

  • 17 October (birthday)
  • 19 October (death)
  • 19 March (feast of Saint Joseph, his patron and name saint)
