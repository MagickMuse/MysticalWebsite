Parvati

Bhutamata, whose name means mother of goblins, is a terrible goddess of the Hindu tradition. She is a frightful form of Parvati, one of the wrathful aspects in which the great goddess assumes command over the classes of spirits and bhutas that populate the wild edges of the world.

Domains

Rulership over the goblin classes and the spirits of the wild edges, the maternal authority that binds the frightful hosts, and the terrible aspect of the great goddess when she turns her face toward the untamed world.

Iconography

Bhutamata is accompanied by a lion, the traditional attendant of Parvati in her fierce forms. Her attributes are a shield, a sword, and, set upon her head, a phallus, a mark of her authority over the generative forces held even within her most terrifying manifestation.

Relation to Parvati

Bhutamata is not a separate goddess but one of the specific frightful forms Parvati assumes when she takes command of the goblin hosts and the wilder classes of spirits. Her lion companion and her wrathful bearing place her in the same iconographic family as Durga and Kali, the other fierce forms through which Parvati manifests as protector against demonic threats.

Precautions

Bhutamata is invoked in the mode of the terrible mother, not the gentle consort. Approach her for the binding and command of unruly forces, not for tender or domestic matters, and address her with the awe due to the great goddess in her wrathful form.
