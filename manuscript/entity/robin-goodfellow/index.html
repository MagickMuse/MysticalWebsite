Magickal Properties

Robin Goodfellow is a huge hairy goat-man with horns, hooves, and carrying a besom broom. He was the star of the 1628 bestseller The Mad Pranks and Merry Jests of Robin Goodfellow. Medieval woodcuts depict a massive Robin presiding over circle dances with donkey's ears, bull's horns, carrying a broom in one hand and a lit candle in the other, with another horn slung over his shoulder.

The son of a mortal woman and Oberon the Fairy King, Robin is a notorious wild man and shape-shifter. As a trickster spirit, he works together with Oberon and other spirits to maintain human fertility and the earth's seasonal rhythms.

Ritual Uses

Invoke Robin Goodfellow for fertility magick and maintaining connection to seasonal cycles. Work with him for trickster magick that disrupts stagnation and brings necessary change. His presence in circle dances suggests he can be called upon for ecstatic ritual work and community celebrations.

Offerings

Traditional offerings to trickster spirits and fertility deities: honey, mead, ale, bread, seasonal foods.

Favored People

Those seeking fertility, seasonal abundance, connection to earth's natural rhythms, those who honor the trickster path.

Petition For

Human fertility, maintenance of seasonal rhythms, earth's abundance, assistance with trickster magick and necessary disruption.

Warnings

Robin is a notorious wild man and shape-shifter, so approach with respect for his unpredictable trickster nature. He maintains earth's fertility and seasonal cycles, so petitions should honor these natural patterns rather than attempting to force or control them.

Additional Notes

Manifestation: Huge hairy goat-man with horns and hooves, donkey's ears, bull's horns, carrying besom broom and lit candle

Parentage: Son of Oberon (Fairy King) and mortal woman

Literary Fame: Star of 1628 bestseller The Mad Pranks and Merry Jests of Robin Goodfellow

Iconography: Medieval woodcuts show him presiding over circle dances

Sacred Role: Works with Oberon and other spirits to ensure human fertility and earth's seasonal rhythms
