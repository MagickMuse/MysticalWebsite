Senjak (pronounced sen-zhahk) is the Kreyol variation of Saint Jacques, French for Saint James. The traditional chromolithograph of Saint James the Greater is used to represent Senjak, but whether Senjak actually is Saint James remains ambiguous.

Domains

Senjak is considered the best possible ally when engaging in battle, whether literal or metaphoric. He is invoked for victory and courage.

Iconography

The traditional chromolithograph depicts Saint James triumphant in battle, riding a white horse, accompanied by a knight in armor. The image of Senjak appears frequently on Haitian sequined flags. His attributes are sword and shield.

Colors

Red

Spiritual Allies

Ezili Dantor, the great Vodou matriarch spirit, is his primary spiritual ally.

Lore

Africans imported to Haiti as slaves were forbidden to venerate their own spirits, so they did so secretly by identifying African spirits with chromolithographs of Catholic saints. Saint James the Greater is a warrior spirit of justice, a knight in shining armor, making him a perfect match for Ogou, the West African warrior spirit of justice and iron.

The traditional image of Saint James on Horseback may represent the official saint, or it may really be intended to portray Ogou. Some perceive the two as one and inseparable. The same image is also identified with Santiago Matamoros.

By papal decree, Saint James the Greater was appointed patron of Hispaniola, the island encompassing modern Haiti and the Dominican Republic. Several of their earliest churches were named in his honor. Documents indicate that enslaved people embraced his cult with great enthusiasm, though many were surreptitiously venerating Ogou in the saint's name.
