Roman Catholic saints venerated individually by all branches of Christianity, though the legend of their sea voyage is specific to Roman Catholicism and certain esoteric French traditions.

The Legend

After the Crucifixion, a small boat departed from either Judea or Alexandria carrying holy refugees. Whether they set sail willingly or were forcibly cast adrift remains disputed. The passenger manifest may have included Joseph of Arimathea, Lazarus, Martha of Bethany, and as many as four women named Mary: Mary Magdalen, Mary of Bethany, Mary Jacobe, and Mary Salomé.

The boat landed in France's Camargue region at an ancient settlement called Ra or Ratis, a city sacred to three goddesses (Artemis, Isis, and Cybele) since the fourth century BCE. The Three Marys are sometimes interpreted as corresponding to these goddesses, with Saint Sarah corresponding to the holy child Horus.

Mary Jacobe and Mary Salomé

These two remained together and are now venerated as the Marys of the Sea. They appear in the Bible among the women who witnessed the Crucifixion. Theories about their identity vary: they may be the Virgin Mary's sisters (Jesus' aunts), the mothers of Saints John and James, Mary's daughters (Jesus' sisters), or Mary Salomé may have been the midwife who delivered Jesus.

The Trinity with Saint Sarah

Though only two Marys are official saints, they form a trinity with the mysterious Saint Sarah, who either met them in France or traveled with them. Sarah may have been their Egyptian servant, a Pagan priestess, or the daughter of Jesus and Mary Magdalen. Saint Sarah is unofficial but is the primary focus of Roma veneration.

Discovery and Pilgrimage

In 1448, René of Anjou discovered relics identified as belonging to Mary Salomé, Mary Jacobe, and Sarah. The town was renamed Saintes-Maries-de-la-Mer in their honor. It became a major pilgrimage site on the route to Santiago de Compostela.

During the annual Roma pilgrimage in May, coffin-shaped reliquaries and statues are carried to the sea and dipped three times. Simultaneously dipping tarot cards or divination tools into the water while all three statues are submerged reputedly enhances their power.

Orthodox tradition rejects the flight to France, honoring Mary Jacobe and Mary Salomé instead as Holy Myrrh Bearers.

Attributes

The Camargue Cross, composed of an anchor, a heart, and a cross.

Sacred Site

Saintes-Maries-de-la-Mer, France, once called Sainte Marie de Ratis or Notre Dame de la Mer (Our Lady of the Sea).

Feast Days

25 May (Mary Jacobe and Saint Sarah), 22 October (Mary Salomé).
