⚠️ Kachinas are sacred to the Pueblo peoples of the American Southwest. These are closed initiatory practices and should only be approached by those within the originating cultures.

Kachinas are ancestral spirits and divine messengers of the Pueblo peoples of the American Southwest. They serve as intermediaries between humans and the divine, bringing rain, ensuring fertility, and maintaining cosmic balance. Kachinas embody natural forces, ancestors, and sacred beings who visit Pueblo villages during ceremonial seasons.

Each Kachina has specific attributes, colors, songs, and dances. They are not gods but rather spiritual beings who act as teachers, protectors, and blessers of the community. Kachina ceremonies are sacred and essential to Pueblo spiritual life.

Ritual Uses

Kachinas are invoked through traditional Pueblo ceremonies involving elaborate costumes, masks, songs, and dances. These ceremonies are deeply sacred and specific to Pueblo nations. Outsiders should not attempt to replicate Kachina ceremonies but can honor their existence through respectful study and support of Pueblo communities.

Warnings

Kachina ceremonies and knowledge are sacred to Pueblo peoples. Do not appropriate or commodify these traditions. Kachina dolls are teaching tools for children, not decorative items or toys for non-Pueblo peoples.
