Classification

Roman Catholic saint.

Epithet

The Saint of Bread and Work.

Life and History

Cajetan (1480-1547) came from Venetian nobility; his father was the Count of Thiene. After earning a law degree and serving as a papal diplomat, he took holy orders at thirty-three and founded the Confraternity of the Divine Love. This organization emphasized spiritual poverty while drawing its membership largely from aristocratic circles.

When Spanish soldiers sacked Rome in 1527, they tortured Cajetan and other members, convinced the confraternity possessed hidden wealth. Cajetan escaped to Venice, where he established the Theatines (a new religious order) and opened a low-interest pawnshop to aid the poor. Miracles were attributed to him even during his lifetime. His close friend and Theatine co-founder eventually became Pope Paul IV.

Cajetan died in Naples, reportedly from grief over the state of the world.

Powers and Domains

In the afterlife, Cajetan has become the patron of gamblers and the unemployed. Like a divine employment agent, he finds jobs for people and shows exceptional sympathy toward those facing financial hardship. He provides quick cash influxes rather than general prosperity or long-term stability.

The Betting Saint

Cajetan loves to gamble and reportedly cannot resist a wager. The traditional approach is to bet him that he cannot produce something for you. Stakes are usually modest: candles, rosary recitations, or small cash amounts. For larger requests, offer to donate a significant sum (whatever "significant" means for you) to a charity benefiting the poor.

If he loses, you've lost nothing. If he wins and produces the miracle, donate your bet to charity in his honor.

Iconography

Wears a black cassock with a jeweled necklace or collar. May wear a flat black hat or appear kneeling beside a table.

Sacred Site

His shrine in Buenos Aires draws thousands of daily visitors. Millions arrive on his feast day.

Feast Day

7 August
