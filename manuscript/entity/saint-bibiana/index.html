Saint Bibiana is renowned for healing depression, migraines, epilepsy, and seizure disorders. She is also the unofficial patron of hangover sufferers.

Classification

Roman Catholic saint.

Historical Uncertainty

No verified records document this Roman virgin martyr's existence, though detailed legends survive. Her name shifted from the original Vibiana to Bibiana over time, possibly because the latter echoes "bibulous" (fond of drinking), which led to her association with hangover cures.

Legend

Vibiana lived in Rome during the Christian persecutions. Authorities deported her father to forced labor and placed her under house arrest with her mother and sister. When both women died of natural causes, Vibiana buried them alone. Her subsequent execution as an openly practicing Christian ended with a priest interring her beside her family. A church eventually rose over their graves.

Alternate Version

A more elaborate tradition holds that her entire family was arrested together. Her father faced deportation, her mother beheading, and her sister died suddenly during the arrest itself, thereby escaping the torments that awaited Vibiana. Authorities sent her to a brothel, but she proved so unmanageable that they transferred her to an asylum. Around 361 CE, she was bound to a pillar and beaten to death with lead-weighted scourges.

Street dogs supposedly refused to touch her abandoned corpse. After burial beside her mother and sister, a mysterious healing herb sprouted in the churchyard, capable of treating seizures, headaches, and mental disturbances.

Powers and Domains

Officially she addresses depression, migraines, epilepsy, and seizure disorders. Her linguistic connection to drinking has made her the unofficial patron of hangover sufferers.

Relic Controversy

Veneration remained confined to Rome until 1853, when relics said to be hers traveled to Los Angeles. Some scholars dispute this identification, suggesting these bones belong to an entirely different martyred Vibiana. The controversy may partly stem from discomfort with her humorous association with hangovers.

Iconography

Often shown bound to a pillar.

Attributes

Pillar, tree branch, lead-weighted scourge.

Sacred Site

The Cathedral of Saint Vibiana in downtown Los Angeles was built in 1876. The 1994 Northridge earthquake severely damaged it, and demolition seemed imminent until preservationists intervened. Since 2002, her relics rest in the Cathedral of Our Lady of the Angels mausoleum. The original building, now deconsecrated, operates as an event venue called Vibiana.

Feast Day

2 December
