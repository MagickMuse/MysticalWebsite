Classification

The Green Lady of Caerphilly is a Welsh goblin or fairy spirit who appears in the folklore of Glamorganshire. She belongs to the broader family of Welsh fairy-folk (Tylwyth Teg) and the wider European tradition of Green Ladies, fairy women clothed in the color of growing things.

Manifestation

She walks the ruins of Caerphilly Castle dressed in a flowing green robe. When she is approached or pursued, she dissolves into a thick growth of ivy clinging to the castle wall. The transformation is seamless: one moment a tall woman in green, the next a curtain of leaves where she stood.

Cultural Context

In Welsh fairy lore, green is a fairy color of deep significance. The Tylwyth Teg are said to wear blue petticoats, but the spirits of woodland and ruin most often appear in green. The Moyddin of Cardiganshire are tiny green-clad fairies who haunt the woods in May; the Green Lady of Caerphilly is their grander Glamorganshire cousin, bound to a single haunted stronghold rather than a forest.

Her transformation into ivy is the key to her nature. Ivy clings, endures, and overtakes ruins, the slow green tide that reclaims abandoned stone. To become ivy is to become the castle itself, to merge with the very wall she haunts.

Working With Her

She is not a deity who answers petitions in the usual sense. She is a place-spirit, tethered to Caerphilly Castle and its ivy-clad walls. Approach her with the respect owed to any fairy of the old country: do not boast, do not grasp, do not name her carelessly. Offerings of milk, bread, or a sprig of ivy laid at the foot of an old wall are appropriate when visiting her haunts.

Those drawn to her energy from afar may work with ivy itself as her token, weaving it into wreaths or growing it along a garden wall as a quiet act of devotion to the green women who tend the ruins of the world.
