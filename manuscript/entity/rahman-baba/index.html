Life and Legacy

Rahman Baba (c. 1663–1707) remains the most cherished and widely read poet throughout the Pashto-speaking regions of Afghanistan and Pakistan. His mystical verses have been translated into English and continue to inspire readers worldwide. His grave-shrine near the Khyber Pass draws pilgrims and poetry lovers throughout the year.

According to Rahman Baba's teachings, the paths toward the divine and the gates of Paradise may be unlocked through music, poetry, and dance. This joyful philosophy attracted many followers but also drew hostility from fundamentalist Wahhabi movements.

The Shrine's Destruction

At the turn of the 21st century, a Saudi-funded Wahhabi madrasa (religious school) was constructed along the path to Rahman Baba's shrine. Students began harassing pilgrims and shrine caretakers. The local Taliban issued warnings that they would bomb the shrine if women continued visiting. On 5 March 2009, militants carried out their threat, destroying the grave itself and badly damaging the shrine. No one was physically harmed.

The attack drew international attention. Writer William Dalrymple, in The Observer on 8 March 2009, compared Rahman Baba to Julian of Norwich in an article titled "Wahhabi Radicals Are Determined to Destroy a Gentler, Kinder Islam." The bombing was particularly shocking because Rahman Baba has long symbolized peace in the Pashto region.

Famous Verses

These lines are widely considered Rahman Baba's most celebrated:

Plant flowers where you live, so that your region becomes a flower garden
Don't plant thorns that will stick in your feet
Don't dig a pit in someone else's path
Your own path might sooner or later lead into that pit.

Powers and Domains

Rahman Baba's gentle nature reportedly gave him the ability to communicate with birds and animals. Sick or injured birds, whether pets or wild, are traditionally brought to his shrine for healing. An aviary situated among the grove of trees near his grave serves as a sanctuary where injured birds may recuperate.

Sacred Sites

His shrine in Hazar Khwani village near Peshawar, in Pakistan's Northwest Frontier Province bordering Afghanistan.
