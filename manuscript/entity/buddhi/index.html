Buddhi, whose name means perception or intellect, is a minor goddess honored in both Hindu Puranic and Jain traditions. She is the personified faculty of clear understanding, the discerning intellect that perceives the true nature of things, and in that role she is invoked as one of the feminine powers that make possible the disciplined life of practice.

Domains

The discerning intellect, the clarity of perception, the fulfillment of understanding that follows the removal of obstacles, and the feminine embodiment of the awakened mind.

Hindu Form

In the Hindu Puranic tradition Buddhi is sometimes identified as the consort of the Maha-Ganapati form of the elephant-headed god Ganesha. In this pairing she is depicted seated upon his knee, an iconographic sign of the intimacy with which the remover of obstacles keeps intellect close at hand. Alongside Siddhi, who personifies spiritual attainment, Buddhi is one of the goddesses invoked with Ganesha as the fulfillment that follows the clearing of the path.

Jain Form

In the Jain tradition Buddhi appears as a minor goddess of comparable domain, honored as the personification of clear intellect within the Jain devotional cosmos.

Association with Ganesha

As consort of the Maha-Ganapati form of Ganesha, Buddhi represents the perception that pairs with the elephant-headed lord of new beginnings. Her seat upon his knee marks her as the intellect held close by the presiding deity of intelligence and wisdom.
