Rabbi Chaim Pinto the Elder (pronounced hi-im or hi-eem) holds the highest place of honor within the Pinto dynasty, a Moroccan Jewish family that produced multiple generations of revered mystics and healers.

Life and History

Born in Agadir, Morocco in 1758, Rabbi Pinto combined deep Talmudic scholarship with practical Kabbalistic knowledge. His reputation for performing wonders during his lifetime continues to draw pilgrims who believe he still answers prayers. The Pinto lineage includes his father, his son, and his grandson, all recognized as saints in their own right. Because his grandson also bore the name Chaim Pinto, the elder rabbi carries this distinguishing title. He served as chief rabbi in Mogador until his death in 1845.

Domains

Devotees approach him for physical healing, miraculous intervention, blessings of all kinds, and especially the welfare of children.

Ritual Uses

Visitors to his grave light candles in abundance. The soot that accumulates on his tomb from these flames holds special significance. Parents apply this residue to their children's foreheads to invoke the rabbi's protective blessing. Another practice involves pouring water over the tombstone, collecting it, and drinking the liquid as a healing remedy.

Sacred Sites

His tomb in Mogador (modern Essaouira), Morocco.

Feast

His hillulah, the annual commemoration of his passing, falls on the twenty-sixth of Elul in the Hebrew calendar, typically occurring during Virgo season.
