Life and Legend

There is little historical evidence regarding Welsh Saint Elian, but he was venerated immediately following his death in the sixth century. He is a well saint, his fame deriving from associations with a healing well now located in the Church of Elian in Llanelian, Caernarvonshire, Wales.

Legend holds that the well miraculously appeared one day when the saint was very thirsty.

The Cursing Well

The water from Elian's well reputedly heals illnesses when petitions are made to the saint, but that's not what it's famous for. In the 19th century, threatening to visit Saint Elian's well was an ominous warning. His well is a cursing well.

Saint Elian was invoked to cause sudden death or grave misfortune to those who had offended the petitioner.

The Cursing Ritual

  1. The petitioner wrote the target's name on a small piece of paper
  2. A crooked pin was pushed through the paper
  3. The well-keeper copied the name onto a pebble or piece of lead
  4. The pebble was dropped into the well

This practice was so prevalent that a well-keeper was arrested in 1854. The well was subsequently drained and eventually hidden by clergy to discourage such practices.

The Saint's Power

The question arises: was the power in the well, or in Saint Elian himself? The saint can be invoked without visiting the well to recover stolen goods and identify thieves.
