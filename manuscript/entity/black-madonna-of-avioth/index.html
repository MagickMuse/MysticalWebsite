Magickal Properties

  • Once literally black but has since been whitened, considered especially ancient comparable to Chartres statues
  • Discovered in hawthorn bush in 1140, basilica built on original site in 14th century
  • Statue refused to budge when attempts made to move it elsewhere
  • Specialty was resuscitating dead babies long enough for them to be baptized and admitted to Heaven

Ritual Uses

  • Dead babies were once left near underground spring by gate of former cemetery near her church
  • Last recorded baby resurrection was 23 February 1786
  • Pilgrimage to her basilica for healing and fertility requests
  • Associated with healing springs which allegedly grant fertility

Offerings

Traditional offerings for miraculous intercession; pilgrimages to her basilica

Favored People

Those seeking resurrection of dead babies, fertility from healing springs, pilgrims

Petition For

Fertility, successful childbirth, resurrection/baptism of dead babies, healing

Warnings

Once literally black but has since been whitened. Statue refused to budge from original site. Her specialty was resuscitating dead babies long enough for baptism.

Additional Notes

  • Located in Avioth, France on Belgian border, once prime Merovingian territory
  • In 17th century, Huguenot captain tried to stable his horse at her altar but immediately dropped dead
  • Associated with Mary Magdalene, but suggestions also made that she is Rosmerta
  • Sanctuary associated with healing springs
  • Prime example of Black Madonna refusing to be moved from chosen location
