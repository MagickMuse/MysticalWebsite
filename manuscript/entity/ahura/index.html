Magickal Properties

The Ahuras are a class of spirits first venerated by the Magi, representing goodness, righteousness, and benevolence in Zoroastrian tradition. They are invoked in opposition to malevolent Daeva spirits in dualistic practices. Their king is Ahura Mazda. Some scholars theorize that Ahuras and Daevas were originally the same type of spirit, but as dualistic philosophy spread, the terms were used to distinguish between benevolent and malevolent spirits.

Ritual Uses

The Ahuras are venerated as spirits of goodness, righteousness, and benevolence in Zoroastrian tradition. They are invoked in opposition to malevolent Daeva spirits in dualistic practices that distinguish between forces of light and darkness.

Offerings

Traditional Zoroastrian fire rituals and pure offerings would be appropriate, including sacred fire ceremonies and offerings that maintain ritual purity.

Favored People

Magi, Zoroastrians, those seeking righteousness and benevolence, followers of dualistic spiritual traditions, and people working to distinguish good from evil influences.

Petition For

Goodness, righteousness, benevolence, protection from evil spirits, spiritual guidance, and assistance in maintaining ethical conduct.

Warnings

Class of benevolent spirits opposed to Daevas. First venerated by Magi in dualistic spiritual tradition distinguishing good from evil spirits. They are consistently benevolent in the Zoroastrian context.

Additional Notes

Alternatively, the Daevas may be an older pantheon eventually suppressed by Ahura devotees. Ahura is etymologically related to Ashura and possibly to Aesir. They represent the principle of cosmic order and divine goodness in the eternal struggle against chaos and evil.
