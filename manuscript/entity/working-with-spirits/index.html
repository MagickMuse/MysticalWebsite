Entity Grimoire

Working With Spirits

The relationship between practitioner and spirit defines the work more than any ritual structure. Where space exists, awareness manifests as varied classes of beings with different natures and powers. Some are localized, some are not. Some speak using only information within your mind to express themselves, others communicate as clearly as a person standing before you. Some influence the material world, some do not.

Psychological vs. Traditional Views

Modern practitioners often interpret spirits as psychological projections, aspects of the practitioner's own mind rather than independent entities. This view suggests spells provide self-empowerment and mystery rather than direct influence on external reality.

This psychological framework limits both perception and results. The assumption that spirits exist only as projected aspects of individual psyches reduces ceremony to theater rather than genuine contact with external intelligence.

The Traditional Approach

Ancient grimoires were written for their rituals to be performed as if the spirit were a separate, discarnate intelligence. Even if you personally believe spirits are psychological constructs, treating them as separate entities during ceremony produces better results. If the mechanism is belief-based psychology, believing more strongly in the spirit's independent existence creates stronger results than treating the work as a mental trick.

Experience across traditions supports the view that spirits are independent intelligences. Some interface with human consciousness to communicate, but they are not limited to or contained by individual human minds. They act in ways that exceed the boundaries of psychological projection.

Working with Spirit Authority

Authority and Will

The most elaborate traditional rites fail if the practitioner lacks knowledge of their true will and access to higher powers. Successful spirit work depends on the practitioner's spiritual authority and connection to divine forces, not on ritual complexity alone. Faith that divine will manifests through the practitioner is what wins confrontations with hostile entities and earns respect from allied spirits.

Practical Considerations

Treat spirits in ritual according to traditional frameworks regardless of your personal metaphysical beliefs. This approach has been tested across centuries and produces consistent results.

Etiquette for Working with Spirits

Certain rules apply when working with spirit helpers, as in any relationship:

  • Ask for assistance - Guides, guardians, and deities respect your free will and may not intervene unless invited to do so.
  • Show respect - Treat spirit helpers as honored teachers and allies, not servants.
  • Avoid micromanaging - When seeking aid from spirit beings, turn over the reins and allow them to carry out your request as they see fit.
  • Do not seek help for harmful purposes - Benevolent spirits will not support harmful causes, and you do not want to attract malevolent entities.
  • Express gratitude - Thank the beings who assist you and consider giving them an offering.

Ways to Attract Divine Assistance

Deities may come to you in dreams, meditation, or visions. Throughout history, countless people have experienced visitations from divine beings. Such appearances can happen to anyone, anytime.

If spontaneous contact does not occur, other methods can catch a deity's attention:

  • Research thoroughly - Learn as much as possible about the deity you wish to attract through myths, spiritual literature, and folklore.
  • Create a dedicated altar - Set up and dedicate an altar to your chosen deity.
  • Display sacred imagery - Collect and display artwork depicting the deities you wish to petition in a prominent place.
  • Make appropriate offerings - Discover what specific deities prefer (particular plants, foods, stones) and place these on your altar or in a special outdoor location.
  • Stay receptive - Keep an open mind and heart when working with deities. Trust your intuition to tune into higher frequencies.
  • Cultivate belief - Trust that spirits will communicate with you. Doubt closes the door between you and magickal beings. When dealing with supernatural forces, you'll see it when you believe it.

Guardian Spirits Across Traditions

Guardian spirits serve as protectors, guides, and intermediaries between the practitioner and higher powers. Different traditions conceptualize and work with guardian spirits in distinct ways.

Tibetan Buddhism works with dharmapalas (wrathful protector deities) who defend practitioners and the dharma itself. These fierce guardians like Palden Lhamo and the Eight Dharma Protectors require empowerment from qualified teachers and ongoing devotional relationships.

Catholic and folk Catholic traditions invoke saints as guardian spirits. Practitioners develop relationships through prayer, offerings of candles and flowers, and petitions for specific forms of protection or aid. Saints act as intermediaries who carry prayers to higher divine authority.

Vodou and related African diasporic traditions work with loa as guardian spirits. The Met Tet (master of the head) serves as the primary guardian loa for each practitioner. Relationships with loa require initiation, regular service, and proper protocols for invocation and offering.

Grimoire traditions assign planetary and elemental spirits as guardians. Olympic spirits from Renaissance grimoires and the four elemental kings serve protective functions when properly conjured and bound to service through traditional ceremony.

Creating Spirit Sigils

Sigils provide a physical focus for guardian spirit relationships. Traditional methods include receiving the spirit's sigil through vision or automatic drawing, using established sigils from grimoires, or constructing sigils through gematria and sacred geometry.

When creating original sigils, enter trance or visionary state and request the spirit reveal its sign. Draw what you perceive without conscious interference. Alternatively, convert the spirit's name to numbers using Hebrew, Greek, or Latin gematria, then plot these numbers on a magick square corresponding to the spirit's planetary or elemental nature.

Protocol for Established Traditions

When working with guardian spirits from living traditions (Tibetan Buddhism, vodou, santería, Catholic saint veneration), respect existing protocols. Seek proper initiation or empowerment where required. Do not innovate or combine incompatible systems.

Guardian spirits tied to specific cultural and religious contexts carry the full weight of those systems. Attempting to work with Yemaya and Oya simultaneously without understanding their relationship within vodou creates spiritual conflict. Working with conflicting Tibetan dharmapalas without proper empowerment creates dangerous contradictions within Tibetan Buddhist frameworks.

Study the tradition thoroughly or work with qualified initiates. Guardian spirits from closed practices require proper entry into those systems.

Maintaining Guardian Relationships

Guardian spirits require ongoing relationship maintenance. Regular offerings appropriate to the spirit's nature, periodic invocations acknowledging their presence and protection, and honoring commitments made during initial contact sustain the relationship.

Neglecting a guardian spirit after establishing the relationship creates problems. The spirit may withdraw protection, demand renewed attention through disruptive manifestations, or sever the connection entirely. Consistency matters more than elaborate ceremony.

Artificial Spirits

Apart from spirits and intelligences attracted through conjuration or offering, practitioners can create artificial spirits. An artificial spirit is made from energy shaped and programmed by the practitioner, similar to an artificial intelligence program. These entities are created for long and short-term use and are known by many names across traditions.

Terminology Across Traditions

In traditional European witchcraft, artificials are sometimes known as a fetch or bud-will. In ceremonial magick they are often called egregores, or when constructed from one or more of the four elements, artificial elementals. Chaos magick practitioners generally call them servitors. Tibetan Buddhism uses the term tulpa (roughly translating to "Mind Emanation"). The construction and use are more or less the same regardless of terminology.

Historical Examples

The Golem of Prague (1580): Rabbi Loeb created an artificial spirit that inhabited and animated a physical form to protect the Jewish community from false accusations of ritual murder. The golem was given life by writing one of the names of God, EMETH, on its head. The spirit was deactivated by changing EMETH to METH (meaning "dead" in Hebrew) by wiping off the first letter. Stories vary about the golem's ultimate fate, but it was said to be sealed in a secret passage in a synagogue where it remains.

Alexandra David Néel's Tulpa (1920s): The French explorer who traveled Tibet disguised as a beggar and lama described creating an artificial spirit in her book Magic and Mystery in Tibet. She sealed herself in a cave and concentrated on creating a short, good-natured monk. After a few weeks, the tulpa-monk followed her on travels and was seen by other members of her traveling party. Problems arose when the monk began changing outside her control, his appearance shifting from portly and good-natured to gaunt and sinister. She dismantled the spirit only with great effort over several months.

GOTOS of the Fraternitas Saturni: This German magickal order regarded an egregore named GOTOS (acronym for Gradus Ordinis Templi Orientis Saturni) as a secret chief. The egregore was fed into by everyone in the order, creating a group mind that could be called upon to advise the order with the weight of its collected wisdom.

Purpose and Function

Artificial spirits can be made for almost any purpose, though this grimoire focuses primarily on magickal defense and protection. In general, artificial spirits are meant to be temporary and designed to dissipate upon completion of their task or upon a certain date.

Permanent servitors can be constructed but must be carefully looked after and fed with energy to keep them in line, lest they begin to take their nourishment and thus their programming from elsewhere, as with Alexandra David Néel's runaway tulpa.

Design Considerations

Form: The form is limited only by imagination and should in some way indicate the spirit's function. A servitor made to warn of danger could take the shape of a cloud of eyes and ears. A servitor meant to guard a door could take the form of a knight in armor. Be aware that the servitor may take on the characteristics of its form. If you want a servitor to charge into battle, don't make it cute and cuddly. Similarly, if you are concerned about causing harm in your response to danger, do not make it into the form of a bear.

Name: The name should represent its function in some way. You can choose one word and jumble the letters, or pick a combination of words that you condense. The word "protector" could become "Rectoport." The words "binder of harm" can become "Binderham" by removing the word "of" and the repeating letter "r" from harm. An artificial spirit created primarily from an elemental or planetary force can be named with a word that recalls that force. Madim (Hebrew name for Mars) could be used for a servitor made with energy from that planetary sphere.

Sigil: Construct a sigil from traditional methods by combining letters into a symbol where individual letters are not immediately apparent. You can trace a sigil upon kameas or Rose Cross Lamen if you know how. You can also make a chart of your own construction and trace a sigil on it.

Physical Link: Though not strictly necessary, writing the name and sigil on paper forms a magickal link and home for the spirit. This paper can be used to feed the servitor and issue further instructions. The spirit's orders and symbols appropriate to the working can be added, as can oils and powders consistent with the spirit's nature. For long-term spirits, engrave the sigil and name onto a statue or other object that will serve as a powerful guardian.

Defense Against Hostile Artificial Spirits

If you suspect an artificial spirit has been sent against you, several methods of combat exist.

Thought-Form Spirits: Some artificial spirits are nothing more than thought-forms projected by a creator without any other type of energy put into them. In this case you can destroy them by thinking them away. Imagine them being obliterated. From mind they came and by mind they go.

Artificial Elementals: If banishing and visualization fail, you have a stronger problem. Trap the spirit in a triangle (such as the Hekatean spirit trap) and attack it with the opposing element and associated weapons. Alternatively, begin feeding it the element it is made from, making it stronger but infusing it with your will in an attempt to usurp control. This is more dangerous but has advantages. Successfully taking over an artificial spirit creates a powerful magickal link to a potentially unknown attacker.

Usurping Sent Spirits: There are cases where spirits sent to attack someone were usurped by the victim and turned upon the attacker. This is done through making greater offerings to the spirit, binding the spirit as in an exorcism, or appealing to the spirit's nature. In Haiti, a bokor (sorcerer) sent one of the barons (lwa associated with death and graveyards) against another bokor. The targeted sorcerer made prayers to the same baron and left it up to the baron to decide who was justified. In the end, the first bokor who initiated the attack was taken to the grave.

This technique of forcing a spirit to choose between two victims in hopes that the unjustified person will be killed appears in the Pulsa Dinura (Lashes of Fire curse), performed in a cemetery invoking the angel of death, asking him to kill a named victim or kill the person performing the curse. It is up to the angel to decide who deserves death.

Using such techniques is obviously dangerous and advanced work. You will generally be better served by using defensive techniques and methods of reversal and counter-magick rather than attempting to usurp hostile spirits.

Healing Through Nature Spirits

Beyond working with specific deities, guardian spirits, or artificial servitors, the land itself offers powerful healing allies. Trees, rocks, rivers, and other natural places each have their own genii loci, spirits of place that can assist in healing when approached with respect and proper offering.

Building Relationship Through Offering

If you have been making regular offerings to the natural world as part of your practice, you have already begun building relationships with these spirits. Even if they have not made themselves known through direct communication or signs, the bond created through consistent offering establishes a foundation of reciprocity.

The spirits of place are not specific entities like named deities or conjured spirits. They are the consciousness of the land itself: the accumulated awareness of an old tree, the presence within a large boulder, the spirit that flows through a river or dwells at the ocean's edge.

The Healing Working

When you need healing after magickal attack or other spiritual harm, these allies can help process and transform what troubles you.

Choosing a Location:

Go to a place in nature where you feel a particularly strong presence. This might be:

  • An old tree that has witnessed decades or centuries
  • A large boulder or rock formation
  • The ocean or a significant body of water
  • A grove or clearing that feels sacred
  • Any natural place where you sense deep presence

The location should feel powerful but not threatening. You are seeking aid, not testing yourself against overwhelming force.

Entering Trance:

Sit or lie down in contact with the earth. Make yourself receptive to the energies of the place. Speak aloud or silently, explaining that you have been hurt and are in need of healing.

Enter trance through whatever method works best for you:

  • Intense drumming (bring a drum or drum on the earth itself)
  • Breath control techniques
  • Self-hypnosis
  • Meditation
  • Any combination of methods

The goal is to shift your consciousness enough to perceive the spiritual dimension of the place.

Accessing the Spiritual Dimension:

Once in trance, attempt to "enter" the spiritual layer of the location. This cannot be explained mechanically. It is a function of the trance state itself and must be discovered through practice.

If trance alone does not provide access, try falling asleep in the location and entering its spiritual dimension through lucid dreaming. This technique works particularly well with trees, where lying against the trunk can facilitate dream communication.

Meeting the Genii:

Within the spiritual dimension of the place, look for the genius loci. These spirits appear differently depending on the location and your own perceptual framework, but they are always at the center of things: the heart of the tree, the core of the stone, the deepest current of the water.

Explain your situation to the genius. Ask if the place can safely absorb your hurt.

Natural places can often take forces that trouble humans and process them without harm, much as soil processes waste into nutrients. What is poison to one being is not poison to all. The genius will indicate whether it can help or whether you should seek healing elsewhere.

Offering Up the Harm:

If the genius agrees to help, offer up what troubles you. Visualize the magickal attack, the spiritual wounds, or the negative energy flowing out of you and into the place. See it absorbed by the tree, the stone, or the water.

Thank the genius for its aid. Express genuine gratitude for the healing work.

Returning to Regular Consciousness:

When the working feels complete, return to normal awareness. The transition should be gentle; take time to reorient yourself.

Make a physical offering to the place as thanks. This could be:

  • Water poured at the base of a tree
  • Honey or milk
  • Flowers or herbs
  • Tobacco or other traditional offerings
  • A formal offering ceremony as taught in your tradition

The offering completes the exchange and honors the relationship you have built.

Important Considerations

Respect Boundaries: If a genius indicates it cannot or will not help, accept this answer. Not every place is suited for every type of healing work.

Reciprocity Matters: Regular offerings before you need help establish the relationship that makes this healing possible. Coming only when you need something, without prior relationship, is less likely to succeed.

Physical Safety: Choose locations where you can safely enter trance or sleep. Avoid dangerous terrain, extreme weather, or places where you might be disturbed by others.

Integration: After the healing, continue your regular offerings to the place. The relationship should be ongoing, not transactional.
