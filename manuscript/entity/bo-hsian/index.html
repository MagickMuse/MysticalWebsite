Bo Hsian is a god of wisdom in the Taoist tradition of China. He is the Taoist counterpart of the Buddhist bodhisattva Samantabhadra, the great figure of universal practice and the ten vows, and his figure preserves in Taoist dress the same theme of wisdom that is worked out in patient, world-embracing action rather than in abstract contemplation alone.

Domains

Wisdom, discernment in study, the steadying power that carries insight into practice, and the deliberate patience that turns knowledge into lived understanding.

Iconography

Bo Hsian is usually depicted upon a white elephant, the same great mount that carries Samantabhadra in the Buddhist tradition. The elephant marks his character as a wisdom that moves with weight, calm, and unhurried progress, a deliberate power rather than a swift or lightning one.

Cross-Tradition

As the Taoist counterpart of Samantabhadra, Bo Hsian shows the deep exchange between Taoist and Buddhist traditions in Chinese religious history, where the great figures of one system often find recognizable counterparts in the other. His identity as god of wisdom, and his mount of the white elephant, mark him as a Taoist reception of a bodhisattva-figure who was in turn received from India through the long transmission of Buddhism into China.
