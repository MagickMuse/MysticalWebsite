Buluc Chabtan is a god of war within the classic-era Mayan pantheon of Mesoamerica, known in older comparative literature by the alphabetic designation God F. He belongs to a wider circle of Mayan deities associated with battle, captive-taking, and the sacrificial rites through which the political and cosmic order of the classic Maya was renewed.

Domains

Warfare, martial conflict, the taking of captives in battle, and the sacrificial rites bound to war, sovereignty, and the maintenance of the calendrical and cosmic order.

Iconography and Attributes

Buluc Chabtan is recognised by a distinctive facial marking: a black line that encircles the eye and extends downward across the cheek. This ocular marking appears in the codical and monumental record and is one of the primary means by which he is identified among the many warlike male figures of the Mayan corpus. His signature form belongs to the classic Mayan visual language of divine kings, warriors, and gods, in which paint, headdress, and body-marking encode the identity of the being represented.

Ritual

Buluc Chabtan is associated with human sacrifice as it was practised in the classic and post-classic Mayan world, a rite in which captives, particularly high-status war captives, were offered as the crowning act of martial and calendrical ceremony. Such offerings were bound to the sovereignty of the ruler, the fortunes of the polity in war, and the continuing motion of the cosmos across the great cycles of the Long Count.

Lore

As a war god Buluc Chabtan stands beside other male deities of battle and sacrifice within the Mayan pantheon and is one of the powers to whom the outcome of conflict was addressed by kings, warriors, and priests of the classic Maya. The Mayan religious tradition to which he belongs is a living inheritance of Maya-descended communities across present-day Mexico, Guatemala, Belize, and Honduras, and its rites, deities, and cosmology are properly the province of those communities and their teachers.
