Saint Nicholas is the patron saint of children, sailors, merchants, pawnbrokers, and border guards.

Venerated as a saint by most branches of Christianity, Saint Nicholas is among the most beloved and widely venerated saints in the world.

Historical Facts

The verified facts about Saint Nicholas are few:

  • He was the bishop of Myra, now known as Demre in modern Turkey
  • He lived in the early fourth century and died in approximately 350 CE
  • He was buried in his own cathedral, which became a significant pilgrimage site
  • His reputation as a wonder worker spread throughout Christendom
  • In 1087, his relics were moved to Bari, Italy, where they remain today

Everything else about Saint Nicholas derives from legend and lore.

Legends and Lore

According to tradition, Nicholas was devout from infancy, refusing his mother's breast on Wednesdays and Fridays to observe fasting days. An only child whose parents died young, he inherited a fortune which he spent on anonymous gifts to those in need. His parents, Nonna and Theophanes, are remembered alongside him; Nicholas is said to promise his aid to anyone who honors them.

The Three Daughters

In his most famous legend, a poor man could not afford dowries for his three daughters. Facing the prospect of prostitution, the family was saved when Nicholas secretly threw three bags of gold through their window at night, one for each daughter.

The Butcher's Barrel

During a famine, a butcher lured three young boys to his house, murdered them, and placed their bodies in a barrel of brine to sell as pork. Nicholas clairvoyantly knew what had happened, confronted the butcher, and resurrected the boys from the barrel, healing them entirely.

Pagan Connections

Saint Nicholas is beloved as a conventional saint, but he is also among the foremost magical saints. Because he is so widely venerated over vast territories, different Pagan spirits masquerade under his name.

In the Mediterranean, bearded Poseidon, King of the Sea, may lie beneath the mask of bearded Saint Nick. After Christianity became the official religion of the Roman Empire, Nicholas of Myra assumed many functions previously fulfilled by Artemis of Ephesus and the sea deity Poseidon. Mediterranean temples dedicated to Poseidon were rededicated as churches honoring Saint Nicholas.

Powers and Domains

Patron of the Sea

Saint Nicholas is patron of mariners with strong associations with the sea. Sailors in the eastern Mediterranean once wished each other bon voyage with: "May Saint Nicholas hold the tiller!" On his feast day in Bari, his votive image is carried to the sea to bless the waters, then returned in a torchlit procession.

According to legend, sailors caught in a terrible storm invoked Saint Nicholas. He suddenly manifested on their deck, told the men to have faith and the water to be still, guided the boat to safety, and disappeared.

Guardian of Animals

Saint Nicholas is the guardian of animals. On his feast day, beasts of burden are traditionally given respite from their labors.

Keeper of Heaven's Keys

Russian lore suggests that Saint Peter is not the only saint possessing keys to the kingdom. Saint Nicholas, perceived as kinder and more generous, has a set of keys that open Heaven's gates as well. It was traditional to place letters to Saint Nicholas in the hands of the deceased before burial, requesting mercy. By extension, Nicholas is patron of border guards, invoked to encourage guards to be generous or look the other way.

Regional Traditions

Albania

Pre-Christian rituals honoring the dead are incorporated into his feast day. A feast is prepared, a candle placed in the window, and a door left open at night, inviting the saint and souls of the dead to enter and dine. The longer the candle stays lit, the more blessings of prosperity and abundance fall upon the house.

Bulgaria

Legend describes how Saint Nicholas saved a sinking ship by plugging its hull with a live carp. Carp is traditionally eaten on his feast day. The cross-shaped crown bone in the carp's head is preserved and may be buried near a house for protection or sewn into a child's clothing to avert the Evil Eye.

Iconography

Nicholas is the saint most frequently represented on Orthodox icons. His icon is carried onto boats for safety at sea. Bulgarian sailors' wives dip icons of Saint Nicholas into the sea while praying for their spouses' safe return. Icons of Saint Nicholas are traditional wedding gifts, believed to bring good luck.

The Russian icon known as Holy Hierarch Nicholas of Mozhaisk portrays Nicholas holding a sword in his right hand and a city in his left, commemorating his miracle of saving Mozhaisk from enemy attack.

In the United States, Saint Nicholas evolved into the red-suited Santa Claus. In much of the world, however, he retains his bishop's garb as a sterner, more dignified figure.

Attributes

Bishop's mitre, a book, three golden balls or three small golden bags (prototype for pawnbroker's balls).

Spiritual Allies

In icons and religious legends, Nicholas is often accompanied by angels, especially Gabriel and Michael.

In ritualized feast-day traditions, Nicholas is frequently accompanied by a helper spirit, typically a Pagan or demonic figure like Krampus, Ruprecht, or Black Pete. While Nicholas rewards well-behaved children, the helper spirit punishes the unruly.

Sacred Animal

In Bulgarian lore, a magical carp is Saint Nicholas' servant.

Sacred Plants

Pine (also sacred to Poseidon), Sanicula species, damiana (known as Saint Nicholas' herb in Mexico), Saint Nicholas root (Stevia serrata).

Sacred Sites

Nicholas was first buried in his cathedral in Myra, which became a tremendous pilgrimage destination. A scented oil known as the Manna of Saint Nicholas flowed from his tomb with powers of healing.

  • Basilica di San Nicola, Bari, Italy: His primary shrine since 1087, housing both Orthodox and Roman Catholic churches. Healing oil continues to secrete from his relics.
  • Noel Baba Kilisesi (Church of Saint Nicholas), Demre-Kale, Turkey: His original gravesite and a few relics remain.
  • Jerpoint Abbey, Ireland: French Crusaders may have brought his body here.
  • Nicholausberg, Lower Saxony, Germany: Named for relics brought by three pilgrims.
  • Church of Saint Nicholas, Lido, Venice: Scientific studies confirm relics here match those in Bari.
  • Saint Nicholas Orthodox Church, Beit Jala: Built on the site of the cave where Nicholas lived during his pilgrimage to Jerusalem (312-315 CE).
  • Saint Nicholas Cathedral, Washington, D.C.
  • Church of Saint Nicholas of the Beggars, Venice
  • Church of Saint Nicholas, Lezha, Albania
  • Mar Nicholas Cathedral, Beirut with the adjacent Saint Nicholas Garden

Feast Days

  • 6 December: Primary feast
  • 19 December: Additional feast observed by some Orthodox churches
  • 9 May: Translation of his relics to Bari
  • 29 July: His birthday, celebrated by some Orthodox churches
