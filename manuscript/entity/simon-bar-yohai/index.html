Simon bar Yohai is renowned for healing terminal illness and desperate cases when all other options have failed. He is often petitioned as the saint of last resort.

Jewish saint, the great second-century Talmudic sage and archetypal Jewish mystic, widely considered the Father of Kabbalah.

Life and Legend

Simon bar Yohai studied under the renowned Rabbi Akiba and became a miracle worker, healer, and exorcist. An outspoken and sometimes cantankerous man, he publicly criticized the Roman occupation of Judea. Rome responded by ordering his execution and placing a bounty on his head.

To escape the death sentence, Simon and his son Rabbi Eliezer, also a great scholar, hid in a cave in Galilee for thirteen years. Miracles sustained them: a freshwater well appeared their first night in hiding, and a large carob tree suddenly grew at the cave's mouth, providing food while camouflaging the entrance. Elijah the Prophet visited to study with them, revealing heavenly mysteries and encouraging Simon to write them down.

The Zohar

During those thirteen years, Simon authored the Zohar (Book of Splendor), the greatest and most significant Kabbalistic text. Written as a mystical novel centering on discussions between Simon and his disciples, it presents foundational teachings on the ten Sephirot, the Tree of Life, and the exile of the Shekhina.

Though reputedly composed in the second century, the Zohar remained unknown until the Kabbalist Rabbi Moses de León published it in thirteenth-century Spain. He claimed to have discovered a hidden manuscript by Simon bar Yohai. The publication sparked the Kabbalah renaissance centered in Spain and Safed, Israel. Debate continues over authorship: academics often attribute it to de León, traditionalists credit Bar Yohai, and others acknowledge multiple authors across centuries.

The Evil Eye

According to legend, when birds finally signaled it was safe to leave the cave, Simon emerged in such a tempestuous mood that he gave the Evil Eye to everyone who aggravated him. He caused such havoc that God sent him back to the cave for another year as a cooling-off period. When he finally emerged, he settled in Tiberias, continuing to teach and perform miracles.

Posthumous Appearances

Following his death, many claim to have witnessed Simon bar Yohai, either seeing him directly or receiving dream visitations. In Morocco, he appeared in dreams advising that he was protecting people and that they could approach him at a mountain in Sefrou in lieu of visiting his grave. His soul returns to earth to assist living students of Kabbalah; he continues teaching classes in Paradise.

He is petitioned for all sorts of needs, especially healing. Often the saint of last resort, he is invoked for potentially fatal illnesses when medical treatment has failed or no solution exists. Traditionally, petitioners leave written notes and entreaties at his grave.

Feast Day

Simon instructed his disciples to mark his death as "the day of my joy." He died on the eighteenth day of the Hebrew month Iyar, corresponding to Taurus. This day is Lag B'Omer, a Jewish holiday also commemorating either the end of a plague or some aspect of the Bar Kochba rebellion. His hillulah is celebrated with bonfires. Funeral workers in some communities celebrate with rituals incorporating drinking games.

Sacred Sites

  • His grave in Meron, Israel, the largest pilgrimage site in the country, drawing over 200,000 visitors annually on Lag B'Omer
  • He is buried beside his son Rabbi Eliezer
  • Their cave near the village of Peki'in in the Upper Galilee
