Magickal Properties

Dakinis are Himalayan flying female spirits who are sources of inspiration and power, both protective and terrifying. Also known as "Cloud Fairies," "Sky Dancers," "Celestial Women," "Space Travelers," and "Sky Walkers." In English, dakini is alternatively translated as "fairies," "furies" or "yoginis." They are sometimes defined as "Spirits of Wrath."

They are powerful female spirits who fly through the sky, serving as sources of profound tantric wisdom and spiritual power. They are both beautiful and terrifying, protectors and destroyers, teachers and testers of spiritual practitioners. Some Dakinis are fully enlightened Buddhas in feminine form, while others are accomplished human yoginis who have achieved transformation. They guard the deepest secrets of tantric practice and grant initiation only to those who are ready.

They teach, assist, and initiate great yogis and yoginis into the secrets of Tantra. Some are full-fledged Buddhas, while others are mortal women who acquired requisite power and wisdom to transform into Dakinis.

Historical Context

In pre-Buddhist times, the Sanskrit word dakini denoted a female death spirit, perhaps similar to a Valkyrie, found at battlefields, cemeteries, and cremation grounds. In modern Hindi, dakin indicates "witch."

From the ninth through at least the thirteenth centuries there was an active, vital spiritual tradition involving veneration of dakini with temples throughout India. Shrines centered on Tantric practice and adoration of 64 dakinis. Dakini rituals were practiced well into the sixteenth century when for now unknown reasons they began to fade from mainstream Hindu religion. Their temples had no roofs, perhaps to allow sky riders to fly in and out.

They are now most famous as Kali's attendants but once had extensive temple worship throughout India with their own temples from the 9th-13th centuries.

Ritual Uses

Dakinis are central to tantric Buddhist practice, invoked for initiation into secret teachings and advanced spiritual practices. They are contacted through meditation, visualization, and ritual ceremonies held on the 10th and 25th days of the lunar month (Dakini Feast days).

Dakini Feasts are held on 10th and 25th days of the month by initiated practitioners. Shamans contact them wearing masks of associated animals.

Offerings

Masks representing their animal aspects, Tantric ritual implements including curved knife (kartika) and skull cup (kapala) containing blood, ritual feast offerings (ganachakra), music and dance, items honoring fierce protective deities, offerings made at open-air sacred spaces.

Favored People

Tantric initiates and advanced practitioners, shamans and yogis/yoginis, advanced spiritual seekers ready for profound initiation, devotees of Kali (whom they attend), those who have proven themselves ready for profound teachings through spiritual practice and devotion, those working with advanced esoteric practices requiring fierce protection.

Petition For

Initiation into Tantric secrets and advanced spiritual practices, spiritual wisdom and transformative power, prophecy and divination, protection during dangerous spiritual work, ability to overcome obstacles to enlightenment, inspiration and guidance for advanced practitioners, recognition of true nature of reality.

Warnings

Dakinis are dangerous and wrathful spirits who may be beautiful and inspiring but can also devour, terrify, or mislead those who approach without proper respect, preparation, or initiation. Some allegedly have taste for human flesh and are described as dangerous. They are shape-shifters who may not be immediately recognizable and must be recognized with the inner or third eye. Some haunt cremation grounds and battlefields. Approach only with proper training and guidance from qualified teachers.

Additional Notes

They manifest as beautiful, desirable, naked but potentially raging women, sometimes with animal heads (avian, canine, equine, or leonine). Their primary attributes are curved knife and skull cup filled with blood, representing power to cut through obstacles and ignorance and transform it into wisdom.

In Tibetan called Khadroma meaning "sky goer." Various poems and hymns attributed to their wisdom and power.
