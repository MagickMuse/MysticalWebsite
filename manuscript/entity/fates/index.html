The Weavers of Destiny

The Fates appear in Greek myth as the Moirai, in Norse myth as the Norns, and in Roman story as the Parcae. These three women personify destiny, pictured as weavers who work the metaphorical thread of life to spin out the fate of every living being, even, it was said, beyond death on the earthly plane. Even the gods were subject to their working and feared them for it.

Their Greek names reflect the labour of cloth-making, the fabric of life: Clotho the spinner spins the thread; Lachesis measures its span with her rule; and Atropos, whose name means unturning and shares a root with atrophy, decides when the thread is cut, that is, when a person dies, and chooses the manner of death as well. This image of the weaver who sets the pattern of a life is shared with the various forms of the spider Goddess.

The Fates are usually shown as heartless, calculating women. A fine portrayal of them survives at Castell Coch, the medieval castle in South Wales restored in the nineteenth century by the Marquess of Bute and designed by William Burges.

Narcissus Wreaths

The Fates wore wreaths of Narcissus. The Greeks twined the white stars of the odorous blossoms among the locks of the Eumenides as well, and crowns of narcissus flowers were placed upon the heads of the dead.
