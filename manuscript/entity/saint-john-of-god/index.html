Saint John of God is the patron saint of booksellers, bookbinders, hospital workers, and nurses.

Saint John of God is renowned for healing any ailment, but especially heart disease, mental illness, and alcoholism.

Classification

Roman Catholic saint.

Early Life

Bells allegedly rang spontaneously at the moment when John Ciudad (8 March 1495-8 March 1550) was born in Evora, Portugal, to extremely poor but devout peasants. When John, an only child, was eight, a mysterious man claiming to be a mendicant priest stopped at the family cottage. John's parents offered him their hospitality. In the morning, the stranger had vanished and John was gone, too.

The man eventually left John with strangers in Oropresa, Spain. John lived with this family until he was grown. (His mother died of a broken heart within a few months of his disappearance; his father joined the Franciscans.)

Dissolute Years

John worked as a shepherd until, at age twenty-two, his employer wanted him to marry his daughter. John ran away to live a debauched, dissolute life. A mercenary soldier, he drank, gambled, swore, patronized prostitutes, and trafficked in slaves. His military career ended in disgrace when he left his post guarding valuables, which were then stolen. He was dishonorably discharged.

The Holy Child

John drifted from job to job but eventually became a spiritual merchant, peddling religious books and holy cards. In 1536, while carrying a heavy load of books on a rocky Pyrenean mountain road, he encountered a ragged, barefoot, beautiful child walking alone. John ended up carrying the boy, who gradually became increasingly heavy.

When they reached a spring, John set the boy under a shady tree and went to get a drink. He heard the boy call him. When he turned, the ragged child had transformed into an apparition of the Holy Child, who handed John an open pomegranate crowned with a cross and told him that, from now on, his name was John of God and his cross would be Granada. The apparition vanished and John traveled to Granada.

The Breakdown

On 20 January 1537, the Feast of Saint Sebastian, the fiery mystic John of Avila was preaching in Granada when someone in the crowd started howling like an animal. It was John of God. He ran howling through the streets, tearing out his hair and beating his breast. He was brought to an insane asylum - in the parlance of that time, a "House of God" - where he was given the standard treatment: tied to the bed and beaten daily.

Nothing helped, until John of Avila arrived to see him and calmly advised him that if he wished to serve God, he should stop being hysterical and do something useful. John's breakdown terminated immediately. Released from the hospital, he made a pilgrimage to the Black Madonna of Guadalupe in Extremadura, who healed him.

The Hospital

John returned to Granada and rented a house, transforming it into a hospital for the poor and indigent with forty-six beds. No one volunteered to help; having witnessed his very public breakdown, everyone kept their distance except the sick and needy. So John did everything in his hospital himself, single-handedly. Eventually, he was reduced to begging in the streets of Granada for alms to care for his patients.

He attracted the assistance of angels, including Raphael Archangel. Finally, other men joined him in his mission of healing. John of God is among the saints who reputedly bore the stigmata.

Death and Legacy

John leaped into a river to save a drowning man and died on his fifty-fifth birthday from what is described as an overexerted heart. After his death, the men who worked with him in his hospital declared themselves a religious order, the Brothers of Saint John of God, or the Brothers Hospitallers. The order was officially approved in 1572 and is now responsible for the pope's medical and dental care.

Attributes

Crown of thorns, heart, alms.

Spiritual Allies

Venerate him beside the Black Madonna of Guadalupe (La Extremena) and the Holy Child, especially Baby Doctor Jesus.
