Overview

The belief that a being who dies leaves behind a shadowy imprint, one that lingers to haunt the places it once knew, resounds around the world. Its very pervasiveness shows how deep a root the ghost has in the human psyche. Even the idea of a Heaven, a Hell, or some other afterlife as a home for the disembodied spirit has not displaced the conviction, held by many, that ghosts truly exist. Nor need a ghost be human, for animals too may haunt the places of the living.

Necromancy and Spiritualism

Communication with ghosts was once called necromancy and was reckoned by superstitious Christians among the Black Arts. Today the same practice goes by the name of Spiritualism, whose rituals open and close with a nod to the Christian God, even though Christian belief held that ghosts still walking the earth were generally those who had not been buried under the auspices of the Church, and so were to be avoided.

Messages and Meaning

Ghosts may carry good or evil intent. At times they appear bearing some essential message they feel bound to deliver to the living, perhaps as proof of survival beyond death. In this they answer the almost universal need to believe in a consciousness that endures after the body has died.

Samhain and the Thin Veil

The old festival of Samhain, later renamed Halloween, is said to be the time when the veil between the two worlds grows thinnest, the ending of the pre-Christian year and the start of the new. It is traditionally set aside to honour the ghosts of the ancestors.
