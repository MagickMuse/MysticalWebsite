Magickal Properties

Angus Mac Og embodies the divine masculine principle of transformative love that can overcome any obstacle and heal any heartbreak. As the Lord of Love who helps lovers in need and peril, he carries the archetypal energy of love that transcends conventional limitations and finds solutions to impossible romantic situations.

His power resonates with impossible love made possible, the healing of broken hearts, protection of lovers facing obstacles, and the divine conception that occurs when time itself can be stopped to allow love to manifest. He represents love as cosmic force rather than mere emotion.

Ritual Uses

Impossible Love Workings: Call upon his energy when love faces seemingly insurmountable obstacles, family opposition, distance, social barriers, or other complications.

Heartbreak Healing: Invoke his comfort and protection for those suffering from lost love, rejection, or romantic disappointment.

Love Obstacle Removal: Petition his assistance to find creative solutions to romantic problems that seem to have no resolution.

Divine Timing Magick: Use his energy to work with timing in love matters, as he has power over time itself.

Offerings

Music and poetry (as befits the son of the divine musician), offerings made at stone circles and ancient sites (particularly Newgrange), acts of kindness toward heartbroken individuals, and celebrations of successful love against all odds.

Favored People

Lovers facing impossible obstacles, those with broken hearts seeking healing, good-looking young men, champions in hurling or other athletic competitions, philosophers seeking wisdom about love, and anyone born under the sign of Leo.

Petition For

Success in impossible love situations, healing from heartbreak and romantic disappointment, protection for lovers facing opposition, creative solutions to romantic problems, courage to pursue true love despite obstacles, and comfort during periods of romantic loneliness or despair.

Warnings

Honor with song and gentle offerings; never weaponize petitions against others' beloveds. Angus Mac Og's assistance with love is powerful but may require significant life changes or sacrifices. His help often comes with unexpected developments that transform your entire situation, not just the immediate problem.

Additional Notes

His conception story, born when the Dagda and Boann stopped the sun to hide their affair and complete pregnancy and birth in a single day, establishes his mastery over time and his connection to love that transcends normal limitations.

His own love story in "The Dream of Angus" demonstrates his teaching about love, he literally fell in love with a woman from his dreams and had to undergo

His residence at Brugh na Boinne (Newgrange), the massive megalithic tomb dated to around 3200 BCE, connects his energy to ancient earth power and the cycles of death and rebirth. This suggests that true love involves ego death and spiritual rebirth.

His role as champion hurler indicates his connection to skillful action and competition. In love matters, this suggests that winning love requires skill, practice, and willingness to compete fairly rather than relying solely on magickal force.

His association with birds (Angus of the Birds) connects him to messages between realms and the soul's ability to transcend physical limitations in pursuit of love.

The fact that he was raised by the Dagda after his unusual birth suggests his connection to earth power and abundance, while his relationship with Boann links him to the flowing, adaptive nature of water.

His patronage of young men specifically emphasizes his role in helping the inexperienced navigate the complexities of mature love relationships.

Period of Worship

Active veneration spans roughly 500 BCE to 400 CE, centered on the Brugh na Boinne complex at Newgrange.

Literary Sources

His stories are preserved in the Books of Invasions and the Cycles of Kings, the medieval Irish manuscript traditions that gathered earlier oral lore.

Parentage

He is the son of the Dagda by the wife of Elcmar, generally identified as Boann, conceived during the day the Dagda stopped the sun to conceal the affair.

The Dream of Aengus and Caer Ibormeith

He fell in love with a woman who appeared to him only in dreams. After a long search she was identified as Caer Ibormeith, who lived in alternating years as a human and as a swan. He found her among a flock of swans on a lake at Samhain, took swan form himself, and the two flew together to Brugh na Boinne.
