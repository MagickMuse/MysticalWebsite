⚠️ Babalu Ayé is venerated within Yoruba, Lukumi/Santería, Haitian Vodou, and Afro-Brazilian traditions (Candomblé, Umbanda). These are closed initiatory practices and should only be approached by those within the originating cultures or with explicit cultural permission.

Babalu Ayé is renowned for healing skin diseases, infectious diseases, and epidemics. He brings both disease and its cure, protecting the poor and marginalized.

Magickal Properties

  • Lord of epidemic and skin afflictions who brings both disease and its cure
  • Protector of the poor, exiled, and marginalized; stern dispenser of justice and mercy

Ritual Uses

  • Petition for healing of infectious and skin diseases, protection from plague, purification, and just redress against arrogance or cruelty
  • Vows of service and humility are favored

Offerings

  • Popcorn, roasted corn, sesame seeds, candy, cookies, cigars, cowrie shells, chicken, white wine, milagros (small or full‑sized)

Favored People

The humble, outcasts, beggars, those suffering illness (including viral/skin diseases), caregivers and healers serving the poor

Petition For

Healing and protection from contagion, relief from suffering, spiritual purification, and justice against the arrogant or immoral

Warnings

Approach with great respect and never casually. Do not walk alone at noon wearing red and do not offer water, which irritates his sores. Observe house protocols.

Brazilian Traditions (Omolu)

In Afro-Brazilian traditions, this spirit is known as Omolu. He not only transmits disease but can heal any illness. His hair and scepter are adorned with cowrie shells because, like the shells of the deepest sea, he is privy to secrets. (Cowries were also perceived as resembling burst smallpox pustules.) His sacred day in Brazilian practice is Monday.

Additional Notes

  • Names and roads include Obaluaiyé, Sagbata, Sakpata, Omolu, Soponnon, Asohin, Asojano
  • Venerated across Yoruba, West African, Afro-Caribbean, and Afro-Brazilian traditions
