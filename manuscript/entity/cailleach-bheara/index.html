Lughnasadh

Landscape Goddess

Irish sovereignty goddess who literally formed geography. Cairns are stones fallen from her apron. Shaped mountains, formed rivers, created Beare Peninsula. Numerous Dingle Peninsula formations bear her name. Sleeps beneath Cailleach Bhéara's Chair cairn in County Armagh.

Corn Mother

Taught grain harvesting secrets. Challenges people to unwinnable reaping contests. Manifests as rabbit in grain fields, reaping last sheaf means "driving out the hare." Sometimes appears as snake (possibly what St. Patrick "banished").

Identity and Forms

Known as Búi in 14th-century manuscripts, one of solar deity Lugh's wives. "Búi" (yellow) may reference sun or smith's fires.

As Cailleach Mor ("Great Hag"): Sends southwesterly gales. Keeper of deer (fairy cattle). May be another name for Cailleach Bhéara. Cailleach Bhéara herself may be one spirit or multiple distinct beings sharing this title across different regions.

The Trinity

Forms holy triumvirate with sisters Cailleach Bolus and Cailleach Corca Duibhne, three sovereignty goddesses who shape, rule, harvest the land.
