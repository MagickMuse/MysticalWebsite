Saint Bartholomew of the Sea is renowned for healing children with developmental disorders and speech difficulties.

Classification

Unofficial saint (Portuguese folk tradition).

Identity Mystery

No church authority recognizes a separate "Bartholomew of the Sea." Officially, only the Apostle bears this name. Yet in Portugal, Bartholomew manifests so differently from his standard form that many question whether they're truly the same figure.

Iconography

Unlike the traditional Apostle shown with flayed skin, this maritime Bartholomew brandishes a sword or trident. At his feet crouches an ambiguous creature: part demon, part dolphin, part canine sea-beast. This entity serves as his messenger and partner.

Hidden Identity

Scholars speculate that the Greek sea god Poseidon wears Bartholomew's mask in Portuguese tradition. The creature at his feet may be Scylla, the beautiful nymph whom Poseidon's jealous wife Amphitrite (or the sorceress Circe, depending on the tale) transformed into a monstrous sea-dog.

Powers and Domains

Like Poseidon (but unlike the Apostle), this Bartholomew presides over horse racing. Gamblers seek his tips on the ponies and should share their winnings as thanks.

He also retrieves lost objects. Direct your request to the saint; his creature-servant will track down and deliver what you seek. Both spirits expect payment. The merdog is a partner, not merely a slave, and deserves respectful treatment.

Mariners invoke him for protection at sea and bountiful fishing. He commands the weather, able to summon, direct, or calm winds, waves, and tempests. Parents also petition him to heal children struggling with developmental challenges or speech difficulties.

Feast Day

24 August (shared with the Apostle). Tradition calls for eating black hens or their eggs in his honor.

Colors

Red for the saint; black for his servant.
