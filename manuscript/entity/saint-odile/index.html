Saint Odile is the patron saint of the blind, the visually impaired, clairvoyants, and fortunetellers. She is renowned for healing blindness, restoring eyesight, and curing all manner of eye diseases and vision disorders.

Saint Odile of Alsace (c. 662–720) is one of the most beloved patron saints of the blind and visually impaired. Born blind to a noble Alsatian family, she miraculously received her sight at the moment of her baptism.

Life and Legend

Odile was born blind to Duke Adalric of Alsace and his wife Bereswinda. Her father, ashamed of her disability, ordered her killed, but her mother secretly sent her away to be raised in a monastery. When Odile was about twelve years old, Bishop Erhard of Regensburg received a vision instructing him to baptize her. At the moment the holy water touched her eyes, she received her sight for the first time.

Her father eventually accepted her back, but when he tried to force her into marriage, she fled. Legend says that a rock miraculously opened to hide her from her pursuers. Eventually, her father relented and gave her his castle of Hohenburg, which she converted into a convent.

Patronage and Powers

Saint Odile is invoked for:

  • Healing blindness and restoring sight
  • Curing eye diseases and infections
  • Protecting eyesight
  • Helping those born with visual impairments

Her intercession is particularly sought by those facing eye surgery or dealing with degenerative eye conditions.

Sacred Sites

Mont Sainte-Odile in Alsace, France, remains one of the most visited pilgrimage sites in the region. A spring near the convent, called the "Spring of Saint Odile," is believed to have healing properties for eye ailments. Pilgrims wash their eyes with its waters seeking cures.

Iconography

Saint Odile is typically depicted:

  • As an abbess holding a book with two eyes resting on its pages
  • With a rooster (symbol of light conquering darkness)
  • Near a spring or fountain
  • With rays of light emanating from her eyes

Feast Day

December 13
