Saint Geneviève is the patron saint of Paris, female soldiers, and candle makers.

Saint Geneviève is renowned for healing and preventing disasters, including floods and drought. She can soothe and calm the possessed.

Classification

Venerated as a saint in most branches of Christianity.

Life and History

When Geneviève (c. 422-500) was seven years old, Germanus, Bishop of Auxerre and future Saint Germain, visited Nanterre, her hometown. He singled out the little girl for a special blessing, predicting she would be a holy woman.

At fifteen, after her wealthy parents died, Geneviève traveled to Paris to live with her godmother. She dedicated herself to spiritual vocations and charity work, subjecting herself to severe austerities. She consumed nothing but bread and water, only twice a week, fasting the rest of the time. Perhaps unsurprisingly, she experienced visions, which she shared publicly.

She evoked strong reactions. Some considered her holy; others were ready to drown her.

The Attila Prophecy

In 451, when Attila the Hun prepared to invade Paris and Parisians prepared to flee, Geneviève pleaded with them not to leave, insisting Attila would retreat. She was denounced as a witch and false prophet. People threatened to drown her in the Seine.

She was absolutely correct. Attila changed course and Paris was spared. Her prayers are frequently credited with diverting him. Bishop Germanus sent an emissary to ensure her safety.

The Frankish Siege

When the Franks under Childeric blockaded Paris, Geneviève organized and led a rescue mission bringing grain to starving Parisians. Though Childeric conquered Paris, he admired her courage. She became a regular guest at court, forming a close friendship with his son Clovis, first king of France, and Clovis's Christian wife Clotilde. She is credited with Clovis's conversion in 496, though Clotilde actually converted him.

Death and Desecration

King Clovis founded the Church of Saints Peter and Paul, where Geneviève was eventually interred. Miracles at her grave caused the church to be renamed in her honor and led to her canonization. In 1791, during the French Revolution, the church was renamed the Panthéon. Revolutionaries publicly burned her remains there in 1793.

Powers and Domains

Saint Geneviève may be invoked for anything, but her specialty is preventing disasters, including floods and drought. She can allegedly soothe and calm the possessed.

Iconography

Portrayed as a shepherdess (understood metaphorically, not literally), or standing in a boat bearing grain.

Attributes

Loaf of bread, key, candle.

Sacred Site

The Chapel of Saint Geneviève in Saint Sulpice is built over a site in Paris once dedicated to Isis.

Feast Day

3 January
