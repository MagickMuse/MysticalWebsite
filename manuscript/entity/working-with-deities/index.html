Working with Deities

A practical guide to establishing relationships with gods and goddesses, understanding ethical considerations, and approaching divine beings with respect.

Simple Rules for Working with Divine Beings

Don't Mix Pantheons Carelessly

Try not to mix pantheons in a single working. If you have a working relationship with deities from pantheons that have a tumultuous past, you know your own practice best. However, just slapping names on a candle can have disastrous results, especially if the cultures represented had wars over it.

Build Relationships First

If you're going to work with the gods, get to know them. Develop a relationship with the deities that carry a deeper meaning for you. It will make your magick more effective, it can deepen your personal practice, and it helps practitioners stay within appropriate bounds and keep from infringing on Black or Indigenous cultures who have already lost so much. Besides, in doing your magickal homework, perhaps you will meet the divine of your dreams and find a way to build relationships.

Deity Work is Optional

Just know that gods and spirits are not a mandatory part of magick. If you're:

  • Uncomfortable working with gods outside of the religion you were raised with (if any), or
  • Concerned about trampling on someone else's faith expression or practice, or
  • Atheist

...that is okay. Magick works without deity involvement.

Practice Ethical Self-Examination

Examining one's own inherent prejudices, feelings, and any potential bias is what makes for responsible, ethical witches.

Etiquette for Working with Deities

Certain rules apply when working with divine helpers, as in any relationship:

  • Ask for assistance - Guides, guardians, and deities respect your free will and may not intervene unless invited to do so.
  • Show respect - Treat deities as honored teachers and allies, not servants.
  • Avoid micromanaging - When seeking aid from divine beings, turn over the reins and allow them to carry out your request as they see fit.
  • Do not seek help for harmful purposes - Benevolent deities will not support harmful causes, and you do not want to attract malevolent entities.
  • Express gratitude - Thank the beings who assist you and consider giving them an offering.

Ways to Attract Divine Assistance

Deities may come to you in dreams, meditation, or visions. Throughout history, countless people have experienced visitations from divine beings. Such appearances can happen to anyone, anytime.

If spontaneous contact does not occur, other methods can catch a deity's attention:

  • Research thoroughly - Learn as much as possible about the deity you wish to attract through myths, spiritual literature, and folklore.
  • Create a dedicated altar - Set up and dedicate an altar to your chosen deity.
  • Display sacred imagery - Collect and display artwork depicting the deities you wish to petition in a prominent place.
  • Make appropriate offerings - Discover what specific deities prefer (particular plants, foods, stones) and place these on your altar or in a special outdoor location.
  • Stay receptive - Keep an open mind and heart when working with deities. Trust your intuition to tune into higher frequencies.
  • Cultivate belief - Trust that deities will communicate with you. Doubt closes the door between you and divine beings. When dealing with supernatural forces, you'll see it when you believe it.

Protocol for Established Traditions

When working with deities from living traditions (Tibetan Buddhism, Vodou, Santería, Hinduism, etc.), respect existing protocols. Seek proper initiation or empowerment where required. Do not innovate or combine incompatible systems.

Deities tied to specific cultural and religious contexts carry the full weight of those systems. Working with deities from closed practices requires proper entry into those systems.

Study the tradition thoroughly or work with qualified initiates.

The Reciprocal Relationship

What do deities gain from this arrangement? Relationships with divine beings are reciprocal. Devotees provide veneration, offerings, and attention; deities provide guidance, protection, and assistance. Either party may initiate contact. Deities sometimes appear in dreams or visions to offer their patronage voluntarily.

This exchange is not transactional in a cold sense, it is relational. The most powerful working relationships develop over time through consistent devotion, not single requests made in crisis.

Finding Your Deities

Many practitioners feel called to specific deities. Pay attention to:

  • Recurring themes - Do certain myths, symbols, or cultures keep appearing in your life?
  • Ancestral connections - What did your ancestors worship? These connections can be powerful.
  • Personal resonance - Whose stories move you? Which deities do you find yourself thinking about?
  • Signs and synchronicities - Repeated encounters with a deity's symbols, animals, or imagery may indicate their interest in you.

Some practitioners work with a single patron deity. Others maintain relationships with multiple deities from one or more pantheons. Your practice should fit your needs and circumstances.

When Deities Don't Respond

Sometimes requests go unanswered. Possible reasons include:

  • The deity is not the right match for you or your request
  • Your approach was inappropriate for that tradition
  • The timing is wrong
  • What you requested is not in your best interest
  • You haven't built sufficient relationship yet

Don't take silence as rejection. Consider approaching differently, choosing a different deity, or examining whether your request truly serves your highest good.
