Magickal Properties

The Aglaurides are the three daughters of Aglauros (a Nymph) and Cecrops (snake spirit and first king of Athens): Aglauros, Herse, and Pandrosos. They tended Athena's shrine and were entrusted with guarding a closed casket containing either a snake, an infant guarded by a snake, or a human/snake composite.

Ritual Uses

Work with the Aglaurides for protection of sacred knowledge and guardianship of secrets. They represent the guardians of serpentine mysteries and sacred thresholds. Their story connects to the transition from older goddess worship to Olympian deities.

Offerings

Appropriate offerings would acknowledge their connection to serpents and their role as guardians of sacred mysteries. Items that honor their guardianship role and serpent associations.

Favored People

Those who work with serpent mysteries, guardians of sacred secrets, people working with threshold magick, and practitioners of ancient Greek mysteries.

Petition For

Protection of sacred knowledge, assistance with serpentine mysteries, guardianship of secrets, and understanding of ancient threshold magic.

Warnings

The Aglaurides' story involves madness and suicide after seeing forbidden contents of Athena's casket. Approach with caution when dealing with forbidden knowledge or attempting to access mysteries beyond your preparation level.

Additional Notes

The name means "children of Aglauros," emphasizing their maternal lineage from before Athens was dedicated to Athena. When they disobeyed Athena's command not to look in the casket, they were struck mad and leaped to their deaths from the Acropolis. They allegedly still haunt the cliffs and have been witnessed dancing near Pan's cave.
