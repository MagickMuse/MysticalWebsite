Overview

Volos is the lord of cattle, wolves, wealth, and well-being. He defends the balance between wolves and domestic animals. Volos has dominion over birth and death: his power is manifest in alternating cycles of birth, growth, death, and decay. Oaths are traditionally sworn upon his name as Volos punishes oath-breakers as well as others who displease him with disease. In some regions, Volos is considered a lord of death and the ruler of an afterlife realm.

Magickal Properties

Volos, a particularly beloved deity, was historically venerated throughout the Slavic world. Even after he was officially banished, Volos did not disappear but transformed into Christian saints. Russian Saint Vlasii is the patron saint of livestock, especially cattle. Russian icons portray him surrounded by cows and sheep. His image is placed in the barn to provide protection. His blessings are invoked when cattle are ailing or infertile. Officially, Saint Veles is just the Croatian pronunciation of Saint Blaise, patron of the city of Dubrovnik. However, local people insist that their Saint Veles is a local saint, not the Armenian bishop. His specialty is guarding flocks.

Manifestation

Volos may appear in the guise of a snake, dragon or a horned, bearded man. He has the capacity to continually regenerate in the manner that a snake sheds its skin and so Volos may appear old or youthful.

Iconography

An image of Volos was kept in Kiev's merchant quarters near the Pochaina River. When Vladimir I, Grand Prince of Kiev (circa 950-1015), converted to Christianity, this statue was allegedly thrown into the river. Likewise his stone image in Rostov, Russia, was destroyed and a church built on the site.

Baltic Aspects

In the Baltic and Lithuanian traditions, Volos is known as Velinas, Velnias, Velas, or Velns. He is the one-eyed patron of seers and prophets and the lord of the dead, riding through the night leading a host of ghosts and ancestral spirits also called the Veles. He is the most popular figure in Lithuanian folklore and allegedly has over two hundred names. In modern Lithuanian, Velnias may be translated as devil.

He has a complicated relationship with people. He blesses and protects but also tricks and seduces. He has a reputation as a Lothario, is very fond of human women, and may engage them in sacred marriage. He loves musicians and asks them to play for him. He can be a guardian spirit who protects against enemies, but he can also be hostile in tricky, indirect ways. His consort is Veliona, the Dark Mother, who rules the dead with him.

He is a master shape-shifter who takes the form of animals, birds, and reptiles as well as people. His Baltic attributes are the noose and the lance.

He presides over Velines, the traditional Lithuanian feast of the dead, which now corresponds to All Souls' Day (2 November) and the preceding eve but was once a substantially longer holiday. He also presides over a spring festival of the dead celebrated on the first full moon Sunday after the spring equinox. He presides over the Golbe spring near Insterburg (now part of modern Germany but once Prussia). Allegedly bathing one eye with the spring's water bestowed the gift of clairvoyance.

Additional Notes

1 January-6 January is the Festival of Wolves.
