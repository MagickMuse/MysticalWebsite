Magical Properties

Mithra/Mitra is an ancient Indo-European deity of contracts, oaths, light, and the sun. Originally a Vedic deity (Mitra), he became immensely popular in the Roman Empire as Mithra, where his mystery cult rivaled early Christianity. He is the god of oaths, covenants, and sacred bonds between people.

Mithra represents the sanctity of agreements, the power of sworn oaths, and the light that reveals truth and exposes lies. He is a solar deity associated with justice, loyalty, and the bonds that hold society together.

Ritual Uses

Invoke Mithra for oath-taking, contract magic, and ensuring agreements are honored. Work with him for justice, especially in matters of broken contracts or violated oaths. His energy supports covenant work and the sacred bonds between people. Call upon him to witness and strengthen vows.

Warnings

Mithra demands absolute adherence to oaths and contracts. Do not invoke him lightly or make promises you cannot keep. Breaking an oath made in his presence brings severe consequences. Observe proper covenant formalities and oath-based protocols.

Mithra and the Birthday of the Unconquered Sun

In the Roman world Mithra was honored on December 25 as the Dies Natalis Invicti Solis, the Birthday of the Unconquered Sun. His story ran close to one that would become far more famous: he was said to be born on that day in humble circumstances to a virgin mother, to have died, and to have risen again, and his faith held out a brotherhood of all people and the promise of eternal life to those who kept a pure moral code. The cult spread through Europe with the Roman soldiers who carried it on campaign, and at least one temple to him still stands in Britain, just south of Hadrian's Wall in Northumberland. That midwinter feast of the returning Sun was in time absorbed into Christmas.
