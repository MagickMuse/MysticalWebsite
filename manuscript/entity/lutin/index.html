Origin and Identity

Lutins are French house spirits, described as resembling goblins or imps. They're tricky and a bit temperamental but not malevolent, evil, or harmful. If they like the people in whose house they live, they will serve as guardians. If angered, they play tricks: they'll hide your shoes, mismatch your shoes, tie the laces together, or hide pebbles in them. They'll blunt knives and scissors.

Note: Lutin refers to the male of this spirit species. Females are known as Lutines.

Behavior and Personality

Lutins can make themselves invisible but you may hear them giggling at your discomfiture. Despite their mischievous nature, they are not evil. They simply demand respect and proper treatment. They represent the traditional French house spirit who, when honored, becomes a devoted protector and helper.

Manifestation

Lutins may manifest in several forms:

  • Small people (resembling goblins or imps)
  • Domestic animals, especially horses
  • White cats

Attributes

Lutins own hats which provide invisibility, typically a red hat with feathers. This magickal headgear is their most prized possession and the source of their ability to move unseen.

Magickal Properties

Lutins embody the spirits of hearth and home in French folk tradition. Like similar house spirits across Europe, they represent the animistic belief that homes themselves have guardian spirits who must be honored and cared for. Their dual nature, helpful when pleased and tricksy when angered, teaches the importance of reciprocal relationships with the spirit world.

Ritual Uses

Lutin can be charmed and bribed into becoming guardians. Be very nice and polite to them and leave food offerings for them in the kitchen after dark. They'll have a bit of whatever you're having except that they dislike salt.

In exchange for kindness, affection, and regular offerings, the Lutin will:

  • Guard children, animals, house, and land
  • Perform household chores at night while the family sleeps
  • Protect the home from malevolent spirits
  • Bring good fortune to the household

Related House Spirits

Lutins represent a widespread class of European house spirits. Compare with Domovoi in Russia, Kobold in Germany, and Brownie in Scotland. Their presence in French folklore demonstrates the pan-European belief in household guardian spirits who require regular offerings and respectful treatment. The detail about their invisibility hats suggests connection to fairy folk traditions, where magickal headgear often confers supernatural powers.
