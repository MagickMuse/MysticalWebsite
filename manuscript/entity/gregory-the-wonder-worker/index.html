Gregory the Wonder Worker is venerated as a saint by most branches of Christianity, renowned for healing through laying on of hands and for providing aid during natural disasters such as floods and earthquakes.

Life

Gregory (c. 213-c. 270) was born in the city of Neocaesaria in Pontus, which is now in modern Turkey. His birth name was Theodore. He came from an elegant, distinguished family; his brother-in-law was the Roman governor of Palestine.

After his father's death when Gregory was fourteen, he and his brother wished to study law at the university in Beirut. They met Origen, the early Christian scholar and theologian, and converted to Christianity, forgetting about practicing law. Gregory eventually returned to Pontus, where he lived as a hermit.

Even without a university degree, Gregory was perceived as extremely scholarly. He was eventually appointed bishop without being asked. He was a famed exorcist and faith healer who healed the sick by the laying on of hands. He won many converts this way and settled disputes between enemies, drawing upon his legal studies.

Miracles

Gregory became famed for miracles. Once, two brothers in his diocese inherited property containing a lake. Unable to decide how to divide it, they agreed on armed combat. The night before, Gregory prayed the matter would be resolved without violence. When the men woke up, the lake had dried up and disappeared.

During violent oppression of Christians, Gregory sought shelter in the mountains. When it was over, he collected the relics of the martyrs and honored them with feasts scheduled to correspond with Pagan festivals, so that Pagan traditions were gradually subsumed by Christian feast days. Gregory is among those most credited with encouraging the cult of the saints.

Powers and Domains

Gregory is hailed as a wonder worker, and reputedly no miracle is impossible for him. Thus he is invoked for forgotten, impossible, and lost causes. His help is sought during natural disasters like floods and earthquakes.

Iconography

Saint Gregory is often portrayed in the process of banishing demons.

Number: 17

When Gregory left Pontus for Beirut, there were seventeen Christians in his birth city. In 275, he asked how many Pagans were in Neocaesaria and was advised that seventeen were left. He died in his sleep after hearing this news on 17 November.
