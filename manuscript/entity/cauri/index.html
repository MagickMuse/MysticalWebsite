Overview

Cauri is a goddess of terrifying appearance in the Buddhist tradition of Tibet, one of a group of eight gauri goddesses who form a retinue within the wrathful mandalas of the mother tantras of Vajrayana practice. Her name belongs to the gauri family, whose common Sanskrit root gauri means the fair one or the shining one, and in the Buddhist wrathful context that fairness is turned into the searing brightness of the wrathful mandala rather than the pale beauty of a peaceful goddess. She belongs alongside Candali and the other six gauris of the group as one of the eight fierce retinue goddesses.

Classification

Goddess of terrifying appearance in the Buddhist Vajrayana tradition of Tibet. One of the eight gauri goddesses of the wrathful mother-tantra mandalas.

Domains

Wrathful protection under the Vajrayana frame, the binding of ignorance through the noose that is her particular attribute, the retinue office of the fierce feminine gathered around the central yidam of the mandala, the searing brightness of the wrathful goddesses of the mother tantras.

Iconography

Depicted of yellow color, standing or dancing in the wrathful stance characteristic of the gauri goddesses. Her particular attribute among the group is the noose, which she holds ready to bind the roots of ignorance and the escape of the practitioner's own confused mind. Her wrathful ornaments follow the standard iconography of the tantric goddesses of Tibet: skull crown, bone ornaments, tiger-skin skirt, and severed-head garland.

Attributes

Noose.

Colors

Yellow.

Sacred Sites

Within the wrathful mandalas of the mother tantras of Tibetan Vajrayana practice, particularly the mandalas of the Cakrasamvara and related yidam cycles in which the eight gauris appear as retinue goddesses. Her worship belongs to the transmitted sadhana rather than to independent temple cults.

Ritual

Her practice is undertaken within a transmitted lineage of the Vajrayana tradition. In her retinue office she is invoked to hold the fierce protective ground of the mandala together with the other seven gauri goddesses, and she is not approached as an isolated personal deity outside that mandala frame.

Lore

The eight gauri goddesses of the wrathful mother-tantra mandalas are one of several groups of retinue goddesses in the Vajrayana tradition, and their office is to hold the perimeter of the mandala at each of the eight directions while the central yidam works at the center. Their names distinguish them by their attributes and colors, and Cauri, the yellow gauri of the noose, holds her particular office within that eightfold structure. The yellow color places her in the ratna, the jewel family of the mandala, whose wisdom is the wisdom of equanimity, and the noose is her tool for binding whatever would obstruct the equanimous recognition of every being.
