Ancient White One

Origin: Wales

The Legend

Henwen is a goddess of fertility, abundance, and prosperity venerated in the form of a great white sow. She was tended by Coll ab Collfrewi, one of Britain's three great swineherds, who pastured her in a valley in Cornwall.

All was well until Henwen conceived. A prophecy foretold that Henwen's progeny would harm Britain, and so King Arthur attempted to capture and kill her. (An alternative version suggests that Henwen was specifically a threat to him personally.)

The Great Escape

Arthur assembled a host of knights against this one big pig, but she escaped by tunneling through Earth until she reached the sea. An ocean may stop a regular pig but not a goddess. Henwen plunged into the depths with Coll hanging on to her bristles the whole time. (It's unclear if he's just hanging on or if he was instrumental in trying to save her.)

Gifts to the Land

Periodically emerging from the waters, wherever Henwen touched Earth, she brought forth from her own body:

  • Incredibly fine wheat and barley
  • Bees that produce the best honey in Britain
  • An eaglet
  • A wolf
  • A ferocious cat (no domestic cats existed in Britain at this time, only wildcats)

Exactly what happened to Henwen is unclear. She was not captured and so is presumably still free and roaming.

The Hidden Goddess

A hidden goddess lurks beneath this legendary pig, possibly similar to that other great white sow, Cerridwen. Henwen was an oracular sow with profound prophetic powers.

Oracular Method

Letter sticks formed from rods made of ash and carved with runic symbols were stuck into the ground. When asked a question, Henwen would point her nose at different sticks in turn, spelling out an answer. Her oracular powers were only invoked in times of emergency.

Invocations

Invoke Henwen for:

  • Fertility and abundance
  • Adventure and courage
  • Prophetic answers (especially in emergencies)

Offerings: Ale, mead, barley wine

Children's Literature: The Rainbow Bridge by Audrey Wood (Voyager Books, 1995) is a lavishly illustrated retelling of this Chumash creation myth.
