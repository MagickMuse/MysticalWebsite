Kami are the divine spirits of Shinto, the indigenous religion of Japan. The term encompasses an enormous variety of spiritual beings: nature spirits inhabiting mountains, rivers, trees, and rocks; deified ancestors; protective household spirits; and great deities. Everything that inspires awe or possesses exceptional qualities can be kami.

Kami are not gods in the Western sense but rather spiritual presences that permeate the natural and human world. They range from local spirits of specific places to major deities like Amaterasu. Humans can become kami after death.

Ritual Uses

Kami are honored through shrine visits, offerings, prayers, and maintaining purity. Each kami has specific protocols. General practices include bowing, clapping, ringing bells, and leaving offerings at shrines. Approach with respect and follow proper Shinto etiquette.

Warnings

Kami traditions are sacred to Shinto practice. If working with specific kami, research their individual protocols. Maintain ritual purity when approaching shrines or making offerings. Respect sacred boundaries and prohibitions.
