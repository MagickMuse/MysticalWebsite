Bombay Kamayan is a local disease goddess of the Hindu tradition of northern India. She belongs to the wide class of Indian disease goddesses, whose office is at once to bring illness and to withhold it, so that petition is directed toward the same goddess who is understood as the source of the affliction.

Domains

Disease and its withdrawal, the health of the community, and the local sovereignty of the goddess over the illnesses that pass through the households of her region.

Cult Center

Bombay Kamayan is particularly worshiped at Gaya, an ancient sacred city in the Indian state of Bihar long known as a place of pilgrimage in the Hindu tradition. Her connection to Gaya sets her within the wider religious geography of the north Indian sacred landscape, though her cult remains local in character.

Petition For

Protection from illness and epidemic, the relief of suffering in the sick, and the intercession of the disease goddess in matters of communal and household health.

Precautions

Bombay Kamayan belongs to the class of Hindu disease goddesses whose worship is embedded in local ritual custom. Approach her with the recognition that her power is understood as double, source and withholder of the affliction alike, and that her worship traditionally belongs to the community that lives within her cult area.
