Overview

Ganga is the goddess of the Ganges, the most sacred river of the Hindu world and the very image of a river understood as a living, divine mother. She is revered less through statues than through the flowing water itself: devotees garland the river, bathe in it, drink it, and carry it home as a sacred substance.

Magickal Properties

Fertility, healing (physical, emotional, spiritual), removal of anger, shame, or guilt, blessings of abundance, purification.

Heavenly Origin and Descent

Ganga is said to flow first in heaven and to come to earth only through a great act of grace. In the best known account, a king performs centuries of fierce austerities to bring her down so that the ashes of his ancestors, burned by the wrath of an offended sage, can be purified and freed for their onward journey. Ganga warns that the force of her fall would shatter the earth, so Shiva agrees to catch her on his head. She lands in his tangled hair and is divided into many streams, one of which winds its way across the land to redeem the dead. Other accounts have her pour into the world through a crack in the vault of heaven opened by Vishnu's stride, or be carried in Brahma's water pot, so that she touches the great gods before she ever reaches the earth.

The Great Purifier

Ganga is honored above all rivers as a remover of pollution. To see, touch, drink, or bathe in her waters is held to wash away sin and sickness, and even a breeze carrying a single drop of her water is said to cleanse. Though she carries away the impurities of all who come to her, she herself is believed to remain forever pure, potent, and gracious. To die in her waters, or to have a few drops placed on the lips at the moment of death, is held to grant liberation.

Bridge Between the Worlds

A sacred river is understood as a crossing place between the human and the divine, and Ganga is the crossing place above all others. The dying are brought to her banks, especially at the holy city on her shore, in the hope of release, and the ashes and bones of the dead are given to her current to ensure a safe passage to the realm of the ancestors. Just as her waters are imagined descending endlessly from heaven to earth, so a steady procession of souls is imagined ascending her stream toward the higher worlds. She is called a flowing staircase to heaven.

Mother Ganges

Her most beloved title means simply Mother Ganges. She is approachable, tangible, and all-accepting, turning none away. Those cast out by everyone else, judged too polluted for any other holy place, are still received and cleansed by her. Her waters are likened to milk and to the nectar of immortality, and she is praised as a mother who sustains her children with her own body.

Nourisher of the Land

As a mother she feeds the land she flows through. The plains along her banks are richly fertile, renewed by the sediment of her floods, and she is often shown carrying a dish of food and an overflowing pot. Farmers set her water in the fields before sowing to ensure a good harvest, and newly married women pray to her for children and for the long life of their husbands. Her waters are also used to restore the sick to health.

The Cooling Consort of Shiva

Ganga stands beside Parvati as a consort of Shiva, and like Parvati she represents the cool, nourishing, fertile force that calms and balances his fierce, burning nature. When Shiva's seed proves too hot to be held by any vessel, it is cooled at last in Ganga's waters, and within her the war god Karttikeya takes form and is born. The continual pouring of her water over Shiva's emblem in his temples carries the same meaning: she softens and grounds his austere, dangerous power and carries his presence out into the world and to his devotees.
