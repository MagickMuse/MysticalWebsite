Nguyen Trung Truc

Nguyen Trung Truc (1837 to 27 October 1868) is a Vietnamese folk saint, the leader of resistance against French colonialism in the 1860s. He led the raid resulting in the attack on the French warship L'Esperance. Enraged French authorities repeatedly tried to capture him but failed. He finally surrendered after authorities took his mother and other civilians hostage, and was publicly executed by firing squad in the marketplace.

Secret Veneration

During the era of French rule, Nguyen Trung Truc was venerated secretly, his identity concealed behind that of the Vietnamese whale deity Ca Ong (Sir Fish). Only after the French abandoned Vietnam was it legally possible to venerate an enemy of their rule openly.

Domains

Nguyen Trung Truc is considered an especially responsive spirit, petitioned for virtually everything. His shrine flourished under the government of South Vietnam. After the Communist victory, magical and spiritual practices at his shrine were forbidden. The shamans and fortune tellers who proliferated there were banished. Instead, he was honored merely as an important Vietnamese resistance hero. In recent years, however, veneration has become more open and widespread.

Sacred Sites

His primary temple and mausoleum in the southern Vietnamese port of Rach Gia.

Festival

An annual festival is held in conjunction with the commemoration of his death on the eighteenth and nineteenth days of the tenth lunar month of the Vietnamese calendar.
