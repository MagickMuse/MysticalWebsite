Magickal Properties

Chimeras are composite beasts, living collages of various animals. In Greek myth, the Chimera is a specific spirit, but the term is also applied generically to any composite creature. The classic Chimera has a lion's body, snake's tail, and goat's head emerging from its back. The snake hisses, the goat bleats, and the lion roars. The goat head allegedly breathes fire.

Ritual Uses

Chimeras are not typically invoked for positive magickal work. They are dangerous hybrid predators representing chaotic, destructive forces. Do not attempt to hunt, bind, or summon a chimera. If confronted by one, strong protective and banishing rituals are essential.

Offerings

Not applicable. Offerings are not made to chimeras as they are dangerous predatory spirits.

Favored People

Chimeras do not favor humans. They are antagonistic spirits representing untamed, chaotic natural forces.

Petition For

Chimeras are not petitioned. They represent danger and chaos rather than beneficial spiritual forces.

Warnings

Do not hunt, bind, or summon a chimera. They are dangerous hybrid predators with no interest in helping humans. Use strong protective and banishing rites if you encounter one. The classic Chimera of Greek myth was eventually slain by the hero Bellerophon riding Pegasus.

Additional Notes

The Chimera's mother is Echidna, a formidable spirit herself. Beyond the Greek Chimera, various cultures feature composite beasts - griffins (lion-eagle), manticores (human-headed lions with scorpion tails), and sphinxes. Each represents different combinations of animal powers merged into single dangerous entities.

Omens and Origins

The Chimera is first named in Homer's Iliad. Her father bore a hundred snake-like heads that each breathed fire, and she herself was said to be the mother of the sphinx. To glimpse her was a dire omen, foretelling storms, shipwrecks, and other disasters, volcanoes above all, and some hold that she embodies the very spirit of volcanic action.
