Classification

Aztec deity, Quetzalcoatl's twin and psychopomp of the dead.

Magickal Properties

In Meso-American cosmology, the assistance of a dog is required to reach the next realm. When someone dies, a spirit dog is expected to appear to lead the soul on its lengthy, arduous journey to the realm of death, and to supervise that journey. Xolotl, spirit of death, is simultaneously man and dog, living and dead: a magickal crossroads spirit who lives in many realms at once.

Xolotl is Quetzalcoatl's alter ego, shadow side, or double, known in Aztec terminology as a nahualli (nagual: a sacred animal double or ally). He is a faithful, devoted companion, assistant, and guide. His name refers to the concept of twinship; throughout the Americas, twins have simultaneously been feared and respected, perceived as extremely dangerous but also sacred.

He is the evening star counterpart of Quetzalcoatl's morning star. Every night he pushes the sun into darkness and guides it on its journey through the Underworld. He accompanied Quetzalcoatl when Quetzalcoatl went to steal the bones of the dead from Mictlan.

Ritual Uses

Xolotl is the root word from which the modern word "cholo" ("gangster") derives. He has dominion over ailments and physical disorders.

Manifestation

Xolotl may manifest as a man, a skeleton, a dog, or a man with a dog's head. Whether man or dog, one ear is usually ragged like a dog's, with sores or a constant ear infection. His face is deeply furrowed.

Attributes

A flint knife. Planet: Venus. Element: fire.

Correspondences

His namesake is the Xoloitzcuintli breed of dog (the Mexican Hairless), as well as creatures that undergo transformation or metamorphosis, like tadpoles becoming frogs, caterpillars becoming butterflies, and snakes that shed their skin and appear renewed.
