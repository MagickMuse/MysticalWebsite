Criminal Spirits as Spiritual Bodyguards

Malandros are the spirits of criminals (thieves, hoodlums, murderers), particularly those who died violently in criminal activities or gang warfare. Sometimes it takes a thug to beat a thug. Despite their criminal backgrounds, they are invoked as spiritual bodyguards and protectors, especially by those facing dangerous situations or threats. They represent the complex morality of street justice and protection through force.

They are venerated by those who cannot avoid dangerous streets, neighborhoods, and locations.

Spiritual Protection

The Malandros are petitioned to travel beside you, serving as a spiritual bodyguard and protecting from all danger. They are also invoked for justice, legal or otherwise.

The María Lionza Court

Malandros belong to the Court of María Lionza, one of the many spiritual courts in the Venezuelan tradition. They are part of the more recent additions to the pantheon, reflecting contemporary urban realities. Because they are criminals, the Malandros are sometimes asked to fulfill petitions other spirits might reject.

Manifestation

Malandros appear as tough street criminals, often dressed in contemporary urban clothing, wearing gold chains, and carrying weapons. They embody the aggressive, protective energy of gang members and street warriors.

Magickal Properties

The Malandros represent the pragmatic reality that sometimes protection requires calling upon those who understand violence and danger intimately. As spirits of those who lived outside the law, they are uniquely qualified to protect against criminal threats. Their inclusion in the María Lionza tradition demonstrates the practical, non-judgmental approach of Venezuelan Espiritismo, recognizing that spirits of all types have roles to play and that sometimes conventional "good" spirits are not equipped to handle certain types of danger.

Working with Malandros

Those who work with Malandros typically:

  • Face genuine danger in their daily lives
  • Need protection that conventional spirits cannot provide
  • Seek justice that may not come through legal channels
  • Understand that working with criminal spirits requires respect, clear boundaries, and ethical considerations

The Malandros are not "evil" but they are fierce, street-wise, and operate by their own code. They protect those who show them respect and who genuinely need their particular form of assistance.

Cultural Context

The veneration of Malandros reflects the realities of urban violence and the need for protection in dangerous environments. They represent a form of spiritual self-defense for those who may not have access to conventional means of protection.
