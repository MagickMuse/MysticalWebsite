Magickal Properties

Coyote is the paramount trickster spirit of Native American traditions, especially among Navajo and other Southwestern nations. He is both creator and fool, wise teacher and destructive troublemaker. Coyote brought fire to humanity and helped shape the world, but also causes chaos through his appetites, impulsiveness, and love of mischief. He represents the principle that rules can be broken and order disrupted.

Ritual Uses

Approach Coyote with extreme caution. He can teach important lessons about adaptability, survival, and seeing through illusions. He may help when you need to break free from rigid thinking or challenge unjust rules. However, any petition to Coyote will likely come with unexpected complications and ironic twists.

Offerings

Food offerings (especially meat), tobacco, items left at crossroads or wild places, offerings made with humor and acknowledgment of life's absurdities.

Favored People

Tricksters and boundary-crossers, those who challenge authority, people who can laugh at themselves, survivors who adapt to changing circumstances, those humble enough to learn from mistakes.

Petition For

Adaptability and survival skills, breaking free from rigid patterns, challenging unjust rules, seeing through deceptions, learning through experience (often hard experience), understanding the value of chaos.

Warnings

Be extremely specific and honest in any bargains with Coyote, he is a master trickster who exploits ambiguity and loopholes. Do not call on Coyote to harm others, as the magick will likely backfire in ironic ways. Expect unexpected consequences from any interaction. He may help you, but probably not in the way you anticipated. His lessons often come through embarrassment and failure.

Additional Notes

In Navajo tradition, Coyote is called Ma'ii. He is simultaneously sacred and profane, creative and destructive. Stories tell how he scattered the stars across the sky in impatience, introduced death to the world, and constantly gets into trouble through his appetites. Yet he also teaches crucial lessons about survival, adaptability, and the dangers of taking yourself too seriously. His animal form, the coyote, shares his characteristics, adaptable, clever, thriving even in hostile environments.

Coyote and the Milky Way

⚠️ A Diné (Navajo) story belonging to its originating culture; recorded here with respect. In one telling, Coyote is running across the desert with a bag of tepary beans slung over his shoulder when he trips. The beans scatter into the sky and become the Milky Way, the long pale band of stars that crosses the dome of night. In another telling, the First People had been placing stars in the sky one careful star at a time, out of a bag, and Coyote, impatient with the slow work, snatched the bag and flung the whole thing skyward at once. The Milky Way in this telling is the bean-bag that Coyote threw. The tepary bean (Phaseolus acutifolius) is native to the American Southwest and is one of the most drought-hardy crops on the continent; the story binds it to the night sky for as long as people look up.
