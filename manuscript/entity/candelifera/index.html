Overview

Candelifera is a minor goddess of birth in the Roman tradition, whose name means the one who carries the candle. She belongs to the wide company of small Roman goddesses assigned to particular acts and moments of childbirth and infancy, an approach to the sacred by which the Roman religion assigned a divine presence to each recognizable step of a life passage rather than gathering all such moments under a single great goddess. Candelifera's proper office is bringing the newborn child into the light, and she is usually honored alongside Lucina, the goddess of the birth-light itself, and the Carmentes, the birth-attending goddesses associated with Carmenta.

Classification

Minor goddess of birth in the Roman tradition. One of the small domestic and natal deities honored in the family cult around childbirth and infancy.

Domains

The first moments in which the newborn is brought into the light after birth, the candles and lamps carried in the room during labor and immediately after, the small ceremonies of welcome performed by the household in the days following the birth.

Iconography

Her iconography has not been preserved in surviving sculpture. Her name and office suggest a modest domestic image of a woman bearing a lit candle or lamp, an image consistent with the household-scale worship in which the small Roman birth goddesses were honored.

Attributes

Candle or lamp, the newborn wrapped for presentation, the doorway and threshold across which the newborn is carried into the house.

Sacred Sites

The birthing chamber and the household hearth. Candelifera's proper place is the domestic interior in which a birth has just occurred, and her worship belongs to the private cult of the family rather than to the public temples.

Ritual

Honored in the family ceremonies performed in the days after a birth, alongside Lucina and the Carmentes. Household candles or lamps lit in the birthing chamber express her presence, and she receives the small offerings appropriate to the domestic cult: incense, cake, wine, or the burning of a fresh light in her name.

Lore

Candelifera stands with a number of other minor Roman birth deities, each assigned to a particular threshold of the childbirth process, in the pattern by which Roman religion distributed the sacred across the fine details of everyday life. Her partnership with Lucina, whose name carries the root lux meaning light, and with the Carmentes places her at the moment when the child emerges into the ordinary light of day, a moment the Roman religion honored as its own act with its own attending goddess. This distributed pattern of birth deities, small in scale but comprehensive across the process, is one of the most characteristic expressions of the Roman religious mind.
