Saint Claire is the patron saint of clairvoyants, psychics, fortunetellers, and television workers.

Classification

Anglican and Roman Catholic saint.

Life and History

Chiara Offreduccio (1194-1253), the future Saint Claire of Assisi, was born to a wealthy, devout noble family; her father was a count. When she heard Francis of Assisi preach in the streets, he ignited her soul and transformed her life.

On Palm Sunday night 1212, Claire secretly fled home to join Francis, possibly escaping a marriage her parents were arranging. Francis placed her in a Benedictine refuge, cut off her long hair, and gave her sackcloth to replace her finery. When family and friends finally tracked her down days later, trying everything to bring her home, Claire clung to the altar cloth with such force that no one could physically move her. Sixteen days later, her younger sister Agnes ran off to join her. Eventually their mother followed too.

Frustrated Desires

Claire longed to emulate Francis completely, spending most of her life unsuccessfully fighting for the right to share his vow of poverty. She had expected and wanted to accompany him as a fellow wandering beggar. This was deemed unacceptable for a young lady, and she was forced into cloistered life instead.

Their relationship was considered scandalous at the time. Neither was yet a hallowed saint; they were spiritual revolutionaries, often suspected of unorthodox practices and forbidden rites.

Extreme Austerities

Compensating for her restricted life, Claire practiced severe mortifications. She slept on the ground, walked barefoot on rough streets, and renounced food as Francis had renounced wealth. She fasted completely (no food or water) on Mondays, Wednesdays, and Fridays. On other days she ate almost nothing.

She starved herself to the point of painful illness. Francis eventually ordered her to eat at least an ounce and a half of bread daily. Later in life, Claire came to doubt her extreme practices and advised Saint Agnes of Bohemia against harsh physical austerities, writing: "Our bodies are not of brass and our strength is not the strength of stone."

Powers and Domains

Claire helps find marital partners and grants children, especially daughters. She heals eye disorders and vision problems of all kinds.

Her image holding a monstrance (the vessel displaying the communion host) resembles a crystal-ball gazer, making her patron of clairvoyance, psychics, fortunetellers, and mediums. Candles bearing her image are burned for foresight, second sight, intuition, and prophetic dreams.

Vodou Connection

Vodouistes interpreted Claire's glowing monstrance as resembling the moon. In some Haitian traditions, she evolved into Clairmesine Clairmeille, a beautiful, sweet-voiced, moon-gazing mermaid. Traditional Claire images represent this spirit, as do mermaid imagery.

Patron of Television

Once, while too ill to leave her bed, Claire longed to see the official Christmas nativity display. Miraculously, she "saw" it projected on her cell walls as if watching television. Her detailed descriptions matched exactly what eyewitnesses reported. In 1958, Pope Pius XII named her patron saint of television.

Iconography

A nun holding or gazing intently at a monstrance. In many images, the host within glows like the sun.

Attributes

Monstrance, pyx, lamp.

Color

White.

Sacred Sites
