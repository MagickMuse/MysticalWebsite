The Four Sons of Horus

Hapi belongs to a group of four minor gods who protect the organs removed during mummification. Each son guards a specific organ and is associated with a cardinal direction and a protective goddess.

Hapi, depicted with a baboon's head, protects the lungs. His direction is North. The goddess Nephthys watches over him. This Hapi should not be confused with the Nile flood god of the same name.

Role in Egyptian Magick

During the seventy-day mummification process, embalmers removed and separately preserved the stomach, lungs, liver, and intestines. Canopic jars held these organs, their lids shaped like the heads of the Four Sons. Hapi's baboon-headed jar contained the lungs.

Amulets depicting the Four Sons were placed over the mummy's abdomen during wrapping. These images ensured the organs remained protected and that the deceased maintained bodily integrity in the afterlife.

Iconography

Hapi appears as a mummiform figure with a baboon's head. He is painted on coffin midsections and represented on canopic jar lids.

Associations

  • Organ: Lungs
  • Protective goddess: Nephthys
  • Material: Often rendered in faience or stone for amulets and jar lids
