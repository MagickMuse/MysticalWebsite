Also known as

Chandali, Candali (outcast woman)

Overview

Candali is a goddess of terrifying appearance in the Buddhist Vajrayana tradition of Tibet. Her name means outcast woman, a term that in the Indian social vocabulary marked those set outside the fourfold varna system, and its adoption for a Buddhist tantric goddess belongs to the wider tantric habit of taking figures excluded by mainstream society and honoring them as vessels of the very power the mainstream fears. She is counted among a group of eight gauri goddesses of wrathful and cremation-ground character who appear together in the tantric mandalas as fierce protectors and as embodied aspects of the awakened mind cutting through delusion.

Classification

Deity of terrifying appearance. One of the eight gauri goddesses of the Vajrayana Tibetan Buddhist tradition.

Domains

The wrathful edge of awakening, the purifying inner fire that consumes ignorance, the honored place of the outcast in the tantric reversal of social hierarchy, cremation-ground compassion.

Iconography

Depicted in wrathful form, of red color or of blue color depending on the mandala in which she appears, standing on the corpse of ignorance in the posture of dance. Her hair rises upward in flame, and flames themselves are her chief attribute, blazing about her body and held in her hands. She wears the ornaments of the wrathful deities: garland of severed heads, tiger-skin skirt, and the crown of five dry skulls that marks the fivefold wisdom.

Attributes

Flames, held as her primary emblem and blazing around her body.

Colors

Red or blue.

Sacred Sites

Within the tantric mandalas of the Vajrayana traditions preserved in Tibet and the wider Himalayan Buddhist world, particularly in the practice cycles that include the eight gauri as retinue figures of a central yidam.

Ritual

Invoked only within transmitted Vajrayana practice, in the sadhanas that call the eight gauri as retinue of the yidam at the center of the mandala. The name candali also appears in the wider tantric vocabulary as a term for the inner heat, a yogic practice of the subtle body, and this shared name has led some traditions to link the goddess Candali with the practice of that inner fire.

Lore

The naming of Candali and her sister gauri goddesses is a deliberate tantric reversal. The figure the ordinary world names outcast is taken by the tradition as a form of the enlightened mind, her supposed impurity understood as a mistaken judgment of the very power that cuts through impurity itself. Candali is thus at once a warning about the ordinary social order that produces outcasts and an image of the power that dwells in what the order excludes. The flame she carries is both the cremation-ground fire that consumes the body at death and the inner fire that consumes the delusion in the practitioner who works with her.
