Classification

Roman Catholic and Eastern Orthodox saint.

Life and History

Saint Natalia (died c. 311 CE) was the wife of Saint Adrian of Nicomedia. Though her husband was initially a pagan Roman soldier, Natalia was secretly a Christian.

Supporting Adrian's Conversion

When Adrian declared himself a Christian after witnessing the courage of Christian martyrs, Natalia was overjoyed rather than distressed. She visited him in prison and encouraged him to remain steadfast in his new faith, even unto death.

The Martyrdom

Natalia accompanied Adrian throughout his imprisonment and torture. According to tradition, she held his hand as his limbs were broken on an anvil. She asked only that his hand be preserved as a relic after his death.

After Adrian's Death

Natalia preserved Adrian's relics and refused to remarry despite pressure from Roman officials. She eventually fled to Argyropolis (near Constantinople) with other Christian widows, bringing Adrian's relics with her. She died peacefully while praying at his tomb.

Iconography

Natalia is depicted as a Roman noblewoman, often shown:

  • At her husband's side during his martyrdom
  • Holding Adrian's severed hand
  • In widow's clothing

Feast Days

  • December 1 (Roman Martyrology)
  • August 26 (Eastern Orthodox, shared with Adrian)

Veneration

Natalia and Adrian are often venerated together as examples of Christian marriage and mutual support in faith.
