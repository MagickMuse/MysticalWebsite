Zumbi (1655-20 November 1695) was the legendary leader of Quilombo dos Palmares, a free African state in colonial Brazil. He is venerated as an unofficial saint and powerful spirit of liberation. His name is pronounced zoom-bee.nn## DomainsnnFreedom, liberation, resistance against oppression, black empowerment, justice, leadership. Petition Zumbi to heal the wounds of oppression and strengthen those fighting for freedom.nn## Life and HistorynnZumbi was born in Palmares, a settlement of escaped slaves and free Black people that functioned as an elected republic with a population estimated between 20,000 and 50,000. The population included Indigenous peoples, those of European descent, and every possible combination, making it essentially a Central African state in the midst of Brazil.nnCaptured in a raid when only a few days old, Zumbi was raised by a priest who taught him to read and write in Portuguese and Latin. At age fifteen, he ran away and returned to Palmares, though he maintained contact with his old friend the priest, who was forever ostracized for this relationship.nnAs chief of Palmares for approximately twenty years, Zumbi led the resistance against Portuguese forces who had attacked the settlements repeatedly for almost a century. He was eventually betrayed and killed. His body was mutilated after death, his severed head packed in salt and brought to Recife where it was impaled on a stick for public viewing.nn## Spiritual SignificancennZumbi is venerated by Spiritualists, Kardecian Spiritists, Roman Catholics, and secularists alike. He speaks through mediums in spiritual consultations. Afro-Brazilian traditions classify him as both a Preto Velho (Old Black spirit of wisdom) and an Exu spirit. He is a major component of the Roman Catholic inculturated Mass, which incorporates African influences. He remains among the most important symbols of Brazil's secular black-empowerment movement.nn## Spiritual AllynnDandara, Zumbi's wife, was a warrior and strategist who bore three of his sons. She is venerated alongside him as a spirit of feminine strength and resistance.nn## LorennPalmares was finally destroyed after nearly a century of resistance. Zumbi's brutal execution was meant to demoralize the resistance, but instead transformed him into an eternal symbol of freedom. November 20, the date of his death, is now celebrated as Black Consciousness Day in Brazil."
