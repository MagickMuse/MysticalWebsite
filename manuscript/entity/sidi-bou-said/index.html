Sidi Bou Said is a legendary figure who may be conflated with two historical persons: the Crusader King Louis IX of France and the Sufi saint Sidi Bou Said El Beji. It can be difficult to distinguish between them.

The Legend

According to North African tradition, the crusader King Louis IX of France came to Tunis to wage war but fell in love with a Berber princess. He changed his mind about fighting, changed his name to Sidi Bou Said, and converted to Islam. He lived happily ever after in the village now named in his honor, twenty kilometers northeast of Tunis.

The Historical Account

The other side of the story holds that Louis Capet, the Crusader King of France, died of dysentery in Tunis, where he had hoped to convert the sultan to Christianity. The Roman Catholic Church canonized him as Saint Louis. Some of his relics were left in Tunis and became associated with miracles. Legends arose that he had converted to Islam, and he is simultaneously considered a Muslim saint.

Sacred Sites

Louis Capet has two tombs on two neighboring hills in Tunisia. He is venerated as the Catholic Saint Louis at the Tomb of Saint Louis in Carthage, and as Sidi Bou Said, Muslim saint, in a nearby village named in his honor.
