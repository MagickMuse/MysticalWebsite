Saint Fiacre is renowned for healing hemorrhoids, fistulas, and sexually transmitted illnesses.

Classification

Roman Catholic saint.

Life and History

Saint Fiacre (died 670) was a skilled healer and master herbalist who traveled to Brittany, where he established a hermitage near a spring. Believed to be of Celtic origin (whether Ireland or the Scottish Highlands is disputed), he reputedly had such a green thumb that all he had to do was poke a stick into the earth to produce a lush garden instantly.

The First Taxis

In 17th-century Paris, the Hotel Fiacre rented out carriages, which the public called fiacres. It's still the French word for a Parisian taxi.

Powers and Domains

Saint Fiacre is invoked for fertility: personal, creative, botanical, and agricultural. He is also invoked:

  • Against venereal disease (petition him to prevent or heal sexually transmitted illness)
  • To heal hemorrhoids (a stone in Brittany allegedly bears the imprint of his buttocks; sitting on it cures hemorrhoids)
  • To heal fistulas

Warning: Misogyny

Saint Fiacre is as renowned for his misogyny as for his glorious gardening. He reputedly banished women from his monastery, threatening them with bodily harm if they ignored his injunction.

He was considered a master healer and people flocked to him. Women suffer from hemorrhoids, fistulas, and venereal disease as well as men. Presumably he will heal them; however, he may be more inclined to help if a man offers a petition on their behalf.

Attributes

Spade, basket of fresh vegetables.

Feast Days

His feast day is disputed; among days celebrated are:

  • 1 August
  • 18 August
  • 30 August
  • 1 September
