Bhrkuti-Tara, whose name means she who frowns, is a mother goddess of the Buddhist-Lamaist tradition of Tibet. In Lamaism particularly she appears as a cruel form of Tara, the mother of the Buddha, and she is called the so-called yellow Tara. She is understood as an emanation of Amitabha and is also identified as a female bodhisattva or buddha-designate.

Domains

The stern maternal aspect of Tara, the wrathful mother's willingness to frown upon what obstructs the path, and the transmission of the awakening lineage through fierce compassion rather than gentle reassurance.

Iconography

Bhrkuti-Tara is depicted with three eyes and colored yellow. Her attributes are the image of Amitabha, the lotus, the rosary, the staff, the trident, and the water jar, carried among her hands as marks of her tantric authority.

Colors

Yellow.

Relation to Other Taras

Bhrkuti-Tara is distinct from the tranquil abundance-bestowing Yellow Tara. Where that Yellow Tara embodies serene fertility and prosperity, Bhrkuti carries the frowning, wrathful maternal aspect within the same yellow color-family. The two are sometimes conflated under the shared title yellow Tara but represent different currents of Tara's manifold self-manifestation.

Petition For

Fierce protection along the path of practice, the stern maternal guidance that will not indulge harmful patterns, and the transmission of tantric authority through the frowning aspect of the mother.
