Saint Zdislava (c. 1220-1 January 1252) was a Czech noblewoman, healer, and mystic renowned for her extraordinary charity and self-giving nature.

Domains

Family harmony, healing the sick, charity to the poor, protection of refugees, peace in marriage. Petition Saint Zdislava for healing of family discord, illness, and troubled marriages.

Life and History

Born in Brno to a noble family with Sicilian maternal roots, Zdislava displayed early signs of spiritual calling. At age seven, she ran away to the forest seeking the hermit's life, only to be forcibly returned home. She received training both as a noblewoman and as an herbal healer.

Married as a teenager to Havel, Duke of Lemberk, Zdislava bore four children despite a difficult marriage. Her husband resented her extreme piety and what he perceived as excessive charity. She took daily communion, fasted intensely, and experienced ecstatic visions.

Zdislava insisted her husband build a hospice for the poor, then secretly visited the construction site at night to move beams according to her own vision. She personally bathed and cared for the sick, and welcomed Tatar refugees into her own home against her husband's wishes. She also convinced him to build a Dominican convent.

She died young from illness. After her death, Duke Havel experienced a vision of his wife in full glory, transforming his understanding of her life's work.

Iconography

Depicted as a noblewoman in modest dress, often shown caring for the sick or poor, sometimes with healing herbs.

Sacred Sites

The Church of Saints Laurence and Zdislava in Jablonné v Podještědí, Czech Republic, which she built and where she is buried. The healing well from which Zdislava drew water still exists.

Lore

Considered a saint during her lifetime by the local populace, Zdislava remained an unofficial saint for centuries. Pope John Paul II canonized her in 1995, describing her "extraordinary capacity for self-giving." Her story illustrates how sanctity can flourish even within unhappy marriages, transforming domestic conflict into spiritual witness.
