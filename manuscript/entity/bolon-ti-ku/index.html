Bolon Ti Ku is a collective name for a group of nine chthonic underworld gods of the classical Mesoamerican Mayan tradition of Mexico. The name is a Mayan term whose most direct translation is nine gods, and it is used in the surviving inscriptional and codical record to refer to this defined group as a whole rather than to any single member. The individual identities and functions of the nine are not clearly distinguished in the sources that survive, and the group is generally invoked as a corporate body rather than by the names of its members.

Domains

The great below in its Mayan configuration, the sovereignty over the nine layers of the Mayan underworld, and the collective authority under which the classical Mayan dead pass through the strata beneath the earth.

The Nine and the Layers

In Mayan cosmology the underworld, known as Xibalba, was ordered into nine layers, each with its own presiding conditions and challenges. The Bolon Ti Ku are the sovereigns of these nine layers, taken together, and the classical texts and inscriptions treat them as a group whose corporate power supports the ordered descent through the levels of the great below.

Continuing Devotion

The Bolon Ti Ku are probably still invoked by indigenous communities in Mexico who preserve or continue elements of the Mayan religious inheritance. Their name and function belong to a specific cultural and initiatory context that has never been fully broken, though it has been substantially reshaped through centuries of colonization, evangelization, and cultural transformation.

Precautions

⚠️ Bolon Ti Ku belongs to the Mayan religious tradition of Mexico, a closed practice rooted in specific cultural and initiatory context. Approach this entry as reference only. Do not attempt to petition, invoke, or work with these deities outside the living lineage held by the indigenous Mayan community and its authorized keepers.
