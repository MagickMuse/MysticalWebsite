Saint Triduana is renowned for healing eye disorders, restoring sight, and curing blindness.

Classification

Roman Catholic saint.

Overview

The details of Saint Triduana's life vary across different accounts. Some sources describe her as a beautiful young Greek woman from the fourth or fifth century who traveled to Scotland accompanying the relics of Saint Andrew. Others claim she was born in Scotland during the eighth century. What remains consistent across all versions is her exceptional devotion and her striking blue eyes. She is sometimes also described as Pictish.

The Legend of Her Eyes

Having taken a vow of chastity, Triduana was devoted entirely to Christ. Nectan, King of the Picts (the indigenous people of Scotland), became enamored with her and sought her hand in marriage. Hoping to win her affection, he complimented the beauty of her eyes. Triduana's response was dramatic and final: she gouged out her own eyes with a thorn and sent them to the king with the message that he could have her eyes, but he could never have her.

Healer of the Blind

Triduana settled in Restalrig (now a suburb of Edinburgh), where she became renowned as a healer, particularly of visual ailments. People traveled great distances to seek her help. After her death, she was buried in Restalrig, and her well became a pilgrimage site known for healing blindness and other eye conditions.

Sacred Sites

  • Triduana's Shrine at Restalrig Collegiate Church: Her burial site and place of veneration.
  • Triduana's Wellhouse in Restalrig: A healing well with a long reputation for curing visual ailments, including blindness. Pilgrims traveled from across Europe, especially Scandinavia, to visit. The well remained a pilgrimage site until the Protestant Reformation discouraged such visits in the late sixteenth century.
  • Various other chapels and well shrines throughout Scotland may also have been dedicated to her.
