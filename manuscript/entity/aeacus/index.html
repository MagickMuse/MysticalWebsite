Ritual Uses

Aeacus serves as a judge in Hades' palace, trusted to be keeper of the keys to Hades and to hold Hades' scepter. He was appointed judge after death and lives in the underworld rather than becoming a twittering shade like most souls.

Offerings

Traditional Greek offerings that honor his family connections, including images of Zeus, Achilles, and Aegina would be appropriate. Alexander the Great claimed descent from Aeacus via his mother.

Favored People

Those seeking justice, especially in matters that extend beyond the grave, legal arbitrators, and people needing ethical guidance. Pirates should avoid him as he protects against them.

Petition For

Justice in legal or ethical disputes that extend beyond the grave, assistance in matters requiring fair judgment, protection from pirates, and ethical guidance in difficult situations.

Warnings

Aeacus is a judge in Hades and keeper of the keys to the underworld. He is trusted with Hades' scepter and maintains high ethical standards. He has close family ties to water spirits.

Additional Notes

When he had no one to rule (his mother was imprisoned on an uninhabited island), Zeus transformed the island's ants into people called Myrmidons. He was eventually venerated as a deity with shrines in Athens and Aegina. His role as underworld judge makes him appropriate for justice matters that transcend death.

Tribunal of the Dead

Aeacus serves as one of three judges of the dead alongside Minos and Rhadamanthos, the trio who weigh the souls passing into Hades.

Lineage

Plato names him as the son of Zeus and Aigina, the nymph for whom the island of Aegina is named. Hesiod records his consort as the sea nymph Psamathe and gives their son as Phocos.

Literary Sources

His judicial role is preserved in Plato's dialogues, while his family lineage and the tale of Psamathe and Phocos come down through Hesiod.
