Cagn

Shrine
The land itself, especially the Kalahari and its creatures, functions as the living shrine held by San communities

San

Origin of life on earth, ancestral relationship to the Kalahari and its creatures, sustenance of the bond between people, animals, and land
Gender
Male

Type
Deity

Overview

Cagn is the creator god of the San peoples of the Kalahari in southern Africa, honored in the received tradition as the progenitor of all life on earth. In San cosmogony he stands at the beginning of the living world, the source from which animals, plants, and human beings first came into being, and the ongoing sustainer of the web that binds them together.

⚠️ Cagn is venerated within living San religious tradition of the Kalahari. Treat as closed to outsiders; approach only with the direct guidance of San elders and initiates.

Domains

Cagn presides over the origin of all living things, the ancestral memory of the Kalahari, the sustaining bond between the human community and the wild creatures that share the land, and the wider work of engendering and renewing life on earth.

Ritual

Cagn belongs to the living religious tradition of the San communities of the Kalahari, held within specific cultural and initiatory contexts. Approach this entry as reference only. Do not adapt or perform San rites outside the lineage carried by the communities themselves.

Lore

As progenitor of all life on earth, Cagn is placed at the very root of the San cosmogonic account. The San tradition frames his creative work not as a distant event but as an ongoing relationship between the creator and the beings and land he first brought into being, so that the everyday life of the Kalahari is understood to remain in continuous contact with his originating power.

Terminology

The comparative sources that place this deity under the label Kalahari Bushmen use a colonial-era term. The communities themselves are appropriately named as San, with more specific group names such as Ju|'hoansi, ǃKung, Nharo, and others held within that wider designation. This entry retains San as the primary reference.
