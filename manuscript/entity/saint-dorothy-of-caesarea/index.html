Saint Dorothy of Caesarea is the patron saint of florists, gardeners, brewers, brides, and midwives.

Classification

Venerated by most branches of Christianity.

Life and Martyrdom

Dorothy was a Christian girl living in what is now Turkey who was martyred in 311 CE. During her execution, a lawyer in the crowd mocked her, telling Dorothy to send him some fruit and flowers from Heaven.

Shortly after her death, in the middle of winter, a mysterious child, now believed to be an angel, arrived at the lawyer's house carrying Dorothy's veil. Wrapped inside were three apples and three beautiful, fragrant roses, although roses were completely out of season. The lawyer converted to Christianity and was eventually martyred himself.

Powers and Domains

Dorothy is matron saint of florists, gardeners, brewers, brides, newlyweds, and midwives. Fruit trees are blessed on her feast day.

In medieval Sweden, she was considered the fifteenth member of the Fourteen Holy Helpers.

Iconography

Dorothy appears in various traditional forms:

  • A beautiful young woman crowned with roses, holding a basket of roses
  • A veiled woman with flowers in her lap
  • Holding a branch laden with apples
  • Kneeling before an executioner surrounded by stars
  • In the company of an angel
  • In an apple orchard with the Christ child

Attributes

Crown of stars, crown of roses.

Feast Day

6 February
