Saint Alexius is the patron saint of doormen, beggars, bellhops, pilgrims, and the homeless.

Classification

Venerated as a saint by most branches of Christianity. He is also counted among the Holy Unmercenaries.

Life and Legend

Alexius (died c. 404-417) was born to a wealthy Roman senatorial family. His father Euphemianus was renowned for his charity, feeding the poor daily at his table. Alexius was raised in luxury and given an excellent education.

The Flight from Wealth

On his wedding night, Alexius gave his bride his ring and belt, then fled Rome. He traveled to Edessa in Syria (now Şanlıurfa, Turkey), where he distributed all his remaining wealth to the poor and lived as a beggar at the Church of the Virgin Mary for seventeen years. He survived on alms and slept in the church portico.

The Unrecognized Return

When his sanctity became publicly known in Edessa, Alexius fled again to avoid fame. His ship was blown off course and landed in Rome. Alexius returned to his father's house and begged permission to live there as a servant. His father, not recognizing his own son, allowed him to stay.

For seventeen more years, Alexius lived under the stairs of his family home, enduring mockery from servants who threw dirty water on him. He watched his parents and abandoned bride grieve for him daily, never revealing his identity.

Death and Recognition

Alexius died under the stairs, clutching a document revealing his true identity. Only when Pope Innocent I read the paper aloud did his family discover that the beggar they had sheltered was their long-lost son. His body was carried in triumph to a church on the Aventine Hill.

Powers and Domains

Saint Alexius is invoked by:

  • Doormen and bellhops: Because he guarded his own family's door unrecognized
  • Beggars and the homeless: He chose voluntary poverty
  • Pilgrims and travelers: He wandered for years as a pilgrim
  • Those estranged from family: He experienced the pain of separation
  • Those seeking humility: His life exemplified radical humility

Iconography

Portrayed as a beggar in rags, often shown lying under stairs or holding his identifying letter. May also appear as a well-dressed young man fleeing his bride.

Attributes

Staircase, letter/document, pilgrim's staff, beggar's bowl.

Feast Days

  • July 17 (Western Church)
  • March 17 (Eastern Church)

Sacred Sites

  • Basilica of Saints Boniface and Alexius on the Aventine Hill in Rome, built over the site of his father's house
  • The stairs under which he allegedly lived are preserved in the church

Related Entities

Saint Martin of Tours, Saint Elizabeth of Hungary, Saint Nicholas
