Saint Benno of Meissen

Benno of Meissen

Classification

Roman Catholic saint.

Life and History

Benno (1010-16 June 1106) was born into Saxon nobility and was as intensely political as he was spiritual. He served as chaplain to Holy Roman Emperor Henry III before being appointed bishop of Meissen in 1066.

Imprisonment

Benno spent a year in prison for backing Pope Gregory VII and the local nobility against Holy Roman Emperor Henry IV.

The Legend of the Keys

When Benno was later summoned to Rome, he ordered the gates of the cathedral locked in case Henry sought to enter in his absence. Henry did demand entrance, which was granted. When the emperor left, he took the cathedral's keys with him and tossed them into the river to indicate that the doors could not be locked against him.

When Benno returned and heard what had happened, he walked down to the river, where a fish delivered the keys to him.

Death and Legacy

Benno remained controversial. Martin Luther campaigned vigorously against his canonization. He died of natural causes and was buried in the Cathedral of Meissen. Many miracles are associated with his relics.
