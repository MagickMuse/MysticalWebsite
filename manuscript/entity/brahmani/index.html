Overview

Brahmani, also called Brahmi and Brahmayi, is a mother goddess of the Hindu Epic and Puranic traditions. She is the sakti of the creator god Brahma, the feminine power through whom his creative and ordering activity moves in the world. In the later development of Sakta theology she was drawn into two important groupings of mother goddesses. She is counted among the astamataras, the eight mothers, and in another arrangement among the navasaktis, the nine saktis. Both groupings gather together the feminine powers of the principal male deities of the classical Hindu pantheon into a single collective of divine mothers.

Groupings

Within the astamataras or matrkas she is regularly listed first, taking the position appropriate to the consort of the creator and reflecting the priority of Brahma among the deities whose saktis compose the group. The other members of the astamataras are the saktis of Vishnu, Shiva, Skanda, Indra, and other major gods, and their combined worship is often invoked for protection, victory, and the completion of complex ritual work. In the navasakti arrangement she is again included among the mothers whose powers together support the cosmos.

Iconography

Brahmani is attended by a goose, the same vehicle that carries her consort Brahma. She wears a yellow robe. Her recognized attributes are the book, the label, the rosary, the trident, and the water jar. The book and the rosary connect her to sacred learning and to the recitation of the Vedas. The water jar carries the association of pure water used in ritual initiation. The trident locates her within the wider iconography of the sakti mothers.

Domains

Brahmani presides over the creative office in its feminine aspect, over sacred learning and the transmission of the Vedas, over ritual initiation and purification, and over the collective mother power gathered in the astamataras and navasaktis. She is a benign and dignified figure whose worship is closely allied to that of Brahma and to that of the wider group of divine mothers.

Ritual

Brahmani is honored within the collective worship of the mother groupings, especially where the astamataras are called upon together, and she may also be approached individually by devotees of Brahma who wish to petition his creative power through its feminine aspect. Approach her with the ritual purity and quiet dignity appropriate to a goddess of learning and priestly authority, not as a shortcut to worldly power.
