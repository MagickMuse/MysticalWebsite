Overview

Ammavaru is the primordial goddess who exists before all things, the great mother from whom even the highest gods are born. In the myth that carries her name she is the original source of creation, older and greater than the trinity of male gods who are usually placed at the summit of the cosmos.

Mother of the Gods

In the beginning, moved by the desire to create, Ammavaru brings forth three eggs, and from these are born the three great gods. When she wishes to wed them so that creation may continue, they refuse, saying it would be improper for a mother to take her sons as husbands. At last one of them agrees to marry her, but only on the condition that she surrender her middle eye, the seat of her primordial power. She gives it up, and having yielded her power to the god she is destroyed by him.

The Source of the Village Goddesses

From the body of the fallen primordial mother are created the many saktis, the powers who become the village goddesses scattered across the land. In this way the countless local mothers of the villages are understood to spring from one original source, fragments of a single immense feminine power that preceded and gave rise to all the gods.

In Magick

Ammavaru is called on as the primal creatrix, the feminine origin standing behind every divine form. She is approached in workings of creation and generation, for fertility, and for the recovery of the truth that the source of all power is the mother. Her myth carries a hard warning as well, of how an original power can be coaxed into surrendering itself, and so she also speaks to the guarding of one's own strength.
