Overview

Arianrhod is a Celtic Welsh goddess whose name translates to "silver wheel", a phrase that likely references spinning, the moon itself, the lunar cycle, or the lunar year. Though relatively little information about her has survived, she is considered a powerful deity who rules magick and manifestation. Among her myths is the account of her giving birth to a spirit of the sun and a spirit of the sea.

Magickal Properties

Lunar goddess of the Silver Wheel who governs naming, fate, and celestial order; keeper of fortresses in sky and sea.

Ritual Uses

Petition for rightful names, destiny alignment, and protection of kin. Seek insight on moon rites, tides, and star‑lore.

Offerings

Silver tokens, moon‑white candles, clean sea water, and vows to honor names and taboos.

Favored People

Those seeking naming, initiates, fate‑workers, and sea‑adjacent communities.

Petition For

Bestowal of a true name, clarifying destiny, lunar protection.

Warnings

Offer silver or moon tokens and respect naming taboos because she grants names and fates. Do not try to seize or override her authority in naming rites.

Additional Notes

Caer Arianrhod (Northern Crown) and a tidal fortress bear her name.

Encyclopedia Source Notes

Chthonic earth goddess. Celtic (Welsh). Responsible for initiation of souls in the otherworld in the tower of Caer Sidi. Mentioned in the Mabinogion texts as the possible daughter of Beli, consort of Don, and mother of Llew Llaw Gyffes and Dylan.
