Gerard Encausse, a Gnostic saint, was a physician, occultist, and hypnotist born on 13 July 1865. The author of several works, including Tarot of the Bohemians and Astrology for Initiates, his nom de plume, Papus, means "physician." Encausse, who had a Spanish mother and a French father, was born in Spain but moved to Paris at age four.

Education and Career

In addition to medicine, Encausse studied Kabbalah, mysticism, and the magickal arts. He received a Doctor of Medicine degree from the University of Paris in 1894 and opened a clinic.

Mystical Societies

Encausse was active in various mystical, spiritual societies. He joined the French Theosophical Society shortly after it was founded, but soon left because he disliked its emphasis on Eastern spiritual traditions.

  • 1886: Founded the modern Martinist Order, a mystical Christian esoteric society. The original Martinist Order was founded in France in approximately 1740. The Martinists are named after French mystical philosopher and Rosicrucian Louis Claude de Saint-Martin (1743-1803), not Martin of Tours.
  • 1888: Cofounded the Kabbalistic Order of the Rose-Croix
  • 1893: Consecrated a bishop of the Gnostic Church of France, founded in an attempt to revive Catharism

He was also a member of the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn and the Hermetic Brotherhood of Light, and assisted in the formation of the O.T.O. Gnostic Catholic Church.

The Russian Connection

Encausse visited Russia in 1901, 1905, and 1906, serving as both physician and occult counselor to Tsar Nicholas and Tsarina Alexandra. Acting as a medium, Encausse allegedly summoned the spirit of Nicholas' father, Tsar Alexander II, who warned Nicholas that he would meet his doom at the hands of revolutionaries.

Encausse allegedly told Nicholas that, as long as he (Encausse) was alive, he could magically ensure that this decree would be averted. In fact, Nicholas lost his throne 141 days after the death of Encausse.

Death

Encausse joined the French military at the start of World War I, serving as a physician. He contracted tuberculosis and died, age fifty-one, on 25 October 1916.
