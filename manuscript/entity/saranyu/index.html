Saranyu is a minor goddess of the Hindu Vedic tradition. She appears only a handful of times in the Rg-veda, and the references are too sparse and too brief to draw out a fully developed personality.

In Vedic literature

Saranyu is named as the daughter of the artisan god Tvastr and the sister of Visvarupa. She marries the god Vivasvat and bears him twin children, Yama and Yami, who in later tradition stand as the first mortals and the progenitors of the human race.

Her name has been read as meaning "quick, speedy, nimble," which has invited scholars to imagine her as a goddess of impetuous or restless temperament. The Rg-veda itself, however, does not develop this aspect; the suggestion rests on the etymology rather than on any narrative or descriptive material that survives in the hymns.

Cited hymns

Key Rg-veda reference: 10.17.1 (her marriage to Vivasvat).
