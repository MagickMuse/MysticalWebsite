Overview

Tezcatlipoca means Smoking Mirror. He is a spirit of magick and chaos, an omniscient, all-knowing, all-powerful somewhat dangerous figure who sees everything in his obsidian mirror. Tezcatlipoca is a divine sorcerer. He is now most famous as the rival of his brother, Quetzalcoatl, the Plumed Serpent, whose fall from grace was maneuvered by Tezcatlipoca's smoke and mirrors.

Christian missionaries perceived the tragic Quetzalcoatl as a Christ-figure. They identified his opponent Tezcatlipoca, the unrepentant sorcerer, as Satan. Much Aztec mythology was destroyed. In many of Tezcatlipoca's surviving myths, he appears threatening and malevolent. It is unclear whether this was always the case or whether his malevolence was emphasized by storytellers.

Magickal Properties

Tezcatlipoca is Lord of the Crossroads. He presides over the ancient magickal art of nahualism, the complex, intense soul relationship between people and animal allies, characterized by transformation. He is a very complex spirit: a destroyer and a creator. His relationship with Quetzalcoatl may be likened to that between Egyptian rival deities Set and Horus. They balance each other.

Tezcatlipoca brings disaster but also good fortune, prosperity, and fertility. He is Quetzalcoatl's partner in creation of this world. Tezcatlipoca is the master of subterfuge; the ruler of illusion and sleight of hand, especially when these skills are needed for survival, not just for tricks.

Manifestation

Tezcatlipoca may be missing one foot. He may limp. He is a shape-shifter. Favored forms include coyote, jaguar, monkey, owl, and skunk.

Additional Notes

Tezcatlipoca favors the temescal, the traditional Aztec bathhouse. He lives within the Earth's core in a mirrored realm inhabited by jaguars. His nahual is the jaguar. The constellation Ursa Major (what we see as a bear, the Aztecs understood as a jaguar).

Lord of the North

Among the Aztec deities of the four cardinal directions, Tezcatlipoca governed the north. In that role he embodied duality: on his productive side he ripened the harvest; on his destructive side he withheld the rains and brought drought. At night he was said to roam the earth wrapped in a gray cloak, appearing sometimes as a shadow slipping through the dark and sometimes taking the form of a jaguar.
