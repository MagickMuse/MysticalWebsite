Magickal Properties

Ebisu, kami of happiness, luck, and abundant harvests from land and sea, is one of the Shichi Fukujin, Seven Spirits of Good Fortune. He is guardian of the morning sun and often partnered with Daikoku. Ebisu brings the fish while Daikoku brings the rice. Together they deliver complete and abundant bounty.

Ritual Uses

Place his altar in the kitchen. Merchants and shopkeepers often maintain altars honoring Daikoku and Ebisu. Ebisu is the divine physician and may be invoked to safeguard the health of young children. He is patron spirit of fugu restaurants and the guardian against fugu poisoning.

Offerings

Serve him Yebisu beer which features a picture of him on the label, pickled daikon radish, and incense.

Favored People

Fishermen, those who serve in the fishing industry, children.

Petition For

Abundant catches and harvests, protection for restaurants and chefs including against fugu poisoning, good fortune and commercial success, and the health of young children.

Warnings

Ebisu is a bit deaf. In Osaka, devotees strike the walls of his shrine with mallets to make sure they have his attention. Should you ever be unfortunate enough to suffer fugu poisoning, he is the spirit to invoke for help, though no known medical antidote exists.

Additional Notes

Myth identifies Ebisu with Hiruko, the leech baby who was born either lacking limbs or lacking bones as punishment for spiritual transgressions. When he was almost three and still unable to stand, he was placed in a boat of reeds and cast out to sea. He survived and healed. The only obvious sign of his previous disability is his limp. The twentieth day of the tenth month of the traditional Japanese calendar is his sacred day, known as the month without kami. He is also venerated in Osaka on January 9-10 and October 20.
