Zoroastrian saint, the last Zoroastrian queen of Iran.

Life and Flight

Shahbanu Hastbadan was the wife of Yazdgird III, the last Sassanid king, and mother of their children. When the Islamic conquest swept through Iran, the royal family fled but became separated during their desperate escape. The shrine now associated with her marks the spot where her pursuers finally caught up with her.

With nowhere left to hide, the exhausted queen lay down on a rock to pray. According to legend, one of two miraculous events followed:

The Rock: The earth itself offered her sanctuary as the rock opened up to swallow Shahbanu Hastbadan and her two ladies-in-waiting.

The Well: With no place left to run and determined to preserve their honor, the queen and her attendants leaped into a well.

If she died, it was in the year 651 CE.

The Dream and the Shrine

Years later, a Zoroastrian pilgrim secretly entered the shrine of Shahvir, the revered shah of Khorasan, near Mashhad, a site then forbidden to non-Muslims. He was discovered, arrested, and sentenced to hang the following morning.

Falling asleep in his prison cell, the condemned man dreamed of Shahbanu Hastbadan's desperate flight. In the dream, a voice instructed him to build a shrine for the queen in the place where he awoke. The next morning, he found himself miraculously transported miles away to Yazd. He immediately began constructing her shrine. Prison records from Mashhad apparently confirm the mysterious disappearance of a Zoroastrian prisoner.

The Mother of All Pirs

Pir refers to sacred Zoroastrian shrines. A famous pilgrimage route connects six pirs associated with the Sassanid royal family. Because Shahbanu Hastbadan was both queen and mother, tradition dictates that pilgrims begin with her shrine first. Seti Pir, the shrine dedicated to her, is known as the Mother of All Pirs. Some scholars believe the shrine may predate its association with Shahbanu Hastbadan and could be significantly older.
