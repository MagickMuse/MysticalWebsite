Saint Erkembode is renowned for healing depression and bone ailments, especially helping children learn to walk.

Classification

Roman Catholic saint.

Epithet

The Messenger.

Life and History

Erkembode (died 742) was a Benedictine monk from Ireland who became bishop of Thérouanne in northern France. His diocese was enormous, stretching from Ypres (now in Belgium) to the Somme River Valley.

Erkembode walked constantly, searching for land to buy for the poor. That ceaseless walking took its toll: by the time he died, he was crippled and virtually paralyzed.

Powers and Domains

Saint Erkembode is the patron of children who have difficulty walking. His assistance is requested on their behalf. He is also invoked against depression and to heal ailments involving bones.

Petition Tradition

Children's shoes are left at his grave as part of the petition process or as testimonials to miracles received.

Sacred Site

His grave in Saint-Omer Cathedral, France.

Feast Day

12 April
