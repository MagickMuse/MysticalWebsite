Overview

Before Hermes was the winged messenger of Mount Olympus, he was a virile, rustic, pastoral spirit, presiding over the fertility of women and livestock. Hermes was among the best-loved of all Olympian spirits. He is the lord of:

  • Animal husbandry
  • Language, communication, trade, travel
  • Divination (especially cleromancy)

Domains & Powers

Lord of the Crossroads

Hermes' specialty is cleromancy, originally divination using small pebbles, which eventually evolved into dice. He is the trickster lord of the crossroads, spirit of luck and patron of gamblers, especially those who play with dice.

Protection: Hermes protects thieves and also protects against them. He may be invoked for:

  • The gift of gab
  • Business success
  • True omens
  • Mother-wit and cunning

Psychopomp & Shaman

Hermes is a shaman: he travels between realms, hence his eventual role as Zeus' messenger. He is a psychopomp who conveys the souls of the dead to Hades. Sacrifices were made to Hermes on the final day of Greek festivals of the dead to ensure that he would escort the dead souls back to Hades.

If a ghost refuses to vacate your premises, Hermes may be asked to escort them to a more appropriate place. Just be careful: Hermes is not violent or particularly aggressive, but he does enjoy playing tricks and the periodic practical joke. He will be a troublesome spirit for those lacking humor and humility.

The Killer of Argus

Hermes killed Hera's guardian Argus by boring him to death. After he had put him to sleep, Hermes touched the guard with his staff to kill him. He is sometimes considered the patron of euthanasia: the gentle death.

Dream Bringer

Hermes prowls around at night, bringing dreams. You can request that he deliver prophetic dreams or that he provide relief from nightmares.

Birth & Family

Hermes was born in a cave to the goddess Maia; Zeus is his father. He makes his home in Mekone, "Poppy town." Hermes is associated with birth, death, and sex. Although now often portrayed as androgynous, Hermes was originally a very virile phallic deity. His sexual partners include Aphrodite and countless Nymphs. The Greek-born Italian goddess Carmenta may be his official wife.

Inventions

Hermes is credited with many inventions:

  • The musical scale
  • The alphabet
  • Boxing and gymnastics
  • Weights and measures
  • Olive culture (Athena may have brought the olive tree, but Hermes taught people how to process the fruit and oil)
  • The lyre: fashioned from the shell of a tortoise and the gut of a sacrificed bull
  • The flute: which he later traded to Apollo in exchange for instruction in magick and a golden caduceus

Worship & Correspondences

Favored People: Merchants; gamblers; travelers; thieves (but only clever, tricky thieves, not thugs or muggers); those who live by their wits; boxers

Manifestation: Hermes traditionally wears a traveler's broad-brimmed hat and sandals

Iconography: Hermes has been through many transitions:

  1. His most ancient images portray him in the form of an erect phallus
  2. He is represented by a cairn of stones
  3. Herms: tall rectangular pillars displaying his head on top and his erect penis sticking out (placed at crossroads; women seeking fertility would place flower garlands around his neck or elsewhere)
  4. A robust man carrying a lamb around his shoulders
  5. Finally: a winged, sandaled, androgynous messenger (most familiar today)

Attribute: Caduceus, a staff entwined by two snakes, the emblem of the modern medical profession. He can use the staff to induce sleep.

Spirit Allies: His mother Maia; Pan; Nymphs

Sacred Sites: Hermes is an extremely unpretentious spirit, among the Greek spirits least often honored with formal temples. Instead he is present at crossroads and in wild nature. Invoke his presence or summon him by erecting a herm or cairn of stones, especially at a four-way crossroads (X-shaped).

Sacred Animals: Dog, tortoise, snake

Tree: Palm

Number: 4

Offerings: Cakes; honey; olives; goat or sheep's cheese; wine; water; incense

Post-Christianity

Hermes as Divine Child wears sandals and carries a staff: his image closely resembles that of the Holy Child of Atocha. Post-Christianity, many of Hermes' functions were assigned to Michael Archangel.

Origins in Arcadia

Hermes began his incarnation as a fertility spirit in Arcadia. Born in Mekone, "Poppytown," he has powerful associations with opium, shamanism, and botanical magick. His sacred animals include horned livestock: cows, goats, and sheep. Hermes occasionally takes the form of a horned deity, though by the classical Greek era he was most commonly depicted in his familiar winged messenger form.

Shamanic Guide and Psychopomp

As a master shaman, Hermes is able to travel between the realms of the living, the dead, and spirits. His role as psychopomp includes:

  • Serving as guide when Orpheus traveled to Hades to rescue Eurydice
  • Escorting Persephone as she travels between Life and Death
  • Acting as spell master, providing Odysseus with the antidote against Circe's magick

Patron of Thieves and Cleverness

Hermes is Patron of Thieves and Tricksters but also protects devotees against tricks and theft. He famously enjoys cleverness and despises violence, only protecting non-violent criminals, those using wit, not brawn.

Hermes as Horned God

Hermes is among those spirits classified as horned gods, though his myth is so extensive that he transcends such classification. That Hermes is considered a horned spirit might surprise many: the Hermes of Classical myth is invariably depicted as a man dressed in a uniform of traveler's hat and winged sandals.

Originally, however, Hermes was a deity from rural Arcadia. He began his incarnation as a deity involved with human and agricultural fertility. He is the Lord of Flocks and is often depicted carrying a young lamb or kid, both horned animals.

Like many horned gods, Hermes carries a stick: in his case, it's the caduceus, a short staff entwined by two serpents, still the emblem of the medical profession. He often carries a large bag, too, from which he dispenses gifts and treasures. Hermes frequently limps and is sometimes described as missing one sandal.

Hermes is the father of the goat-god Pan. According to myth, Pan's mother was horrified by the goat-child she bore but Hermes was delighted and it was he who raised and protected his son.

Hermes in Magick and Divination

In addition to shamanism and botanical spells, Hermes sponsors other aspects of magick:

  • As Hermes Trismegistus he was identified with alchemy and ceremonial magick
  • He is the model for the mountebank, the traveling conjurer who may be a true magus, a master illusionist, a charlatan, or all or any of the above
  • Hermes, dressed in his traveler's cap, formed the prototype for the Tarot card The Magician

Hermes as a Boundary God of the Oikos

Within Hellenic household worship, Hermes is one of three Gods who guard the Oikos from outside threats, working alongside Hekate and Apollo Agyieus. His station is at the threshold of the home and just outside its perimeter fence, taking the same office He holds along the road: the herm, the upright pillar that fixes the limits of a place.

Where space allows, the household sets a niche beside the doorway for His herm together with the smaller working tools of the rite, the incense burner and the offering bowl among them. In a modern apartment, a shelf or ledge takes the place of the fence niche. Devotional images here should be three-dimensional, statues or carvings rather than flat pictures, which are reserved for ordinary household decoration.

Daily practice is uncomplicated. Outside of specific sacred days it consists of a simple offering and the burning of incense whenever the practitioner enters or leaves the home.

The Mercury Connection

The word Mercury shares its root with the Latin mercator, meaning merchant, underlining his domain over trade, commerce, and the movement of goods, money, and information.

The Emerald Tablet

Hermes Trismegistus is credited with the Emerald Tablet, the cornerstone of the Great Work in alchemy. Written in deliberately oblique language, the Tablet admits of several interpretations and has been pondered by philosophers and alchemists for centuries. Hermes brings attention to the great mysteries, and also provides the means by which those mysteries may be unravelled.

Role in Hellenic Household Worship

Within the household economy of offerings, Hermes receives tongue-shaped tokens, rosemary, frankincense and styrax incense, and the wood or branches of the almond and pine. Mint is reserved for His chthonic aspect, Hermes Khthonios. His tokens are the Caduceus, the winged sandals, and the shepherd's flute. His colours are saffron and gold. Sacred animals include the cow, ram, hawk, turtle, and hare; wild strawberry (purslane) and saffron stand among His sacred plants.
