Sufi saint, among Morocco's most renowned holy men, who allegedly made a pact with the animal and spiritual worlds granting him extraordinary magical powers.

Life and Legend

Sidi Mohammed ben Aïssa (1465-1526) possessed the ability to transform leaves from trees into silver and gold coins. Originally a local Moroccan saint, veneration has spread across all of North Africa from Morocco to Egypt and beyond, reaching Europe and North America.

The Aïssawa Brotherhood

Sidi ben Aïssa founded the Aïssawa Brotherhood (also spelled Issawa), an intricate spiritual confraternity combining traditional Sufi rituals with trance healing and spirit possession. Two origin stories explain its most distinctive practice.

The Enslaved Workers: According to one tradition, Sidi ben Aïssa served as spiritual patron to 50,000 men enslaved by Sultan Moulay Ismail and forced into hard labor. When the starving workers complained of hunger, the saint blessed them and instructed them to eat anything they could find. They consumed scorpions, poisonous plants, even glass, and survived unharmed.

The Desert Exile: Another version holds that Sultan Moulay Ismail expelled Sidi Mohammed ben Aïssa and his followers from Meknes, forcing them to flee into the desert. Starving, they survived by eating whatever they could find: cacti, scorpions, and snakes.

Powers

Sidi ben Aïssa repeatedly bestows the power to consume anything safely, including ground glass and poison, with no ill effects. He breaks malevolent spells and heals physical, mental, and spiritual illnesses. Women invoke his blessings for travel safety, particularly when journeying alone.

Brotherhood Rituals

The Aïssawa Brotherhood developed a reputation for dramatic possession and prayer-induced trance rituals featuring singing, dancing, drumming, and the invocation and banishing of djinn. Before French colonial authorities prohibited the practice (1904-1956), crowds of 50,000 gathered for rituals commemorating the saint's death. Entranced devotees pierced their cheeks and tongues with daggers and consumed glass and scorpions. The Moroccan government has since restricted what it considers more extreme expressions of devotion.

Sacred Animals

Cobras and other snakes. Cobras play a central role in Aïssawa spiritual rituals, and Brotherhood members refrain from killing them. Some members work as snake charmers and caretakers, and may be summoned by others to remove snakes, functioning like a wildlife patrol.

Feast Day

His moussem is celebrated on the eve of Mouloud, the movable feast honoring the Prophet's birth. Devotees gather at his sanctuary and a procession featuring snake charmers parades through Meknes.

Sacred Site

His mausoleum in Meknes. A sanctuary honoring Sidi Aïssa was constructed in 1776.
