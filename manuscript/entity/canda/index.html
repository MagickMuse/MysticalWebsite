Overview

Canda is a terrible goddess of the Hindu tradition, recognized as a distinct form of Durga and one of the nine navadurgas, the nine forms of Durga worshipped through the nine nights of Navaratri. Her name means violent or fierce, and she carries the same root, √cand, that runs through Candi, Candika, and the compound name Camunda. In one telling of her story she is not only a form of Durga but was also one of the demons of that name whom Durga killed. In that battle Canda and his companion Munda were defeated by the form of Durga who thereafter took their two names into her own, becoming Camunda, and Canda's own status as goddess is understood as the aspect of the demon that was absorbed and turned back into a power of the goddess. In this way she stands as both adversary overcome and form assumed. She is also identified as a form of Mahisasuramardini, Durga in her office of slayer of the buffalo demon Mahisa.

Classification

Terrible goddess of the Hindu Sakta tradition. Distinct form of Durga, one of the navadurgas, and a form of Mahisasuramardini.

Domains

Fierce protection, the destructive edge of Durga's power, the transformation of enemy into aspect, the ninefold turning of Durga through the nights of Navaratri, the slaying of demonic beings.

Iconography

Depicted with a large number of attributes carried in her many arms, in the manner of the fierce forms of Durga: sword, trident, bow, arrow, discus, mace, noose, and severed head. She is generally shown of dark color with an open, ferocious face, garlanded with skulls, and standing or seated upon her chosen mount. In her identification with Mahisasuramardini she stands over the buffalo demon Mahisa, driving her trident through him.

Attributes

Sword, trident, discus, bow, arrow, mace, noose, severed head, and the buffalo demon beneath her feet in her Mahisasuramardini form.

Sacred Sites

Honored across India within Durga temples, particularly during the nine nights of Navaratri when the navadurgas are worshipped in sequence. Sakta temples of Bengal, Odisha, and the northern Indian regions where Navaratri observance is strongest.

Ritual

Invoked during Navaratri as one of the nine navadurgas, taking her turn among the nine nightly forms. She receives the fierce offerings appropriate to the wrathful forms of Durga: red flowers, red cloth, and, in traditions that continue the older practice, animal sacrifice or its symbolic substitutes.

Lore

The paradox at the heart of Canda's identity, that she is both a demon slain by Durga and a form of Durga herself, is characteristic of the Sakta understanding of the goddess as the sole reality of which both gods and demons are aspects. When Durga defeats a demon in the Devi Mahatmya she does not annihilate him but reabsorbs him, and the pair Canda and Munda are the clearest example of this reabsorption becoming a name of the goddess. Their two names, joined into Camunda, become the name under which the aspect of Durga that slew them is thereafter known, and Canda herself continues to be honored as a goddess of the navadurga cycle.
