Saint Philothea (c. 1206 to c. 1218) is an Orthodox saint, known as the Protectress of Romania. She was born in Turnovo, the ancient capital of Bulgaria.

Life and History

Philothea's mother died when she was young, and her father, a farmer, married a wicked stepmother who accused Philothea of disobedience and giving their possessions to the poor without permission, which was in fact true. Philothea's daily tasks included bringing meals to her father while he labored in the fields. On her way, she passed poor, hungry children begging in the streets and inevitably shared his food with them.

Eventually, her father complained to his wife that the meals she sent were insufficient. His wife retorted that what she sent was suitable, but that he should ask his daughter what she did with the food. The father decided to spy on Philothea. When he observed her giving away food, he lost his temper and impulsively threw his axe at her, killing her.

The Miracle of the Body

Her father attempted to lift the small corpse but could not; she was too heavy. He sought assistance from the local archbishop, who was also unable to move her body. Recognizing that Philothea was unhappy with her father's plans and did not wish to remain at home, the archbishop began listing churches, monasteries, and potential burial spots. When he named the Monastery of Curtea de Arges in what is now modern Romania, her body instantly became light. Philothea was transported in a sacred procession and immediately began performing miracles.

Domains

Saint Philothea is renowned for performing miracles of healing and ending droughts.

Every year on her feast day, Philothea's relics are carried in processional around the monastery courtyard. She performs miracles of healing and ends droughts.

Iconography

Her attribute is an axe.

Sacred Sites

Philothea is buried in a small chapel in the bell tower behind the Curtea de Arges (pronounced ar-gesh) Monastery Church, approximately 150 kilometers from Bucharest.

Feast Day

December 7
