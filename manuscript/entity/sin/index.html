Overview

Sin, as in venal or deadly, does not derive from the lunar deity Sin's name, but Mount Sinai does. Sin is his Akkadian (Semitic) name. His Sumerian name is Nanna. Sin is the Moon God of the Assyrian and Sumerian pantheons, and one of a divine triad that includes his children Shamash the Sun God and Ishtar, the divine personification of the planet Venus. That the Moon is cast as father of the Sun reflects the nomadic nature of the peoples who worshipped him, for whom the mutable phases of the Moon were far more informative about time and place than the constancy of the Sun. Sin embodies the idea that light emerges from darkness. He was a major deity whose cult was long lasting: the mother of the last king of Babylon (556-539 BCE) served as his priestess.

Magical Properties

Sin was chief deity of the city of Ur, birthplace of the patriarch Abraham. During the era when Ur was in political ascendance, Sin was considered head of Mesopotamia's spiritual pantheon. His other primary cult center was the biblical city of Harran, where Abraham settled with his clan after leaving Ur. It is theorized that ancient Hebrew tribes carried devotion to Sin with them from Mesopotamia to the Sinai Desert.

Iconography

He was envisioned as an old man with a long, flowing lapis lazuli beard. He traveled through the sky in a barque shaped like the crescent moon, which may be the distant origin of the "man in the Moon" notion.

Powers and Protection

Because Sin brought light to the night sky, he was considered the enemy of evildoers and was said to be continually under attack by dark spirits. The solar eclipse was explained as one such attack. His wisdom was so great that at the end of each month all the other Gods came to consult him, bringing their problems for him to solve.

Attributes

A tripod (possibly for incense or for divination purposes)

Additional Notes

Mount Sinai (literally the Mountain of the Moon) and the Sinai Desert are his sacred sites.
