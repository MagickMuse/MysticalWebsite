Magickal Properties

  • Boldog Asszony literally means "Happy Woman", title indicating relationship so close it's impossible to conceive having wedding/funeral without her
  • Presiding spirit of life cycles, especially births and weddings, grants fertility and oversees pregnancy
  • Generally benevolent, patient goddess not given to temper tantrums displayed by some Birth Fairies
  • Has seven daughters who bring good fortune, called Kis Boldogaszony ("Little Boldog Asszony")

Ritual Uses

  • Lay offering table for her immediately after birth and formally thank her
  • Honor her at weddings for marital happiness and future fertility
  • If family fails to honor her, displeasure may take years to manifest (baby may never have happy marriage)
  • Opportunity exists to apologize and make amends in years between birth and marriage

Offerings

Water, wine, pastries, dried and fresh fruit, Palinka (Hungarian fruit brandy)

Favored People

Families celebrating births and weddings, those seeking fertility and joy, expectant mothers

Petition For

Fertility, successful childbirth, happy marriages, abundance and joy, oversees pregnancy

Warnings

Honor her immediately after birth with formal offering table; failing to thank her may affect future happiness in marriage. Do not do laundry or pollute water on Tuesday (her sacred day).

Additional Notes

  • Originally goddess with dominion over joy, fertility, abundance among primary deities of Hungarian pantheon
  • Title, not name, now generally applied to Virgin Mary but original was distinct goddess
  • Saint Gellert (11th century converter of Hungarians) wrote Church was associating her with Mary, calling her Queen of Hungary
  • To differentiate from daughters, called Nagy Boldogaszony ("Big/Great Boldog Asszony")
  • Sometimes identified with Saint Anne while Little Boldog Asszony identified with Mary
  • Sacred day Tuesday, taboo against laundry or water pollution on her day
  • Some scholars suggest a deeper link to the Sumerian birth and healing goddesses Bau and Gula, raising the possibility of an ancient syncretism beneath her Hungarian form
