Also known as

Chandavati

Overview

Candavati is a goddess of the Hindu tradition, a distinct form of Durga and one of the nine navadurgas, the nine forms of Durga worshipped through the nine nights of Navaratri. Her name is formed from candi, the feminine of canda meaning violent or burning, with the possessive suffix vati meaning possessing or endowed with, so that her name marks her as the one who possesses or is endowed with the fierce power of the goddess. She belongs to the wider family of goddesses whose names sound the canda root, including Canda, Candanayika, Candarupa, Candogra, and the compound name Camunda.

Classification

Goddess of the Hindu Sakta tradition. Distinct form of Durga, one of the navadurgas.

Domains

The possession and holding of fierce power, the ninefold turning of Durga through the nights of Navaratri, the wrathful sovereignty by which Durga is recognized among her navadurga aspects.

Iconography

Depicted in the manner of the fierce forms of Durga, multi-armed and richly armed, mounted upon the lion, adorned with the ornaments of the wrathful goddesses. As with the other navadurgas, her image belongs to the cycle as a whole rather than to a separate independent iconography.

Attributes

Sword, trident, bow, arrow, discus, mace, noose, and severed head carried across her multiple arms.

Sacred Sites

Durga temples across India that observe the navadurga cycle during the nine nights of Navaratri.

Ritual

Invoked as one of the nine navadurgas during Navaratri, taking her turn on the nightly worship. She receives offerings appropriate to the fierce forms of Durga: red flowers, red cloth, and the recitation of the Devi Mahatmya and related Sakta hymns.

Lore

Candavati holds her particular place within the navadurga cycle as the one endowed with the fierce power, an attribute-name that names her by what she carries rather than by what she is in herself, and she is worshipped in that carrying office on her allotted night of Navaratri.
