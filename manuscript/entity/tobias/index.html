Classification

Biblical figure and protagonist of the Book of Tobit, venerated as a saint in Catholic and Orthodox Christianity.

The Journey with Raphael

Tobias is the son of Tobit, a devout Jewish man living in exile in Nineveh. When his elderly father became blind and impoverished, young Tobias set out on a dangerous journey to retrieve money Tobit had deposited years earlier in distant Media. For the trip, Tobias hired a traveling companion who called himself Azarias, unaware that his guide was actually the archangel Raphael in human disguise.

During their travels, a large fish attacked Tobias as he bathed in the Tigris River. Raphael instructed him to catch the fish and preserve its heart, liver, and gall, explaining that these organs possessed powerful properties. The heart and liver, when burned, could drive away demons; the gall could cure blindness.

Marriage to Sarah

Raphael guided Tobias to the home of a relative named Raguel, whose daughter Sarah had been afflicted by the demon Asmodeus. Seven previous bridegrooms had died on their wedding nights at the demon's hands. Despite this terrifying history, Tobias agreed to marry Sarah. On their wedding night, following Raphael's instructions, Tobias burned the fish's heart and liver on incense. The smoke drove Asmodeus to Upper Egypt, where Raphael bound him. Tobias and Sarah's marriage proved blessed and enduring.

Healing His Father

Returning home with his bride and the retrieved money, Tobias applied the fish's gall to his father Tobit's eyes, restoring his sight. Only then did Raphael reveal his true identity as one of the seven angels who stand before God's throne, explaining that he had been sent in response to the prayers of both Tobit and Sarah.

Veneration

Tobias exemplifies filial devotion, trust in divine providence, and courage in the face of supernatural danger. His story emphasizes that angels walk among humans unrecognized, offering guidance to those who act righteously.
