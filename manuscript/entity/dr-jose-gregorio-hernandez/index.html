Magickal Properties

Dr. José Gregorio Hernández is an unofficial saint with canonization pending.

Life and History

José Gregorio Hernández (1864-1919) was a Venezuelan physician renowned in his lifetime for his generosity to the poor. Dr. José Gregorio, as he is popularly known, often waived his fees and donated his services. He paid for medicine for the poor out of his own pocket.

He never married. He attempted to enter the priesthood twice but was refused because of his own health problems. He was struck by a car and killed while delivering medicine to a patient on a Sunday.

Death Couldn't Stop the Doctor

Death couldn't stop the doctor, who continues to perform miracles of healing from beyond the grave. Adored and admired during life, crowds flocked to his funeral. His coffin was carried on devotees' shoulders, first to the cathedral, then across the entire city of Caracas to the cemetery. He was popularly proclaimed a saint even before he was buried.

Dr. Gregorio had a reputation for never refusing anyone in need. The expectation was that he would continue this role from an even more powerful position. His image is ubiquitous in Venezuela, appearing on murals, key chains, and postage stamps. Veneration is worldwide.

Canonization

The cause for Dr. Gregorio's canonization was opened in 1949. He was recognized as Venerable in 1985. It is considered highly likely he will eventually be canonized.

Numerous Venezuelan orphanages, hospitals, and clinics are named in his honor. He is the leader of the Court of Healers in the Venezuelan spiritual tradition, María Lionza.

Ritual Uses

Invoke Dr. José Gregorio to:

  • Maintain good health
  • Receive healing
  • Gain luck, love, and business/academic success
  • Protection against accidents, evil people, and malevolent witchcraft
  • Create barriers of protection for yourself or others

Iconography

For decades, traditional images depicted a man in a black suit and hat. More recent imagery shows his suit and hat as white. Sometimes he wears a physician's white lab coat over black suit pants.

  • Church of Jesús del Gran Poder in Guayaquil, Ecuador (some relics in crypt)
