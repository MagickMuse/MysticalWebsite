Ildiko is a goddess of love, hunting, fertility, and death who bestows life and takes it. Ildiko controls gateways between realms: she may be petitioned for fertility as well as for easy, painless death. The name Ildiko may be a Hungarian variant of the Germanic Hilda, meaning "battle" and a name associated with Valkyries. It may also be related to a word for "moon."

Ritual Uses

As a hunting goddess, Ildiko governs the balance between the needs of animals and hunters, simultaneously providing for hunters while protecting animals. Ildiko, like Artemis, is a deer hunter and the guardian of wild forest animals. A shamanic goddess, post-Christianity Ildiko was identified with witchcraft.

Warnings

Post-Christianity, Ildiko was identified with witchcraft. Her shamanic and gateway-controlling aspects made her suspect to Christian authorities.

Hungarian art nouveau tapestry artist Sandor Nagy (1860 to 1950) portrayed Ildiko in the guise of a falconer. Her attributes are bow and arrows. Her sacred animals are bear and deer, and she is associated with the Moon. Ildiko was the name of Attila the Hun's wife, though whether she was named for the goddess is now unknown.
