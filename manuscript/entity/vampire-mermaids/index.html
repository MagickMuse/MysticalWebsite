Overview

Many mermaids have associations with death: some are psychopomps who provide escort for drowning victims, while others are lethal killers. Legends describe vampire mermaids luring people into the water for the express purpose of killing them, with little reason or motivation given. Many of the killings seem random, not the work of justice spirits who target only the guilty, nor guardian spirits, since they actually lure people into the water rather than simply punishing trespassers.

Some of these stories may be bad press; most mermaids are benevolent and have been venerated since time immemorial. Other stories do derive from pre-Christian traditions. What many killer mermaids have in common is their method of dispatching victims: they don't drown or devour their prey, but absorb its life-essence. Vampire mermaids suck breath, not blood; stealing life-essence seems to be their motivation.

Magickal Properties

Vampire mermaids are cold, calculating hunters. Many are extremely beautiful and sexy, with an erotic component usually present in their seduction. They more closely resemble traditional vampires than benevolent mermaids, their unique quality being vampires who reside in water. Although some possess fish tails, others are indistinguishable from human women.

Additional Notes

The mermaid as femme fatale, with a lover caught in a fatal embrace, is a favorite theme of Salon, Symbolist, and Pre-Raphaelite artists, and she occasionally appears as a villain or monster in horror movies.
