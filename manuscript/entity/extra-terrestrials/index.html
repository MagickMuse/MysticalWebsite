Magickal Properties

Why are spirits and deities called celestial beings? Because many come from the sky. Once upon a time, people told tales of supernaturally powerful beings arriving from the sky and called them gods. In a scientific world, very similar beings are now called extra-terrestrials.

The stories are old, but the perpetrators have been reevaluated. Tales of human women forcibly impregnated to spawn offspring for invaders from the sky could as easily be told of Zeus as aliens. Stories of alien abduction and people eventually returned, but never the same, could be as easily told of Fairies as of aliens. The Raelian Movement suggests that extra-terrestrials created Earth. It denies the existence of a being called God; instead extra-terrestrials sparked spiritual traditions, which evolved into major religions.

Ritual Uses

In the second season of the new Dr. Who television series, Madame du Pompadour addresses the time-traveling alien known as the Doctor as a lonely angel. Each avatar of the Doctor has a title; the title given the tenth Doctor is the lonely god. In episode two of the fourth season, The Fires of Pompeii, a family that the Doctor has rescued from the eruption of Mount Vesuvius is shown worshipping the Doctor at a very authentic Roman household altar. The Doctor and Donna, his traveling companion, are venerated as this family's protective household gods.

On the other hand, spirits are also invoked to prevent alien abductions. Modern Mexican exvotos testify to help given by the Holy Mother and various saints in protecting devotees from aliens.

Warnings

The phenomenon of extra-terrestrials raises questions about the nature of spiritual beings and their relationship to physical reality. Stories of abduction and forced breeding parallel ancient myths of gods and fairies interacting with humans in non-consensual ways.

Additional Notes

The October 5, 2007 issue of The UK Times Online listed the Lord God as the number-one top ranking of the forty most memorable aliens. This observation highlights how the categories of deity, spirit, and extra-terrestrial may overlap or be culturally reinterpreted versions of similar phenomena.
