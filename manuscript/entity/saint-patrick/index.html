Saint Patrick is venerated as a saint by most branches of Christianity. He is the patron saint of Ireland, credited with converting the island to Christianity in the fifth century. His feast day is celebrated worldwide with secular activities honoring Ireland.

Life and History

Patrick was a fifth-century Roman whose family lived in Britain. When he was about sixteen, he was kidnapped by Irish raiders and sold into slavery. He escaped from Ireland six years later, returned to his family, and entered the Church. He eventually returned to the scene of his enslavement as a missionary.

The Snakes of Ireland

Even people who know nothing about saints know that Patrick expelled the snakes from Ireland. Many point out that Ireland has no native snakes, implying the myth is wrong, but the snakes symbolize Ireland's pre-Christian religious traditions. Many Celtic deities were closely affiliated with snakes. When Patrick banished the snakes, he was banishing those for whom snakes symbolized healing, not temptation to sin.

Domains

Patrick is venerated both conventionally and unconventionally. Conventionally, he is honored as a great Christian and powerful intercessor. Unconventionally, his image is easily mistaken for that of a wizard or snake-master. In the traditions of the Spiritual Churches of New Orleans, Patrick is invoked to subdue and banish enemies. His associations with green refer to Ireland, but green is also associated with cash and prosperity. Patrick is invoked for blessings of prosperity, luck, abundance, and sometimes for quick cash.

Iconography

Patrick is traditionally described as a white-bearded, blue-robed man. His votive image depicts him accompanied by snakes. Ironically, his image is used to represent the Vodou snake spirit Damballah because of the snakes inevitably portrayed.

Colors

Although now almost universally associated with green, Patrick was originally associated with a shade of dark blue named Saint Patrick's Blue.

Sacred Sites

Saint Patrick's Purgatory in Lough Derg, County Donegal, is an ancient pilgrimage site. Saint Patrick's Cathedral in Dublin is built beside a sacred well. Patrick is linked with various Irish holy wells also associated with druids and fairies. Saint Patrick's Well in Dromard, County Sligo, is the site of numerous miraculous healings involving vision. The Hill of Tara, Ireland's ancient ceremonial capital, features a sacred well and church named in his honor. Saint Patrick's Cathedral in New York City contains a reliquary said to hold his bone fragments, sealed inside the high altar in 1942.

Altars

Decorate his altar with the color green, living green plants, and emblems of prosperity.

The Shamrock as His Emblem

Around the seventeenth century, the shamrock came to be worn as a public emblem in his honor on his feast day. At the close of the day's celebration, the leaf was placed at the bottom of a glass to toast Padraig.
