John the Conqueror, an African American folk saint and Hoodoo spirit known as "The Hope Bringer," is among the most beloved spirits of African American folklore, a trickster hero originally from West Africa who triumphs over adversity through wit and cunning. Also called High John, High John the Conqueror, or High John De Conquer, author Zora Neale Hurston described him as "the Hope Bringer." He may be an avatar or manifestation of Eshu-Elegbara.

According to legend, John was an African prince who was enslaved but never spiritually broken. No matter what happened to him, he always laughed, for he was free in his mind. In a typical tale, John outwits his enslaver. Sometimes, though, John suffers even while triumphing, a common theme in African American folk traditions. John may or may not be the same figure as High John the Conqueror Root.

High John the Conqueror Root

The root bearing John's name is a staple of Hoodoo magick. It is the root of a flowering morning glory (Ipomoea jalapa or Ipomoea purga), not to be confused with the entirely different Little John to Chew, also known as galangal. In botanical terms, the root is actually a tuber.

In Hoodoo, the root is the very essence of male sexuality. Simply carrying the root increases personal power and provides luck in gambling and games. The root is used for self-empowerment, protection, luck in love, and virility. Anointing the root with essential oils is believed to enhance its power.

Iconography

High John the Conqueror is portrayed on commercial products with the image of a white king, similar to a playing card. However, John is an African spirit whose African origins should be honored.

Spiritual Allies

In occult stores, John the Conqueror products sit on the same shelf as those marketed to Saint Anthony of Padua and Martha the Dominator, suggesting he harmonizes with these spirits. John the Conqueror is associated with Saint Calogero.

Magickal Protection

Chanting "John over John" or "John the Conqueror" is believed to protect against malevolent magick.

The White King and the Crusader

High John the Conqueror root comes from a tropical morning glory native to Mexico, purely a Western Hemisphere plant, and its present magickal use comes almost entirely from African American tradition. Even so, when John the Conqueror is personified he is usually shown as a white king, much like the traditional image on The Emperor. At times the image blends with that of a crusader resembling Saint James the Greater, which may be a subtle reference to Ogun, the African spirit who protects soldiers.
