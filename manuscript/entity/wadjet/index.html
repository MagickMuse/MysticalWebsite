The Cobra Goddess

Wadjet is the cobra goddess and uraeus on the pharaoh's crown. Originally guardian of the ancient city Buto in the Nile Delta, she did her job so well that everyone wanted her as their guardian. She became matron of Lower (Northern) Egypt and eventually the pharaoh's own special protector.

The Uraeus: Fire-Breathing Protection

The uraeus ("rearing cobra") on the pharaoh's forehead represents Wadjet. Of all the snakes known to the ancient Egyptians, the hooded cobra was especially significant. The cobra was an aspect of the sun and embodied its powerful heat, emitting venomous flames to save the king from harm.

Wadjet is one of the two traditional goddesses who protected the pharaoh (the other being Nekhbet, the vulture goddess of the south). Together they represent the unified kingdom of Upper and Lower Egypt.

Role in Egyptian Magick

Protection Against Spirits and Poison

Wadjet heals, protects, and punishes via the power of snake venom, exponentially powerful since she has the abilities of a goddess rather than a plain snake. Her other weapons include heat and fire.

She can be invoked for:

  • Protection from snakes
  • Protection from poison from any source
  • Protection against enemies
  • Healing through fire and venom

The Uraeus as Magical Symbol

The uraeus, the rearing cobra on royal and divine foreheads, offered protection against malevolent spirits. Magicians placed clay cobra figures with flames in their mouths in the four corners of sleeping rooms to ward off nightmares and hostile ghosts (mwtw).

Guardian of Isis and Horus

Wadjet assisted Isis when she was hiding in the Delta swamps with baby Horus, and is sometimes considered Horus's foster-mother. This connection makes her a powerful ally for protective magick, especially concerning mothers and children.

Manifestation

Wadjet may appear as:

  • A cobra
  • A cobra with a woman's head
  • A woman with a lion's head
  • A huge winged serpent wearing the Egyptian Crown of the North
  • A woman wearing the crown

She is most commonly portrayed as an erect, rearing cobra poised to strike at enemies of the Pharaoh and Egypt.

Attributes

  • Scepter: A stalk of papyrus with a snake twined around it, considered a talisman of strength and virility
  • Element: Fire
  • Plant: Papyrus
  • Creatures: Snakes (especially cobras), lions, mongoose

Spirit Allies

  • Nekhbet: Her primary ally and constant companion; together they represent Upper and Lower Egypt
  • Isis: Whom she protected in the Delta
  • Horus: Her foster-son
  • Anubis: Closely associated

Wadjet is sometimes considered the left Eye of Ra.

Center of Veneration

Buto, an ancient city in the Nile Delta near modern Alexandria. Her shrine there contained a famous oracle.
