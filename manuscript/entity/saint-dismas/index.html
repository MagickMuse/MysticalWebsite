Saint Dismas is the patron saint of antiquarians and art dealers.

Classification

Eastern Orthodox and Roman Catholic saint.

The First Saint

Saint Dismas holds a unique distinction: he is the very first Christian saint, and some consider him the only one whose sainthood can be claimed with absolute certainty. Jesus himself promised Dismas a place in Paradise.

The Crucifixion

All four Gospels describe Jesus being crucified between two other men. The details about Dismas come from the Gospel of Luke. One man complained to Jesus: "If you are the anointed one, why don't you save yourself and us, too?" Dismas told him to be silent, acknowledging that they deserved punishment but that Jesus was innocent. Jesus promised Dismas they would be united in Paradise that very day.

Revolutionary, Not Thief?

Jesus's two companions are typically described as thieves, but this isn't technically accurate. The original Greek text calls them Lestai, which translates as "brigands" but was also the Greek term for the Zealots, Jewish revolutionaries dedicated to liberating Judea from Rome. The same word describes Barabbas. Had they been ordinary thieves, they would have been spared crucifixion. Roman law reserved this horrific punishment for specific crimes: treason, crimes against the state, magic, and sorcery.

Dismas may therefore be considered patron of political prisoners as well as common criminals.

Powers and Domains

Dismas protects condemned prisoners and those on Death Row. He is invoked against being condemned to death. He also patronizes undertakers and funeral workers.

His associations with thievery led to magical formulas for protection against theft.

Iconography

Portrayed on or near a cross.

Attributes

Chains, club, cross.
