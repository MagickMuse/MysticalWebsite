The Hemp Princess

Origin and Legend

Ma Gu looks like a teenager, but she's an Immortal. Legend says this fifth-century Taoist shaman and alchemist was so adept she could walk on water in her shoes. Her husband murdered her, dumping her body in a lake. Her primary temple stands where her body washed ashore. Ma Gu still walks over the surface of the lake; many claim to see her, especially at the beginning and end of each lunar month.

Ascension to Immortality

After she died, Ma Gu ascended to the celestial zone where she attained Fairy status and became Hsi Wang Mu's handmaiden. As divine waitress, she serves the peaches of immortality to the Jade Emperor and the Heavenly Court. Ma Gu is venerated throughout China. Her devotees and shrines suffered terrible persecution during the Cultural Revolution (1966–1976).

Guardian of Peace

Her own life cut short by murder, Ma Gu abhors violence. Bloodshed is strictly tabooed in her sacred temple precinct: fishing and hunting are banned by order of Ma Gu, who personally enforces the prohibition. Allegedly, violators drown in her lake or become hopelessly lost.

Divine Domains

  • Ma Gu is a goddess of good health and longevity
  • She presides over spiritually powerful, intoxicating substances
  • She protects women, who may call on her if they feel endangered

Iconography and Cultural Significance

The iconic image of Ma Gu bearing her dish of peaches (or alternatively the elixir of immortality) remains a standard image for birthday greetings in China. This image is considered an auspicious birthday gift symbolic of good health, longevity, and immortality.

Manifestation

Ma Gu resembles a beautiful eighteen-year-old dressed in a shimmery, iridescent gown. She wears her hair in a bun, but several strands inevitably loosen and fall to her waist. Her fingernails resemble long bird talons.

Sacred Attributes

Direction: East

Magickal Properties

Ma Gu embodies the transformation from mortal shaman to immortal celestial being. Her mastery of Taoist alchemy and her ability to walk on water demonstrate her profound spiritual attainment. As an Immortal who serves the peaches of immortality, she has become synonymous with longevity and health blessings in Chinese culture.

Ritual Uses

Invoke Ma Gu for:

  • Health and longevity blessings
  • Protection of women from violence and danger
  • Working with spiritually powerful substances (particularly cannabis and intoxicating elixirs)
  • Alchemical and shamanic practices
  • Birthday blessings and wishes for long life

Her image is traditionally given as a birthday gift, carrying blessings of health, longevity, and immortality.

Sacred Sites

Initially venerated in caves, she eventually had several large shrines:

  • The Yue Gu Temple complex in Yantai, Shandong Province, contains her tomb
  • Ma Gu Temple on Mount Heng in Hunan, one of China's five sacred Taoist peaks, is now part of Magu Fairyland, a major tourist attraction

Sacred Date

Her birthday falls on the sixth day of the sixth Chinese month.

Spirit Allies

The standard iconographic image portrays her bestowing peaches of immortality and a mystic elixir, which may be wine or an alchemical potion brewed from mushrooms or cannabis. Her role as handmaiden to Hsi Wang Mu connects her to the highest levels of Taoist celestial hierarchy. She is among the Eight Immortals' related spirits and shares connections with Bao Gu, Ho Hsien-Ko, and Ma Zu.
