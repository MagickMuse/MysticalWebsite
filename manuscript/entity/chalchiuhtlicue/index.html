Magickal Properties

  • Queen of rivers, lakes, and standing, still waters
  • Name refers to a jade skirt, metaphor for shining expanses of still water
  • Presides over irrigation and agriculture, especially cotton (plant with huge moisture requirements)
  • Goddess of provision and destruction
  • Regent of previous world known as 4 Water, destroyed by flooding
  • Presides over amniotic fluids
  • Goddess of human and agricultural fertility

Ritual Uses

  • Irrigation and water magick
  • Agricultural fertility work, especially cotton
  • Human fertility and childbirth protection
  • Rain and moisture regulation
  • Birth rituals involving baptism

Offerings

Offerings that reflect her domains: water, jade and turquoise stones, corn (ears or cobs), cotton, prickly pears

Favored People

Farmers needing irrigation, those cultivating cotton, pregnant women and those seeking fertility

Petition For

Irrigation and water access, agricultural success, healthy cotton crops, rain and moisture, human fertility, safe childbirth, balance of life-sustaining waters

Warnings

Respect fresh waters and springs. Do not pollute them and remove perishables promptly because she withdraws favor when waters are dishonored.

Additional Notes

Without Chalchihuitlicue, there is no irrigation. Pre-Christian Aztecs possessed birth rituals involving baptism in which she played prominent, presiding role.

She appears as a woman whose face is decorated with a pair of short black lines running vertically down her lower cheeks. She wears a headband studded with balls of cotton. Her skirt is adorned with jade, and she often wears a tasseled shawl. Her attribute is an ear of maize corn or corn cobs. Her consort is Tlaloc. Her colors are the various shades of jade and turquoise. Her minerals are jade and turquoise.
