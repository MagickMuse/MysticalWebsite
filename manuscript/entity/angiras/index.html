Overview

Angiras is one of the great rsis or seer-sages of the Hindu Vedic tradition. He is counted among the mind-born sons of Brahma and stands as the ancestor of the Angirasa priestly lineage, one of the oldest brahmanical family lines of Vedic religion. He is celebrated as the father of Brhaspati, the astral god who presides over the planet Jupiter and serves as the guru of the later Hindu pantheon.

In Vedic literature

Angiras appears throughout the Rg-veda as a sage-priest associated with the sacred fire and with the recovery of the stolen cattle of the dawn. His hymns and those of his descendants form a substantial portion of the Vedic corpus, and the family of the Angirasas came to be regarded as one of the two great priestly lineages of ancient India alongside the Bhrgus. He is credited with introducing many of the sacred formulas by which Agni, the fire god, is called into the ritual.

Domains

Angiras presides over the priestly office, over the transmission of Vedic knowledge through teacher and pupil, over the fire of sacrifice, and over the ancestry of the guru tradition that flows through his son Brhaspati into the later Hindu pantheon.
