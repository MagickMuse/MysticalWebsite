Saint Sebastian is the patron saint of athletes, archers, armorers, arrowsmiths, and ironmongers.

Saint Sebastian is renowned for healing and protecting against epidemics, plagues, and AIDS.

Saint Sebastian (died c. 288) is venerated by most branches of Christianity. He joined the Roman army and quickly rose through the ranks to become a favorite of Emperor Diocletian, who promoted him to captain of the guard.

Domains

Sebastian offers protection from epidemics and plagues. He was identified as a savior during the Black Death era and is now invoked for healing miracles for those suffering from AIDS. He grants luck and good fortune. Though not part of official Church hagiography, Sebastian is an important patron of LGBTQ+ people, serving as matchmaker for same-sex couples. Modern retablos from Mexico City thank him for protection and for providing lesbian and gay lovers.

Martyrdom by Arrows

Sebastian was tied to a post or tree and shot with arrows by fellow soldiers. Iconography always depicts him pierced by multiple arrows, yet he survived this execution attempt. He was later clubbed to death, but the image of the arrow-pierced saint became his defining symbol.

Syncretism with Ochossi

In African diaspora religions, particularly in Brazil and Cuba, Saint Sebastian became syncretized with Ochossi, the Orisha of the Hunt. Both are associated with arrows—Sebastian as victim of arrows, Ochossi as master of the bow. This syncretism allowed enslaved Africans to continue worshiping Ochossi under the guise of Catholic devotion.

Iconography

The earliest depictions show Sebastian as a mature, bearded man in court dress. During the Renaissance, he began to be depicted as a young, handsome, sometimes androgynous naked or nearly naked youth tied to a tree and pierced by arrows. His attribute is arrows.

Spiritual Allies

Sebastian and Saint Roch are often venerated together, especially to prevent or halt outbreaks of infectious disease.

Sacred Sites

The Church of Saint Sebastian in Venice and the San Sebastian Church in Manila, Philippines (the only all-steel church in Asia) are major pilgrimage sites.

Lore

While in the army, Sebastian visited the ailing and imprisoned, comforting and healing them. He restored a blind girl's sight and actively converted many to Christianity. When discovered, Diocletian ordered his execution by archers.

Bound and shot full of arrows, Sebastian amazingly survived. Saint Irene rescued him and nursed him back to health, begging him to flee Rome. Though presumed dead, he instead stayed and deliberately sought out Diocletian to denounce him. He was clubbed to death and his body thrown onto a dung heap. Shortly afterward, an apparition of Sebastian appeared to a Christian widow, advising her of his body's location and requesting burial near the catacombs.

Sebastian has long fascinated artists including Botticelli, Leonardo da Vinci, Michelangelo, Dürer, Rubens, and Keith Haring. He inspired self-portraits by Egon Schiele and obliquely Frida Kahlo's "The Wounded Deer." Oscar Wilde used Sebastian's name as an alias in Paris near the end of his life.
