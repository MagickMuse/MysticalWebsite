Bahaudin Nakhshbandi is a Sufi saint.

Life and History

This 14th-century mystic and Islamic scholar (1317-1388) gave his name to the Nakhshbandi Sufi Order. He established a teaching center near Bukhara along the Silk Road that grew into Central Asia's premier institution for Islamic studies. Even during his lifetime, seekers traveled great distances to learn from him and receive his blessings.

Sacred Site

His tomb lies near Bukhara in modern Uzbekistan and remains the region's most significant Sufi pilgrimage destination. Interestingly, every attempt to construct an elaborate mosque over his grave failed. Devotees came to understand that the saint wished to rest beneath open sky rather than stone vaults.

Pilgrimage Practices

Visitors observe specific customs to maximize spiritual benefit:

  • Cross the threshold right foot first; exit left foot first
  • Offer prayers at the Stone of Desire
  • Walk around the sacred tree, touching it and fastening wishes and donations to its branches

A resident mullah dispenses counsel and blessings from beneath a mulberry tree. Traditional gifts include bread, honey, and melons (though monetary offerings are welcomed). In return, the mullah provides a special pastry called bui, believed to draw benevolent spirits to the recipient.

Historical Note

Soviet authorities converted the site into an atheism museum and banned pilgrimages. The shrine reopened in 1989 and has since been restored.

Powers and Domains

Devotees seek his aid for fertility, healthy babies, good fortune, and miraculous healing.
