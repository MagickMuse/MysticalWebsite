Magickal Properties

Papa Legba is the master of the crossroads. He is the lord of communications, guardian of gates. He is the owner of all roads, paths, and thresholds, literal and metaphoric. He owns the highway, the path to your door, and the Road to Success.

As Eshu Elegbara, he is venerated throughout West Africa. He is incorporated into different pantheons, and thus different myths are told of him; however, his essence remains the same. Because he is venerated by different cultures with different languages, different variations of his name exist: Eshu, Elegba, Legba. He journeyed to the Western Hemisphere with enslaved devotees, where he emerged as one of the most significant, widely venerated spirits of African-Diaspora religions. He is called Papa Legba in Haiti, Elegua in Cuba, and Exu in Brazil.

Papa Legba opens and closes roads. He is invoked to remove obstacles from your path and to obstruct enemies and misfortune so they can't reach you. He is an intense trickster but also a force for justice. Papa Legba is a lover of truth. Nothing can be hidden from him. Papa Legba mediates between people and the other spirits. Traditionally when one wishes to communicate with other spirits, one first asks Legba to open the door. He is the first spirit invoked in African-Diaspora spiritual ceremonies. Legba opens the gate for the other spirits to follow.

Papa Legba may be invoked when you stand at any crossroad and need direction. If you are literally lost and don't know which way to turn, call his name and wait for a sign. Invoke him when spiritual communications are not going well. Theoretically there is nothing Papa Legba cannot do. He enables the lame to walk. He transforms bad luck into good and vice versa. Papa Legba blesses devotees with the gift of manual dexterity, thus he is beloved by musicians as well as gamblers, dice players, and practitioners of three-card monte. Legends of bluesmen like Robert Johnson receiving extraordinary musical talents at the crossroads may derive from vestigial memories of Papa Legba. All one must do to receive these blessings is go to the crossroads and ask.

Ritual Uses

Invoke Papa Legba at crossroads for guidance and blessings. Petition him to open roads, remove obstacles, and facilitate communication with other spirits. He is the first spirit invoked before any African-Diaspora spiritual ceremony. Always open a ritual through Legba with proper offerings and politeness. Bring your instrument to the crossroads to be blessed by him.

Offerings

A glass of rum once a week and a cigar or some candy on special occasions keeps him content. Rum, candy, cigarettes, cigars, candy cigarettes, toys, keys. He likes his food spicy: lace with hot sauce and peppers. Offerings at the start of every ritual.

Favored People

Musicians, gamblers, dice players, practitioners of three-card monte, Vodou practitioners, those seeking crossroads blessings, travelers, anyone needing spiritual communication, people working with crossroads magic, the poverty-stricken, practitioners of Afro-Brazilian traditions.

Petition For

Opening roads and doors, removing obstacles, communication with spirits, transformation of luck, manual dexterity blessings, justice, guidance at crossroads, enabling the disabled, crossroads magic, access to mysteries, healing, opportunities, good fortune, protection at thresholds, truthful communications.

Wheel of the Year Connection

Papa Legba brings the energy of the Sun, and so it makes sense that one of his feast days is June 13, right around Litha (summer solstice). As gatekeeper between realms and psychopomp who guards the passage between the mundane and spiritual, honoring him at midsummer creates a powerful connection.

To honor Legba at Litha, go to the crossroads and leave a penny at the center with a cigar. You can also set aside space on your altar with red and black candles, an offering of coffee, and a simple pipe filled with tobacco.

Warnings

Papa Legba is something of a practical joker. He likes word games. Be careful how you phrase requests, and cultivate a sense of humor. He is an easily pleased spirit and not particularly avaricious, but don't ask for his help unless you're ready for change. At worst, Legba will ignore you and you're left where you started. There's no selling of souls involved.

Always open a ritual through Legba with proper offerings and politeness. Do not skip his rites because he governs access to other spirits.

Additional Notes

He may have over 121 paths and manifestations. His original form in Africa was as a handsome, virile young man. This manifestation did not survive the Middle Passage. In the Western Hemisphere, Papa Legba may manifest as a merry, tricky little boy or as an old limping man, weary from walking the world. He may dress in Masonic garb. In Africa, he is sometimes venerated in the form of a phallus. His iconic image is a ritually prepared concrete head with cowrie shell eyes and ears. Statues of the Holy Child of Atocha are used to represent him. His image is usually kept near or behind the front door.

His attributes include key, cross, crutches, cane, shepherd's crook, walking stick. His colors are red and black. His number is 3 (two legs and a phallus). His sacred animal is the mouse. His planet is the Sun. His day is Monday. His sacred tree is the Calabash.

Brazilian Evolution into Exu

In Brazil, Papa Legba evolved into Exu (pronounced "Eshu"), a special kind of spirit. The Exus are a category unto themselves: there are many Exus, or at least many individual aspects or manifestations of one Exu. He serves as a messenger and medium. This definition of Exu is common to Afro-Brazilian traditions but does not occur in other spiritual traditions such as Santería or Vodou, where he is known, respectively, as Elegua and Papa Legba.

Perceptions of Exu depend upon the spiritual convictions of the perceiver. Those with more purely African or Pagan orientations perceive Exu as dangerous and volatile but not inherently evil. He performs valuable services for people including healing, and he provides opportunities and good fortune.

Many possessing a strong Christian orientation, however, perceive Exu as evil or even as the devil. In Brazil, Exu is closely identified with the Christian devil; the two are sometimes perceived as synonymous. Like the Christian devil, Exu signals his appearance with the scent of sulphur and carries a pitchfork. In Africa and elsewhere, Eshu-Elegbara carries a shepherd's staff. It can be impossible to distinguish statues of Exu from Satanic imagery.

Exu's sacred colors are red and black; he is depicted as a red devil or a black one. He is usually envisioned as a naked, horned man, sometimes with a little beard, always with a perpetually erect phallus. Sometimes he has one cloven hoof; sometimes he just limps. Like the Christian conception of the devil, Exu is a smooth-talking man who hangs around crossroads.

In Brazil, Exu has a consort, Pomba Gira. She, too, is a road-opening spirit who shares many characteristics with Exu, including the sacred colors red and black. She is considered the matron saint of prostitutes, transvestites, the poverty-stricken, and the downtrodden. Pomba Gira is sometimes depicted as a beautiful, lascivious, horned woman. Like Exu, she sometimes also sports one cloven hoof.
