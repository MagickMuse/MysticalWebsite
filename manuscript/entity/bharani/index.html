Bharani

Bharani, whose name means "bearing," is a minor Hindu goddess of misfortune belonging to the Epic and Puranic traditions. She is a naksatra, one of the lunar mansions personified as a daughter of Daksa and wife of Chandra, the moon god also called Soma. Among her sister naksatras she carries a specifically malevolent character, and her appearance in astrological and ritual contexts is treated as an occasion for caution.

Domains

Misfortune and the harder side of fate, the burdens that must be borne, and the astrological influence of her lunar mansion.

Parentage and Consort

Daughter of the creator god Daksa. One of the twenty-seven daughters given as consort to Chandra, the moon god (also called Soma), who moves nightly through each of the naksatras in turn.

Astrological Role

Bharani is the second of the twenty-seven naksatras of Hindu astrology, associated with the constellation known in Greek tradition as parts of Aries. Her nature is regarded as intense, transformative, and often difficult, and her period is marked by attention to endings, burdens, and the passage through hard experience.

Precautions

Rites and undertakings during her period are approached with care. She is not a goddess to be petitioned lightly; devotion to her is honored through acknowledgment of what must be borne rather than through requests for ease.
