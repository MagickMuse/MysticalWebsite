✨Egyptian Knotted Linen Miscarriage Spell

The Weaver Goddess

Tait is the Egyptian goddess of weaving and linen, presiding over one of ancient Egypt's most sacred materials. Linen was plant-based, connected to agricultural cycles and purity. It was used in mummification wrappings, temple rites, and magical amulets.

Sacred Linen

Linen held special significance in Egyptian magic. Fresh, undyed linen served as the material for knotting spells, where a magician would tie knots while reciting incantations. Each knot 'locked' the spoken words into the fabric. Linen strips became wearable amulets, tied around the neck or applied to affected body parts.

The phrase "the land of Tait" appears in protective spells as a metaphor for purity and proper order.

Role in Pregnancy Protection

Tait is invoked in spells against miscarriage and excessive bleeding during pregnancy. One such spell instructs:

"Anubis has come forth to keep the flood from trespassing on what is pure: the land of Tait. Beware of what is in it. This spell to be said over the threads on the border of a textile, with a knot made in it. To be applied to the inside of the vagina."

The spell calls for a strip of linen with a fringe on one edge, where threads were left unwoven. Knotting the textile both fixed the magician's words into it and increased its absorbency. Heavy bleeding is compared to a Nile flood that has exceeded its proper bounds, a good, healthy thing turned destructive.

Connection to Anubis

Anubis's association with the sacred linen bandages used in mummification explains his appearance in spells invoking Tait. The wrapping of mummies, like the knotting of protective amulets, embedded protection and wholeness into cloth.
