The Watcher; The Untiring Eye; The All Seeing, Ever Watching

Classification: Titan

Origin: Greece

Domains & Sacred Objects

  • Sun

Overview

Helios is the sun as well as the original spirit of the sun worshipped in Greece. He was eventually superseded by Apollo, but very little information now survives regarding his actual veneration. Scholars consider that he was an extremely important pre-Hellenic deity who was actively venerated and possessed many shrines.

Powers & Domains

All-Seeing Witness: Oaths were taken in Helios' name. Helios sees all and knows all, at least if it occurs during daytime. He is the witness to all crimes committed in daylight hours.

Partnership with Hekate: Hekate witnesses all crimes committed at night. The relationship between the two deities is mysterious. Helios' wife is named Perse, sometimes a title for Hekate. Hekate may also be his cousin or descendent. When Persephone cannot be found, Hekate leads Demeter to Helios, who ultimately reveals Persephone's fate.

The Illuminator: Helios is the illuminator, both literally and metaphorically. According to Greek mythology, when the sun sets at night, Helios is visiting his other realm, either a realm of spirits or the realm where he lives with his mother, wife, and kids.

Family & Offspring

Consort: Perse (sometimes considered a title of Hekate)

Children with Perse:

  • Circe
  • Phaeton
  • Aietes (father of Medea)
  • Pasiphae (mother of Ariadne and the Minotaur)

He had other children too, born of mortals and goddesses.

Favored People: Helios' children can be detected by the golden glint in their eyes.

Manifestation & Attributes

A radiantly handsome man crowned with the sun drives a flaming chariot drawn by fire-breathing horses (or incendiary oxen) across the sky.

Animals: Helios owns cattle, one for every day of the solar year

Spirit Allies: His sisters, Eos (Dawn) and Selene (Moon)

Sacred Sites

Many of his shrines, located on mountaintops, were rededicated to Saint Elijah, who also drives a fiery chariot.

Rhodes: Helios owns Rhodes, given to him by Zeus. He married Rhodos, who may be the indigenous goddess of the island or Poseidon's daughter. Together they have seven sons, the Heliadae.

Worship & Offerings

Traditional Offerings: Gymnastic competitions were held in his honor

Related Entities
