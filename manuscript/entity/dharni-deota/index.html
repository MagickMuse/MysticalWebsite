Overview

Dharni Deota, whose name means earth the deity, is the chief goddess of the central provinces, a village mother identified with the earth itself and with the order of settled, cultivated life. She presides over the village as the power of the tilled field, the hearth, and the home, set against the wild and chaotic world beyond.

Goddess of the Cultivated World

Dharni Deota embodies the orderly, refined, and secure realm of the village. Hers is the world of the plowed field and the safe dwelling, and she is the guardian who protects the settlement from the raw, demonic forces of the surrounding jungle. Where many goddesses of the wider tradition are imagined as dangerous when independent, the village mother of the central provinces is instead the very principle of stability and civilized order, powerful precisely so that she can defend her people.

Her Companion of the Wild

Her husband and companion is a god of the hunt, a being of the jungle and the wild beasts who belongs to the untamed country outside the village. The pairing sets the goddess of the settled, ordered village beside a male consort of the wilderness, the two halves of a single world of field and forest.

In Magick

Dharni Deota is called on for the fertility of fields and the success of the harvest, for the stability and order of the household and the settlement, and for protection of cultivated land against disorder and harm. Her workings ground and steady, drawing a firm boundary between the safe inner world one tends and the wildness that presses on it from without.
