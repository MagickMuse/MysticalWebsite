Magickal Properties

Avalokitesvara embodies the compassion of all Buddhas. The Bodhisattva delays final release to answer the cries of the world, safeguarding travelers, healing the sick, guiding the desperate, and granting children to those who seek them.

Kannon (Japanese): The Japanese manifestation appearing in 33 different forms to meet the needs of different people and situations. One of the most beloved Buddhist deities in Japan, Kannon embodies infinite mercy and responds to all who call upon her with sincere hearts. She delays her own enlightenment to remain accessible to those who suffer. She grants children to those who pray for them, protects travelers, comforts the dying, and relieves all forms of suffering. Her compassion extends to all beings without distinction or judgment.

Ritual Uses

Chant the six-syllable mantra OM MANI PADME HUM for protection, healing, the banishment of evil, and passage to paradise. Petition in peril, illness, and sorrow, and for conception.

Invoke Kannon when suffering feels overwhelming, when seeking fertility or safe childbirth, or when needing guidance through difficulty. Recite the name or mantra to invoke compassionate presence. This deity appears in dreams offering guidance and comfort.

Offerings

Sincere prayer, recitation of the mantra, acts of mercy and generosity. White lotus flowers, clean water, pure water, incense, vegetarian food, prayers of compassion.

Favored People

All beings who ask for help, especially devotees across the Tibetan and broader Buddhist worlds; those suffering physical or emotional pain, parents seeking children, travelers, the dying, anyone in need.

Petition For

Compassion, protection, healing, deliverance from danger, help conceiving and welcoming children, mercy, fertility, safe childbirth, relief from suffering, guidance for the dying, protection of children.

Warnings

None given; approach with honesty and compassion. Invoke with compassion and clear ethical intent. Do not use this deity's name to manipulate others or substitute ritual for sustained compassionate practice.

Tibetan Incarnations

Avalokiteshvara is venerated throughout the Buddhist world, but especially in Tibet, where he has manifested in many forms and incarnations throughout history:

  • He is the traditional ancestor of the Tibetans.
  • In one incarnation, he was King Songtsen Gampo, who first brought Buddhism to Tibet in the mid-seventh century.
  • He was the first Dalai Lama, all Dalai Lamas, including the present one, are considered incarnations of Avalokiteshvara.

According to an ancient Tibetan folk legend that predates Darwin by millennia, Avalokiteshvara was a monkey living in Tibet's Yarlung Valley during the primordial era. Tara was an abominable snow-woman (a yeti) living nearby. The two fell in love and became the parents of six humans, the ancestors of the Tibetan people.

Scriptural Origins

The first documented description of Avalokiteshvara derives from the Lotus Sutra, which dates from approximately the first century CE. According to the Lotus Sutra, Avalokiteshvara will, if called upon, assist those swept away by floods or menaced by other deadly perils. He bestows the gift of fertility. He travels to all corners of all realms helping all beings who seek his help. Call him by name or through his mantra and he will come.

Iconography

In India, Avalokiteshvara is portrayed as a young prince with the Buddha nestled in his hair, or as an ascetic who closely resembles the Hindu deity Shiva. He may have two, eight, or 1000 arms. (According to legend, Avalokiteshvara received 1000 arms from Buddha, the better to help those in need.) In China, Avalokiteshvara transformed into Kwan Yin and so is depicted in female form. In Japan, Avalokiteshvara manifests as Kannon in 33 forms.

Attributes

Lotus flower, crystal mala (Buddhist rosary).

Encyclopedia Source Notes

The name Avalokitesvara is translated as "merciful lord." Buddhist tradition from India identifies him as a bodhisattva or buddha-designate. His period of worship extends from circa 500 BC to the present, with a pan-Asiatic cult center. Nineteen other forms are listed under his name. Art references include metal and stone sculptures and paintings. Literary sources include the Sadhanamala and Tantric ritual texts.

He is one of the most important deities of the Mahayana sect of Buddhism. In Lamaism he is the tutelary god of Tibet. He equates with Visnu in Hinduism and bears links with Padmapani. In cosmic mythology he is a creator deity. His Sakti is Pandara and his attendant animal is a lion. Many forms of Avalokitesvara exist, which may include varieties with up to eleven heads, sometimes arranged in a pyramid.

Color: white or red. Attributes: blue lotus, image of Amitabha on the topmost pyramidal head, lotus, rosary, sword, and water jar.

In Chinese Buddhism he is represented by the goddess Kuan-Yin, and in Japanese by Kwannon.
