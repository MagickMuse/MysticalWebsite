Overview

Camulos is a war god of the Celtic tradition of Britain and neighboring Celtic regions, most strongly attested among the Trinovantes and Catuvellauni of southeastern Britain. His name is preserved in the ancient place name Camulodunum, the fortress of Camulos, which is the Latin name for Colchester in what is now Essex, England, and which served as the first Roman colonia and effective capital of Roman Britain in the first century. He is known chiefly from Romano-Celtic inscriptions and from pre-Roman British coinage of the Trinovantes and Catuvellauni that bears his name or the boar emblem associated with him.

Classification

Deity, tribal war god of the Celtic British and neighboring Celtic peoples.

Domains

War, martial victory, the defense of tribal territory and stronghold, the sovereign strength of the war leader, the boar as sign of ferocity and courage.

Iconography

Represented on Iron Age coinage and on Romano-Celtic inscriptions primarily by the boar, an emblem carried on standards, helmet crests, and shield bosses across the wider Celtic world. Under Roman occupation he is identified with Mars and appears in dedications as Mars Camulus, taking on the standard iconography of a helmeted, armored war god while retaining the tribal name.

Attributes

Boar, warrior's arms and armor, fortified stronghold. Under the Mars Camulus interpretation, the Roman military kit of helmet, cuirass, spear, and shield.

Sacred Sites

Camulodunum, the fortress named for him at the site of modern Colchester in Essex, was the principal center of his cult in Britain. Inscriptions naming Mars Camulus have also been recovered from Gaul, including from the territory of the Remi around Reims, and from the Rhineland, showing that his worship was carried across Celtic-speaking regions and into the frontier zones of the empire.

Ritual

Honored with the dedications appropriate to a Celtic war god and, in Romano-Celtic form, with the standard votive practice offered to Mars: altar dedications, weapons offerings, and vows made in exchange for victory or safe return from campaign. Coinage bearing his boar emblem functioned as both currency and tribal statement of allegiance to the god of the fortress.

Lore

Camulos belongs to a wider Celtic pattern in which tribal war gods lend their names to the strongholds that shelter their peoples, so that the god and the place cannot be cleanly separated. The Roman conquest of southeastern Britain under Claudius in 43 turned Camulodunum into the first colonia in the province, and the site retained ritual importance until it was sacked during the Iceni revolt led by Boudica in 60 or 61, when the newly built Roman temple there was destroyed. Under the interpretatio Romana he was recognized as a form of Mars and continued to be honored under the compound name Mars Camulus throughout the Romano-Celtic period.
