Kappa are Japanese water spirits who inhabit rivers, ponds, and other bodies of water. They are amphibious creatures depicted with turtle shells, scaly skin, webbed hands and feet, and a water-filled depression on top of their heads. This depression contains their life force; if the water spills out, the kappa loses its power and may die.

Kappa have a reputation for drowning people, especially children, and are particularly dangerous. They lure victims into the water or pull them under. However, kappa also have a code of honor and an obsession with politeness. If you bow to a kappa, it will bow back, spilling the water from its head depression and rendering it powerless.

Ritual Uses

Do not invoke kappa directly. Instead, work with protective deities and spirits to ward against them. Leave offerings at safe shore altars to appease them and keep them from causing harm. Kappa have an inexplicable love of cucumbers, which can be used as protective offerings.

Warnings

Never accept rides from strange water horses or let children play near water edges at dusk when kappa are most active. Kappa lure and drown victims. Do not fight them directly as they are extremely strong in water. Use politeness and offerings instead of confrontation.
