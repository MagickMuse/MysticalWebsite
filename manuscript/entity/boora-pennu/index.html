Boora Pennu is a deity of the Khond people of the Orissa province of India. He is the god of light, the local counterpart, or synonymous form, of the sun god Bella Pennu, and stands as one of the principal figures of the Khond pantheon.

Domains

Light and the sovereignty of the sun, local authority over the Khond community, and the generative office through which the earth and the other great gods came into being.

The Creation of Tari Pennu

In the Khond cosmogony Boora Pennu created the earth goddess Tari Pennu as his consort, and through her he engendered the other great gods of the pantheon. His generative work sets him in the position of a primal father-figure whose union with the earth is the ground of the fuller divine order.

Sacred Sites

Orissa (Odisha), India.

Ritual

Until recently, Boora Pennu was the subject of the meriah rituals of the Khond, sacrificial observances that involved violent human sacrifice offered to the earth-consort Tari Pennu and to Boora Pennu himself. The meriah was the appointed victim, understood in the ritual as an offering that renewed the fertility of the earth and secured the favor of the great gods for the community. These practices were suppressed during the mid-19th century under British colonial administration and are not currently observed. Modern Khond religious life includes continuing devotion to the great gods of the tradition, in transformed and non-sacrificial form.

Precautions

⚠️ Boora Pennu belongs to the closed religious tradition of the Khond people of Odisha. Approach this entry as reference. Do not attempt to petition, invoke, or reconstruct the ritual practices of his cult outside the living community and its authorized keepers.
