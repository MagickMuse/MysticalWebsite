Danu is a minor goddess of the Hindu Vedic tradition, distinct from the Irish mother goddess of the same spelling. The references to her in the Rg-veda are few and lacking in description, and her distinctive character must be assembled from scattered hints rather than from any sustained portrait.

In Vedic literature

Danu is named as the mother of the cosmic demon Vrtra, who is slain by Indra in one of the central myths of the Rg-veda. One verse likens her to a cow, although her serpentine son is described as without hands or feet and is consistently imagined in reptilian form.

The word danu appears elsewhere in the hymns with the sense of the waters of heaven. On the strength of that usage, Danu has been read as a goddess of the formless primordial waters that lay outside or before creation, the same waters in which Vrtra was said to lurk and which he held back from the world until Indra struck him down and released them.

Disambiguation

This page concerns the Vedic Danu, mother of Vrtra. She should not be confused with the Irish Danu, the primordial mother goddess of the Tuatha Dé Danann.

Cited hymns

Key Rg-veda reference: 1.32.9 (Danu likened to a cow, in the Vrtra hymn).
