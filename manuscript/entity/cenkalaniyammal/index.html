Cenkalaniyammal

Also known as

Cenkalaniy Ammal, Lady of the Red Paddyfield, Senkalani Ammal

Overview

Cenkalaniyammal is a local goddess of the Hindu-Dravidian religion of the Tamil region of southern India. Her name means the lady of the red paddyfield, and it names her by the reddish standing water and the red-earthed field that give her image its particular character. Her office is the guardianship of the paddyfield, the flooded rice field on which the food of the community depends, and she belongs to the wide Tamil family of ammal goddesses, the local mother-goddess figures whose worship holds the everyday life of the village together.

Classification

Local goddess of the Hindu-Dravidian tradition of the Tamil region. Guardian goddess of the paddyfields.

Domains

Guardianship of the rice paddy and the water within it, protection of the growing crop, blessing on the labor of the paddyfield workers, the reddish standing water and the red-earthed soil that give her image its particular color, the everyday life of the rural community whose livelihood rests on the paddy.

Iconography

Depicted as a village mother-goddess figure in the Tamil ammal style, standing or seated at the edge of the paddyfield, sometimes with a small child at her hip or with the tools of the paddy worker at her feet. Her association with the reddish water and the red earth of the field gives her image a warm red coloring, and she is often marked by the ordinary jewelry of the Tamil village woman: bangles, anklets, and the small nose-jewel of the region.

Attributes

A handful of paddy grain, a sickle, a winnowing tray, an earthen pot of water, the red flowers of the countryside, the small oil lamp of the village shrine.

Sacred Sites

Small shrines at the edge of paddyfields across the Tamil country, particularly in the southern districts of Tamil Nadu where paddy cultivation has been the central agricultural work for centuries. Her shrines are typically small open-air platforms or modest buildings, cared for by the local community rather than by any wider temple institution.

Ritual

Honored in the ordinary rhythm of the paddy year: at the flooding of the fields, at the transplanting of the young rice, at the ripening of the crop, and at the harvest. Offerings appropriate to her cult include rice, cooked rice cakes, coconut, betel leaves, and the flowers of the countryside. Small oil lamps are lit at her shrine at the boundaries of the paddyfield to mark her continued presence over the crop.

Lore

The ammal goddesses of the Tamil country are one of the most vital and continuous religious traditions of southern India, older in many places than the wider temple Hinduism and continuous with the deep religious life of the Dravidian peoples. Cenkalaniyammal holds her particular place within that family as the goddess of the red paddyfield, the guardian of the flooded rice paddy on whose harvest the community's food depends. Her worship carries the intimate directness characteristic of the Tamil village religion, in which the goddess is felt to be present in the very earth and water of the field she watches over.
