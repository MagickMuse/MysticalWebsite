Beautiful Woman

Szépasszony is a pre-Christian Hungarian love goddess now demoted to a beautiful but dangerous witch spirit. Her true name has been suppressed. Szépasszony, a euphemism, literally means "beautiful woman." Spirit of romance, fertility, birth, and death, she is a water goddess associated with storms and rain.

The White Lady

Her gifts, if she chooses to give them, are beauty, romance, and intoxicating ecstasy. She is an authority on love spells and potions. Szépasszony is a true White Lady, very fair-skinned with long silver-white hair; her dresses are incandescent white.

Sacred Sites

Cropped grass circles mark her dance floors. Szépasszony Valley in Eger, Hungary's wine country, was allegedly the center of her veneration.

Additional Lore

Post-Christianity, Szépasszony developed a malevolent reputation as a femme fatale and dangerous demon accused of luring away children, similar to the Snow Queen. It is unclear how much of this reputation reflects genuine danger and how much reflects fear-mongering meant to discourage her veneration, or alternatively, her fury at the loss of that veneration.

Szépasszony adores dancing and ventures out to dance whenever it storms. She is considered responsible for hail and white winter storms, and as a sorceress she uses water as the primary ingredient in her spells. Watch for standing puddles, said to be among her magical tricks for ensnaring victims; those who walk through them and catch cold, or worse, are perceived as bewitched, and she is held responsible for life-threatening illness caused by inclement weather. She may also be invoked to heal such illnesses. Her dance floors, the cropped grass circles noted above, are surrounded by taller grass, and it is considered bad manners, and dangerous, to venture inside them.
