Classification

Venerated as a saint by Shia Muslims, Sufis, and the Gnawa.

Life and History

Born in the late sixth century, Sidi Bilal was an Ethiopian Christian who lived as a slave. He became Islam's first muezzin, the person who calls the faithful to prayer, serving the Prophet Muhammad directly.

Origin Stories

Two traditions explain his path to freedom. In one account, he converted to Islam while still enslaved, prompting his master to torture him for his new faith. Abu Bakr as-Siddiq, a close companion of the Prophet, learned of this cruelty, purchased Bilal, and immediately freed him. Bilal then entered Muhammad's personal service.

Another version describes Bilal as a Christian slave in Muhammad's own household who won his freedom by healing the Prophet's daughter Fatima of an illness through the power of song. He then embraced Islam and became the first muezzin.

The Gnawa Connection

The Gnawa Brotherhood claims spiritual descent from Sidi Bilal. This tradition combines healing practices with music, mirroring Bilal's legendary cure of Fatima. The Gnawa trace their origins to slaves brought from Ghana, Guinea, and Mali to serve as guards for Moroccan sultan Moulay Ismail (1672-1727). Bilal's name appears throughout their sacred songs.

Color

Green.

Sacred Site

His tomb-shrine stands in Damascus's Bab al-Saghir Cemetery. Petitioners traditionally tie green cloth to the window grille when making requests, sometimes returning to remove it if their prayers are answered.
