Herne the Hunter is the Earth Father, God of Nature, and Spirit of the ancient woodlands and all growing things. He is the Greenman and Lord of Animals, known as Hu Gadarn, the Horned God of Fertility, and Guardian of the Gates to the Otherworld. He is closely associated with Cernunnos.

Herne is an antlered man, swathed in deerskins. It is unclear whether the antlers are his or whether it is a removable crown. Sometimes Herne leads the Wild Hunt riding a fire-breathing stallion and accompanied by a pack of hounds.

Herne is the ancient keeper of England's Windsor Park and allegedly may be found wandering around an old oak in the forest at night. The tree is known as Herne's Oak. He usually carries a firebrand, a musical instrument or a birch broom.

Ritual Uses

Herne can be invoked as a horned deity of the hunt, the forest, and the Wild Hunt. He represents the wild masculine energy of the woods and the liminal space between life and death.

Work with Herne for connection to English woodland spirits and traditions, for hunting magick (both literal and metaphorical), for leading the dead to their rest, and for accessing the power of the Wild Hunt.

He is particularly appropriate for those with English heritage or those working with British woodland magick.

Warnings

Herne is a liminal figure associated with death and the Wild Hunt. Approach with appropriate caution and respect. The Wild Hunt is dangerous to encounter unprepared. Work with Herne only if you are ready to face the wild, untamed aspects of nature and death.

Sacred Animals

Herne's sacred animals are the stag, bull, goat, and bear. He is strong and terrible if his domain is violated, but joyful and full of lifeforce when approached with reverence and quiet respect.

Hibernian Invocations

The Hibernians invoked Herne through chanting circle dances, wearing stag horns and garments sewn with leaves and vines.

Herne as the Witch's Mercury

Some witchcraft traditions identify Herne with the same guiding power that other cultures call by different names: Hermes to the Greeks, Thoth to the Egyptians, Odin to the Scandinavians, and Woden to the early Anglo-Saxons, who also knew this power as Earendel, the Morning Star. Vodou calls the same power Maitre Carrefour, Master of the Crossroads, also titled Psychopompos, Guide of Souls. Across all these names runs a common thread, a mediator between worlds, tied to the sky, storm winds, and the crossroads, called upon before any act of divination. See Mercury for the fuller correspondence, including a consecration method using the Square of Mercury.

Legends

Various legends surround Herne the Hunter:

  • He is the ghost of an English wizard who still haunts Windsor Great Park
  • He was an innkeeper (and wizard) who was hung from an oak as punishment for his involvement with the occult
  • Herne is a generic horned deity
  • Herne is Lord of the Realm of the Dead, Leader of Dead Souls, and possibly Cernunnos in disguise
  • The Christian Church identified Herne as the devil

Reference is made to Herne in Shakespeare's Merry Wives of Windsor, where he is described as a hunter who hanged himself from an oak in Windsor Forest. This literary reference has helped keep his legend alive.

Like many horned deities, Herne was identified with the devil by Christianity, but from a Pagan perspective he is a powerful spirit of the wild hunt and English woodlands. His antlers link him to the sacred stag and to other antlered deities like Cernunnos.
