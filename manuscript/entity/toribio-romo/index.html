Classification

Roman Catholic saint and Cristero War martyr.

Overview

Toribio Romo González was born on April 16, 1900, in Santa Ana de Guadalupe, Jalisco, Mexico. Ordained as a priest in 1922, he ministered during a turbulent period in Mexican history when the government enforced harsh anti-clerical laws. He was killed during the Cristero War, a Catholic uprising against the Mexican constitution's restrictions on religious practice. On February 25, 1928, government soldiers broke into his home and shot him. He died in his sister's arms at only twenty-seven years old. He was canonized by Pope John Paul II in May 2000, alongside twenty-four other Cristero War martyrs.

Patron of Border Crossers

Toribio Romo has become renowned as the patron saint of those crossing the US-Mexico border. Numerous witnesses report encountering a mysterious stranger who guides them safely across, provides food, and helps them find work. This stranger asks only that they visit him someday in Santa Ana de Guadalupe.

The first documented account involves a man named José who arrived at the border without papers. Despairing of ever crossing safely, he was approached by a helpful stranger who promised safe passage, a meal, and employment. When José asked the cost, the man simply requested that José visit him in Santa Ana de Guadalupe. All the stranger's promises came true. When José eventually returned to Mexico to thank his benefactor, villagers directed him to Toribio Romo's grave shrine.

Since then, sightings of Saint Toribio have multiplied. He is not always immediately recognized, but he consistently provides his "address" in Mexico to those he assists.

Manifestation

Described as an angelic figure dressed in dark clothing.

Sacred Sites

  • Santa Ana de Guadalupe, Jalisco, Mexico: His main shrine where pilgrims come to repay favors and request new ones. His coffin is displayed by the altar, along with the bloodstained clothing he wore when he died.
  • Saints Peter and Paul Catholic Church, Tulsa, Oklahoma: Contains a shrine dedicated to Toribio Romo.
