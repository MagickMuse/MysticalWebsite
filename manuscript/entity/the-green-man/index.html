Magickal Properties

The Green Man is the spirit of wildlife and Spring, the embodiment of vigorous Earth and wild consciousness. He represents the untamed pulse of growth that drives all living things, the green force that surges through root and branch alike. Where he walks, the wild world thrives in its fullest expression.

Ritual Uses

Work with the Green Man when seeking to connect with the raw, living energy of the natural world. He is called upon for Spring rituals, fertility workings tied to the land, and any magick that draws on the vitality of growing things. Invoke him to awaken wild consciousness within yourself or to honor the spirit of the forest.

Warnings

The Green Man is not domesticated. He embodies the wild, not the cultivated. Approach with respect for untamed nature and be prepared for primal, unfiltered energy.

Additional Notes

The Green Man appears in architecture and folklore across Europe, his face peering from a mask of leaves, vines sprouting from his mouth, eyes, and ears. He is the living face of the forest itself. Related figures include Cernunnos, Herne, and Pan, though each carries distinct qualities. The Green Man is the vegetative spirit in its purest form: wild consciousness expressed through leaf, vine, and blossom.

Symbol & Iconography

The term Green Man was coined in the UK in the 1930s, though related foliate figures are described across the British Isles and as far afield as India, Asia, and Arabic countries. Depictions show a head built from leaves and vines, sometimes human and sometimes animal. One early example survives as stonework on an Irish obelisk dated to around 300 BC. Irish myth also records a figure called the Derg Corra, meaning man in the tree, which some suggest may be the same figure.
