San Pascualito is an unofficial saint, the skeleton saint of Chiapas, Mexico's southernmost state. Though sometimes described as an "Indian misinterpretation" of Saint Paschal Baylon, this assertion is false. Skeleton deities were venerated throughout Mexico and Central America long before Christianity arrived. The earliest documentation of San Pascualito comes from Spanish observers in 1601, who described a painted, ornamented skeleton worshipped by the Zoques people. He remains passionately venerated today, though the Church has historically persecuted his devotees.

Domains

San Pascualito is a saint of death who drives a cart in which he collects the souls of the deceased. He is not a killer but a psychopomp, an escort to the next realm who travels between the lands of the living and dead. As Lord of Borders, he helps shamans communicate with the dead and assists devotees in crossing forbidden human borders, including those between nations.

Devotees may petition him for more time, but once the sound of his approaching cartwheels becomes audible in a building where someone is dying, death is guaranteed. He prefers to sleep during the day and emerge at night to collect souls. As he passes cemeteries, he raises the dead and may be accompanied by a parade of ghosts.

Iconography

San Pascualito manifests as a crowned skeleton, though you are more likely to hear than see him. His cart is integral to his iconography and distinguishes him from other skeleton deities. He is depicted lying in a casket in his cart, seated in it, or driving it. When approaching a dying person, he gives three dry gasps to indicate his presence and warn of impending death.

Colors

Associated with the colors of death and bones.
