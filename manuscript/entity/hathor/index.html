Hathor is the primordial Egyptian goddess; she was ever-present from the beginning. Many of her characteristics were eventually transferred to Isis but Hathor never lost her popularity.

Hathor has dominion over love, sex, reproduction, music, dance, intoxication, cosmetics, perfume, and all the joys and pleasures of life. Matron of women, Hathor is the guardian of females of all species. She embodies the female principle and grants fertility.

She is also a warrior spirit, known as the Eye of Ra: when the supreme god Ra is in trouble, Hathor is his main line of defense. She was the guardian of pharaohs too.

Ritual Uses

Invoke Hathor for love, joy, beauty, music, dance, fertility, and all pleasures of life. She is particularly appropriate for women's magick and for celebrating the sacred feminine. Work with her for fertility magick, to enhance beauty and attractiveness, for success in the arts (especially music and dance), and for protection.

Hathor is excellent for magick involving cosmetics, perfumes, and adornment. These are sacred practices in her tradition, not vanity. She teaches that beauty, pleasure, and joy are spiritual practices.

Warnings

Hathor is an ancient and powerful goddess. Approach her with respect but also with joy. She delights in celebration, not grim solemnity. However, as the Eye of Ra, she has a fierce warrior aspect. Do not underestimate her power.

Hathor is a shape-shifter and takes many forms (the cat goddess Bastet may or may not be Hathor's alter ego); however her primary and perhaps most ancient manifestation is as a horned cow.

Hathor's Horned Manifestations

  • Hathor wears a horned headdress that combines lunar and bovine imagery
  • Hathor is the celestial cow; the Milky Way spills from her breasts
  • Hathor, one of the very few Egyptian deities commonly depicted full-face rather than in profile, has a special distinctive hair-do that resembles a "flip": the ends curl up sharply in imitation of horns
  • Hathor manifests as a cow. You'll know it's her, not just any old cow; the clue to her identity is the impeccable eye makeup sported by the Hathor-cow.

Hathor is often portrayed as a gold cow. Some scholars believe that the biblical Golden Calf was either Hathor or her son. Hathor has dominion over the Sinai Peninsula where the events surrounding the Golden Calf are believed to have occurred.

The Power of Horns

Hathor embodies the powers implicit in horns. She is the Horn of Plenty providing devotees with all the bounties of life, but she also embodies the power of the horn as an aggressive weapon of self-defense.

Her horns represent both abundance and protection, the nurturing mother who also fiercely defends. This dual nature makes her one of the most complete representations of female divine power in the ancient world.

Healing

Among Hathor's many epithets is Lady of Myrrh — her sacred essence is expressed through fragrant myrrh. Once upon a time, Hathor's temples also served as centers of healing, similar to those of Asklepios. Less information survives regarding Hathor's holy hospitals; apparently some form of hydrotherapy was performed.

The following spells invoke Hathor's healing presence and complement each other:

Hathor Myrrh Bath: Add essential oil of myrrh to the bath to invoke Hathor's healing presence.

Hathor Myrrh Fumigation: Burn myrrh tears. Waft the resulting smoke over the person and/or the room.

Syncretism

When the ancient Greeks encountered Egyptian culture, they identified Hathor with Aphrodite based on their shared domains of love, beauty, perfume, and music. This identification became standard in the Hellenistic period.

Role in Egyptian Magick

The Cow Goddess

Hathor's primary and most ancient manifestation is as a cow. In Egyptian art, cows and cattle held sacred significance. When tomb paintings showed precious cattle crossing canals, magicians performed protective spells to guard them from crocodiles.

Visual Recognition

In Egyptian art, major deities developed standard characteristics that make them easy to recognize. Hathor is one of the very few Egyptian deities commonly depicted full-face rather than in profile.

Fertility and Sexuality

As the goddess of sexuality and fertility, Hathor presided over love, lovemaking, and conception. The sensual love poetry of ancient Egypt was often set among lush gardens or drunken festivals in her honor. Her festivals involved music, wine, and drunkenness, meant to lure Hathor back to Egypt after she had "flounced off in a huff." According to myth, Hathor's return was necessary for Ra to regenerate himself overnight; the continued survival of the cosmos depended on her.

Offerings at Hathor's temple at Deir el-Bahri included faience bowls decorated with tilapia (a fertility symbol), lotus flowers, and women strumming harps. Both male and female devotees honored her there.

The Sistrum

The sistrum (shesheshat in Egyptian, named for its soothing, rattling sound) is Hathor's sacred musical instrument, shared with Isis. Its jingling, rattling sound pleased the goddess and drove away evil influences.

Hathor and Childbirth

In later periods of Egyptian history, Hathor was closely linked to Bes and invoked in protective magick for childbirth. One spell for easing labor pain calls on "Hathor, the lady of Dendera, to fetch her healing amulet, so that Hathor will make this woman give birth!"

Birth temples (mammisi) built in the Late Period featured Hathor cow-face emblems framing scenes of divine mothers with infants. The Seven Hathors, aspects, avatars, or daughters of Hathor, appeared at births to pronounce a child's destiny and were invoked in love spells.

Deir el-Medina

At the village of Deir el-Medina, houses had elevated platforms in the front room decorated with images of Bes and young female musicians. These may have offered spaces for lovemaking and childbirth, or simply served for prayers and offerings aimed at ensuring household wellbeing.
