In Witchcraft

Habondia is named in traditional witchcraft as one of the titles of the Lady of Delight, the great feminine force presiding over love, enchantment, and the abundance of nature. She stands alongside Hulda, Herodias, Aradia, Diana, and Hecate as a name by which the witch-goddess may be addressed. In workings of romantic love and philter-craft she may be invoked at the opening of the rite with words such as: "In thy name, Habondia, and that of thy Ministers of Love, do I proceed in this work of Love."

The Dark Aspect: Nocticula

Habondia holds a second, darker face known as Nocticula, the Lady of Night and Death. In this aspect she is no longer the abundant, nurturing goddess of the pantry and the hearth, but the cold, Saturnian power that binds, incarcerates, and destroys. As Nocticula she is associated with the dark phase of the moon, with Saturn's restrictive energies, and with the workings of binding, cursing, and vengeance.

In traditional witchcraft, the Nocticula aspect of Habondia merges with and mirrors the dark face of Hecate: both are queens of the night, rulers over baneful workings, and mistresses of the spirits of the dead. When approached as Nocticula, Habondia is petitioned not for abundance but for arrest, cessation, and the freezing of an enemy's power.

Saturnian workings in her name employ black candles, black clothing, the imagery of binding and chains, and timing under the dark or waning moon on Saturdays. She is the witch's ally in work that must cut off, seal away, or bring to an end that which harms.
