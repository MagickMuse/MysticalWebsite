Magickal Properties

John Barleycorn is the personification of the barley harvest and, more broadly, a Spirit of Nature who embodies the green life that rises, is cut down, and rises again. He is the grain made flesh, the living symbol of earthly sustenance and of the spiritual pleasures drawn from it, and he stands close kin to The Green Man as a figure of the vegetative spirit.

Lore

The folk ballad that bears his name tells of three men who swore that John Barleycorn must die. They plowed him under, buried him, and heaped clods upon his head, yet from that burial he rose again. The verses follow the whole labor of the harvest as a drawn out martyrdom: every stage the barley endures, from reaping to threshing to malting, is sung as a form of torture visited upon him.

There was three men come out o' the west their fortunes for to try
And these three men made a solemn vow, John Barleycorn must die
They plowed, they sowed, they harrowed him in, throwed clods upon his head
And these three men made a solemn vow, John Barleycorn was dead

Death and Resurrection

Because barley can be both eaten as bread and brewed into ale, John Barleycorn carries the double meaning of material and spiritual nourishment. His torment, death, and return have long been read alongside other sacrificial figures: the resurrection of Christ, whose body is likened to bread, and the slain Hiram Abiff of Masonic legend, whose death in the name of honor rendered him immortal. Like them, John Barleycorn suffers and dies for the good of all so that bread may be made, and like them he can always look toward his rebirth in the spring.

Additional Notes

He belongs to the season of the grain harvest and the turning of the year toward winter, when the last sheaf is cut and the long cycle of sowing and reaping begins again.
