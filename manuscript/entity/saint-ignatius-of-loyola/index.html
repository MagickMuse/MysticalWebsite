Saint Ignatius of Loyola is the patron saint of soldiers, Jesuits, and educators. He is renowned as a powerful exorcist and master of spiritual discernment.

Classification

Roman Catholic saint, founder of the Society of Jesus (Jesuits).

Life and History

Íñigo López de Oñaz y Loyola (1491–31 July 1556) was a Spanish Basque nobleman and knight. During the Battle of Pamplona in 1521, a cannonball shattered his leg. During his long recovery, he read lives of the saints and experienced a profound spiritual conversion.

After his recovery, Ignatius made a pilgrimage to Montserrat, where he hung up his sword before the Black Madonna and exchanged his fine clothes for a beggar's garments. He spent nearly a year in a cave at Manresa, experiencing visions, practicing extreme asceticism, and developing what would become his famous Spiritual Exercises. In 1540, he founded the Society of Jesus and was canonized in 1622.

The Spiritual Exercises

Ignatius's most enduring contribution is the Spiritual Exercises, a set of meditations, prayers, and contemplative practices designed to help practitioners discern God's will and overcome attachment to sin. The Exercises include detailed methods for examining one's conscience and distinguishing between spirits—skills essential for exorcism.

Powers and Domains

Ignatius is invoked for:

  • Exorcism and protection against demonic influence
  • Spiritual discernment and decision-making
  • Conversion of heart
  • Protection of soldiers
  • Success in education

His expertise in discerning spirits makes him particularly powerful against demonic deception.

Iconography

Depicted in black Jesuit robes, often holding a book (the Spiritual Exercises or Constitutions), with the IHS monogram (the Jesuit emblem) nearby. Sometimes shown with a globe at his feet, representing the worldwide mission of the Jesuits.

Attributes

IHS monogram, book, chasuble, globe.

Sacred Sites

  • Church of the Gesù in Rome (his tomb)
  • Sanctuary of Loyola in the Basque Country, Spain (his birthplace)
  • Cave of Saint Ignatius at Manresa, Catalonia

Feast Day

31 July
