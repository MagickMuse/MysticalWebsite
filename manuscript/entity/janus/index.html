God of Doorways

Janus is a uniquely Roman deity with no precise counterpart in other traditions, though scholars have noted a possible link to the Greek concept of Chaos. As the God of Doorways, his defining physical feature is that he has two faces or heads, allowing him to see in both directions simultaneously.

Keys and the Staff

Janus carries two implements suited to his office: a key, with which he locks and unlocks the doors and gates he guards, and a staff to drive away anyone who approaches a threshold without the right to cross it.

God of Gates and Communication

Because he governs gates, Janus is by extension the ruler of arrivals and departures and of all forms of communication. Nothing enters or exits without passing his boundary.

God of Beginnings

Janus holds jurisdiction over beginnings. He presides over the daybreak, admitting the Sun through the eastern gate of the sky each morning. Before the creation of the Universe, when all the elements were still a formless mass, Janus was known as Chaos. After their separation and the coming of order, his name became Janus, but he continues to govern the moment of transition between one state and another.

January and the Janitor

The month of January takes its name from Janus: the god who looks back over the year just ended while simultaneously looking forward into the one beginning. The word janitor too derives from his name, the earthly guardian of doorways and corridors. He was honoured on the first day of every month.
