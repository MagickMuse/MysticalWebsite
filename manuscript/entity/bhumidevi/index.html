Bhumidevi, whose name means the earth goddess, is a fertility goddess of the Hindu Epic and Puranic tradition, particularly beloved in southern India. She is the earth herself, the great fertile body of the ground, worshipped as the second wife of Vishnu (or, in his Krishna form, of Krishna). Her son is Naraka, born of her union with Vishnu in his Varaha avatar. In the north she is worshipped under the name Pusti, the goddess of nourishment.

Domains

The fertile earth, the productive ground, the harvest, the marital consort's mediating tenderness, and the maternal generosity that gives rise to and sustains all creatures who walk upon her.

Iconography

Bhumidevi is often depicted standing at the left, or occasionally the right, hand of the Varaha avatara of Vishnu, in the posture of the rescued earth held tenderly beside the boar-headed savior who lifted her from the primeval waters. In her own right she is often depicted sitting on a lotus throne with bared breasts, an iconographic sign of the earth's abundant, milk-giving generosity.

Attributes

Blue lotus, lotus, lute, pomegranate, pot with herbs, pot with vegetables, and water jar.

Colors

Green and gold, the colors of the fertile ground and the ripened field.

Kinship

Bhumidevi is the second consort of Vishnu, beside Sri-Lakshmi who is his first wife. Her son by Vishnu-Varaha is Naraka. She is closely identified with Prthivi, the older Vedic earth goddess, and with Satyabhama, the Krishna-queen who is considered an avatar of Bhudevi. In the north she is worshipped as Pusti.

Petition For

Fertility of the ground, abundant harvest, the fertile womb of the earth, the health of what grows, and the intercessory love of the divine consort who mediates between the earth and the great preserving power.

Cross-Tradition Note

Her name Vasudhara is also used, in the Buddhist tradition, as the name of a distinct treasure-holder goddess sometimes called Yellow Tara. The two goddesses share the name at a distance but belong to different religious streams.
