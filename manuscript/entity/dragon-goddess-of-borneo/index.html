⚠️ The Dragon Goddess of Borneo is venerated within Dayak Indigenous tradition. This is a closed practice and should only be approached by those within the originating culture or with explicit cultural permission.

Magickal Properties

The Dragon Goddess of Borneo is the supreme creator and guardian deity of the Dayak peoples of Borneo. She is the cosmic serpent or dragon who created the world and continues to protect it. She represents the power of the earth, water, and underworld, serving as guardian of the home, fertility, and the realm of the dead. Her image adorns Dayak homes and sacred objects, protecting families and ensuring prosperity. She embodies both creative and destructive forces, able to bring life or take it away.

Ritual Uses

The Dragon Goddess is invoked for household protection, fertility, agricultural abundance, and safe passage for the dead. Her image is placed on homes, boats, and ritual objects to provide protection. She is petitioned during planting and harvest, for childbirth, and in funerary rites to ensure the deceased reach the afterlife safely. Work with her for deep connection to earth powers and ancestral protection.

Offerings

Rice and food offerings, textiles with dragon motifs, traditional Dayak ceremonies with proper protocols, carved dragon imagery in homes, ritual objects bearing her image, prayers during planting and harvest.

Favored People

Dayak people and their descendants, families seeking home protection, those honoring indigenous Bornean traditions, practitioners working with earth and water powers, those seeking ancestral connection.

Petition For

Home and family protection, fertility and successful childbirth, abundant harvests, safe passage for the dead to the afterlife, protection during water travel, connection to ancestral wisdom.

Warnings

Honor her as the Dayak guardian of home, fertility, and the dead with local offerings and respectful placement of dragon imagery. Do not appropriate or commercialize her iconography. Always follow Dayak protocols. She has both creative and destructive aspects and must be approached with proper respect.

Additional Notes

Her image features prominently in traditional Dayak longhouse architecture and decoration. She is often depicted as a serpentine dragon with elaborate scales and fierce features. The Dragon Goddess appears in Dayak creation myths as the cosmic serpent who shaped the world. She guards the transition between life and death and ensures cosmic balance. Her worship is deeply integrated with Dayak agricultural cycles and ancestor veneration.
