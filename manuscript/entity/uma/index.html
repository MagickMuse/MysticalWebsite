Overview

Uma is the daughter of Himavat, Lord of the Himalayas, and the Apsara Mena, and sister of the river goddess Ganga. Different versions of the myth exist, but in all of them, Uma determines to marry Lord Shiva, who had retreated from the world in grief following the death of his beloved Sati. Uma may be a reincarnation of Sati, but Shiva, living an extreme ascetic existence, took no notice of her.

Finally, a sage (rishi) sent by Indra advised Uma to simultaneously visualize Shiva, practice austerities, and chant the mantra OM NAMAH SHIVAYA. The sage assured her that if she did this long enough and intensely enough, Shiva would come to love her. Uma practiced austerities, chanting, and meditation for eighteen thousand years to gain Shiva as her spouse. She abandoned her luxurious life as a princess, traveling to a forested mountain to meditate on Shiva. She wore bark clothing and ate only fruit and leaves that fell from the trees. She surrounded herself with five fires: four bonfires around her and the sun above. She finally abandoned eating altogether, and stood endlessly on one leg, as in the yoga position (asana) called Ardha Chandrasana, chanting her mantra, focused on Shiva and her goal.

The sage had not lied: Shiva appeared, impressed and moved by her austerities and determination, and took her as his bride. Uma is usually venerated alongside Shiva as an aspect of Parvati. However, she is also worshipped independently, especially in the Himalayan nation of Nepal. Female ascetics in particular venerate her as a role model.

Magickal Properties

Uma embodies fierce devotion, austerity, and luminous resolve. As Parvati’s aspect, she channels disciplined love that moves mountains and wins Shiva’s heart.

Ritual Uses

Petition for determination, long‑term austerity, yogic discipline, and sacred marriage with purpose.

Offerings

Simple, pure offerings aligned with tapas: water, fruit, and time in meditation.

Favored People

Female ascetics and any practitioner pursuing disciplined spiritual goals.

Petition For

Determination, achieving goals through austerities, marriage, and steadfast devotion.

Warnings

Results arrive through patience, self‑denial, and unwavering focus.

Additional Notes

Venerated with Shiva and independently, especially in Nepal.

History

Austerities and Union

After immense tapas spanning ages, Uma won Shiva’s recognition and became his bride, modeling steadfast spiritual practice.
