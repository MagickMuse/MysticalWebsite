Antu is a creator goddess of the Mesopotamian Babylonian-Akkadian tradition (Iraq). Her known period of worship spans circa 2000 BC, evolving from prehistory, until circa 200 BC.

Centers of Cult

Uruk and Babylon.

Sources

Art references include glyptics and stone carvings. Literary sources include the Babylonian creation epic Enuma Elis and documents relating to the akitu festival.

Origin and Role

Antu is a Babylonian goddess derived from the older Sumerian Ki, though the cosmogony has been altered to suit a separate tradition. She is the consort of Anu, the god of heaven, and was a dominant feature of the Babylonian akitu festival until as recently as 200 BC. Her later preeminence is possibly attributable to identification with the Greek goddess Hera.

Sumerian Sky Cow Form

In early Sumerian iconography she appears as a celestial sky goddess in the form of a cow whose udders produce rain. In the Akkadian pantheon she becomes Antu, also rendered Antum, and is identified as the mother goddess Anunitu, mother of the Anunnaki and Igigi.
