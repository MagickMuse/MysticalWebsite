Saint Peter is renowned for healing through the laying on of hands and the shadow of his presence, as recorded in the Acts of the Apostles.

Classification

Venerated as a saint across virtually all Christian traditions. Principal feast June 29 (shared with Saint Paul). Additional feasts: Chair of Saint Peter (February 22), Saint Peter in Chains (August 1), Confession of Saint Peter (January 18).

Overview

Simon Peter was a Galilean fisherman who became the foremost of Jesus Christ's twelve apostles and the first Pope of the Catholic Church. Known originally as Simon, Jesus renamed him Cephas (Aramaic) or Petros (Greek), both meaning "rock," declaring that upon this rock he would build his church. Peter holds the keys to Heaven, with power to bind and loose on earth and in the spiritual realm. He was martyred in Rome under Emperor Nero, traditionally crucified upside down at his own request, feeling unworthy to die in the same manner as Christ.

Life and Legend

Peter and his brother Andrew were fishermen on the Sea of Galilee when Jesus called them to become "fishers of men." Despite his prominent position among the apostles, Peter famously denied knowing Jesus three times on the night of his arrest, fulfilling Christ's prophecy. After the resurrection, Jesus restored Peter by asking three times if Peter loved him, commissioning him to "feed my sheep."

Peter became the leader of the early Church in Jerusalem before eventually traveling to Rome, where tradition holds he served as the first bishop. His martyrdom occurred during Nero's persecution of Christians following the Great Fire of Rome in 64 CE.

Magical Properties

Saint Peter commands power over all doors, gates, and thresholds—both physical and metaphorical. He can open roads to success, unlock opportunities, and remove obstacles from your path. Conversely, he can close doors against enemies, bind harmful influences, and lock away misfortune. His keys work both ways: he can cross or uncross, bind or loose, open or shut.

In African Diaspora traditions, Saint Peter is syncretized with Eshu-Elegbara, the orisha of crossroads, communication, and fate. Both figures control access and passage, making Saint Peter a powerful ally for those working within syncretic traditions.

Ritual Uses

Saint Peter's Uncrossing Spell

Perform this working on the Vigil of Saint Peter in Chains (evening of July 31) or on any Tuesday:

  1. Obtain an image of Saint Peter, a key, and a black candle
  2. Write your name and situation on paper
  3. Light the candle and petition Saint Peter to unlock your chains and remove all crossed conditions
  4. Pass the key over your body from head to toe, visualizing obstacles falling away
  5. Carry the key with you until the condition is resolved

Saint Peter's Crossing Spell

To close a door against an enemy or harmful situation:

  1. On a Tuesday, obtain a padlock and key, a red candle, and Saint Peter's image
  2. Write the enemy's name or the situation on paper
  3. Light the candle and petition Saint Peter to close all doors to this person/situation
  4. Lock the padlock while speaking your intent
  5. Dispose of the lock in running water or bury it at a crossroads; keep the key hidden

Offerings

Keys (especially antique or iron keys), fish, roosters, red candles, red cloth, images of keys or gates. In syncretic practice, offerings appropriate to Eshu-Elegbara may also be given.

Favored People

Fishermen, popes and clergy, locksmiths, bridge builders, those unjustly imprisoned, those facing closed doors, anyone needing to open or close pathways.

Petition For

Opening doors and opportunities, removing obstacles, uncrossing and hex-breaking, protection, release from bondage or prison, longevity, closing doors against enemies, binding harmful influences.

Warnings

Saint Peter's keys work both ways—be certain of your intent before petitioning him. What he opens cannot easily be closed again, and what he closes may remain shut. Approach with respect and clarity.
