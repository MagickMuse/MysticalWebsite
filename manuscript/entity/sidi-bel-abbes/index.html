Sidi Bel Abbes is renowned for healing vision disorders, restoring eyesight to the blind, and curing leprosy.

Venerated as a Sufi saint, Sidi Bel Abbes (born 1130) is among the most prominent and beloved Moroccan saints. He is one of the Seven Saints of Marrakesh and the patron saint of that city.

Life and Legend

Born in Ceuta, now an autonomous city of Spain on the North African coast, Sidi Bel Abbes came to Marrakesh at age twenty and preached there for forty years. He lived in a hermitage until Sultan Yacoub El Mansour summoned him to teach publicly in the heart of the city.

Magical Properties

Sidi Bel Abbes was a miracle worker whose specialty was restoring eyesight to the blind. No blind person went hungry in Marrakesh when Sidi Bel Abbes was in residence there. He also reputedly cures leprosy. The afflicted traditionally come to live in his sanctuary until they are healed.

Sacred Sites

His zawiya (sanctuary) near the Bab El Khemis, one of the fourteen gates leading into Marrakesh's medina (old city), remains a refuge for the blind and handicapped, as it has been for centuries. Residents are supported by visitors' donations and by Morocco's royal family.

Other Saints Named Sidi Bel Abbes

At least three distinct Moroccan saints share this name:

  • The Muslim patron saint of Marrakesh
  • A Jewish saint also called Rabbi Yehuda, buried in Ksar el-Kabir in northern Morocco
  • Lalla Rivka of the Bene-Moshe (Sons of Moses)

Offerings

Gifts of food, clothing, and money are first dedicated to Sidi Bel Abbes, then immediately distributed to the poor. This can be done from anywhere; no special pilgrimage is required.
