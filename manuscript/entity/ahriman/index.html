Ahriman is the chthonic underworld god of darkness in the Persian Zoroastrian tradition. He is the chief antagonist of Ahura Mazda, the destructive principle who stands opposed to the Wise Lord across the long arc of cosmic struggle.

Names

Ahriman is the later Persian form of the name. The original Zoroastrian name is Angra Mainyu, also rendered Angru Mainyu, meaning evil spirit. Both names mark the same figure, the dark counterpart whose existence frames the dualist structure of Zoroastrian cosmology.

Cosmic Role

Within the Zoroastrian system Ahriman is the source of chaos, lies, and destruction, the force that opposes the cosmic order of asha. Ahura Mazda creates the good. Ahriman generates its opposite. The struggle between them is the structure of the world, and devotees are called to align themselves with Ahura Mazda by choosing good thoughts, words, and deeds.
