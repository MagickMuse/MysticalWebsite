Shamanic Forest Spirits

Vila are beautiful, charming, dancing forest spirits of Central and Eastern Europe and the Balkans. The many variations in the pronunciation of their name indicate the vast territory they inhabit. Vila are shamanic spirits: magickal dancers, skilled healers, herbalists, and teachers of witchcraft. They are guardians of women, animals, and the forest.

Reputation and Reality

Vila are now most famous as punishing spirits: they punish hunters who fail to perform correct spiritual rituals and punish men who betray women or leave them abandoned at the altar. They have developed a reputation as dread spirits. To describe something as giving you the "willies" is to compare it to the shiver of horror Vila evoke. This may be a Christian attempt to discourage their previously widespread veneration. Notably, no legends describe Vila punishing or killing women. Instead, some women join Vila in the forest to dance and receive instruction in herbalism and other magickal arts.

Teachers and Sponsors

Vila are the sponsors of village sorceresses, midwives, and healers. They teach shamanic arts to women as well as the men they favor. They were the sponsors and guardians of the Balkan hero Prince Marko, and depending on the legend, he may have been suckled on Vila's milk in infancy.

The Dance

Vila are literally alluring spirits who prefer passive-aggressive punishment over a sudden strike. Their primary weapon is dance. Vila first seduce men with their beauty and charm, luring them deeper into the forest and encouraging them to join the dance. Eventually, when the Vila tire of the game, those men realize they are literally unable to stop: the Vila dance them to death. Other legends suggest no seduction is needed. Men wandering into the wrong woods suddenly find themselves compulsively dancing. Sometimes dancing has nothing to do with it: other legends describe men who chance upon Vila, become enchanted, fall hopelessly in love, and since the love is unrequited, waste away and die.

That said, Vila will engage in sacred marriages with mortal men if a Vila desires it. Individual Vila have names and unique personalities. Some have been venerated independently, especially in Serbia.

Relationship to Valkyries

Some perceive Vila and Valkyries as cut from the same cloth. They share a resemblance in name and in their associations with death, and both are intensely identified with wolves and swans. Both may descend from primordial swan goddesses. The legends of Vila form the basis for the ballet Giselle.

Manifestation

Vila are incredible shape-shifters, manifesting as swans, horses, snakes, and wolves, but most famously as beautiful, long-haired women. Sometimes they dance naked; sometimes they are dressed in diaphanous white. Bulgarian Vila ride deer using snakes for bridles.

Sacred Sites

Although domestic altars may be built for Vila, offerings and petitions are traditionally brought to them at springs, wells, forest caves, and clearings.
