Overview

Meskhenet, Goddess of Childbirth, protects laboring women and newborn babies. She is a mistress of magic and an expert on charms, spells, and incantations. Meskhenet is a spirit of destiny who may reveal or ordain the child's future at birth. She also assists with rebirth into the afterlife, following a person's death.

Manifestation

She manifests as a beautiful woman wearing a headdress in the form of a cow's uterus. She sometimes appears to laboring women in her guise as the Four Meskhenets: dancing girls. They also dance at Khnum's side, chanting incantations and banishing evil.

Role in Egyptian Magic

The Birth Bricks

Meskhenet's name literally means "bricks"u2014the four sun-baked clay bricks that supported women during labor. Women delivered in a seated or squatting position, using the bricks to squat on or lean against. The four bricks corresponded to the four cardinal directions.

In the tales told to entertain King Khufu, Meskhenet is one of four goddesses who arrive at the house of Redjedet to help deliver her triplets.

The Abydos Birth Brick

A rare decorated birth brick was excavated at Abydos in 2001. Made of mud, its largest surface depicts an elegant woman seated on a throne with an infant, attended by two other women. All three have blue hair (lapis lazuli color signifying divine status). Hathor cow-face emblems frame the scene, and protective figures with blue bodies decorate the edges.

Connection to Judgment and Rebirth

Meskhenet sometimes appears in the form of a brick next to the balance scales in Book of the Dead judgment scenes, emphasizing the connection between birth and rebirth. Birth bricks were placed in the four walls of royal burial chambers during the New Kingdomu2014the conceptual link between a child's birth and the dead's rebirth.

The Four Meskhenets

Meskhenet sometimes appears to laboring women as the Four Meskhenets: dancing girls who chant incantations and banish evil alongside the creator-god Khnum.
