Windigo

Ainu

Wendigo, Widigo

Classification

Dreaded Native North American cannibal spirits, encompassing two interrelated types: spirit Windigos and human Windigos.

Etymology

The name derives from an Algonquian root meaning "evil spirit" and "cannibal." Jesuit missionaries reported Windigo sightings as early as the 1600s.

Manifestation

The spirit Windigo is incredibly tall and gaunt, a spirit of harsh winter, frost, and starvation, with a heart made of ice. Human Windigos do not achieve the immense height of their spirit counterparts but are exceptionally tall, gaunt, and emaciated, usually lacking clothing no matter how far below zero the temperature falls. Windigos may be sensed before they are seen or heard; those in close proximity feel chills, and the spirit's arrival is sometimes accompanied by blizzards. Windigos sometimes travel in packs and allegedly like to play catch or kickball with human skulls.

Magickal Properties

Windigos are always hungry. Their scream paralyzes victims, leaving them unable to escape. The sight of a Windigo is so horrific that many witnesses die of fright on the spot, which is considered the lucky outcome; those who survive the encounter may instead be eaten alive slowly.

Windigos can devour people outright, but the spirit can also possess them. If a Windigo devours you, you die. If a Windigo possesses you, you become a Windigo yourself and join its ranks. Roaming packs may therefore be made up of both spirits and possessed humans, since the same word is used for both. Possession is really more an infection than an occupation; rather than the Windigo spirit entering or manipulating a human from within, the human simply takes on the Windigo's characteristics and behaves exactly like one, preying on people and consuming them. This infection may be acquired involuntarily, through actual physical contact with a Windigo spirit, through being attacked or bitten, or even through dreaming of a Windigo.

Infection may also be sought voluntarily, through ritual: for power, for protection against enemies, to punish others, or because Manitous are believed to possess fonts of hidden wisdom. To pursue this path, a person travels into the forest where Windigos are said to live, fasts for days, and offers themselves. The Windigo may accept the petitioner as its own child, thereby transforming them into a Windigo, or mercifully devour them instead. If adopted and infected, the human transforms: they become perpetually cold, extremely hairy, and develop a craving for human flesh, potentially even devouring their own family.

Windigos tend to go on seasonal rampages and become more active during winter.

Warnings

Possession may be averted by throwing feces at a Windigo, which allegedly causes temporary confusion and allows escape.

Correspondences

Time: winter.
