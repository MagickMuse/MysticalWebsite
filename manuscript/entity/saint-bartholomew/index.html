Saint Bartholomew is renowned for healing nervous disorders, tics, anxiety, and neurological ailments. He also protects from nightmares.

Classification

Venerated as a saint by most branches of Christianity. One of the twelve Apostles.

Epithet

The one "without guile," a spirit of truth.

Life and History

Bartholomew was among Jesus' twelve original disciples. His full Hebrew name was Nathanael bar Tolomai (or Nathanael bar Talom), though why he became known by his patronymic rather than his given name remains unclear. Nathanael, a common name of the era also belonging to a powerful angel, means "Gift of the Lord." Bar means "son."

The meaning of Tolomai or Talom is mysterious:

  • It may be a variant of the Greek name Ptolemy, suggesting a family of Hellenized Jews
  • It resembles a Hebrew word for "ploughman," making him "Nathanael, the ploughman's son"
  • Some theorize a connection to the spirit Ben Temalion, as Ben and Bar are synonyms meaning "son," and Talom and Temalion may be related

Much of Bartholomew's life remains mysterious. He may have authored a gospel and was possibly among the first to bring Christianity to Armenia, the first state to officially adopt the faith. He may have preached in Asia Minor, India, and Ethiopia.

Martyrdom

Bartholomew suffered a brutal death. According to most accounts, he was flayed alive in either Armenia or Azerbaijan before being beheaded or crucified upside down. Coptic tradition holds that he was sewn into a sand-filled sack and drowned at sea, which may connect him to his Iberian manifestation, Saint Bartholomew of the Sea. His feast day has historically been associated with violence, including the 1572 Saint Bartholomew's Day Massacre and the 1349 Mainz Pogrom.

Powers and Domains

Over centuries, Bartholomew developed a reputation as a powerful and generous healer responsible for many miracles, leading to numerous hospitals being named in his honor.

Invoke Saint Bartholomew for:

  • Protection from violence and violent death
  • Safe, successful surgery (light red candles before the procedure; offer larger gifts upon success)
  • Truth revelation, which he may deliver through dreams
  • Protection from nightmares and disturbing dreams
  • Healing of twitching, nervous disorders, tics, anxiety, and neurological ailments
  • Protection from occupational hazards of butchers, cobblers, and tanners, including knife injuries
  • Possible assistance with Tourette's syndrome

Iconography

Bartholomew's imagery is notably graphic, emphasizing his martyrdom by flaying. He may appear with a blood-red body or as a skinless figure holding his own skin, with only his facial features intact.

Attributes

Butcher's knife, tanner's knife, book, flayed human skin.

Color

Red.

Botanical

Sunflower.

Peridot or chrysolite.

Sacred Sites

  • Saint Bartholomew-on-the-Tiber Church, Rome (houses relics)
  • Canterbury Cathedral, England (houses relics)
  • A small church in Cana, Israel, reputedly built over his former home
  • St Bartholomew the Great Church, London: Founded in 1123 by Prior Rahee, formerly King Henry I's jester, who was healed of malaria through a dream vision of the saint while on pilgrimage to Rome. Many testified to healing miracles received here since the 12th century.

Feast Days

  • 11 June (Orthodox)
  • 24 August (Roman Catholic)
  • 11 September (Coptic)
