Andjety is a chthonic underworld god of the Lower Egyptian tradition, a minor deity in anthropomorphic form known from the Pyramid Texts. He is identified with the ninth nome, the district that holds his cult.

Domain

Andjety is responsible for rebirth in the afterlife. He is regarded as a consort of several fertility goddesses, the masculine partner whose role within the cycle of death and renewal pairs him with the goddesses who carry the generative current.

Cult at Busiris

Andjety was revered at Busiris, where he clearly heralded the cult of Osiris. The site that became the great center of Osirian worship first held Andjety, and his presence prepared the ground for the later god whose attributes echo his own.

Attributes

Andjety wears a high conical crown similar to the atef crown of Osiris, decorated with two tall plumes. He carries the crook and flail. In the early Pyramid Texts, the feathers of the crown are replaced by a bicornuate uterus, an anatomical sign that marks his function as a god of rebirth.
