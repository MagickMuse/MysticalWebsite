The Fighter

Aha is a protective spirit whose name means 'fighter' or 'combatant.' He appears prominently on Egyptian birth tusks, hippopotamus ivory wands incised with protective spirits and demi-gods.

Appearance

Aha resembles Bes: a dwarf-like figure with leonine features, often depicted in a protective or threatening stance. On birth tusks, he appears alongside other protective figures including lions, baboons, lion-hippo hybrids, frogs, turtles, and a fantastic winged wildcat with a falcon's head.

A similar female figure with breasts appears on birth tusks and the Abydos birth brick, usually grasping snakes in either hand. This figure may be related to Beset.

Protective Function

The images on birth tusks were not decorative but magickal. They represent a coherent cosmic realm of protective forces that magickal practitioners could invoke during the centuries around 2000-1500 BCE.

While much of this symbolism connects to childbirth, protection against harm was something everyone needed, starting with the smallest and newest members of the community. Aha's 'fighter' nature made him especially suited to defending the vulnerable.
