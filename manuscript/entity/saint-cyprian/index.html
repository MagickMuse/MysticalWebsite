Saint Cyprian is the patron saint of alchemists, witches, wizards, magicians, and occultists.

Classification

Venerated as a saint by most branches of Christianity.

Two Saints, One Identity

Two third-century archbishops named Cyprian died as Christian martyrs:

  • Cyprian of Antioch
  • Cyprian of Carthage

Both are now difficult to distinguish. One or both was a converted pagan magician who renounced sorcery before embracing Christianity. The magician is usually identified as Cyprian of Antioch. Very little historical documentation exists for either saint. Folk tradition has merged them into a single powerful figure.

The Most Magical Saint

Saint Cyprian may be the most magical of all magical saints. Under the Spanish variant of his name, San Cipriano, he is considered the father and patron saint of Iberian magic.

As part of his conversion, Cyprian the magician burned his substantial occult library. Centuries after his death, he apparently regretted this and decided to make amends. According to the preface of The Book of San Cipriano, the saint dictated this grimoire to German monk Jonas Sulfurino in 1000 CE.

Various versions of this grimoire exist in Spanish and Portuguese (no English translation as of this writing). It became incredibly influential throughout Iberia and Latin America. The book is controversial: an old legend warns that reading it backward attracts Satan.

Powers and Domains

Cyprian is all-powerful. There is virtually nothing he cannot do or provide:

  • Healing
  • Locating missing persons and goods (lost or stolen)
  • Acquiring werewolf skills (in certain folk traditions)
  • Protecting magical practitioners
  • Protecting against magical practitioners
  • Banishing unwanted or dangerous spirits (invoking his name allegedly sends mid-level evil entities packing)

Before his conversion, Cyprian allegedly commanded a host of demons as his personal army. Church tradition suggests he may have authored exorcism prayers in the Priest's Prayer Book.

The Conversion Story

Cyprian converted after a love spell he cast for a client failed against the target's purity of faith. This young virgin, Justina of Antioch, defied all his magical skill. He is now invoked to help reconcile lovers.

Iconography

Portrayed dressed as a bishop. May also appear being tortured alongside Saint Justina.

Spiritual Ally

Cyprian is often venerated with Justina of Antioch. Legend suggests they were eventually martyred together.
