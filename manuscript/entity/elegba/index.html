⚠️ Elegba is venerated within Yoruba Indigenous religion and its diasporic expressions including Lucumí/Santería, Candomblé, and Haitian Vodou. These are closed initiatory practices and should only be approached by those within the originating cultures or with explicit cultural permission.

Elegba (also known as Eshu, Exu, Legba, or Papa Legba depending on the tradition) is a foundational spirit in the Yoruba religion and its diaspora expressions, Santería, Candomblé, Vodou, and related practices.

He is the master of crossroads, doorways, and communication. No ritual in Yoruba-derived traditions begins without first honoring Elegba, as he controls access to all other spirits and to the divine. He is a trickster, clever, unpredictable, and not bound by ordinary rules.

Syncretism

In nations shaped by both Spanish colonialism and the African Diaspora, Elegba became syncretized with the Holy Child of Atocha, a Catholic folk saint depicted as a richly dressed child. Whether the Holy Child is the Christ Child or Elegba wearing a mask is a matter of genuine theological discussion within these traditions.

For practitioners who prefer to work directly with Elegba rather than through the syncretic Catholic image, his traditional form is a concrete head with cowrie shell eyes and ears, available at spiritual supply stores.

In Court Case Workings

As the spirit who controls roads and access, Elegba can be petitioned to open favorable paths in legal proceedings, delay or prevent lawsuits, and create unexpected openings in situations that seem closed.

How to Work With Him

  • Place his image facing the front door, or just behind it
  • Offer rum and a fine cigar
  • Best day: Monday
