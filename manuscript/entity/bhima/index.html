Bhima, whose name means the terrible, is a warrior god of the Hindu Epic and Puranic tradition. He appears in the Mahabharata as one of the princely sons of the mythical Pandu family, and he stands among the central heroes of the epic. His strength is understood as the might of the wind made flesh, and he is counted a son of Vayu, the god of the winds.

Domains

Immense physical strength, martial ferocity, the fierce willingness to strike where a gentler brother would hesitate, and the wind-born energy that breaks obstacles by sheer force.

Iconography

Bhima is usually depicted wielding a sword and a club, with the club counted as his signature attribute. His figure is that of a heavily built warrior whose stature and musculature answer to his reputation for overwhelming power.

Character and Kinship

Bhima is perceived as a god of immense strength and great cruelty, and this cruelty is what separates him from the heroic figure of Arjuna, his brother, with whom he is linked throughout the epic. Where Arjuna carries the silvery poise of the archer, Bhima carries the raw ferocity of the storm; the two brothers together represent complementary poles of martial excellence within the Pandu family.

Petition For

Immense physical strength, courage in overwhelming odds, and the ferocious energy needed to break through what cannot be moved by ordinary force.

Precautions

His strength is answered by his cruelty. Approach him for tasks that call for direct, overwhelming force rather than for matters that require restraint or subtlety, and hold clear intent before invoking his ferocity.
