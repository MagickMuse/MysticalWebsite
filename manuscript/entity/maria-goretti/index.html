Maria Goretti (16 October 1890 to 6 July 1902) was a Roman Catholic saint, the daughter of poor Italian sharecroppers. Her father died of malaria when she was nine, plunging the family deeper into poverty. At age eleven, she was attacked by their landlord's son, twenty-year-old Alessandro Serenelli, who tried to rape her. She resisted and was stabbed fourteen times. Maria survived for twenty hours after emergency surgery performed without anesthesia, during which time she publicly forgave her attacker.

Serenelli served thirty years of hard labor. Six years into his imprisonment, Maria appeared to him in a vision, offering flowers. He repented, experienced a religious conversion, and joined the Capuchin Order as a lay brother after his release on Christmas Day 1928. He prayed to Maria daily, calling her his "little saint," and testified on behalf of her canonization.

In 1950, Maria's killer joined her mother and a crowd of half a million people when Maria became the youngest officially canonized saint in the modern process. The canonization was held in Saint Peter's Square to accommodate the crowds. Pope Pius XII described her as a model and protector for young girls.

Domains

Maria Goretti is petitioned for help with lying, cheating, and abusive spouses. Allegedly, she encourages them to behave.

Iconography

Fourteen lilies symbolize the fourteen stab wounds she endured. The knife represents the weapon used in her attack.

Attributes

Fourteen lilies, knife.

Colors

Pink.

Sacred Sites

Her shrine is located at the Santuario di Santa Maria Goretti in Nettuno, Italy.

Lore

Some survivors of rape feel bitterness toward Maria Goretti's canonization. For years, educators distorted her story, using her as an example to teach that death was preferable to loss of virginity. Maria Goretti was canonized for the saintly act of forgiving her killer and for miracles attributed to her after death, not for resisting rape or preserving her virginity.
