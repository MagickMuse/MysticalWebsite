Rukmini is the principal wife and queen of Krishna, considered an avatar of Lakshmi and one of the Ashtabharyas (eight principal queens of Krishna). Unlike the illicit love of Radha, Rukmini's relationship to Krishna is that of the devoted, righteous wife — chaste, loyal, and divinely ordained.

Overview

Rukmini is the daughter of King Bhishmaka of Vidarbha. She is deeply in love with Krishna but is betrothed by her brother Rukmi to the king Shishupala, whom she does not love. To prevent this unwanted marriage, Rukmini sends a secret letter to Krishna asking him to abduct her on the day of her wedding — a bold and unconventional act for a princess, driven entirely by love and devotion. Krishna obliges, arriving on her wedding day and carrying her away to Dwaraka, where they marry.

Rukmini is described as extraordinarily beautiful, deeply devoted to Krishna, and possessed of great intelligence and wisdom. She is his most beloved queen and the mistress of his palace at Dwaraka. In contrast to Radha — whose love for Krishna is illicit, adulterous, and characterized by aching separation — Rukmini's love is the love of the faithful queen: constant, dignified, and secure.

As Avatar of Lakshmi

Rukmini is widely identified as an avatar of Sri-Lakshmi, the goddess of prosperity, good fortune, and wifely devotion. Just as Lakshmi eternally accompanies Vishnu, Rukmini eternally accompanies his avatar Krishna. Her role is the divine feminine counterpart to Krishna's royal and cosmic nature — she is sovereignty and auspiciousness personified.

Relation to Radha

In the Ramayana-era mythology in which Krishna is primarily a royal and heroic figure, Rukmini is his supreme consort. In later devotional traditions centered on the young, pastoral Krishna of Vraja, however, Radha supersedes Rukmini in theological and emotional importance. Where Rukmini represents the queen and lawful wife, Radha represents the beloved — the one who loves without law, obligation, or security. The two form complementary archetypes of wifely devotion and ecstatic longing within the Krishna tradition.

Source

Kinsley, David. Hindu Goddesses: Visions of the Divine Feminine in the Hindu Religious Tradition. University of California Press, 1986.
