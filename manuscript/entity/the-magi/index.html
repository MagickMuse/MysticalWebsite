"In the time of King Herod, after Jesus was born in Bethlehem of Judea, wise men from the East came to Jerusalem, asking, 'Where is the child who has been born king of the Jews? For we observed his star at its rising, and have come to pay him homage.'"

This excerpt from the Gospel of Matthew is the only biblical reference to the Magi. They appear in no other Gospel. The English translation "wise men" is a euphemism for the original word magi, the plural of magus, meaning magician or sorcerer. The historical magi are so closely identified with magical arts that the word "magic" derives from their name.

Origins

The original magi were a learned class of Medes and Persians who practiced magickal arts and indigenous Iranian spiritual traditions. The magickal art most associated with them is astrology. The biblical Magi may have used astrological charts rather than literally following a star.

Different traditions suggest different identities:

  • Western Church: Balthazar, Melchior, and Gaspar
  • Ethiopian Orthodox: Kesad, Awnsom, and Albetar
  • Iranian tradition: Claims the city of Saba, near modern Tehran, as their hometown
  • Ethiopian tradition: Identifies them as Men of Divination, descendants of Zoroaster

The standard Western legend suggests one king came from Africa, one from Asia, and one from Europe. In Puerto Rico, they are known as Los Reyes Magos (the Wizard Kings or Sorcerer Kings).

Magickal Connections

In Vodou, the Magi are syncretized to the snake spirit Simbi, renowned as a great magician. They are invoked in many folk magick spells.

Spell to Locate Missing Persons or Objects

  1. Inscribe their names (Balthazar, Caspar, Melchior) into a flat beeswax tablet
  2. Place the wax beneath your pillow when you go to sleep
  3. Pay attention to your dreams for revelations about locations or perpetrators

Protection Spell

On Epiphany Eve (January 5), write the initials C, M, B on your door to protect against malevolent spellcraft. These initials form an acronym for Christus mundum benedicat ("Christ blesses the world") and provide spiritual protection. Holy Water may be most effective for writing.

Attributes

The Magi bore gifts of gold, frankincense, and myrrh. The historical magi caste is also identified by the baresman (or barsom), a bundle of twigs used in Zoroastrian rituals.

Botanical

Star of Bethlehem (Ornithogalum spp.), a primary component in Rescue Remedy.

Ritual

A King Cake is traditionally served on their feast. A baby-shaped toy or small object is baked into the cake. Whoever receives the slice with the charm is crowned king or queen of the feast, or must provide next year's cake and party.

In Spanish-speaking countries, January 6 is El Día de Reyes. Children receive gifts on this day rather than Christmas, leaving beverages for the kings and grass and water for their mounts the night before.

Sacred Sites

  • Marco Polo described seeing the graves of Magi in Saba, Iran
  • Legend says Saint Helena discovered their bodies in Jerusalem, transporting them to Constantinople, then Milan, and finally Cologne
  • Their relics are reputedly housed in a golden sarcophagus (the largest reliquary in the Western world) in Cologne Cathedral, containing three golden-crowned skulls
