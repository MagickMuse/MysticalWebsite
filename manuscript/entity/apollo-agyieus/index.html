Overview

Apollo Agyieus ("Apollo of the Streets" or "Apollo of the Roads") is a specific cultic aspect of Apollo as a guardian of doorways, house entrances, and streets. The name derives from the Greek agyia ("street" or "road").

In ancient Greece, small shrines to Apollo Agyieus were placed just outside the front doors of homes, alongside similar shrines to Hermes and Hekate. This trio of liminal deities was understood to guard the threshold, ensuring good things entered and bad things were kept out.

Sacred Form

Apollo Agyieus was typically represented not as a human figure but as a conical stone pillar or post set at the entrance to a home or street. This aniconic form emphasizes his function as a boundary marker and threshold guardian rather than a personality cult.

Role in the Home

  • Guards the front entrance and doorway of the home
  • Part of the liminal spirit trio of the ancient Greek household (with Hermes and Hekate)
  • Associated with streets, roads, and the movement of people and spirits across thresholds
  • Protects against malevolent forces entering through the door

Modern Practice

You may honor Apollo Agyieus by placing his image (a picture, holy card, or statue) near your front door or window and anointing it with:

  • An oil dedicated to Apollo
  • A protection condition oil
  • Regular olive oil you have prayed over yourself

Keep the image waterproof (laminated or framed) so it can be regularly anointed.
