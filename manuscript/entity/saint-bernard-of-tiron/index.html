Classification

Roman Catholic saint, founder of the Tironian Order of monks.

Life and History

Saint Bernard of Tiron (c. 1046 to 25 April 1117) was a French monk and hermit who founded the Tironian Order, also known as the Order of Tiron or the Grey Monks.

Early Life

Born in Abbeville (or Ponthieu) in northern France, Bernard entered monastic life at a young age. He became known for his strict asceticism and desire for a more austere form of religious life than was common in his time.

Founding of Tiron

After periods as a hermit and itinerant preacher, Bernard founded the Abbey of Tiron in 1109 in the forests near Chartres, France. The order he established emphasized manual labor, poverty, and withdrawal from worldly affairs.

Miracles

Bernard was known for miraculous deeds during his lifetime. Most notably, he appeared alongside Mary Magdalen to rescue Saint Adjutor from imprisonment in Jerusalem. Together, they lifted Adjutor (chains and all) and flew him back to France.

Feast Day

April 14 (some sources list April 25)

Legacy

The Tironian Order spread throughout France, Britain, and Ireland. The abbey at Tiron became an important center of monastic reform.
