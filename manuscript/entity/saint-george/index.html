Saint George is the patron saint of assassins (in Brazilian folk tradition), soldiers, and knights.

Saint George is renowned for healing STDs (syphilis, herpes), leprosy, mental illness, and fibromyalgia. He also heals abscesses, cysts, and anything requiring lancing.

Classification

Venerated in virtually all branches of Christianity.

Overview

Saint George lancing the dragon is among the most recognizable motifs of the Western world. He is among the most beloved of all saints and is venerated by virtually every branch of Christianity. George is also among those saints who have both a conventional Christian identity and a magical alter-ego. There is no historical documentation regarding Saint George whatsoever.

The Legend

According to legend, George was an early fourth-century Christian soldier who came to a Libyan city terrorized by a dragon lurking in surrounding marshes. The dragon was offered a new virgin daily, dressed in bridal gowns as if collecting a harem. When the lot fell to the king's daughter, George rode into town on his white horse, killed the dragon, and the grateful citizens converted to Christianity. He was later martyred in Palestine for refusing to sacrifice to Apollo.

Legend says George's birthplace was Lydda (now Lod, Israel), very close to Jaffa, where the mythical Perseus slew the water dragon that menaced Andromeda.

Origins of the Dragon Motif

George was not originally a dragon-killer. His earliest iconography shows him without the dragon. The prototype may derive from Egyptian hero deity Horus on horseback spearing Set as a crocodile, or Greek Apollo slaying Typhon or the Delphic python. The image may be interpreted as Christianity (shining knight) victorious over Paganism (loathsome worm) or over Satan himself.

  • A ninth-century Georgian icon depicts George's horse trampling Emperor Diocletian
  • In Russian folk religion, the dragon he battles is explicitly identified with witch goddess Baba Yaga

Saint George's Eve Magic

George is among the saints most associated with magic and witchcraft. The eve of his feast is among the most potent times for divination and spell-casting. In Bram Stoker's Dracula, a woman warns Jonathan Harker: "It is the eve of St. George's Day. Tonight, when the clock strikes midnight, all the evil things in the world will have full sway."

In Hungary, Saint George's Eve is when witches revel. If you went to a crossroads on Saint George's Day, you met all the local witches collecting dew with long sheets, later bottled for potions. In Romania, spectral blue lights visible only on Saint George's Eve allegedly reveal buried treasure.

George's True Love Vision Ritual

  1. Go to a remote crossroads on Saint George's Eve
  2. Comb your hair backward
  3. Prick the smallest finger on your left hand with a needle and allow three drops of blood to fall on the earth
  4. You may receive an immediate vision; if not, go home silently without turning around
  5. Go to sleep. Your love should appear in your dreams. Do not speak until you have recorded your dream.

Two Georges

In Romania, there are two Saint Georges:

  • Saint George, venerated in church on his feast day
  • Green George, who presides over fertility rituals on Saint George's Eve

Russia also acknowledges two who are simultaneously one:

  • Egorii the Brave, Dragon Slayer and Upholder of Christianity
  • Springtime Egorii, who unlocks frozen earth after winter and protects cattle and horses

In Russian folk religion, George rules all animals, including reptiles. Invoke him for protection from snakes or alligators.

Syncretism

  • George is identified with the Green Man, epitomizing the primordial male generative force
  • Muslims identify him with Al Khidr
  • In African Diaspora traditions, he is identified with the warrior spirit Ogun
  • Many practitioners believe that beneath George's saintly mask lies the ancient spirit Baal, one of several saints whose magickal behavior diverges significantly from official hagiography
  • In Barcelona, La Diada de Sant Jordi (April 23) is like Valentine's Day: lovers exchange roses and books

Healing Powers

Classified among the Fourteen Holy Helpers, George heals sexually transmitted diseases (syphilis, herpes), leprosy, mental illness (Coptic and Eastern Churches), fibromyalgia, abscesses, cysts, and anything needing lancing. He banishes evil spirits and heals and preserves animal health.

Iconography

  • Handsome knight on white horse killing a prone dragon (Western)
  • Coptic image: on horseback with small figure behind him holding coffee pot (page, servant, or rescued princess)
  • The Black George icon (British Museum) shows him on a black horse, possibly indicating his magical guise
  • George is virtually always depicted on a white horse

Apparition

An apparition of Saint George leading an army of ghostly bowmen appeared at the Battle of Mons (WWI) supporting British forces. Similar legends from Georgia describe a knight on a white horse descending from the skies to lead armies.

Attributes

Red cross on white background (Cross of Saint George), lance, dragon, princess, white horse.

Colors

Red, white, green.

Sacred Plants

Nettles, harebell.

Feast Days

  • April 23 (Western; preceding eve is magical)
  • May 1 (Coptic), May 5 (Orthodox Syrian), May 6 (some Eastern)
  • November 3 (Russian); Ethiopian Church: 23rd of each month
  • Trinity Sunday in Mons, Belgium (dragon battle reenacted)

Sacred Sites

An exceptionally large number of churches are dedicated to George: 41 in Moscow alone, 365 in Georgia (one per day), 350+ Coptic churches. Key pilgrimage sites include:

  • Church of Saint George in Lod, Israel (his tomb)
  • Saint George's Cathedral in Jerusalem
  • Monastery of Saint George in Old Cairo (miraculous iron collar and chains that heal mental illness)
  • Saint George Bay in Beirut (where he slew the dragon)
  • Grotto of Saint George in Jounieh Bay, Lebanon (where he cleaned his sword; waters enhance fertility)
  • Saint George's Chapel in Windsor Castle, England
  • Church of Saint George (Beta Ghiorghis) in Lalibela, Ethiopia

Spiritual Allies

Russian legend suggests George is the miraculous baby brother of Faith, Hope, and Charity. Also venerated with Michael Archangel, who comforted him during martyrdom.
