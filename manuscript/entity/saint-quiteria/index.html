Saint Quiteria is renowned for healing and protecting against rabies, dog bites, and dog attacks.

Saint Quiteria was once widely venerated throughout France, Spain, and Portugal. The city of Santa Quiteria, Brazil, is named after her. No documented information about her exists beyond her name and the fact of her veneration.

Domains

Quiteria protects against rabies and dog attacks. According to legend, she once kept two lunging rabid dogs at bay using only the sound of her voice.

Iconography

Quiteria climbs a mountain or emerges from the sea, carrying her severed head in her hands. When depicted with her head intact, she is usually shown with a dog.

Colors

Red, Pink

Sacred Sites

The Church of Sainte-Quiterie in Aire-Sur-l'Adour, France, once housed her relics before Huguenots scattered them. The church sits on the Compostela pilgrimage route. In Zubiri, a Basque town also on the pilgrimage route, the Gothic stone bridge over the River Arga is called the Puente de la Rabia (Bridge of Rabies). Local tradition holds that Quiteria's relics are contained within the bridge's central pier, and walking an animal over or around it bestows immunity to rabies or heals the disease.

Lore

According to legend, Quiteria was the eldest of nine sisters known as the Nine Holy Twins, born in second-century Minho (now Portugal). Their mother gave them up at birth expecting them to be killed, but they were hidden and raised as Christians. When reunited with their parents as young women, conflict arose. The sisters were arrested but escaped, liberating other prisoners and splitting into groups to lead guerrilla warfare against the Romans. All were eventually caught and martyred at different times and places.

Quiteria's name, 'Kuteria,' means 'the red one' in Phoenician and was a title of the goddess Astarte. The similarity to Aphrodite Cythera (Kythera) has led some to theorize that Quiteria represents a Christianized form of the goddess, or that her legend preserves memories of armed Phoenician or Celtic resistance against Rome.
