Saint Nectan of Hartland (died c. 510 CE) is a Celtic Christian saint, one of twenty-four children allegedly fathered by the Welsh king Brychan. Nectan is a cephalophore, a saint who carried his own head after beheading.

Life and History

Nectan became a hermit in North Devon, England, living in a forest clearing near a waterfall and spring. He kept one cow, surviving from her milk. When thieves rustled her, Nectan attempted to convert them to better lives. In response, they killed him, cutting off his head. According to legend, Nectan picked up his head and walked half a mile to his hermitage, where he died.

The Foxglove Miracle

Foxgloves now grow wherever drops of his blood fell on that final walk. Foxgloves remain associated with Saint Nectan. Every year on his feast day, foxgloves are placed on his old well.

Domains

Saint Nectan is invoked for healing and protection, especially at sacred wells. Believers still visit Saint Nectan's Well for healing purposes.

Sacred Sites

Saint Nectan's Glen, near Tintagel, Cornwall, is a place of tremendous psychic phenomena, including ghostly apparitions and spirits. In legend, Nectan's silver bell still hangs in the waterfall. It was, and allegedly still is, a harbinger of doom: hearing it ring heralds death. Some believe that Nectan was associated with this sacred space long before Christian times and that he was named for the Celtic sea god Nechtan.

Another spring associated with Saint Nectan exists within the churchyard at Welcombe in North Devon, not far from where he allegedly lived. Believed to have healing powers, it was a pilgrimage destination during medieval times.

Feast Day

June 17

Iconography

Saint Nectan is depicted as a hermit, sometimes shown:

  • Carrying his own severed head
  • Near his waterfall and spring
  • Surrounded by foxgloves
  • With his silver bell
