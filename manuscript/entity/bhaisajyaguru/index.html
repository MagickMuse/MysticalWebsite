Bhaisajyaguru, whose name means "the supreme physician," is a physician god of the Buddhist and Lamaist traditions, especially venerated in Tibet. He is counted among the medicine buddhas known collectively as sMan-bla in Tibetan practice, and in Lamaist tradition he holds the position of the fifth in the series of manushi-buddhas. His work is the healing of the body, the soothing of the mind, and the cure of the deeper illness of ignorance from which all suffering arises.

Domains

Healing of body and mind, the cure of physical illness and spiritual affliction, and the compassionate release of beings from the roots of suffering.

Iconography

Bhaisajyaguru is typically depicted with the stretched earlobes and gently smiling countenance shared with other buddhas, and a row of small tight curls fringes the forehead beneath the ushnisha, the crown of enlightenment. His skin is colored either deep blue, the lapis-lazuli shade for which he is celebrated, or gold. His attributes are fruit, especially the myrobalan, the healing fruit of Buddhist medicine, sometimes held together with a bowl in which the medicine is offered.

Petition For

Relief from illness, mental peace during recovery, guidance for healers and physicians, and the deeper healing of the roots of suffering.
