Saint Donato is the patron saint of bakers.

Saint Donato is renowned for healing epilepsy, seizure disorders, and conditions characterized as "fits."

Classification

Roman Catholic saint.

Life and Legend

Donato was a fourth-century bishop, possibly originally from Ireland, who aggressively proselytized in Arezzo and throughout Tuscany. According to one legend, he was beheaded on August 7, 301 CE atop a hill where a cathedral now stands. His head rolled downhill, and where it stopped, a pieve (rural church with baptistery) was built.

However, his history is murky. Several Saint Donatos exist and their legends may be conflated. Various sources place him in the second, fourth, fifth, or sixth century. He's rumored to have been a childhood friend of Emperor Julian, who tried to reinstate Paganism.

Miracles

  • Resurrected a woman named Euphrosina
  • Restored sight to a blind woman named Syriana
  • Exorcised demons tormenting the local prefect's son
  • Most famously, killed a dragon that had poisoned a local well

The Magical Saint

Donato's legends likely combine historical details with elements from a syncretized pagan spirit. He is intensely associated with epilepsy, which is traditionally considered his method of communication. He "owns" the disease, determining who suffers from it.

Mystically understood, epileptic episodes, especially sudden onset, may be Donato's attempts to contact the sufferer. Once communication is established and his "call" answered, the ailment is believed to be relieved.

Powers and Domains

Though strongly associated with epilepsy, many devotees don't suffer from it. Donato heals rabies, paralysis, seizure disorders, and any condition characterized as a "fit." He nullifies the Evil Eye and protects against curses and malevolent witchcraft.

Iconography

Holds or stands atop a crescent moon; portrayed battling a dragon.

Attributes

Chalice, crescent moon, sword, dragon.

Sacred Animal

Frogs.

Amulet

His image is frequently incorporated into Italian amulets. A popular design places his image atop a crescent moon inscribed with the number 13. Though often considered unlucky, wearing 13 as an amulet allegedly transforms it into a vehicle of good luck. Thirteen is also the number of months in a lunar year.

Sacred Sites

  • Santa Maria della Pieve in Arezzo houses his relics (built 12th century, probably atop a site once dedicated to Mercury)
  • Church of Santa Maria e Donato on Murano Island near Venice also holds some relics, along with remains of the dragon he killed, brought by returning Crusaders

Feast Day

7 August
