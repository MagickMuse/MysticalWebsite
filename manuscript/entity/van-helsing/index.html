Van Helsing is the learned Dutch professor and physician who appears in the Gothic vampire tradition as the bulwark between the predatory dead and the living. He is petitioned for the protection of the sick, the warding of bedrooms and thresholds, and the marshalling of folk apotropaics in cases of supernatural attack.

Classification

Legendary figure of the Dutch and wider Gothic European tradition. A literary character who has accreted folkloric weight, treated in practice as a guardian of the sickbed and a teacher of warding rite.

Nature of the Figure

He stands at the intersection of medicine and magick. He carries the bearing of a physician, the temper of a scholar, and the seriousness of a folk practitioner. He does not jest when warding is in progress, and he expects the same gravity from those he is helping.

Method

His signature work is the garlic ward. He fastens the windows. He takes handfuls of fresh garlic flowers and rubs them across every window sash so that no breath of air enters without the garlic scent. He rubs the door jamb above, below, and at each side. He rubs around the fireplace. Last, he weaves a wreath of garlic and places it himself around the throat of the one he is protecting. The patient is instructed to keep the wreath undisturbed and to open neither window nor door until morning.

Iconography

An older man with an iron jaw and bushy brows, often portrayed bearing a wisp of garlic flowers, sometimes with a crucifix or a small case of physician's tools at hand.

Ritual

See Garlic Wreath at the Sickbed.

Lore

He is summoned to a young woman pale and failing, with two small wounds at the throat and a hunger for sleep that brings dread rather than rest. He arrives with garlic flowers telegraphed in from a friend's glasshouses. He fixes the wreath around her neck. That night she writes that the smell brings peace, that she does not dread being alone, that she does not mind the flapping at the window. The ward holds for as long as it is kept whole.

Petition

Call on Van Helsing when a sleeper is troubled by visitations, when a household feels exposed at night, or when a sickroom needs sealing against intrusion of any kind. Speak to him plainly. Make the wreath yourself if you can. Treat the work as obedience, and silence as part of obedience.
