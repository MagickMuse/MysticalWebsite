Magickal Properties

Dhumavati is surrounded by the apocalyptic smoke of the burning universe and represents the goddess as widow. She is the matron goddess of beggars, lepers, the ailing and miserable, and those who are abandoned, overlooked, forgotten, and marginalized. She is an angry, raging, resentful goddess who is not afraid of the apocalypse because life as it exists holds no hope. She is clever, quick-witted, quick-tempered, and volatile, an expert on the arts of survival.

Ritual Uses

Invoke Dhumavati during wartime or disasters for help surviving and even thriving. She is petitioned by devotees so that they will be the ones who benefit from disasters. Call upon her when desperate and really down-and-out. She can be propitiated to avoid falling into desperate states.

Offerings

Consistent offerings are crucial, she is acquisitive and hungry and never forget or delay her offerings. Light incense for her, but also give her food and drink. Give her whatever you're having because if you don't she'll think you're hoarding the good stuff.

Favored People

Beggars, lepers, the ailing and miserable, people who are sick and not getting better, widows, orphans, and inconvenient, needy, unpleasant relatives.

Petition For

Survival during disasters, help when desperate and down-and-out, cursing enemies, thriving in poverty and defeat, assistance when life holds no hope.

Warnings

She is acquisitive, resentful, and always feels like she's being shortchanged. Never forget or delay her offerings. She evokes the power of poverty, defeat, and despair and is invoked for cursing.

As a Mahavidya

Dhumavati is one of the ten Mahavidyas, the great wisdom goddesses of the Hindu Tantric tradition. She is the only widow among them, the only goddess in the group who exists without a male consort. In a tradition where widowhood is inauspicious and socially marginal, this makes her theologically distinctive. She embodies the goddess stripped of all conventional feminine virtues: youth, beauty, fertility, and the support of a husband.

Her iconographic description is precise: pale complexion, tall, stern, unsmiling expression, dressed as a widow in dirty clothes, disheveled hair, no teeth, long pendulous breasts, afflicted with hunger and thirst, large crooked nose, quarrelsome nature, holding a winnowing fan, riding a crow. This is the goddess as survivor of apocalypse, the one who remains when everything else has burned.

Within the Mahavidya origin myth she is one of the ten forms Sati assumes when she surrounds and terrifies Shiva. The Mahavidyas collectively demonstrate the Devi's overwhelming autonomous power. Dhumavati contributes the specific terror of the inauspicious, the unlucky, the hungry, and the forgotten: the aspects of existence that cannot be wished away.

Additional Notes

She is old, tall, and very thin with very few teeth left, perhaps one reason why she's always hungry. She's always thirsty too. Her sacred bird is carrion crows who pull her chariot. She haunts ruins, old battlefields, bombed-out markets, and other scenes of devastation.
