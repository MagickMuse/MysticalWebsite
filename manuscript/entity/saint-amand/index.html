Classification

Roman Catholic and Orthodox saint.

Life and History

Saint Amand (born c. 584 in Poitou, France; died c. 675 in Elnone, now Belgium) became a monk at age twenty, much against the wishes of his wealthy, noble French family. Fearing he was under the influence of a cult, they kidnapped him and attempted the seventh-century version of deprogramming. It did not work. Amand returned to his vocation and spent the next fifteen years living in a cell, subsisting on bread and water.

Missionary Work

Sent to France as a missionary, Amand aggressively converted Pagans. He apparently had little luck until he resurrected a hanged criminal, earning a reputation as a miracle worker. He then became a prominent churchman.

Conflict with Royalty

When he criticized the Merovingian king Dagobert I on moral grounds, he was summarily kicked out of France. Dagobert later apologized and requested that Amand come serve him at court. Amand declined the honor.

Later Life

He founded over twenty monasteries and died peacefully in bed at approximately ninety years old.

Sacred Site

Given a large land grant in Elnone on the banks of the River Scarpe near some mineral hot springs, Amand built an abbey. After his death, the town was renamed Saint-Amand-les-Eaux.

Feast Days

1 February and 6 February
