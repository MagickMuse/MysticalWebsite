Birgitta of Sweden is a Roman Catholic saint.

Life and History

Birgitta (1303-1373) came from a wealthy Swedish noble family with royal connections on her mother's side. For a time she served as chief lady-in-waiting to the Swedish queen. She married young, raised eight children (including the future Saint Catherine of Sweden), and earned a reputation for exceptional generosity and kindness.

Visions

Ecstatic visions visited Birgitta from childhood and intensified as she aged. Though recognized as a holy woman in her later years, her youthful piety and visionary experiences drew mockery rather than reverence. She eventually renounced her position as princess because it interfered with her spiritual calling.

Later Life

After her husband died, Birgitta dedicated herself entirely to spiritual pursuits. She traveled to Rome seeking papal approval for her monastic order, the Briggittines. Roman society embraced her warmly, and she remained there (apart from pilgrimages) until her death on July 23, 1373. The Church canonized her in 1391, barely two decades later.

Powers and Domains

Those overwhelmed by visionary experiences may seek her guidance. She helps distinguish between benevolent and malevolent spirits. Some traditions hold that she can reveal to petitioners the exact time of their death.

Iconography

Depicted as an abbess wearing Briggittine robes.

Attributes

Pen and book (often shown recording her visions), pilgrim's staff.

Sacred Sites

  • Vadstena Abbey in Sweden, the Briggittine motherhouse built to Birgitta's own specifications and containing her relics
  • The Brigitta Chapel in Vienna

Feast Days

  • 23 July
  • 7 October
