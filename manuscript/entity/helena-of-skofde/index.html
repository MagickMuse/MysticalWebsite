⚖️Accusations❤️‍🩹Healing

Helena of Skofde, a Roman Catholic saint, is renowned for healing, particularly restoring sight to the blind. Healing springs spontaneously appeared at her murder site and wherever her floating coffin touched the shore.

Life

Helena (c. 1101-1160) was a noble widow in Sweden. She had a daughter who married an abusive, violent man. When he beat his wife, their servants intervened, prompting the husband to attack them. Soon after, the husband was killed. His relatives accused Helen of playing a part in his death and sought revenge.

Helen got out of town by going on a yearlong pilgrimage to Jerusalem. Upon her return, she was killed in an ambush near Skofde on 31 July.

Alternative Legend

Helena departed on her pilgrimage before her son-in-law's death, a grueling journey for an older woman. Her son-in-law's servants, suspected of his murder and never expecting Helena to return, accused her of instigating the murder to shift blame.

Miracles

A blind man led by a child soon passed the murder site. The child, seeing a rosebush strangely filled with light, stopped and saw a severed finger wearing a pilgrim's ring from Jerusalem. The blind man, putting out his hands, touched Helena's blood. When he next touched his eyes, his sight miraculously returned. A spring, known as Helen's Spring, spontaneously gushed out at the site.

Another legend describes how Helena's body was placed in a stone coffin that refused to sink. Wherever it bumped the shore, another healing spring appeared.

Helena was canonized in 1164. In 1596, a Lutheran archbishop attempted to seal off Helena's Spring. Piles of dirt were dumped in it, but water continually gushed forth.

Lifetime Charity

During her lifetime, Helena was renowned for her kindness and generosity to the poor. Following her husband's death, she donated most of her possessions to the poor and built the Church at Skofde using her own funds.

Attributes

A severed finger and a sword.

Feast Day

  • July 31

Sacred Sites

  • Saint Helena's Church in Skofde (relics removed during Protestant Reformation)
  • Various springs, including one on the coast near Kattegat where Midsummer's Eve festivities are traditionally celebrated
