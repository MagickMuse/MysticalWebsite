Baal Samin, whose name means "lord of heaven," is the head of the Phoenician pantheon and a Western Semitic god of rain and vegetation, likely originating in Canaanite culture.

Domains

Rain, vegetation, thunder, the heavens, and storms at sea. He also serves as a patron deity of mariners.

Iconography

On Seleucid coinage he is depicted wearing a half-moon crown and carrying a radiate sun disc.

Sacred Sites

His major sanctuary stood at Byblos, according to inscription built by Yehemilk. At Karatepe his name appears at the head of a list of national deities. His cult spread as far apart as Cyprus and Carthage.

Lore

Baal Samin is first mentioned in a fourteenth-century BCE treaty between the Hittite king Suppiluliuma and Nigmadu II of Ugarit. His cult persisted into the time of Solomon. By the Hellenic period he was equated with Zeus in the Greek pantheon, and the Romans identified him as Caelus, god of the sky. He is closely equated with the local tutelary deities Baal Malage and Baal Sapon, both Phoenician forms of the same figure.
