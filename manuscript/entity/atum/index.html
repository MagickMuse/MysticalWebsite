Encyclopedia Source Notes

Sun god and creator god of the Egyptian tradition. Worship is attested from the Old Kingdom circa 2700 BC through the end of Egyptian history circa 400 AD. He is also known as Atum-Re or Re-Atum. His chief cult center is Heliopolis. Art references include wall paintings, especially in New Kingdom tombs in the Valley of the Kings at Thebes, alongside votive inscriptions and contemporary sculpture. Literary sources include the Pyramid Texts, coffin texts, and the Book of the Dead.

Atum is one of several interpretations of the major creator god of Egypt, whose company reflects the fragmented pre-Dynastic tribal history of the country. He shared Heliopolis with another sun god, Re, and eventually fused with him as Atum-Re or Re-Atum. He was self-created from the primeval ocean, and by masturbation he produced the next two great deities of the Egyptian cosmos, Shu and Tefnut, who in turn begin the pantheon of nine Heliopolis deities known as the Ennead. He is generally shown in human form and often wears a crown that combines those of Upper and Lower Egypt. He is also represented as various animals including the bull, lion, snake, and lizard. He was regarded as the progenitor of the Egyptian pharaohs. Both Atum and Re are represented by a divine black bull, Mnevis or Mer-wer, who wears the sun disc and uraeus serpent between its horns and acts as an intercessor between the sun god and his priests at Heliopolis.
