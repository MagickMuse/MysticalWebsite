Saint Jerome is the patron saint of archivists, librarians, translators, and scholars.

Classification

Doctor of the Church, venerated by most branches of Christianity.

Life and History

Jerome (c. 342–420 CE), born Eusebius Sophronius Hieronymus in Stridon (modern Croatia/Slovenia border), was one of the most learned men of the ancient Church. He mastered Latin, Greek, and Hebrew, and spent years as a hermit in the Syrian desert practicing severe asceticism.

The Vulgate

Jerome's greatest achievement was translating the Bible into Latin from the original Hebrew and Greek texts. This translation, known as the Vulgate, became the standard Bible of Western Christianity for over a millennium and remains the official Latin text of the Catholic Church.

Temperament

Despite his holiness, Jerome was famous for his sharp tongue and fierce polemics. He engaged in bitter disputes with other Church fathers, including Augustine, and was known to savage his intellectual opponents with withering criticism.

Powers and Domains

Jerome is invoked for success in translation, scholarly research, and academic pursuits. He protects libraries, archives, and all repositories of knowledge. Translators seek his blessing for accuracy and eloquence.

The Lion

According to legend, a lion entered Jerome's monastery, causing all the monks to flee except Jerome. He discovered the lion had a thorn in its paw, removed it, and the grateful lion became his devoted companion. The lion is now his most recognizable attribute.

Iconography

Depicted as an elderly, bearded scholar in cardinal's robes (anachronistic, as cardinals didn't exist in his time) or as a half-naked hermit. Almost always shown with:

  • A lion at his feet or beside him
  • A skull (memento mori)
  • Books and writing implements
  • Sometimes beating his breast with a stone (penitence)

Attributes

Lion, cardinal's hat, skull, books, pen, stone.

Sacred Sites

  • Basilica of Santa Maria Maggiore in Rome (his relics)
  • Church of Saint Catherine in Bethlehem (near his hermitage)

Feast Day

September 30 (Western Church)

Related Entities

Saint Augustine, Saint Ambrose, Saint Gregory the Great (the four Latin Doctors of the Church)
