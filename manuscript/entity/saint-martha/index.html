Saint Martha is the patron saint of homemakers, housekeepers, cooks, and domestic workers. She is renowned for healing gallstones and kidney ailments.

Martha of Bethany, venerated by most branches of Christianity, was the sister of Lazarus and Mary and a close friend of Jesus. The Gospel of Luke describes Jesus visiting the siblings' home, when Martha complains about her sister leaving all the housework to her. In John's Gospel, Martha affirms her faith in Jesus, who rewards her by resurrecting her brother Lazarus after four days in the tomb.

Beyond these Gospel references, Martha appears in the Gnostic Pistis Sophia and stars in extensive folklore. According to Catholic legend, she traveled to France with Mary Magdalen and the Marys of the Sea. Hearing of troubles in Tarascon, a town terrorized by a dragon called the tarasque, Martha went to help.

Domains

Martha is invoked to prevent, banish, or tame bad bosses. She protects against evil spirits and malicious people, guards against animal attack, and heals gallstones and kidney ailments.

Lore

The people of Tarascon initially scoffed at the meek woman who came to save them. Martha simply approached the dragon and charmed it, removing her girdle for the beast to wear like a leash. The tarasque followed her willingly like an obedient dog. Tragically, when Martha led the tamed dragon before the townspeople, the mob overwhelmed her and killed the creature, causing Martha great grief.

She disappears from history until the 12th century, when a body in a crypt beside a well in Tarascon was identified as hers. Martha then became immensely popular. Most folklore about her stems from this period but may preserve older oral traditions.

Eastern Orthodox tradition contains no dragon lore. Instead, Martha, Lazarus, and Mary traveled to Cyprus, where they died.

Iconography

Traditional images portray Martha with the tarasque. Recent Catholic iconography tends to substitute Gospel scenes, such as Vermeer's or Velázquez's paintings of Christ in the House of Mary and Martha.

Spiritual Allies

Martha appears on traveler's amulets alongside Jesus and Julian the Hospitaller, as well as her siblings Lazarus and Mary.

Attributes

Keys, ladle, broom, girdle.

Colors

Green.

Sacred Sites

Saint Martha's Collegiate Church in Tarascon. René d'Anjou, king of Provence, organized a festival there in her honor still celebrated annually on the last Sunday in June.
