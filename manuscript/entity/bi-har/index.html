Bi-har is a guardian deity of the Buddhist-Lamaist tradition of Tibet. He is counted among the guardian maharajas, the great kings whose office is to defend the world of practice against demonic and hostile forces. As one of these maharajas, Bi-har stands at his appointed quarter of the cosmos and holds his portion of the vast defensive perimeter that surrounds the community of practitioners.

Domains

Guardianship of the cardinal quarter, protection of the practitioner's ground against demonic incursion, and the martial authority through which the wrathful protection of the dharma is exercised.

Iconography

Bi-har is a white deity, his color marking him as belonging to the peaceful side of wrathful protection, the pale radiance that expels demonic forces rather than merely fighting them on their own terms. He is three-eyed, the third eye set upon his forehead marking his capacity to perceive the hidden and the concealed. He is attended by a lion, the traditional companion of martial and protective figures in the Tibetan iconographic tradition.

Attributes

Arrow, bow, knife, staff, sword, and trident. This full armament of ranged and hand weapons marks Bi-har as a deity equipped for every mode of the defensive struggle, from the distant strike of the arrow to the close work of the knife and the ceremonial authority of the staff.

Petition For

Protection from demonic and hostile forces, guardianship of the four cardinal quarters, and the martial authority that keeps the practitioner's ground safe for practice.

Precautions

Bi-har belongs to the Tibetan Buddhist and Lamaist tradition, whose practice of the guardian maharajas is embedded in a formal lineage of transmission. Approach his figure as reference; direct petitioning of the Tibetan guardians is a lineage matter.
