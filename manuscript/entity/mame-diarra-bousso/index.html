Life and History

Mame Diarra Bousso (1833-1866), known as The One Who Lives with God, holds double significance: as mother of the renowned Senegalese saint Sheikh Amadou Bamba, and as a powerful saint herself. "Mame" is a Wolof honorific for a revered grandparent; "Diarra" means "the one who lives with God." Her birth name, Mariama, invites reverent comparison to the Virgin Mary.

From an exceptionally devout Muslim family, she could read the Quran by age ten. She ate sparingly except during religious feasts and gave generously to anyone in need, offering food and clothing without hesitation. In death as in life, she is considered boundlessly generous.

Legendary Devotion

Her complete dedication to her husband produced remarkable stories. When the household ran out of firewood, rather than go gather more, she burned her own clothing so her husband wouldn't wait for breakfast.

During a violent storm threatening their yard's straw fence, her husband asked her to help hold it up. As the wind intensified, he eventually sought shelter, assuming she would follow. Come morning, he found her still holding the fence. Asked why she hadn't sought refuge, she simply replied that she had stayed where he told her to stay.

Death and Legacy

She died at thirty-three. Devotees describe her spirit as generous as the sea, incredibly patient and bountiful.

Iconography

No photograph survives. She is typically depicted holding up a fence.

Attributes

Prayer beads, tea kettle, mortar and pestle.

Sacred Site

Her grave in Porokhane, Senegal, draws enormous annual pilgrimages, especially by women. Rituals include tying fabric to the tree where she once hung her laundry.
