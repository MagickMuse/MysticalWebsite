Saint Zoë was a fifth-century Cappadocian woman whose dramatic encounter with the hermit Saint Martinian led to her transformation from harlot to wonder-working healer.nn## DomainsnnHealing, protection, overcoming temptation, victory over addiction, transformation, sacred sexuality mysteries. Petition Saint Zoë to heal addiction, overcome temptation, and transform harmful patterns.nn## Life and HistorynnIn early fifth-century Cappadocia (modern Turkey), Zoë worked as a harlot who set out to destroy the local ascetic Martinian's vow of chastity. Whether this was her own whim or she served as an unwitting tool of darker forces remains debated.nnHer name suggests she may have been a devotee of a Gnostic tradition rather than an ordinary prostitute. She may have sought Martinian as a partner in the Great Rite, the sacred sexual union of male and female energies. A spiritual man who had conserved his sexual energy for so long would have been a prized partner.nnGaining admittance to his lonely cell through subterfuge on a dark, stormy night, Zoë attempted seduction. Martinian's response shocked her: rather than acquiesce or throw her out, he built a great fire and walked into it, choosing to burn his body rather than risk his soul to hellfire.nnZoë did not flee. When Martinian did not die but collapsed, she rescued him and cared for him through his recovery. His actions had tremendous spiritual impact upon her. Eventually he persuaded her to travel to the Bethlehem home of Paula, the renowned Christian woman and friend of Saint Jerome who was later sainted herself.nnZoë moved in with Paula, spending the rest of her life in fasting and prayer. She eventually gained renown as a wonder worker and miracle healer.nn## IconographynnOften depicted alongside Saints Martinian and Photina. May be represented by a red candle on an altar.nn## Ritualnn### Three Saints AltarnnUse the image of a monk to represent Martinian, or alternatively a tarot Hermit card. Place him in the center of the altar between a red candle representing Zoë and a chalice of salted water representing Photina.nnThe three saints are conventionally invoked for healing and protection. They possess particularly potent powers regarding avoiding and overcoming temptation, making them valued allies in battles against addiction.nnLess conventionally, the three are invoked to preside over sacred Tantric mysteries, especially those involving manipulation of sexual energy.nn## LorennSaint Martinian escaped union with Zoë through fire. He later escaped union with Photina through water. The three saints are now venerated together, their feast shared on February 13. Whether Zoë manifested healing powers before her encounter with Martinian remains unknown, as traditional hagiography displays no interest in her earlier life."
