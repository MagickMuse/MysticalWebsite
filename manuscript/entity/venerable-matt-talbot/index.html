Classification

Unofficial saint. His canonization process began in May 1947 and is not yet complete. The future Pope John Paul II wrote a paper about him in his youth.

Matt Talbot (2 May 1856 to 7 June 1925) was the second of twelve children born to a Dublin dock worker and charwoman. Most men in his family, including his father, were heavy drinkers. Matt began drinking at age twelve when he left school and found work at a wine merchant's shop, where he helped himself to the wares. He was profoundly alcoholic from age twelve to twenty-eight, once selling his boots for liquor money and stealing an old man's fiddle to pawn for drink.

Matt Talbot is renowned for healing addiction and alcoholism, offering hope and strength to those battling substance dependencies.

Domains

Matt Talbot is the sacred sponsor of alcoholics and addicts of all kinds. He is petitioned for sobriety, strength to overcome addiction, and perseverance in recovery.

Lore

One Saturday, Matt walked to O'Meara's pub on Dublin's North Strand with his two youngest brothers. Without cash, they stood outside waiting for someone to offer them a drink, but acquaintance after acquaintance walked past. Matt experienced an epiphany and went home, shocking his mother by arriving early and sober. He announced he was going to take the pledge, an oath before a priest never to drink again.

At Holy Cross College, Matt sought to pledge for life, but a realistic priest told him to swear for three months first. If he lasted that long, he could return for a longer pledge. Matt kept his pledge for forty-one years until his death.

He devised his own sobriety plan: up early for five o'clock Mass, then off to work at a construction site. After work, back to church until time to go home, eat, and sleep. He adopted extreme austerities, sleeping on a wood plank with a wood block pillow. He paid off all debts and scoured Dublin searching for the fiddler whose instrument he had stolen, but never found him. He lived with his mother until her death, then rented a small room, donating most of his salary to charity or needy neighbors.

Matt died suddenly of a heart attack while walking to Mass on Trinity Sunday. No one could identify him. When undressed at the hospital, the extent of his austerities was revealed: he wore a heavy chain around his waist, chains around one arm and leg, and cords around the other. Word spread that a holy man had died, and crowds flocked to his funeral. Matt Talbot became an icon for the temperance movement.

Sacred Sites

A cross marks the spot where he died on Granby Lane, Dublin. He is enshrined at Our Lady of Lourdes Church in Dublin, where his tomb has a glass panel so that his coffin is visible.
