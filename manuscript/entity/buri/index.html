Buri is the first of the gods in Nordic cosmogony, the primal ancestor from whom the line of the Aesir descends. In the received Nordic cosmogonic tradition of Iceland, Buri came into being when the cosmic cow Audhumla licked at the salty blocks of primordial ice that filled the void of Ginnungagap. Over the course of three days her tongue drew him out of the ice: on the first day the hair of his head appeared, on the second day the head itself, and on the third day the whole being emerged, complete, from the frozen substance of the primal world.

The Line of the Gods

Buri had a son, Bor, whose name means the born one, and it was Bor who took Bestla, daughter of the frost-giant Bolthorn, as his wife. From this union came three sons: Odin, Vili, and Ve. These three, the grandsons of Buri, slew the primordial giant Ymir and shaped the ordered cosmos from his body. Through Buri, then, the line of the Aesir gods stands connected all the way back to the ice of Ginnungagap and to Audhumla's slow, patient tongue.

Domains

The hidden potential within primordial matter, the emergence of shape and personhood from the substance of the world, and the ancestral seed of the gods themselves.
