Magickal Properties

  • Beautiful Japanese dragon goddess of sex, fertility, music, dance, love, and wealth
  • Has dominion over all Earth's treasures, material and emotional, brings happiness and joy
  • Only female member of Shichi Fujukin (Seven Spirits of Good Fortune)
  • Daughter of dragon king, lives happily with dragon husband under Lake Biwa near Kyoto

Ritual Uses

  • Petition for removing obstacles from lovers' path to happiness
  • Practice magickal art of money laundering, wash coins at her shrines, spend within week for three-fold return
  • Invoke protection against dragons and for control/pacification of troublesome dragons

Lore

Benten's signature attribute is the biwa, a Japanese lute, reflecting her dominion over music and the arts. Her element is water, and she is venerated at Lake Biwa, Enoshima island, and the Zeniarai Benten shrines where devotees wash coins in her sacred springs to multiply wealth. Dragons and white snakes serve as her messengers, and an unexpected glimpse of a white snake is considered a harbinger of good fortune or a message from Benten herself. Her sacred day is the first serpent day of February in the Japanese calendar.

Snakes and Marital Precaution

Snakes, believed to stand for jealousy, are often coiled around her statues. Because of this association, married couples are traditionally reluctant to visit her shrines together, since it is said Benten may sow discord between them out of divine envy.

Priesthood and Syncretism

Her priesthood is both Shinto and Buddhist, and she is closely linked with the Hindu goddess Sarasvati. In Buddhist tradition Benten is often identified as a Japanese form of Sarasvati, sharing dominion over music, learning, water, and eloquence.
