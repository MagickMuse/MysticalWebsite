Overview

Elatha, also called Elatha mac Delbaeth, is a Fomorian king of the Celtic Irish tradition. He is remembered as a shining figure of the sea, described in the tale of the coming of Bres as a beautiful stranger who arrived from the ocean at night, clothed in gold and drawn by a horse of great beauty. His union with the Tuatha Dé Danaan goddess Eriu produced Bres Macelatha, who would later become Lord of Ireland and preside over the Battle of Moytura.

In Irish literature

Elatha appears chiefly in the mythological cycle preserved in the Book of Invasions and in the Cath Maige Tuired, the account of the Second Battle of Moytura. Unlike the majority of the Fomorians, who are depicted as monstrous adversaries of the Tuathá Dé Danaan, Elatha is described in noble and beautiful terms, and his line carries the mixed inheritance of both the Fomorian and Tuatha peoples through his son Bres. He is sometimes counted among the more ancestral Fomorian figures, standing behind Balor and other later champions of that race.

Domains

Elatha presides over the Fomorian sea, over night starlight, and over the kind of sovereignty that passes across the boundary between enemy peoples through kinship and marriage. He is a figure of the threshold between the older sea powers and the newer land-holding gods of Ireland.
