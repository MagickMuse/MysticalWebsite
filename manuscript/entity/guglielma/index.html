Guglielma, known as The Heretic Saint, is an unofficial saint renowned for healing headaches and migraines. She is also invoked to increase milk flow in nursing mothers.

Life and History

Around 1260, a mysterious woman named Guglielma (died 1281), rumored to be a Bohemian princess, settled in Milan. She may have been daughter of Premysl Otakar I, king of Bohemia, and possibly first cousin of Saint Elizabeth of Hungary. She is believed to have been a widow, arriving with a young man described as her son.

The Spiritual Teacher

A spiritual woman, Guglielma was a healer and teacher. A devoted following surrounded her. Some became convinced she was more than a holy woman. Andrea Saramita proclaimed her the incarnation of the Holy Ghost, destined to inaugurate a new age.

Although Guglielma apparently denied these claims, devotees were convinced she was divine. When she died, many expected canonization. An inner circle awaited her resurrection.

The Guglielmites

Canonization never came. Devotees began a spiritual movement based on veneration of the Holy Spirit in Guglielma's guise. Paintings portrayed her as part of the Trinity, standing beside Jesus Christ. Her primary disciple, Sister Manfreda de Pirovano, was proclaimed the first female pope.

The Inquisition

Milan was then a hotbed of heresy. Inquisitors examined the Guglielmites. Devotees insisted Guglielma was the Holy Spirit incarnate and that Pagans, Jews, and Muslims could be saved through her.

Two heresies condemned them:

  • The notion that the Holy Spirit could incarnate as a woman
  • The belief in salvation without conversion to Roman Catholicism

Saramita and Manfreda were burned at the stake in 1300. Guglielma was posthumously declared a heretic. Her remains were exhumed, bones burned, ashes scattered. Even the empty tomb was destroyed to prevent pilgrimages.

Continuing Veneration

Devotees discreetly continued venerating her, some honoring her under the guise of Catherine of Alexandria or Margaret of Antioch. Since at least 1450, she has been venerated in the remote village of Brunate as Saint Guglielma, performing many miracles, especially healing headaches and migraines. She is also invoked to increase milk flow in nursing mothers.

Iconography

An image resembling the traditional tarot Pope card shows a man and nun (possibly Saramita and Manfreda) kneeling before a woman robed in purple. Some interpret it as depicting Guglielma proclaiming Manfreda as pope.

The Papess (Female Pope/High Priestess) card from the 15th-century Visconti-Sforza tarot may depict Manfreda, who was related to the Visconti family who commissioned that deck.

Using Tarot

Devotees may use the Pope or Hierophant card to represent Guglielma as the enthroned central figure.

Feast Days

  • 24 August (anniversary of death)
  • Fourth Sunday of April
  • Pentecost (Whitsun), her reputed birthday
