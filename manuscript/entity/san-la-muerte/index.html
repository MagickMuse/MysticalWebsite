Unofficial saint of Argentina with origins in shamanic Guarani traditions.

Epithet

Saint Death.

Pronunciation

Mwair-tay.

Overview

When God appointed him Lord of Death, San La Muerte complained that no one would love him if his only function was ending lives. In response, God blessed him with expanded powers to enhance and improve lives as well. Many consider San La Muerte the most powerful of Argentina's numerous folk saints. Death is invoked to protect life.

Although he is invoked to curse and even kill, he is more usually petitioned for miracles involving family, finances, health, employment, and love. He implements justice, and devotees are cautioned to ask him only for what is just.

Origins and Identity

The name San La Muerte refers to both a magical talisman and the folk saint. The talisman is the older form, initially a magical device used to injure or kill enemies. The power was concentrated into a tiny figure, then ritually released toward its target. The talisman eventually evolved into a personified spirit.

Myths explaining his origins involve encounters between the Guarani and Catholic missionaries. Different versions exist, but the consistent theme is that he starved to death and was reduced to a skeleton:

  • A just king who protected the poor, overthrown and chained to his throne until he starved. In Heaven, God gave him a room filled with candles, one for each soul on earth, which he protects and collects when extinguished.
  • A shaman arrested by Catholic priests and imprisoned with lepers. He refused to eat and stood leaning on his L-shaped staff until he became a skeleton. The staff evolved into a scythe.
  • A shaman who fasted during such a lengthy vision quest that he became a skeleton and his soul entered the spirit realm.

Talismans and Amulets

San La Muerte amulets are carved from bone, bullets, or wood from a cemetery or roadside shrine cross. The most powerful are believed to be those carved from human bone or from a bullet extracted from a corpse. The most prized amulets are carved by prisoners, because San La Muerte died while imprisoned on a Good Friday.

These amulets can render someone invulnerable to bullets, knives, or any lethal weapon. They are carried or inserted under the skin for constant protection and as devotion. Devotees literally become a shrine for San La Muerte.

⚠️
When people are ready to die, their San La Muerte amulets should be removed and buried with them. If amulets worn beneath the skin are not removed before death, they may prolong the dying process agonizingly and indefinitely.

Iconography

Two traditional images exist: one squatting (reminiscent of Guarani burial positions) and one standing with a scythe (recalling the Grim Reaper). He manifests as a naked skeleton or wearing a black cape. Images are traditionally kept private, though this is changing as his popularity grows.

Altar

San La Muerte requires his own altar. Do not place other saints or spirits on his altar.

Feast Days

The date varies but is always in August, with festivities lasting a week. Possible start dates include 13, 15, or 20 August. Legal permits for his fiestas have historically been difficult to obtain.

Sacred Sites

San La Muerte has a shrine in Posadas, Argentina. His annual fiesta is celebrated in Empedrado, Argentina.

Syncretism

San La Muerte is syncretized with Jesus Christ, specifically Christ's skeleton. He is also identified with the Fourth Horseman of the Apocalypse: "And behold a pale horse: and his name that sat on him was Death..." (Revelation 6:8).

Warning

His methods are described as akin to the Mafia. Do not ask for favors unless you are sure you can and will pay your debt. If not properly thanked, San La Muerte revokes miracles.
