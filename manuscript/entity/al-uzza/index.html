Magickal Properties

Al Uzza, "The Mightiest One" or "Most Powerful," was an extraordinarily popular pre-Islamic Arabian deity, possibly the preeminent Arabian goddess. She was the primary female spirit venerated by the Nabataeans in Petra and appears in the center of a surviving zodiac wheel, apparently presiding over the wheel of the year. Al Uzza seems associated with wells and springs, life-saving water in a desert region.

Ritual Uses

Work with Al Uzza for protection, fertility, and spiritual guidance. She had a shrine on the road leading to Mecca where she was believed to reside in acacia trees, emerging from them when invoked. She may have been Mecca's guardian spirit before Islam and was beloved until her idol was destroyed.

Offerings

Fruit and fragrance (incense, perfume). Present offerings that honor her connection to fertility, protection, and the life-giving properties of water sources.

Favored People

Those seeking protection, fertility, and guidance, people working with wells and springs, practitioners honoring pre-Islamic Arabian goddess traditions, and those seeking access to life-saving resources.

Petition For

Protection, fertility, guidance through life's challenges, connection to wells and springs, access to life-saving resources in difficult times, and spiritual guidance in desert or challenging environments.

Warnings

Was extraordinarily popular pre-Islamic Arabian goddess, possibly preeminent. Associated with wells and springs. Her idol was destroyed at the Kaaba. The Romans identified her with Isis and Aphrodite.

Additional Notes

Archaeological evidence suggests that Petra's Temple of the Winged Lions was dedicated to her. She was venerated in surviving imagery as a beautiful woman and also in the form of a black stone with a cleft. The Nabataeans were renowned stargazers with extremely sophisticated astrology, and her position at the center of their zodiac wheel shows her importance in their cosmic understanding.
