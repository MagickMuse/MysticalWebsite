Saint Camillus is renowned for performing healing miracles and particularly aids gambling addicts seeking to quit.

Classification

Roman Catholic saint.

Epithet

The Red Cross Saint.

Life and History

Camillus (1550-1614) was a Neapolitan mercenary, professional gambler, and cardsharp. Tall (over six feet), handsome, and popular with women, his luck eventually turned. By twenty-four, he had lost everything, including the shirt on his back, and wound up begging on the streets.

The only people willing to hire him were the Capuchins, who employed him in construction. This work sparked a spiritual transformation. Camillus dedicated his remaining life to God and those in need.

Revolutionary Hospitals

Entering the Church, he founded the Brothers of Saint John of God (also called Ministers of the Sick). For thirty years he nursed the sick and established hospitals that were revolutionary for their time: they actually attempted to heal patients rather than simply providing comfortable places to die.

His hospitals pioneered quarantine wards for contagious patients, healthy diets, cleanliness, and proper ventilation, setting new standards for healthcare.

Powers and Domains

Camillus performs healing miracles and particularly aids gambling addicts seeking to quit. Having rebuilt his own life from nothing, he helps others do the same. Petition him when you've reached rock bottom.

Ritual

Light a white candle and offer some cash along with something representing your addiction. Once the candle burns down, give the cash to the first beggar, charity, or needy person who asks for help.

Iconography

Wears a black habit bearing the Red Cross of his order. Due to abscessed feet he suffered during his destitute years, he may appear with a pronounced limp.

Feast Day

14 July
