Bhumi is a collective name for a group of goddesses in the Vajrayana Buddhist tradition. The name carries the meaning the earth on which all things are formed, marking the group as a set of foundational spiritual grounds. The bhumis are twelve in number, and each personifies one of the spiritual spheres through which a bodhisattva or buddha-designate passes in the quest for the perfection of knowledge.

Domains

The deified spiritual grounds of the Vajrayana path, the twelvefold ladder of stations along which a bodhisattva ascends toward buddhahood, and the qualities a practitioner must cultivate while moving through each successive sphere.

Iconography

The common attribute of the bhumis is a staff, carried by each of the twelve as the mark of her sphere.

Attributes

A staff.

Members

Among the twelve bhumis attested in the Entity Grimoire are Abhimukhi (the friendly disposed), Acala (the immovable), Adhimukticarya (the practice of confidence), and Arcismati. Each bhumi is worshipped as a distinct goddess in her own right, and the group together forms the earth-like foundation on which awakening rises.
