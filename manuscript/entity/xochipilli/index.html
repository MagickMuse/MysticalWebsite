Classification

Aztec lord of flowers, love, maize, beauty, fun, games, music, art, dance, shamanism, and prophecy.

Overview

Xochipilli is patron of all life's pleasures, embodying the very essence of creative potential and masculine beauty. He is responsible for sexual lust and desire; those lacking libido may ask him to provide the spark. He radiates the warm, fertilizing powers of the sun and can supply any kind of creative energy that is lacking, breaking artists' creative blocks.

Magickal Properties

Xochipilli rules sexuality, and rituals in his honor incorporate sexual fasting, prescribed periods of celibacy. He punishes those he is angry with through boils, hemorrhoids, and venereal disease, but he can heal these same afflictions. He protects the souls of dead warriors, who return to Earth as hummingbirds.

Manifestation

A fifteenth century statue of Xochipilli was unearthed on the side of the volcano Popocatepetl near Mexico City. He sits cross-legged on a pedestal, mouth half open, eyes up-tilted, appearing entranced. The base of the pedestal is ornamented with images of flowers, including tobacco, Ololiuqui, Sinicuichi, and possibly cahua xochitl (one flower remains unidentified), along with depictions of Psilocybe aztecorum mushrooms. His scepter has a string of hearts attached.

Attributes

He rules shamanic, potentially psychoactive plants and mushrooms, including tobacco, Ololiuqui (a species of morning glory), and Sinicuichi ("Sun Opener"). Devotees ornament their bodies with tattoos of flowers.

Correspondences

Consort: Mayahuel. Spirit allies: Tlaloc, Cinteotl, and especially his twin sister Xochiquetzal. Time: high noon. Planet: the sun. Bird: hummingbird.
