Anna Maria Taigi (29 May 1769 to 9 June 1837) was an Italian visionary, prophetess, and mystic born in Siena. Her parents owned an apothecary but went bankrupt when she was six, forcing the family to move to Rome where they became servants. After only two years of schooling, Anna entered the workforce as a maid. She fell in love with and married a porter named Dominic, despite her mother's hopes she would marry better. Her mother's harsh treatment is among the reasons this saint is traditionally invoked against verbal abuse.

Domains

Anna Maria Taigi is invoked for protection against verbal and spousal abuse. She is also petitioned for healing, prophetic guidance, and spiritual counsel. During her lifetime, she served as advisor to popes, cardinals, and neighbors alike, despite raising four children on her husband's modest porter salary.

Iconography

Anna Maria Taigi is portrayed gazing into her luminous globe, a miniature sun surrounded by thorns that appeared to her in 1790 after the birth of her first child. She saw this globe constantly, though it was visible only to her. She interpreted it as representing eternal wisdom, and when she gazed into it, present and future events anywhere in the world were revealed to her, along with the secret contents of people's souls.

Attributes

Luminous globe surrounded by thorns.

Lore

Soon after her marriage, Anna had a profound spiritual epiphany and sought a religious vocation. She attended Mass daily and joined the Trinitarian order. She raised four children while three others died. Her parents came to live with her and Dominic, who adored Anna but struggled with his temper. He was known to dump dinner on the floor if it displeased him, which is another reason Anna is invoked for those dealing with spousal abuse.

Anna became a gifted and renowned seer and healer. She never charged for her services, refusing payment even from the wealthy. She healed both in person and at a distance via her globe. She accurately foretold deaths so people could prepare themselves, once urging Pope Pius VII to receive last rites four days before his death. Anna Maria Taigi received the stigmata.

She is perhaps most famous for her apocalyptic prophecies. According to Anna Maria, God will send two punishments to humanity. The first originates on earth in the form of war, revolution, and familiar ills. The second originates in Heaven: all lights will extinguish and earth will be plunged into darkness for three days and nights. Artificial light sources will fail, with the sole exception of blessed candles. Doors and windows must remain sealed as the air will fill with deadly pestilential demons. Anna advised that during these three days, people should stay home, pray the rosary, and beg for God's mercy.

Canonization Status

Anna Maria Taigi was beatified in 1920. Canonization remains pending, in part because she was as widely regarded as an occultist as she was a prophetess during her lifetime, and those associations linger.

Sacred Sites

Her body was initially incorrupt but has deteriorated due to humidity and inadequate enshrinement. A wax figure now encloses her remains, which are enshrined at Rome's Basilica of San Crisogono in Trastevere.
