Mahishasura is the great buffalo demon of Hindu mythology, the primary antagonist of the goddess Durga and the central figure in her most celebrated myth. His name means "buffalo demon" (mahisha = buffalo, asura = demon). His defeat at the hands of Durga is among the most important stories in the Hindu goddess tradition and the central narrative of the Devi Mahatmya.

Overview

Mahishasura performed heroic austerities (tapas) and was granted the boon of near-invincibility by Brahma: no male being — god, demon, or mortal — could slay him. Empowered by this boon, he waged war against the gods, defeated them, and usurped their heavenly positions, throwing the cosmic order into chaos. The gods, unable to defeat him themselves, emitted their combined energies in a great blaze of fire and power, which condensed into the form of a beautiful woman: Durga, the warrior goddess.

The Battle with Durga

Mahishasura, upon seeing the radiant goddess, assumed she was no match for him in battle and attempted instead to make her his bride. He sent messengers and declared his love, insisting that as a woman she was too delicate to fight and required the protection of a man. Durga rejected his overtures with fierce, combative language, promising to tear him apart in battle. Mahishasura, interpreting her ferocity as love play, persisted — and was annihilated.

In the battle, Mahishasura shapeshifted through multiple demonic forms trying to escape Durga's assault. He appeared in turn as a lion, a man with sword, an elephant, and finally returned to his buffalo form. Durga dismounted from her lion vehicle, pinned the buffalo form with her foot, and drove her trident through his chest as he half-emerged in his human form. This iconic image — Durga with trident raised, foot on the buffalo carcass, demon emerging from it — is the most common iconographic representation of Durga and the central image of Durga Puja.

Significance

Mahishasura represents the forces of adharma (cosmic disorder) that periodically overwhelm the male gods and require the intervention of the goddess to restore balance. His assumption that a woman could not be a warrior — and his fatal underestimation of Durga — is a central irony of the myth. The story is told in detail in the Devi Mahatmya (Markandeya Purana, ca. 5th–6th century CE), which is recited in its entirety during Durga Puja.

Durga's most popular epithet, Mahishasuramardini ("she who crushed Mahishasura"), derives directly from this myth.
