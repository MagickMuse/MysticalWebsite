Overview

Caturmurti is a god of the Hindu Puranic tradition whose name means four-formed or four-imaged, from catur meaning four and murti meaning form or image. He is understood in two related senses. In his first sense he is a specific iconographic form of Visnu with four faces turned to the four directions, an image in which the god's cosmic sovereignty is expressed by his facing every quarter of the universe at once. In his second sense he is the syncretic gathering of Brahma, Visnu, Siva, and Surya into a single fourfold form, an extension of the well-known trimurti of Brahma-Visnu-Siva by the inclusion of Surya, the sun.

Classification

God of the Hindu Puranic tradition. Four-faced form of Visnu, and syncretic gathering of Brahma, Visnu, Siva, and Surya.

Domains

Cosmic sovereignty expressed by facing every direction at once, the theological unity of the great gods gathered into a single form, the fourfold reading of divinity that extends the trimurti by the inclusion of the sun, the meditation upon the god who is many gods held together.

Iconography

As the four-faced form of Visnu, portrayed with four heads facing the four cardinal directions, standing or seated in the standard sovereign posture of Visnu, holding across his multiple arms the emblems of the god: conch, discus, mace, and lotus. As the syncretic gathering, portrayed with the distinctive marks of each of the four great gods drawn together into one form, so that the creating Brahma, the preserving Visnu, the transforming Siva, and the illuminating Surya are all recognizable at once within a single body.

Attributes

Conch shell, discus, mace, and lotus of Visnu; the water pot and rosary of Brahma; the trident and drum of Siva; and the sun disc of Surya. In the four-faced Visnu form the attributes are the four Visnu emblems distributed across the arms; in the syncretic form the attributes of all four gods are gathered together.

Sacred Sites

Vaisnava temples across India that observe the four-faced Visnu iconography, and philosophical centers of Hindu theology in which the syncretic understanding of the great gods is meditated upon.

Ritual

Honored within the wider Vaisnava temple worship in his four-faced form, and honored philosophically and meditatively in his syncretic form as the fourfold gathering of the great gods. Meditation on the caturmurti is a way of holding the wide horizon of Hindu theology in a single image, and it belongs to the more philosophical registers of Hindu devotional practice.

Lore

The caturmurti concept extends the well-known trimurti, the threefold form of Brahma the creator, Visnu the preserver, and Siva the transformer, by the addition of Surya, the sun, as a fourth great god. This addition places the caturmurti in the philosophical tradition that has always held the trimurti in tension with the wider polytheism of the tradition, and that has sought various ways to gather the great gods into higher unities without dissolving their distinctness. The four-faced Visnu form belongs to the more iconographic register of the same idea, expressing through the sculptural fact of four faces the theological claim that the sovereign god faces every direction of the cosmos at once.
