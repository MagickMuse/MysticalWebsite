Magickal Properties

Dorothy of Montau is an unofficial saint, beatified but not yet canonized, renowned for healing and protection against miscarriage.

Life and History

Dorothy (1347-1394) was an exceptionally devout child from a large family in Montau, a village on the Vistula River then under Teutonic Knights' rule (now in Poland). She began practicing austerities very young, especially extreme food deprivation, which brought her into conflict with her mother.

Dorothy developed nausea at the smell, taste, or touch of food. She consumed so little she ceased urinating. She would have eaten nothing at all had her confessor not intervened.

Marriage and Motherhood

Dorothy was passionately devoted to the Eucharist and entered trances frequently. At seventeen, she married a wealthy swordsmith in his forties. He complained, probably accurately, that while entranced or in ecstasy, she neglected household duties: she forgot to shop, neglected cleaning, and when cooking was so distracted she forgot to scale fish or clean vegetables.

Dorothy experienced a mystical pregnancy in addition to her frequent trances. She wished to be free of her husband, preferring begging to housework. She bore nine children, but only one survived (a daughter who became a Benedictine nun). Four died at birth; four died of plague.

Her husband was impatient and reputedly a heavy drinker. Whenever Dorothy spent too much time in church, he beat her, once so severely he feared he had killed her. Frightened and repentant, he promised to go on pilgrimage with her if she revived. He kept his vow, and they traveled together to Aachen and elsewhere.

Anchoress

Dorothy later pilgrimaged to Rome alone, begging to fund the trip. By her return, her husband had died. She became an anchoress, moving into a cell against the cathedral wall in what is now Kwidzyn, Poland. She gained reputation as a holy woman whose visitors sought her advice. Her confessor recorded her visions and revelations.

Ritual Uses

Dorothy is a miracle worker whose protection is invoked against miscarriage.

Status

Venerated from the moment of her death, Dorothy was beatified on January 9, 1976, but canonization remains pending. She is nonetheless called Saint Dorothy and is particularly popular in Central Europe.

In Literature

Dorothy's tale, as told by her husband, appears in Gunter Grass's 1989 novel The Flounder.

Iconography

May be portrayed surrounded by arrows.

Attributes

Lantern, rosary.
