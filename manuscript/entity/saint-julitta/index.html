Saint Julitta is renowned for protecting children from illness and preventing the death of children, especially from terminal illness.

Classification

Venerated as a saint by most branches of Christianity.

Life and History

Julitta was a rich Christian widow from Anatolia who moved to Tarsus with her baby son, Cyriacus, and two household maids in order to escape the persecution of Christians. Persecution followed her; Julitta was denounced and arrested. Her son was brought before the magistrate with her.

Martyrdom

Julitta was defiant and the three-year-old mimicked her. She was tortured and forced to witness his death. Julitta remained defiant, refusing to weep; instead celebrating that little Cyr, as he is popularly known, had earned the crown of martyrdom. In anger, the magistrate increased her torture. She was killed slowly and horrifically, finally dying by beheading.

The bodies of Julitta and Cyr were tossed onto a dung heap but rescued by the two maids, who buried them together in a nearby field.

Later Veneration

Centuries later, Saint Cyr revealed himself to Charlemagne; as a result, he and his mother became popularly venerated saints. Julitta is usually venerated with her son.

Powers and Domains

Julitta is petitioned for:

  • Domestic happiness and peace
  • Preventing the death of children, especially from illness, but for any reason
  • Protection of vulnerable families

Iconography

Various images of Julitta exist:

  • Depicted holding Saint Cyr
  • Being burned at the stake
  • Dead with a fountain emerging from her blood
  • Standing beside oxen

Feast Days

  • June 16
  • July 15
  • The Ethiopian Church honors her on the fifteenth day of each month, along with her son
