Balarama, whose name means "strength of Rama," is a Hindu deity of the Epic and Puranic periods and an incarnation of the god Visnu. He may have originated in Vedic times as an agricultural fertility deity.

Domains

Agricultural fertility, strength, joy, and divine kinship.

Iconography

He is usually depicted on the right side of Krsna and rarely stands alone. Balarama is light-skinned in contrast to the dark-skinned Krsna.

His attributes include the arrow, club, drinking cup, fan palm, honey pot, lotus, pestle, pitcher, plough, prayer wheel, shield, and sword.

Attributes

He enjoys characteristics similar to Krsna but fails to attract the same popularity. Among his epithets is Ananda, meaning "joy." In Jain tradition he is known as Baladeva.

Lore

Balarama is the son of Vasudeva and Devaki, though he was born from the womb of Rohini. Jointly with Krsna, his brother, he is identified as the eighth avatara of Visnu, or, alongside Rama, as the seventh.

Legend describes how Visnu impregnated the belly of the goddess Devaki with two hairs, one black and one white. To ensure their safety against a demon king, the two were transferred before birth to Rohini. Krsna grew to be dark-skinned, and Balarama light.

His consort is Revati and his sons are Nisatha and Ulmuka.
