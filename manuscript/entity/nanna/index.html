Overview

Nanna is one of the oldest deities in the Mesopotamian and Sumerian pantheons, having been considered the supreme deity for a time before others rose to prominence alongside him. As the moon god and keeper of celestial time, Nanna governed the 30-day lunar month, the number 30 itself being one of his symbols and sacred representations.

Appearance

Nanna is most commonly depicted as a wise old man with a lapis lazuli beard, seated upon a throne. He also appears in the form of a bull or cowherd, reflecting the moon's crescent resemblance to horns.

Family and Associations

Nanna's family illuminates the ancient Sumerian understanding of the moon's influence on the world. His wife was Ningal, goddess of fertility and the reed marshes. His children further reveal the scope of lunar power: his son was Utu (also called Shamash), the sun god, and his daughters were Inanna-Ishtar, goddess of love and sexuality, and Ereshkigal, queen of the dead. The moon thus presided over the sun, fertility, love, and death alike. That Nanna fathered the sun god may speak to an older cosmology in which the moon held greater primacy than the sun, perhaps from a time before the agricultural revolution, when nomadic peoples navigated primarily by the night sky.

Magickal Properties

Nanna became increasingly known for his wisdom over the many ages of his worship. He is a protector of humanity, a master of time, and a divine figure who grants insight through divination. Petitioning Nanna is appropriate for any working tied to the lunar cycle, the marking of time, the gaining of wisdom, or seeking protection.
