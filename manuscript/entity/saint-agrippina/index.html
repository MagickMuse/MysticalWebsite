Saint Agrippina is renowned for healing leprosy and bacterial infections.

Classification

Roman Catholic and Orthodox saint. She was removed from the official Roman Catholic roster of saints for lack of historical documentation, though the Orthodox Church still considers her an official saint.

Life and History

Agrippina was a patrician Roman woman who betrothed herself to Christ. She was allegedly martyred in approximately 256 CE. Her death was particularly brutal: she was flogged with rods until her bones were crushed. An angel appeared to comfort her during her suffering. Death was caused by either beheading or scourging.

Translation of Relics

Three young holy women removed Agrippina's corpse from Rome, carrying it until they arrived in Mineo, Sicily, on 17 May 261, where Agrippina was laid to rest. Her remains exuded a sweet odor of sanctity for the duration of the journey. Angels accompanied the little entourage.

Agrippina began demonstrating her power over malevolent spirits during this journey. Her very presence swept demons out of their path, while the sight of her reliquary alone was allegedly sufficient to perform exorcisms. A shrine was built for her and Agrippina commenced producing miracles.

Powers and Domains

Agrippina banishes ghosts and demons. She can terminate cases of involuntary spirit-possession and possesses the power to command and control malicious spirits. Agrippina also has dominion over thunderstorms, leprosy, and bacterial infections.

Iconography

Agrippina is portrayed as a beautiful, crowned, blonde woman.

Attributes

Cross, palm frond symbolizing martyrdom.

Feast Day

23 June

Festivals

Since 1914, during the first weekend in August, an annual feast is held in Agrippina's honor in Boston's North End.

Sacred Sites

  • Church of Saint Agrippina in Mineo, Sicily
  • Chapel of Saint Agrippina di Mineo in Boston
