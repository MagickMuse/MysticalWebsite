Overview

Mama Quilla, whose name means "Mother Moon," is the Incan goddess of the moon and a powerful protector of women, marriage, and the menstrual cycle. She is the daughter of the Incan creator deity and Mama Qucha, goddess of the sea. Her sister is Pachamama, goddess of the earth, and her husband is also her brother, the sun god Inti, a divine union that mirrors the relationship between sun and moon across the sky.

Appearance

Mama Quilla is said to weep tears of silver, associating her with the moon's pale, luminous light and with precious metals.

Role and Power

Mama Quilla presided over the Incan calendar, which was moon-based, making her the keeper of time for the civilization. She was a defender of women in all aspects of their lives: marriage, fertility, and the monthly cycle. Lunar eclipses were understood as attacks upon her by a great animal, and people would respond by shouting, throwing weapons, and making loud noises to drive the creature away and rescue her.

Magickal Properties

Invoke Mama Quilla for protection of women and marriages, for workings tied to the menstrual cycle, and for lunar timing and timekeeping magick. She is a compassionate protector, fierce when those under her care are threatened.

The Celestial Court

Mama Quilla's husband, the Sun God Apu Punchau ("Head of the Day"), forms the paired solar and lunar sovereignty at the heart of Inca cosmology. The rainbow and the planet Venus were understood as attendant presences surrounding the divine couple, and the thunder and lightning deity Catequil was numbered among the celestial companions in their orbit.
