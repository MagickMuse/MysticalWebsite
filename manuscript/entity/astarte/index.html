Magickal Properties

Levantine Lady of abundance, prosperity, love, sex, and war. Guardian of women’s reproductive health; patron of erotic sacred rites and victorious campaigns.

Ritual Uses

Petition for passionate love, fertility, prosperity, and triumph in struggle. Honor her where sea, caves, groves, or hilltops meet.

Offerings

Lilies and roses, sweet cakes (cake is among the most primeval traditional offerings made to female deities), honey, perfume, and henna; Friday observances. Astarte may be among the goddesses known as "Queen of Heaven" to whom ancient families offered ritual cakes.

Favored People

Lovers, sex‑workers, navigators and traders, and those seeking fertile prosperity.

Petition For

Reciprocal love, fecundity, worldly success, and protection in conflict.

Warnings

Approach with dignity; sacred sexuality is not coercion. Keep vows you make.

Additional Notes

Title used for allied West Semitic goddesses; entwined histories with Anat and Aphrodite. Temples from Sidon and Tyre to Cyprus and Sicily.

Encyclopedia Source Notes

Goddess of the evening star, of war, and of sexual love. Her known period of worship runs from circa 1500 BC or earlier until circa 200 BC. Synonyms include Astarat and Attart at Ugarit. Her cult centered predominantly at Tyre, with major sanctuaries also at Sidon, Byblos, Ascalon, Carthage, Kition on Cyprus, Eryx on Sicily, and Malta. Inscriptions from the fifth century BC in her major temple at Sidon suggest she was perceived as an emanation of Baal Samin, personifying his divine power, and she is also named as his consort.

Iconography

Her animal is the sphinx, which typically appears on either side of her throne. She is often represented by baetyls or stone stelae. Astarte is typically depicted naked and, in the Egyptian style, wears a crown of cows' horns enclosing a sun disc, the latter sometimes shown with rays emanating from it.

Hellenic Syncretization

In Hellenic times she became largely syncretized with the Greek goddess Aphrodite. A first century BC inscription in a sanctuary dedicated to Aphrodite at Delos identifies the holy Syrian goddess. The source also names Astoreth, Istar, and Aserah as related figures.

Encyclopedia Source Notes (Astoreth)

Fertility goddess of the Palestinian and Philistine traditions of Israel and Lebanon, venerated from circa 1200 BC or earlier until circa 200 BC. A synonym is Astaroth. Cult centers stretched across the Palestine coastal region including Jerusalem, attested by various sculptures and by inscriptions and the Vetus Testamentum.

Astoreth equates with the Syrian goddess Astarte, both modeled on the Mesopotamian Ishtar. She was typically adopted as goddess of both love and war and is usually depicted wearing a horned headdress. Biblical references include I Kings 11:5 and II Kings 23:13. Solomon is said to have built a temple in her honor near Jerusalem. Some authors hold the name to be synonymous with Astaroth.
