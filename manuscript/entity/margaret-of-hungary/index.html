Life and History

In 1242, the Tatars invaded Hungary. King Bela IV, brother of Saint Elizabeth of Hungary, vowed that if his kingdom was saved, he would consecrate his unborn child to God. As he prayed, a storm rose lasting three days. When it subsided, his enemy was gone. A couple of months later, Margaret (1242 to 18 January 1271) was born.

Childhood in the Convent

In fulfillment of her father's vow, three-year-old Margaret was delivered to the Dominican convent at Veszprem. At twelve, she took final vows as a nun.

The Rejected Marriage

A few years later, Ottokar, Iron King of Bohemia, sought to seal an alliance with Hungary by marrying beautiful Margaret. Everyone except Margaret considered this excellent. Pope Alexander IV agreed to annul her vows. Her parents assured her she would still honor God by sacrificing herself for her country.

Margaret declared she would rather die than abandon her vows. She threatened to cut off her nose and lips to repulse her suitor. Her parents took her seriously and acquiesced. They built her a convent on an island in the Danube, previously called the Isle of Rabbits, now Saint Margaret's Island, where she presided over daughters of Hungary's elite.

Extreme Humility and Austerity

Margaret refused to pull rank as princess. She reserved the worst tasks for herself, cleaning latrines and washing vomit and excrement from seriously ill nuns. She mortified her flesh, ate almost nothing, deliberately froze her hands in icy water, wore an iron girdle and nail-spiked shoes. Her austerities led to her early death at twenty-eight.

Powers and Domains

Margaret could control water, protecting her island from floods by making the Danube rise and fall at will. At age ten, when Dominican friars refused to extend their visit, she told them she would make it rain so hard they could not leave. She did.

Margaret is credited with miracles of healing and resurrection of the dead. She is invoked against floods and may be able to control any form of water.

Canonization

Although the beatification process began only seven years after her death, Margaret was not canonized until 1943.

Attributes
