Classification

Jewish rabbi, Kabbalist, and legendary sorcerer.

Life and History

Rabbi Judah Loewe (c. 1520–17 September 1609) was a renowned Talmudic scholar and chief rabbi in Moravia, then later in Prague. A philosopher and mystic, he published over a dozen books exploring theology, philosophy, and ethics. His circle of associates allegedly included the astronomers Tycho Brahe and Johannes Kepler.

The Golem of Prague

He is most famous for the legend that he created a golem, an artificial man brought to life to protect Prague's Jewish community from persecution. Although there are earlier reports of golems, the Maharal's golem is the most famous and is irrevocably intertwined with the city of Prague.

How the Golem Was Made

According to legend, Rabbi Loewe formed the golem from clay obtained from the banks of the Vltava River. He animated it by placing a shem (a slip of paper inscribed with sacred names) in its mouth or by inscribing EMET (אמת, "truth") on its forehead.

Deactivating the Golem

The golem could be deactivated by removing the shem or erasing the first letter of EMET, leaving MET (מת, "death"). Legend says the golem rests in the attic of the Old New Synagogue in Prague, awaiting reactivation should it ever be needed again.

Sacred Numbers

  • 36: Connected to the Lamed Vav Tzadikim, the 36 hidden righteous ones
  • 216: The number of letters in God's hidden name, derived from Exodus 14:19–21

Death and Veneration

Rabbi Loewe died in 1609 and is buried in the Old Jewish Cemetery of Prague. His grave remains a pilgrimage site. Visitors traditionally leave small stones and written prayers on his tomb.

Iconography

Depicted as an elderly bearded rabbi in traditional garb, often shown with the golem or holding sacred texts.

Sacred Site

Old Jewish Cemetery, Prague. The grave is marked and remains accessible to visitors.
