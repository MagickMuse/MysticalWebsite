Overview

Prthivi is the Hindu goddess of the earth, the terrestrial ground on which humans live their lives. Across the Vedic corpus her role shifts from inseparable partner of the sky god Dyaus to a sovereign deity invoked in her own right, but the same maternal character runs beneath every variation. She is the firm, far-stretched expanse that carries all beings on her back, the patient nurse who feeds plants, animals, and people from her own substance. Later Vedic material gives her an independent voice and surrounds her with a wider circle of male consorts and protectors, yet the core figure stays steady: a stable, fertile, benevolent mother.

In Vedic literature

Pairing with Dyaus in the Rg-veda

In the older Rg-vedic hymns Prthivi is almost never approached alone. She arrives through the dual compound dyavaprthivi, sky-earth, a paired name that yokes her to the male sky deity Dyaus. The two are imagined kissing the center of the world and sanctifying each other through their joined work. Together they are sung as the universal parents who gave rise to both the world and to the gods, with Dyaus called father and Prthivi called mother. One strand of the tradition holds that the pair were once a single body and were only later separated by the decree of Varuna, reuniting again whenever Dyaus releases rain into the earth, though other hymns credit the rain to the couple jointly. Because of this overlap, the older layer of the corpus does not draw a clean line between Prthivi as the earth alone and Prthivi as a being whose reach extends upward into the sky.

Supportive, firm, and abundant

Beyond her maternal aspect, Prthivi is consistently praised, often alongside Dyaus, for her supportive nature. She is called firm, the bearer who upholds all things, the one who encompasses everything, broad and wide and motionless, even as another hymn says she moves freely. Paired with Dyaus she is asked for wealth, riches, and power, and the waters the two release are described as rich, abundant, nourishing, and fertile. The pair are petitioned to shield their worshippers from harm, to expiate sin, and to bring happiness. Their joint domain is a wide, firm realm of plenty and safety threaded through with rta, the cosmic order, which they reinforce and replenish. They are called unwasting, inexhaustible, and rich in seed.

The funeral hymn

A funeral hymn instructs the dead person to enter the lap of mother earth, addressed there as gracious and kind. The mourner asks Prthivi not to bear down on the deceased but to settle over the body gently, the way a mother lifts her skirt to cover her child. Death is framed not as a passage to a separate underworld but as a return into the maternal body of the goddess.

Prthivi alone in the Atharva-veda

The most extended Vedic praise of Prthivi belongs to a long Atharva-vedic hymn devoted to her alone. Here Dyaus drops out of the picture altogether. Indra takes up the role of consort and protector, shielding her from danger. Visnu strides over her surface. Parjanya, Prajapati, and Visvakarma occupy overlapping positions, sometimes guarding her, sometimes providing for her, sometimes named as her consort. Agni is said to pervade her. Even with so many male figures around her, the hymn presents Prthivi as a great deity in her own right rather than as a passive partner to any of them.

The same hymn dwells on her fertility. She is named the source of all plants, with particular weight given to the cultivated crops, and the sustainer of every creature that walks on her. She is called patient and strong, upholding the wicked and the good and the demons and the gods all the same. The singer addresses her as mother and asks her to pour out her milk the way a mother yields milk to her son. She is praised as a nurse to all living things, her breasts said to be full of nectar, and the singer asks her to offer those breasts so that he may live long. Prthivi is also said to dwell in the scent of women and of men, to be the luck and the light in men, and to be the splendid energy of unmarried women.

Character in sum

Across these layers of Vedic material Prthivi shows up as a stable, fertile, benevolent presence. The worshippers who praise her see a warm nursing goddess who feeds and supports everything that moves on her broad, firm expanse. The Rg-veda almost always anchors her to Dyaus, but the Atharva-veda and the later Vedic literature let her step forward as an independent being.

Continuation as Bhudevi in later Hinduism

Prthivi is one of the Vedic goddesses who carries forward into the later Hindu tradition rather than fading from view. In post-Vedic material she becomes associated with Visnu and is often addressed as Bhudevi, the goddess of the earth. Her role in this later body of myth is largely that of supplicant: she comes before the gods to ask for relief from the weight of a notoriously evil demon whose burden she is forced to sustain on her body. The maternal, supportive figure of the Vedic hymns is still recognizable in this later Bhudevi, but the emphasis has shifted from her own bounty toward the strain of holding up what should not exist on her surface. In one well-known rescue she is carried off beneath the primordial waters by a demon, and Vishnu takes the form of a great boar to dive after her and lift her back into place on his tusk. In temple imagery she commonly stands on one side of Vishnu while Sri-Lakshmi stands on the other. The older Vedic picture of the earth as the broad, stable ground that supports every creature and pours out endless fertility did not disappear but was largely taken up by other goddesses, among them Sakambhari, Lakshmi, and the great goddess Mahadevi, while Bhudevi herself came to be remembered above all as the burdened earth who pleads for relief.
