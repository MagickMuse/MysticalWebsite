Jonathan ben Uzziel, a Jewish sage and folk saint, lived during the Second Temple period (1st century BCE to 1st century CE). He was the most distinguished student of Hillel the Elder, one of Judaism's greatest teachers.

The Targum

Jonathan is credited with authoring the Targum Jonathan, an Aramaic translation and interpretation of the Prophets (Nevi'im) section of the Hebrew Bible. According to the Talmud, when he wrote this translation, the spiritual intensity was so great that any bird flying overhead would be consumed by fire.

Early Death

Jonathan died young, before he could marry. According to tradition, he was so devoted to his studies that he never found time for marriage. Some accounts say he deliberately postponed marriage to focus on Torah study.

Powers and Domains

Because Jonathan died without experiencing marriage himself, he is believed to help others find their destined spouse. He is called the "Marrying Saint" and is petitioned by singles seeking love and marriage.

The Pilgrimage to Amuka

Jonathan's grave is located in Amuka, a remote valley in the Upper Galilee near Safed (Tzfat), Israel. It has become one of the most popular pilgrimage sites for Jewish singles seeking marriage.

Traditions at the Grave

  • Circling the tomb seven times
  • Lighting candles
  • Leaving written prayers or names
  • Reciting Psalms
  • Donating charity in his merit

Many people report finding their spouse within a year of visiting the grave.

Feast Day

26th of Sivan (Hebrew calendar), the traditional date of his yahrzeit (anniversary of death).

Sacred Site

Kever Yonatan ben Uziel (Tomb of Jonathan ben Uzziel), Amuka, Upper Galilee, Israel.
