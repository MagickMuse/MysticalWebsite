Saint Luke is the patron saint of artists, painters, sculptors, authors, brewers, butchers, and physicians.

Saint Luke is renowned for miraculous healings. As the first Christian physician, his relics are venerated for their healing power.

Saint Luke, author of the Gospel of Saint Luke and the Acts of the Apostles, was probably born in Syria of Greek ancestry. He may have been a slave, albeit a highly educated one. He was reputedly a talented healer and a great painter. Luke is the only non-Jewish author of the four official Gospels and is considered the first Christian physician. A companion of Saint Paul, he never married nor was martyred, dying peacefully at age eighty-four. His Gospel provides the details about Saint Dismas, the Good Thief who was promised Paradise by Jesus on the cross.

The Black Madonnas

Many Black Madonnas, both paintings and statues, are attributed to Saint Luke, who allegedly created many portraits of Mary and Jesus. He is credited with at least seventy images of the Madonna. Saint Augustine denied this legend, as do many art historians, noting that most attributed images were created around the 12th century.

However, some images may derive from Luke's time, and regardless of art expert opinions, many still identify Luke as their creator. These images are distributed worldwide, with at least ten in Russia and Rome running a close second. Saint Luke's name is attached to all sorts of mysterious, possibly subversive, aspects of Christianity.

Healing Powers

In Serbia, Saint Luke is venerated as a healing saint. His relics, taken to Smederevo in 1453, were acclaimed for producing miraculous healings.

Sacred Sites

  • Luke's relics are in the Basilica of Saint Justina in Padua, Italy. In 2001, DNA testing indicated these relics are likely authentic, with DNA extracted from a molar in the coffin.
  • Saint Luke's cranium was brought to Prague's Cathedral of Saint Vitus in 1364.
