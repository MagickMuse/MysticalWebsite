Classification

Jewish saint.

Life and History

Ephraim ben Israel Ankawa, author, physician, rabbi, master Kabbalist, miracle worker, and saint, arrived in Marrakesh, Morocco in 1391 after escaping Toledo, Spain, where his parents had been burned at the stake for being Jews. After a few years in Marrakesh, he headed east.

Dramatic Arrival

His arrival in Tlemçen, Algeria was dramatic: he rode a lion, using a huge snake for reins.

Tired and thirsty from traveling, he lacked water. Seeing a gigantic rock near the city gates, he touched it with his palm. Water gushed forth, just as it had for Moses in the desert. This spring became known as the Spring of the Rabb, though the name Tlemçen derives from the Berber Tilmisane, meaning "springs," indicating other springs predated him.

Awed by the man riding a lion with a snake, the people of Tlemçen offered their hospitality. Rabbi Ephraim settled in a ruined house on a street later called the Street of the Rabb, devoting himself to spiritual and mystical studies and serving as a healer.

The Sultan's Daughter

The beloved only daughter of Sultan Abu Tachefin (reigned 1389-1393) was severely ill with an ailment that resisted all cures. Someone suggested consulting the mysterious man who had arrived on a lion. Within days of his treatment, she was completely cured.

The overjoyed Sultan offered a reward. Rabbi Ephraim refused gold and silver, saying the reward belonged to God alone. He asked only for permission to build a small synagogue. The local Jewish community covered the costs; he sought only the permit. The Sultan granted it. That small synagogue was eventually replaced by the great Synagogue of the Rabb.

Powers and Domains

Rabbi Ephraim is invoked for virtually everything, but especially:

  • Healing
  • Fertility
  • Finding spouses

Iconography

Portrayed riding a lion and using a snake for reins.

Sacred Sites

  • His grave in Tlemçen, near Algeria's northwestern border with Morocco
  • The 15th-century grotto, the Spring of the Rabb, believed to have miraculous healing properties
  • The Synagogue of the Rabb (restored 1875 and 1890, now reportedly a martial arts center)
  • The Synagogue of Tlemcen in Paris (founded 1972 by Algerian Jewish émigrés), offering a replication of the traditional pilgrimage

Sacred Dates

  • First day of Kislev (usually Sagittarius season): anniversary of his death, communicated in a dream
  • Sixth day of Tishri (usually Libra season): when his tomb was erected
  • Lag B'Omer (18th of Iyar): traditional day to visit his shrine
