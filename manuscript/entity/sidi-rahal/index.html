Sidi Rahal is renowned for healing infertility, deafness, lameness, and throat disorders. He also breaks curses and expels demons.

Moroccan wonder worker, master of thunder and mosquitoes, and patron saint of the Oulad Sidi Rahal brotherhood.

Domains

Sidi Rahal commands thunder and mosquitoes, giving him dominion over malaria and other insect-borne diseases. During his lifetime, he demonstrated his power by taming lions. From the afterlife, he continues to cure infertility, deafness, lameness, and throat disorders.

The Oulad Sidi Rahal

Sidi Rahal is patron of the spiritual brotherhood known as the Oulad Sidi Rahal, meaning "Sons of Sidi Rahal" or "Clan of Sidi Rahal." Their rituals include snake handling and dramatic displays of spiritual power. While in states of voluntary ritual possession, members gulp boiling water and spit it out transformed to ice cold. Through these practices, they heal illness, expel demons, cast out spirits from the involuntarily possessed, and break curses and spells.

Dual Veneration

Sidi Rahal is venerated by both Muslims and Jews, with each community claiming him as their own. Historically, Muslim authorities forbade Jews from entering his shrine, forcing Jewish devotees to visit in secret.

According to legend, there may actually be two Sidi Rahals: one Muslim and one Jewish. The Muslim saint is the Master of Fire; the Jewish saint is the Master of Water. The two were rivals whose feud escalated into a magical battle of the elements. One blocked fire while the other blocked water, and local residents began dying from thirst and flames. Being holy men who never intended to cause suffering, the two saints rose above their rivalry, made peace, and restored both fire and water to the people.

The River of Hades

Legend holds that the Oued R'dat River in Morocco's High Atlas Mountains is one of the seven rivers of Hades. Once violent and deadly to ford, its waters were tamed by Sidi Rahal.

Sacred Site

Sidi Rahal's sanctuary is located in Marrakesh. A small hill near his shrine is known as the Hill of Healers, where the saint allegedly heals those who spend the night.
