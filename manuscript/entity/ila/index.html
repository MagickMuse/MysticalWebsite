Ila is a minor goddess of the Hindu Vedic tradition, tied closely to the sacrificial cult of the Rg-veda. In the Brahmanas she is known by the name Ida. She is most often invoked alongside Sarasvati, Bharati, Mahi, and Hotra, summoned to take her place on the sacrificial grass before a ritual.

In Vedic literature

Of the small group of sacrificial goddesses, Ila has the clearest profile. She appears to be linked to the offering itself, and in particular to the cow as the source of many of the materials used in the rite. The Rg-veda calls her "butter-handed" and "butter-footed," epithets that recall Agni's role as the deity who actually receives the sacrifice and conveys it to the other gods. The other goddesses in her group are mentioned so seldom, and almost always within a longer list of deities, that any reading of them as personifications of particular ritual elements remains tentative; the partial exception is Ila herself, whose ties to the offering and to the cow are at least suggestive. Why Sarasvati is so consistently grouped with these goddesses is not clear, since she has no specific connection to the rivers or materials of the sacrifice in the relevant hymns.
