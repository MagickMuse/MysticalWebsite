Saint Cecilia is the patron saint of singers, composers, musicians, musical instrument makers, and poets.

Classification

Venerated as a saint by most branches of Christianity.

Life and Legend

Cecilia was a second-century Christian martyr from Roman nobility. She had vowed herself to Christ, but her family forced her to marry. On her wedding night, she told her new husband that an angel accompanied her, one he could only see if purified through baptism.

Her husband agreed and, upon actually seeing the angel, experienced genuine conversion. His brother soon followed. Both men ministered to Christians until their arrest and martyrdom. Cecilia buried them at her villa on the Appian Way, an act that led to her own arrest.

Martyrdom

Refusing to make offerings to Roman state gods, Cecilia was condemned for treason. Authorities tried to scald or suffocate her in her mansion's bathhouse, but she proved miraculously impervious to extreme heat. When executioners attempted beheading, her half-severed head continued singing God's praises for three days before she finally died.

Her grave was discovered in 817 CE, her body incorrupt. The relics were transported to Rome's Church of Saint Cecilia.

Connection to Bona Dea

The goddess Bona Dea Oclata, who protected Roman women against blindness and eye disorders, was once venerated at a shrine in Trastevere near where the Basilica of Saint Cecilia now stands. "Cecilia" means "blind," suggesting her cult may have absorbed earlier worship of the goddess.

Powers and Domains

Cecilia clears paths and removes obstacles, both literally (for travelers) and metaphorically. Though not a musician herself, she became patron of music because her heart sang hymns and her severed head continued singing.

Iconography

A beautiful young woman playing a musical instrument. The organ typically shown with her is anachronistic, a type that didn't exist until centuries after her death. The Pre-Raphaelite painter John William Waterhouse depicted her being serenaded by angels.

Attributes

Lute, organ.

Color

Sacred Site

The Basilica of Saint Cecilia in Trastevere, Rome, built over the ruins of her home.
