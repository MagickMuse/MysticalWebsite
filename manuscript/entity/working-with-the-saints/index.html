Working with the Saints

A practical guide to establishing relationships with saints, requesting their assistance, and honoring them through veneration.

The Sacred Nature of Saints

Saints fulfill the needs of devotees and protect from harm. They bless endeavors and intervene in lives. Above all else, saints are sacred beings. This cannot be stressed enough. Standing in a saint's presence means standing in the presence of the holy, regardless of whether that saint lived a particularly holy life while breathing.

This point gets lost easily when focusing on which saint handles which practical concern. When considering how certain saints assist with love matters, travel protection, or locating lost objects, it bears remembering that these are beings worthy of reverence, not personal assistants or magickal vending machines. The saint's mere presence constitutes a gift and blessing. Saints need not perform any service for the relationship to hold profound meaning. Beyond material assistance, saints teach lessons about love, bravery, generosity, and selflessness. The miracles are a bonus, not the primary purpose.

Approaching Saints Based on Your Needs

Your approach depends entirely on what you seek. Perhaps you want nothing at all. Maybe you've discovered a saint you admire or one designated as your patron, the saint meant to serve as your spiritual guardian. Lighting a candle on their feast day as a simple gesture of respect might be entirely sufficient and deeply fulfilling. Wearing a medallion or carrying a prayer card because it brings peace of mind may be all you need. Without requiring concrete assistance, simply enjoying a saint's company is enough.

However, when facing genuine difficulty and needing sacred intervention, saints deliver miracles. This defines them. Three questions arise:

  • How do I ask for a miracle?
  • How will I know my request reached them?
  • Which saint should I approach?

Understanding Veneration

Every tradition acknowledging saints provides approved methods for contact and veneration. Veneration is central here. Saints receive veneration, not worship. That distinction matters. The word comes from Latin veneratus and means:

  1. Regarding someone with reverential respect or admiring deference
  2. Honoring through ritual acts of devotion

Despite sounding formal, veneration is anything but rigid. The word connects to Venus, the Roman goddess of love. At its root, veneration relates to love itself. Beloved saints, the renowned miracle workers, are genuinely loved. Ideally, you will find saints you love who love you in return.

The simplest path to relationship with a saint is through heartfelt veneration, the love within you. Everything else is secondary. Traditional techniques exist for communication and requesting help, but speaking your pain, needs, and deepest feelings directly to a saint works just as well. Words spoken aloud or silently in your heart carry equal weight. Though rituals and spells accompany some saints, all have historically responded to sincere cries from the heart.

Location and Communication

Many journey to pilgrimage sites linked to specific saints, believing pleas carry more weight from certain locations. Better reception may occur at these places, but location poses no barrier. Petitions have been delivered from everywhere.

A statue is not the saint. Statues serve as representations only. Saints are not lifeless objects or human creations. They are vital, responsive sacred beings. They exist beyond imagination or archetype. You speak to them, not at them, and they potentially hear and respond. Admiring a saint from distance differs from veneration, which involves genuine relationship. Healthy relationships flow both ways.

The Reciprocal Relationship

What do saints gain from this arrangement? Many saints vowed to provide help, creating a spiritual component beyond any gift devotees can offer. Still, these relationships remain reciprocal. Devotees provide veneration; saints provide assistance. Either party may initiate contact. Saints sometimes appear in dreams or visions to offer their patronage voluntarily.

Methods of Veneration

Gestures of veneration represent the simplest way to begin a relationship. The most basic and traditional gesture involves lighting candles, oil lamps, or incense. This can happen at shrines, though home altars serve most devotees worldwide. Individual saints may have specific associated rituals. Popular saints often have annual public festivals.

Tradition-Specific Practices

Roman Catholic: Saints may be petitioned or thanked through novenas, nine-day prayer rituals.

Orthodox: Saints are traditionally venerated through sacred images called icons.

Jewish: Saints are venerated at gravesites through psalm recitation or written notes detailing needs and desires, placed directly on the grave or tucked into cracks in the stone.

The Stone Ritual

Saints are not always available. They may be performing miracles elsewhere, visiting celestial realms, or communing with other saints. Leaving a stone on a saint's tomb serves as leaving a message. See The Stone Ritual for the full ritual.

Veneration remains fluid. Cultures and traditions borrow and adapt from each other. Many saints transcend denominational boundaries. If a practice resonates with you, use it or modify it to suit your spiritual needs.

La Promesa: Requesting Miracles

When desperation strikes and you truly need a miracle, the most traditional and effective method is La Promesa ("the promise"). This ancient approach exists across virtually all spiritual traditions with variations. Saints have been venerated this way for thousands of years. See La Promesa for the full ritual.

Saints and Magick

Less conventional approaches exist. Many saints are invoked through magick spells, though this remains controversial and rarely receives official approval. Ironically, saints most associated with magick (the so-called "magickal saints") rank among the oldest, most venerated, and officially recognized. These saints participate in workings they likely would have condemned while alive, at least according to their recorded histories. Perhaps they reconsidered after death. Or perhaps they provide acceptable facades allowing devotees continued access to older spiritual practices.

Not all saints connect with magick. Where applicable, spells appear in individual saint entries.

Spells Versus Prayers

Spells and prayers are closely related, but prayers request while spells demand.

Principles for Working Magick with Saints

Coincidences as Communication: Magickal philosophy holds that coincidences don't exist. They are signs requiring attention. Not all carry equal significance, but all deserve consideration. Saints communicate through apparent coincidences. If you petition a saint in the morning and receive an unexpected image of their symbol by afternoon, don't dismiss it. This may be direct confirmation that your request arrived. Pay attention. More messages may follow. Ignoring coincidences equals discarding unopened mail.

The Core of Every Spell: Many casting styles exist, but every spell's crucial component is its goal. You must want the outcome wholeheartedly, truly, and deeply.

Anyone Can Cast: Experience helps but isn't required. Beginners have luck. Not every spell succeeds, even for the experienced, though they recognize failure faster and adjust. You need not hire professionals. Self-cast spells carry the most power because no one wants your goals more than you do. Spell-casting is a learnable skill. Natural talent varies, but anyone can develop ability through effort.

Emotional Fuel: Focus intensely on your desire while casting. Fear, longing, and passionate emotion activate your spell.

Visualization: During casting, see your goal as already achieved. Seeking health? Visualize yourself vital and pain-free. Seeking fertility? Picture yourself holding a baby. This step is necessary even when painful or when hope feels distant. Force yourself to envision the best outcome.

Present Tense: Spells and affirmations are spoken as if already true. Say "My illness is healed" rather than "My illness will be healed." Future tense keeps goals perpetually out of reach.

Magick Complements Action: Spells supplement practical effort. They don't replace it. Petitioning for employment still requires showing up for interviews. Magick doesn't substitute for medical care. In emergencies, invoke spiritual help and call emergency services.

Working Together

Those who actively venerate saints often speak of "working with" them. Saint and devotee collaborate to solve seemingly impossible situations. The devotee is not a passive recipient. Saint veneration, and spellwork generally, does not encourage passivity. It enables the living to shape their own destiny in partnership with the sacred dead.

When Things Don't Work

Spells are not recipes with guaranteed outcomes. They are unpredictable by nature. Otherwise they would be science experiments, not magick. Sometimes they exceed expectations. Sometimes they work differently than anticipated. Sometimes they fail entirely. The same applies to miracles. If miracles were predictable, they would cease to be miraculous.

Saints sometimes don't answer. Occasionally they explain why through dreams or communication. Often the reason remains mysterious, perhaps revealed only when you can confront them directly in the afterlife.

Possible Explanations for Failure

  • Wrong spell for your situation
  • Spell cast incorrectly (try again, especially if you struggled to focus or lacked privacy)
  • Something angered the saint
  • You requested something you believe is best, but the saint knows otherwise

Many practitioners later feel relieved when desired outcomes didn't materialize. What seemed desperately wanted proved not to serve our true interests. Saints sometimes know something better awaits, something your current request would prevent. You might pray to preserve a relationship while the saint knows a better partner comes after it ends.

Spiritual growth sometimes requires pain we would choose to avoid. Or perhaps the saint simply hasn't received your appeal yet. Or you approached the wrong saint entirely, the spiritual equivalent of knocking on the wrong door. No harm comes from this, but you may be ignored.

Spells, rituals, and appeals succeed most reliably when working with saints who are right for you: your personal allies and guardians.

Finding Your Saints

Thousands of saints exist. How do you find the right one for you? Fortunately, various techniques can help identify your personal saints.

The simplest method is to explore descriptions of different saints. Pay attention to which ones resonate most deeply. Whose story touched you profoundly? If you find yourself spontaneously thinking of a saint late at night or cannot get a story out of your mind, this may indicate a personal connection.

Some people devote themselves to only one saint. Others maintain relationships with an entire pantheon. Each individual's needs are different.

Patronages

Saints possess "patronages," meaning that something or someone is under their charge and care. Saints are affiliated with professions, hobbies, ethnicities, locations, and virtually everything imaginable. If you are a mime, for instance, Saint Julian the Hospitaller is your professional patron and may be appealed to for help.

Some saints patronize illnesses and conditions. Those suffering from Parkinson's disease might appeal to Pope John Paul II. Saints who specialize in specific crises or disasters may assist in those situations even if they are not otherwise your primary saint. If a dangerous storm approaches, you can invoke Saint Barbara even if she is not technically your matron saint.

Saints Across Traditions

Although this upsets some devotees, many (if not most) saints perform miracles for those outside their original spiritual tradition. In death, many saints are far more tolerant than they ever were while alive. In North Africa, Muslims and Jews traditionally invoke each other's saints. The most famous wonder-working saints, like Rita or Anthony of Padua, perform miracles for all. Saints from spiritual traditions not your own may still assist you. Never assume they will not, particularly if other signs indicate some sort of bond.

Clues to Your Personal Patrons

Shared Name: Many traditions consider that sharing a name creates an unbreakable bond. A saint whose name you share may be obligated to serve as your patron. This is one reason why so many name their children after powerful saints.

Birth Date: Were you born on a day associated with a saint? This may indicate patronage. If 17 January is your birthday, then Roseline of Villeneuve, who celebrates her feast on that day, may be your saint.

Birth Location: If you were born in a location or hospital named after a saint, you may be able to claim that saint's patronage as well.
