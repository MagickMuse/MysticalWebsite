Ardhanarisvara, "the lord being half woman," is the god Shiva combined with his Shakti as a single being.

Iconography

In iconographic depiction the left side of the image is female and the right side is male. The form may also appear three-headed. The attendant animal is the bull. Attributes carried on the right (Shiva) side include the blue lotus, cup, hatchet, lute, moon disc, pestle, skin, sword, and trident. Attributes on the left (Shakti) side include the ax, mirror, noose, pitcher, rosary, sacred rope, and trident.

Tutelary Role

Ardhanarisvara is a tutelary deity of eunuchs in India.

Magickal Properties

Represents the inseparable unity of masculine and feminine principles, the perfect balance of Shiva and Shakti. This divine form embodies the cosmic truth that creation requires the union of opposites, and that the ultimate reality transcends gender binaries.

Ritual Uses

Work with Ardhanarishvara for understanding the integration of masculine and feminine energies within yourself, rituals celebrating the unity of opposites, couples seeking deeper harmony, and spiritual practices aimed at transcending duality.

Offerings

Bilva leaves, flowers, water, milk, and incense; offerings that honor both Shiva and Shakti equally.

Favored People

Yogis, artists, couples seeking harmony, gender-fluid individuals, and anyone seeking to integrate opposing forces within themselves.

Petition For

Union of opposites, fertility, creative power, inner balance, spiritual enlightenment, and transcendence of duality.

Warnings

Do not venerate Shiva and Shakti halves separately when invoking this form; approach with reverence and respect for balance and integration.

Additional Notes

Usually depicted as half male (Shiva) and half female (Shakti/Parvati), this form represents the highest philosophical truth that apparent opposites are actually unified.
