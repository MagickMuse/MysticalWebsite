Budha, whose name means awakening, is an astral god who personifies the planet Mercury. He appears in both Hindu and Buddhist traditions, and his worship extends from the Vedic through the Epic and Puranic centuries of the Hindu record into the ongoing planetary devotions of both religions.

Domains

The planet Mercury and its astral influence, the awakening of intellect, and the swift mercurial powers of communication and inquiry that gather around the planet in both Hindu and Buddhist astrology.

Hindu Form

In the Hindu Vedic, Epic, and Puranic traditions Budha is the son of Soma, also called Chandra, the moon god, and of either Tara or Rohini according to the received account of his conception. His mythic origin lies in the well-known episode in which Soma takes Tara from her rightful husband Brhaspati, and Budha is born of that union between the moon and the taken bride.

He rides in a chariot drawn by eight horses, and in some accounts by lions, and sometimes by a single lion. His color is yellow. His attributes are the bow, the club, the rosary, the shield, and the sword. He is also called Candraja, son of Chandra, and Candrasuta, offspring of Chandra, both epithets that trace his descent from the moon god.

Buddhist Form

In the Buddhist tradition Budha likewise personifies the planet Mercury, absorbed into the wider Buddhist astral pantheon. In this form he is depicted standing upon a lotus, and his attributes are the bow and the arrow.

Iconographic Continuity

Across both traditions Budha's yellow color and his bow mark him as the same astral figure, refined in each religious setting into the mercurial power appropriate to that setting. In the Hindu telling he is a princely charioteer with the full array of royal weapons; in the Buddhist telling he is the still, lotus-mounted planetary deity whose bow and arrow signal the swiftness of thought.
