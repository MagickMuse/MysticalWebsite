Agni

Agnayi is a minor goddess of the Hindu Vedic tradition, the consort of the fire god Agni. Her name is built by attaching a feminine suffix to the name of her husband, in keeping with the pattern that produces a small group of Vedic consort goddesses.

In Vedic literature

Agnayi appears only a few times in the hymns and carries no independent character of her own. The texts give her no narrative, no extended description, and no separate sphere of action; she stands in the pantheon as a feminine counterpart to Agni rather than as a goddess with her own following.

The consort goddesses of this type are most often discussed today in connection with the much later concept of sakti, the personified power of a god expressed through his female partner. That theology is not yet present in the Vedic material, but the early existence of figures like Agnayi has been read as one of its distant precursors.
