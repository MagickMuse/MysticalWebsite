Saint Germaine Cousin is renowned for healing ailments of the spine and hip, restoring sight to the blind, and curing terminal breast cancer.

Classification

Roman Catholic saint.

Life and History

Germaine (1579-1601) was born to a poor farming family near Toulouse, France. She was a sickly child with a withered, partially paralyzed right arm and suffered from scrofula, a type of disfiguring skin ailment.

Her mother died when Germaine was an infant. Her father's second wife was the proverbial wicked stepmother.

The Cruel Stepmother

The stepmother forced Germaine to live in the stable, claiming this was necessary to protect her own children from infection. Germaine was sent to labor as a shepherdess at age nine. She never owned a pair of shoes.

Her stepmother beat and starved her and punished alleged infractions by scalding her with boiling water.

Piety and Mockery

Germaine spent much of her time praying. Neighbors and relatives mocked her piety.

The Miracle of the Flowers

The earliest miracles associated with Germaine occurred during her lifetime. Her stepmother accused her of stealing bread from the kitchen. Threatening to beat her with a stick, she ordered Germaine to open her apron and reveal what was hidden within. Germaine complied and flowers, not bread, tumbled out.

Similar miracles are associated with Saints Elizabeth of Hungary and Casilda.

Death and Incorruption

Germaine died on her pallet in the stable at age twenty-two. Forty years later, her body was accidentally exhumed and discovered to be incorrupt, showing no signs of decay. This is traditionally interpreted as a sign of sanctity.

Miracles

Miracles of healing were immediately attributed to her. Over 400 miracles have been documented, ranging from restoring sight to the blind to curing terminal breast cancer. She is especially invoked to heal ailments of the spine and hip.

She is also invoked against poverty and powerlessness.

Iconography

Votive images invariably portray her as a sturdy, healthy, pretty barefoot girl wearing fresh, clean clothing, usually depicted with her sheep or a watchdog. She may have an apron filled with flowers.

Attributes

Distaff, shepherd's crook.

Feast Day

15 June
