Classification

Roman Catholic saint.

Life and Martyrdom

Born in Braga (now in Portugal), Saint Engrácia was martyred with eighteen companions in 303 CE. She is counted among the Martyrs of Zaragoza.

Engrácia was a young woman from an elegant family, engaged to marry a nobleman living in the Pyrenees. She traveled to her wedding with an impressive entourage: her uncle, sixteen knights, and a female chaperon and servant named Julia.

When the party reached Zaragoza (now in Spain), they discovered Christians were being persecuted. Engrácia and her companions protested to the authorities, only to find themselves subject to the same persecution. She was brutally whipped and imprisoned. Her entire party was sentenced to beheading, but Engrácia died of her wounds before the sentence could be carried out.

The Martyrs of Zaragoza

Engrácia's wedding party comprises only part of the Martyrs of Zaragoza. Others were killed as well. Hymns honoring the martyrs were soon composed and their fame spread widely. Although all are venerated as saints, Engrácia, the young beautiful bride, received the greatest devotion.

Powers and Domains

Saint Engrácia protects against the Evil Eye and banishes it.

Iconography

Envisioned as a richly dressed young lady, though classical painters often preferred to portray her being tortured in various states of undress.

Sacred Sites

  • Basilica of Saint Engrácia in Zaragoza, Spain, built over the spot where she and her companions died
  • Church of Saint Engrácia in Lisbon, initially sponsored by a 16th-century Portuguese princess, later rebuilt and converted into Portugal's National Pantheon in the 20th century
