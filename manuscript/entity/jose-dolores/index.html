José Dolores, an Argentine folk saint not officially recognized by the Catholic Church and known as "Protector of the Poor," was a gaucho who lived in San Juan Province during the mid-19th century. Details of his life are sparse, but he became known as a defender of the poor and downtrodden.

Death

José Dolores was killed on February 14, 1858. The circumstances of his death transformed him into a martyr figure. Some accounts describe him dying while defending others or as a victim of injustice.

Powers and Domains

José Dolores is petitioned for:

  • Fertility: Helping couples conceive
  • Healing: General health concerns
  • Eye ailments: Specific patronage for vision problems
  • Social anxiety: Helping those who struggle in social situations
  • Protection: Especially for the poor and marginalized

Veneration

As a folk saint, José Dolores is venerated through:

  • Roadside shrines throughout Argentina
  • Offerings of water bottles (a common gaucho saint tradition)
  • Candles and flowers
  • Prayers and petitions written on paper

Sacred Site

Primary shrine located in San Juan Province, Argentina, where pilgrims gather especially around February 14, the anniversary of his death.

Note on Identity

José Dolores is sometimes confused with the Difunta Correa (Deolinda Correa), another Argentine folk saint. While both are gaucho saints associated with roadside shrines and water bottle offerings, they are distinct figures with different legends.
