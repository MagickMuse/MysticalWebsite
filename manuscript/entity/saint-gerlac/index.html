Saint Gerlac is renowned for healing and protecting animals, especially domestic animals and livestock.

Classification

Roman Catholic saint.

Life and History

Gerlac was born around 1100 in Holland. He led a wild, dissolute life as a soldier, mercenary, and highwayman until his wife died. Completely devastated, perhaps experiencing a spiritual awakening or seeking penance, he transformed himself into a holy wanderer.

He traveled to Rome and Jerusalem and spent seven years caring for the sick before returning to Holland around 1157.

The Hermit in the Oak

Gerlac was wealthy. Upon his return, he donated everything to the poor and became a hermit. He moved into a hollowed-out oak on property he had once owned, subsisting on bread mixed with ashes and making daily pilgrimages on foot to the Basilica of Saint Servatius in Maastricht.

Local peasants perceived him as a holy man, but local monks were annoyed, feeling threatened as people sought Gerlac's guidance rather than theirs.

The Accusation

Appealing to their bishop, the monks accused Gerlac of hoarding wealth by hiding it in his oak tree. The bishop ordered the tree chopped down. Gerlac appealed and received assistance from powerful friends, including Hildegard of Bingen, but to no avail. The oak was cut down. No treasure was found.

The bishop sought to make amends by using the tree's wood to construct a small shack for Gerlac.

Final Miracles

Near the end of his life, mystical phenomena manifested. Water from a local well transformed into wine three times, possibly signs that his time of penance had ended. He died on January 5 (year variously given as 1164-1172).

Legend has it that Saint Servatus himself administered the last rites, which may indicate the local priesthood refused him.

Powers and Domains

Gerlac is the patron of animals, especially domestic animals and livestock. He may also be invoked to protect trees.

Iconography

Portrayed standing within his hollow tree, often with a cow and donkey nearby. He may have a thorn in his foot.

Sacred Site

Sint-Gerlachus Church in Houthern-Sint Gerlach, Netherlands. Features fifteen frescoes depicting his life and contains his tomb.

Feast Day

5 January
