The Sun God

Ra rules as Egypt's supreme solar deity and creator. His daily voyage carries him across the heavens in a celestial vessel. At night he descends into the underworld, the Duat, where the chaos serpent Apophis awaits. Ra defeats this enemy and rises renewed at dawn. This perpetual cycle of destruction and regeneration anchors Egyptian cosmology.

The Secret Name

Egyptian tradition held that Ra possessed a hidden name containing his essential power. He protected this name absolutely. Isis, ambitious to surpass all other practitioners, devised a scheme to obtain it.

Ra had grown old. Saliva dripped from his mouth as he walked. Isis collected this substance, blended it with soil, and formed a serpent. She spoke words of power over the figure, animating it. She placed the creature along Ra's path.

The serpent bit. Venom coursed through the sun god. Agony overwhelmed him. He cried out for aid. Isis appeared, promising healing if he disclosed his true name.

Ra offered alternatives: titles, epithets, public names. Isis refused each. The poison advanced. Sight abandoned him. Finally, Ra allowed his hidden name to transfer directly into her being. She became Weret Hekau, possessing supreme magical authority.

What that name actually was remains unknown. Some suggest it may have been Heka, the word for magic itself, a force predating the gods.

Protector and Protected

Despite surrendering his secret name, Ra retained supremacy. Other deities accompany him in his solar vessel. Set defends against Apophis during the dangerous night crossing. Goddesses including Hathor and Sekhmet serve as the "Eye of Ra," ferocious protectors who strike when he faces threat.

Role in Egyptian Magick

Ra's cycle embodies the pattern of creation, death, and renewal underlying Egyptian magickal practice. The Pyramid Texts, humanity's oldest religious writings, describe deceased kings journeying to join Ra in his eternal voyage. Royal names followed the formula di ankh ("given life"), invoking his generative power.

What Ra represents in Egyptian magick:

  • Creative speech that brings existence into being
  • The recurring pattern of death followed by rebirth
  • Cosmic order (maat) requiring active maintenance
  • The vulnerability that accompanies even supreme power

Receiving Osiris Through the Lotus

The water lily carried Egyptian funerary current as the bloom associated with Osiris. After Set killed and dismembered him, Isis collected all his parts, minus one, and revived him just enough to ensure his passage on to Ra.

Iconography

Ra manifests as a falcon-headed man wearing a solar disc encircled by a uraeus cobra. He also appears as the scarab Khepri (morning sun), the full disc at midday, and the ram-headed Atum (evening sun).

Sacred Associations

  • Creature: Falcon, scarab, ram
  • Trees: Persea, sycamore, willow, acacia
  • Sacred Sites: Heliopolis served as his cult center; temples stood throughout Egypt

Ra-Horakhty

Ra-Horakhty ("Ra-Horus of the Horizon") is a syncretic form combining Ra with Horus. This composite deity represents the sun at the horizon, both rising and setting, embodying the continuous solar cycle. Ra-Horakhty appears on the Metternich Stela alongside the Horus-on-the-crocodiles scene, watching over the protective composition from within the sacred enclosure.

Auf and the Flashlight Blessing

Auf is an aspect of Ra, the god of the Sun in Ancient Egypt, so who better than the guiding light through the underworld to bless your flashlight in case of emergency?

Draw a sun on your flashlight and anoint with diluted frankincense oil, or pass through burning frankincense smoke, as frankincense was burned in the morning. Ask for the flashlight to help you find your way in your own personal underworld in case of emergency.
