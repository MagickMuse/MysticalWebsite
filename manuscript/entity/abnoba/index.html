Magickal Properties

Abnoba is the Goddess of the Black Forest, now in modern Germany, a region with many therapeutic springs including the famed Baden-Baden. The etymology of her Celtic name is related to "wetness" or "river" and is closely related to Avon, a popular name for rivers. She possesses strong associations with wild nature, healing springs, fertility, and women's rites.

Ritual Uses

Create rituals at therapeutic springs or natural water sources. Work with her for healing practices involving spring water. Petition her for preservation of wild nature, forest plants, and animals. Honor her at locations associated with forest healing and women's mysteries.

Offerings

Black Forest Cake, Black Forest ham, spring water, efforts on behalf of wild nature preservation. Pilgrimage to springs near the Black Forest region. Present offerings that honor both her forest and healing aspects.

Favored People

Those with strong associations to the Black Forest or woods in general, environmental conservationists, people seeking healing through natural springs, practitioners of women's mysteries.

Petition For

Help preserve wild nature, forest plants, and animals. Assistance with healing through natural springs. Protection of sacred forest spaces. Support for fertility and women's rites.

Warnings

Decorate her altar like a deep forest with pine or fir cones and spring water. Offer spring-water or local forest foods and remove perishables the same day. Do not substitute urban-style offerings or perform loud public rites because she is a Black Forest spring and wild-nature goddess.

Additional Notes

The Romans identified her with Diana. A Roman altar was dedicated to Diana Abnoba at the warm mineral springs of Badenweiler at the Black Forest's edge. Abnoba also names a mountain from which the source of the Danube River flows, though this mountain's modern identity has not been determined.
