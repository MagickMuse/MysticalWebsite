Ymir is the primordial being of Nordic cosmogony, the first living creature to take shape within the misty void of Ginnungagap. According to the account preserved by Snorri Sturluson in the Prose Edda, Ymir arose at the meeting of the fires of Muspellsheimr with the rime and ice of Niflheimr, the two extremes of the primal void yielding a living body when they met. From the sweat of Ymir's body the first generation of frost giants was born, and Ymir himself is understood in some readings as an androgynous or double-sexed being from whom all subsequent giant lineage descends.

The Nourisher and the Nourished

Ymir was nourished by the milk of the cosmic cow Audhumla, who came into being alongside him. Audhumla in turn fed herself by licking the salty blocks of primordial ice, and through her licking she released a second being, Buri, from within the ice. Buri was the ancestor of the gods: through his son Bor, Buri's line produced the Aesir gods Odin, Vili, and Ve.

The Sacrifice

Odin, Vili, and Ve, sons of Bor, slew Ymir, and from the vast body of the primordial giant they built the ordered cosmos. His flesh became the earth, his blood the seas, his bones the mountains, his teeth the stones, his skull the vault of the sky, and his brains the clouds. The maggots that arose within his flesh were given form and intelligence and became the dwarves. In this sacrificial cosmogony the whole visible world is the transfigured body of the primal giant, and the Aesir order is built upon his substance.

Precautions

Ymir is a primordial cosmic being, not a devotional figure in the ordinary sense. His mythology belongs to the level of cosmogony and the underlying pattern by which the world was made. Approach his name as a reference to the substance of the created world itself, not as a personal deity to be petitioned.
