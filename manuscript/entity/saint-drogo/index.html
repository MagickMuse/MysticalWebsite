Saint Drogo is the patron saint of baristas, coffeehouses, midwives, and the coffee trade.

Saint Drogo is renowned for healing depression, gallstones, kidney stones, and broken bones.

Classification

Roman Catholic saint.

Life and History

Saint Drogo's (1105-1186) mother died in childbirth. Until he was ten, he didn't understand what had happened to her. When he learned, he was devastated, blaming himself for her death. Much of his life was spent trying to expiate what he perceived as a heinous sin.

Orphaned by eighteen, he disposed of all his property and became a wandering pilgrim, traveling to Rome nine times from his home in Flanders. He worked as a shepherd for six years.

Bilocation

Drogo allegedly possessed the ability to bilocate, being in two places simultaneously. Witnesses placed him at Mass while simultaneously working in the fields.

Disfigurement and Isolation

Some physical affliction or skin ailment made Drogo appear frighteningly unattractive. People were scared of him and didn't want to see him. He spent the last forty years of his life as a solitary hermit, subsisting only on water, barley, and communion wafers passed through a small window in his cell. This was his only human contact.

Powers and Domains

Saint Drogo is the patron saint of coffee, presiding over coffeehouses, their employees, and anyone in the coffee trade. If you love coffee passionately, he may be your saint.

He is also invoked against hangovers, mental illness, hernias, headaches, ruptures, gallstones, kidney stones, and kidney diseases. He alleviates depression and heals broken bones.

Iconography

Traditionally portrayed caring for sheep or in pilgrim's garb. He may wear the scallop shell indicating pilgrimage to Compostela. Modern depictions frequently show him drinking coffee.

Feast Day

16 April
