Hyacinth

Hyacinth is the divine patron of gay lovers, described as the youngest and most beautiful son of a Spartan king. Both Apollo and Zephyr fell in love with him, but Hyacinth chose Apollo. The Muse Erato also lusted and competed for him. Zephyr came upon Hyacinth and Apollo playing discus and in a blindingly jealous rage killed Hyacinth. Grief-stricken Apollo caused a plant to spring from Hyacinth's spilled blood.

Ritual Uses

Hyacinth was arrested at an eternally youthful stage. He is a spirit of joy, vigor, Earth's agricultural abundance, and musical skill. In life, Apollo tutored him in sports, divination, and music. In death, Hyacinth transmits this wisdom to people as the conduit to Apollo's knowledge. The two deities are venerated together.

Festival Offerings

At the annual Hyacinthus festival, he naturally received the first offerings, with Apollo receiving the next offerings.

Warnings

The hyacinthos plant was used to delay male puberty, prolonging adolescence. It was a favorite of Greek slave traders as adolescent boys were particularly valuable and brought a high price.

A large statue of Apollo stood before Hyacinth's tomb. His festival was not exclusively male; girls and women also played significant roles. He is also venerated alongside his sister Polyboea.
