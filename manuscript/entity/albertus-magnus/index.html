Albertus Magnus is the patron saint of alchemists, natural scientists, and philosophers.

Overview

Albertus Magnus (c. 1200 to 15 November 1280), known as Albert the Great, was a Dominican friar, bishop, and one of the most influential scholars of the medieval period. His encyclopedic knowledge earned him the title Doctor Universalis (Universal Doctor), and his contributions to philosophy, theology, and natural science made him a legendary figure in both academic and esoteric traditions.

Life and Scholarship

Born in Lauingen, Bavaria, Albert joined the Dominican Order around 1223. He studied and taught at various centers of learning, most notably at the University of Paris and in Cologne, where he established an influential school. His most famous student was Thomas Aquinas, whom he recognized as a genius despite the young student's quiet demeanor.

Albert's intellectual output was staggering. He wrote commentaries on virtually all of Aristotle's works, making Greek philosophy accessible to medieval European scholars. He also produced original treatises on logic, theology, botany, zoology, astronomy, mineralogy, and alchemy.

Natural Philosophy and Alchemy

Albert approached the natural world with a spirit of empirical inquiry unusual for his time. He insisted on direct observation and experimentation, writing detailed accounts of plants, animals, and minerals based on his own investigations. His botanical works contain descriptions of herbs, their properties, and their uses in medicine and magick.

His association with alchemy and natural magick, while complex, established him as a towering figure in Western esotericism. Works attributed to him (some authentic, many pseudonymous) circulated widely and influenced generations of practitioners. The Liber Aggregationis (Book of Secrets), whether authentically his or not, bears his name and contains extensive magickal and alchemical lore.

Canonization and Patronage

Albert was beatified in 1622 and canonized by Pope Pius XI in 1931, who also declared him a Doctor of the Church. He was named patron saint of natural scientists by Pope Pius XII in 1941.

Feast Day

November 15

Iconography

Albertus Magnus is typically depicted in Dominican habit, often holding a book or surrounded by scientific instruments. He may be shown teaching or in scholarly contemplation.
