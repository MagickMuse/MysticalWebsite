Overview

Kanniyakumari is the virgin goddess enshrined at the southern tip of the Indian subcontinent, where the land reaches the meeting of the seas. She is honored as a form of the Great Goddess in her unmarried, self-contained aspect, and her perpetual virginity is the very source of her formidable power.

The Maiden Who Did Not Marry

Her myth tells that she was to wed Shiva but was prevented from the marriage for various reasons. Though she is described as distressed at being kept from the husband she desired, the tradition dwells less on her sorrow than on the immense strength of her unmarried state. In the cultural logic of the Tamil south, a maiden who withholds her sexual energy stores up power in the same way that an ascetic stores up heat by retaining his vital force. Her celibacy is not emptiness but a gathering and a containment of power.

Power in Self-Containment

Quite simply, Kanniyakumari is far more powerful as an unmarried maiden than she would be as a wife. Her independence from any male consort is the ground of her potency rather than a lack. She stands at the edge of the land as a fierce, self-possessed guardian whose strength is entirely her own.

In Magick

Kanniyakumari is called on for strength that is held and concentrated rather than spent, for independence and freedom from domination, for protection, and for the focused will of one who answers to no other. Her pattern teaches the power of withholding, of keeping one's energy gathered and undivided, and turning it toward one's own purpose.
