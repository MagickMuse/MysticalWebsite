Saint Willibrord is renowned for healing chorea (Saint Vitus Dance), epilepsy, seizure disorders, lumbago, herpes, and nervous disorders—especially in children.

Classification

Venerated as a saint by most branches of Christianity.

Overview

Saint Willibrord (658-7 November 739) was an English missionary to Denmark and the Netherlands and the first bishop of Utrecht. He worked among the Pagan communities of the Low Countries, actively destroying shrines, desecrating sacred wells, and toppling idols. He died while founding the Benedictine Abbey in Echternach, Luxembourg. He is also considered a patron of Luxembourg.

Healer of Dance Mania

Willibrord became famous during the Middle Ages as the saint best able to combat and cure the dance mania known as Saint Vitus Dance. Dancing for Saint Willibrord allegedly enables people to gain control over their bodies and dance only when they choose. This dancing is also said to heal nervous disorders.

Powers and Domains

  • Healing chorea (Saint Vitus Dance)
  • Curing epilepsy and other seizure disorders
  • Relieving lumbago
  • Healing all forms of herpes
  • Treating nervous disorders, especially in children

The Dancing Procession

Willibrord is honored at his annual Dancing Procession in Echternach, Luxembourg, held on Pentecost Tuesday. This unique ritual consists of three steps forward and five steps back, first encircling the saint's tomb and then leaving the church. It remains one of the most distinctive religious processions in Christianity.

Sacred Sites

  • Benedictine Abbey, Echternach, Luxembourg: His tomb lies in the crypt behind the high altar.
  • Willibrord Springs: Various springs and wells throughout Luxembourg, Belgium, and the Netherlands bear his name. These are reputed to heal nervous disorders, particularly in children.
