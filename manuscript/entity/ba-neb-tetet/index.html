Magickal Properties

  • Ram‑headed lord of Mendes who upholds Ma’at, sacred order, and truthful speech
  • Patron of councils, negotiations, and peaceful decision‑making

Ritual Uses

  • Petition for conflict resolution, fair mediation, and alignment to just order
  • Seek calm eloquence and wise compromise in legal matters

Offerings

  • Dignified offerings at his shrines; vows to uphold Ma’at in speech and deed

Favored People

Mediators, diplomats, negotiators, and any who must reconcile opposing sides

Petition For

Clarity, peaceful settlements, and restoration of communal balance

Warnings

Do not attempt deceit or manipulation before him; dishonesty disrupts sacred order and offends his presence.

Additional Notes

  • Name forms: Banebdedet, Banebdjedet
  • Associations: Thoth, Ma’at, Osiris, and Horus within councils of judgment

Iconography

Generally depicted in anthropomorphic form, but with the head of a ram.

Family

His consort is the fish goddess Hatmehyt, and he is the father of Harpokrates.

Sacred Sites

His cult is centered on Mendes in the Delta region of Lower Egypt, at Tell el-Ruba, and is closely linked with the mother of Rameses III. Some accounts place him in Upper Egypt on the island of Seheil at the first Nile cataract.

Lore

Ba Neb Tetet stands among the earliest Egyptian fertility deities, invoked especially at his cult center at Mendes in Lower Egypt. In later Egyptian religion, the term ba came to signify the spirituality or soul of a deity, often embodied in a sacred animal such as the bull, or manifested in mortal form in the pharaoh as the living presence of a god.

According to tradition, Banebdjedet was called upon to intercede in the contest for the Egyptian kingdoms between Horus and Seth.
