Kotavi is a fierce, disheveled battlefield goddess of Hindu tradition, sometimes counted among the Matrkas and remembered as a wild, naked guardian who terrifies enemies in war.

Overview

Kotavi is a fierce goddess associated above all with the battlefield. She is sometimes included in lists of the Matrkas, the mother goddesses, and is remembered for her nakedness, her wild and disheveled appearance, and her terrifying presence in combat.

Appearance and Nature

Older Hindu accounts describe her as naked, disheveled, and so dreadful to behold that even great gods turn their faces away from her rather than risk being overwhelmed by anger at the sight. Her chief role is to terrify and distract enemies during battle. Her haunt is the field of war rather than the cremation ground, though both are places of death.

Protector of Banasura

In one well-known myth she comes to the defense of the demon Banasura, described in some accounts as her son. She places herself between him and his divine attacker in an effort to shield him, using her fearsome form to unsettle his opponent.

Connection to Chinnamasta

Kotavi has been proposed as one of the possible older prototypes for Chinnamasta, since both are fierce and naked goddesses. Their characters differ, though: Kotavi is a battlefield figure whose nature is usually malevolent, while Chinnamasta belongs to the cremation ground and is fierce without being described as malevolent. The resemblance lies mainly in the shared imagery of the wild, naked goddess rather than in identical roles.

In Magick

Kotavi is called on in workings that confront enemies, break the courage of opponents, and fiercely protect one's own. Her energy suits protective and battle magick where the aim is to terrify, distract, or drive back a threat.
