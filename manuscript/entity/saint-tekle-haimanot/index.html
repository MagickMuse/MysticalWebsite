Ethiopian Orthodox and Coptic saint, among the most beloved of Ethiopian holy figures.

Birth and Childhood

Tekle Haimanot (c. 1215-1313) claimed descent from a long line of priests. Miracles attended his birth and childhood. Michael Archangel appeared to his parents before he was born, advising them that their future child would be blessed. His very first words were a protest against receiving his mother's milk on a fast day. He memorized all the psalms and was ordained by age fifteen.

Ministry

Tekle traversed Ethiopia performing feats and miracles, including raising the dead. He studied under the country's great monastic saints and made three pilgrimages to Jerusalem. He founded the monastery of Debre Libanos, north of Addis Ababa, which still exists today.

The Wings

Tekle is usually portrayed with wings, for which various explanations exist. According to one version, he prayed for seven years standing on one leg until the other limb dropped off. Angels brought him wings so he would not be immobilized. Alternatively, he lived in a monastery on such a steep hill that ropes were required to descend. When a rope broke and trapped him, he invoked Michael Archangel, who brought him wings.

Tekle died of plague just four months before his hundredth birthday. He promised to protect those who call on him and honor his feast.

Number

Iconography

Tekle Haimanot is depicted with three sets of wings on his back.

Attributes

Eight spears with which he surrounded himself to prevent falling asleep while praying. Bells. His detached leg, which may be shown brandished by an angel, floating toward Heaven, or flying upward on its own wings.

Sacred Day

The twenty-fourth day of each month of the Ethiopian calendar is dedicated to Tekle.

Feast Day

17 August.

Sacred Sites

  • Debre Libanos, the cave hermitage north of Addis Ababa that Tekle founded in the thirteenth century. His tomb may be visited there. The current church was built in 1961. The cave where Tekle prayed, a short walk up the hill, is the source of the monastery's Holy Water, which reputedly protects against demons and heals stomach disorders.
  • Saint Takla Himanout's Coptic Church in Ibrahimia, Alexandria, Egypt, which contains relics of the saint.
