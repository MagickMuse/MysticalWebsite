Overview

Cacoch is a creator god of the classical Mesoamerican Mayan tradition of Mexico, known also under the name Kacoch. In one strand of Mayan cosmogonic tradition, he engendered the water lily from which all the other deities of the Mayan pantheon then sprang. He is also portrayed as a messenger of the great creator god Hachacyum, carrying divine communication between the highest creator and the wider pantheon.

⚠️ Cacoch is venerated within Mayan and Indigenous Mesoamerican traditions. These are closed practices and should only be approached by those within the originating cultures or with explicit cultural permission.

Domains

Cacoch presides over the origin of the gods, the primordial water lily as the seedbed of the divine pantheon, and the office of divine messenger and intermediary. His work belongs to the beginning of the pantheon and to the continuing traffic of message and instruction between the creator and the wider circle of powers.

Iconography

Cacoch appears in Mayan cosmogonic imagery in association with the water lily, the flower from which the gods of the pantheon emerged. His messenger office links him to Hachacyum, and he is placed within the same iconographic family as the other classical Mayan creator and attendant deities.

Ritual

Cacoch belongs to the classical Mesoamerican Mayan religious tradition of Mexico, a closed practice rooted in specific cultural and initiatory context. Treat this entry as reference only. Do not adapt or perform Mayan rites outside the living lineage held by the indigenous communities that carry them.

Lore

The water lily cosmogony marks Cacoch as one of the origin points of the Mayan pantheon: the flower he engenders is the seed from which the wider circle of gods comes into being. His secondary role as messenger of Hachacyum places him at the junction between primordial creation and continuing divine order, so that the beginning he produces is never fully sealed but continues to speak through him.
