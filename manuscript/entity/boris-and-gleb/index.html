Boris and Gleb are the patron saints of equestrians, merchants, and those seeking prosperity.

Boris and Gleb are Russian Orthodox saints.

Life and Death

These brothers were sons of Vladimir, Grand Prince of Kiev and the first Christian ruler of the Rus (the tribal nation that gave Russia its name). They converted alongside their father in 988 CE. Vladimir had multiple wives and reportedly 800 concubines, meaning Boris and Gleb had numerous half-brothers.

When Vladimir died in 1015, Boris and Gleb were away fighting the pagan Pechenegs. Succession proved contentious given their father's complicated marital history. Although Boris had a strong claim to the throne, another brother (usually identified as Svyatopolk) seized power and dispatched assassins.

Boris was attacked while praying in his tent. Surviving the initial assault, he was dragged before his treacherous brother, who ordered him stabbed through the heart. Gleb met his end shortly after, returning to Kiev by ship on November 5th. His cook, actually a disguised assassin, cut his throat with a kitchen knife.

Veneration

In 1019, their brother Yaroslav exhumed the bodies and reburied them together at the Church of Saint Basil in Vysgorod near Kiev. Miracles soon followed, and their tomb became a pilgrimage destination. Unfortunately, their remains vanished during an invasion in 1240.

Though not twins and possibly born of different mothers, Boris and Gleb are invariably honored as a pair. Some scholars suggest their cult absorbed earlier veneration of the divine twins Castor and Pollux.

Powers and Domains

The brothers heal and grant prosperity and commercial success. Tradition holds that completing any successful business transaction on May 2nd (their joint feast) guarantees good fortune for the following year.

Iconography

Two handsome young men standing together, or mounted knights on horseback. Boris wears a beard; Gleb is clean-shaven.

Attributes

Swords, Russian cross with three crossbars, Tree of Life.

Botanicals

Boris corresponds to barley, Gleb to wheat. Gleb also has associations with birch trees.

Colors

Black and red.

Feast Days

  • 2 May (joint feast)
  • 24 July (Boris)
  • 5 September (Gleb)
