Rama is the seventh avatar of Vishnu and the hero of the epic Ramayana. He is revered as the ideal man, ideal king, and supreme deity by millions of Hindus, particularly in North India, where he is considered the highest manifestation of the divine.

Overview

In Valmiki's Ramayana (written between 200 BCE and 200 CE), Rama is initially portrayed as a heroic human prince and avatar of Vishnu. By the medieval period, and especially in Tulsi Das's sixteenth-century Hindi devotional work the Ramcarit-manas, Rama ascends to the position of supreme deity — the lord of the worlds who descends to earth to offer his devotees the opportunity to worship him.

Rama is the son of King Dasharatha of Ayodhya and is wed to Sita, who is identified as an avatar of Sri-Lakshmi. His story centers on his banishment from the kingdom, the abduction of Sita by the demon king Ravana, and his ultimate defeat of Ravana and recovery of Sita. His reign — Ramrajya — is the Hindu ideal of a golden age: an era of harmony, longevity, fruitful crops, righteous order, and social perfection.

As Supreme Deity

After Valmiki's Ramayana, Rama increasingly ascended to a position of supreme deity for many Hindus. The shift in his status is most clearly evident in Tulsi Das's Ramcarit-manas, where the central point of the narrative is Rama's descent to earth in order to provide his devotees a chance to worship him. In the devotional tradition, Rama and Sita together are praised as the world's father and world's mother (Kavitavali 1.15), and the mere sight of them together has redemptive effects on devotees.

In popular Hinduism, Rama is worshiped in temples throughout India. He is often depicted with Sita, his brother Lakshmana, and the devoted monkey hero Hanuman. Hanuman is the supreme model of Rama devotion, and temples dedicated to Rama almost always include images of the full retinue.

As Ideal King

One of Rama's central mythological functions is as the ideal king whose vigor and virtue draw forth the fertility and abundance of the earth. His marriage to Sita — who is literally born from the earth when King Janaka plows a field — represents the sacred union between kingly power and earthly fertility. The result of this union is Ramrajya, the paradigm of righteous rule in Hindu thought.

Source
