Magickal Properties

Bereginy refers to a host of Slavic water spirits in the retinue of a primal goddess named Bereginia (also Berehinia, Perehinia). Bereginy is Russian; the Polish variant is Bóginki.

Bereginia means "earth" and "shore," and so perhaps indicates the threshold where land and water meet. River, forest, and lake spirits, the Bereginy are often depicted in the guise of double-tailed mermaids. Some believe these primeval Slavic spirits are the ancient ancestors of the Rusalka and Vila; others suggest that they are sister spirits, with the Bereginy inhabiting banks overlooking the waters where the Rusalka dwell.

The first recorded historical reference to Bereginia is from a sixth-century Greek lexicon naming gods and goddesses who were taboo for Christians. Among the spirits listed to avoid is "the Berehinia."

The Bereginy remained publicly honored by Slavic women as late as the Middle Ages, with secret devotions continuing long after. Christian chroniclers complained that the Bereginy were dangerous spirits because of the persistence with which women continued to serve them secretly. Rituals once held openly on the banks of rivers would eventually be held in sacred, secret, private places like the bathhouse.

Ritual Uses

  • Women's riverbank rites (historically public, later secret in bathhouses due to Christian persecution)
  • Petition for protection from baby-theft and changelings
  • Conduct water-blessing rituals for domestic peace

Offerings

Traditional women's water offerings conducted in secret

Favored People

Slavic women preserving ancestral rites, bathhouse tenders, those protecting infants

Petition For

Protection from baby-theft and changelings, blessing of water-crossings, domestic peace

Warnings

Dangerous spirits precisely because of persistence with which women continued to serve them secretly. Not entirely benevolent, can steal babies. Secrecy preserved them across Christian persecutions.

Additional Notes

  • Manifestations: Beautiful women or mermaids
  • Iconography: Double-tailed mermaids
  • Realms: River, forest, and lake
  • Christian chroniclers complained they were dangerous because women continued secret devotions
  • Rituals moved from open riverbanks to secret bathhouses under persecution
