The Queen Who Lives in the Waters; Mother of the Waters; The More Than Beautiful Woman; The Crocodile Woman; The Laughing Water Goddess

Overview

Mami Waters is a complex, volatile spirit who has become the most successful traditional African deity of modern times. While other traditional deities are marginalized by Christianity and Islam, Mami Waters is more popular now than ever. Fifteen years ago, she was barely known outside Africa; now she is venerated worldwide. She brings joy, wealth, ecstasy, success, and prosperity to her devotees.

Iconic Image

Mami Waters is identified with an iconic snake charmer image painted circa 1880 in Hamburg by a now-unknown artist (the model was a Hamburg zookeeper's wife). Later rendered as a chromolithograph, it was distributed as a poster throughout Africa, evoking tremendous spiritual response. The image portrays a beautiful dark-skinned woman with long thick hair holding a large snake, with another snake twining around her. A smaller inset image depicts a woman playing a double flute and charming snakes.

Nature as "Living" Spirit

Mami Waters is a "living" spirit, meaning that she sometimes manifests as a flesh-and-blood woman, physically, not just in dreams or visions. Many people describe actual physical encounters with her, whether on a city street or by a stream or river. She has sex with those who appeal to her.

Manifestation

  • Whether as woman, mermaid, or half-woman/half-snake, she is consistently gorgeous and charismatic
  • In urban areas, she may appear as a stylish young woman so beautiful she draws gasps, stops traffic, turns heads
  • Her skin may be literally white like chalk, or she may be a very fair-skinned black woman (this represents white clay or chalk traditionally used to delineate death or demarcate sacred territory, not Caucasian ethnicity)
  • If bejeweled, her gold is blinding in its brilliance
  • Her hair is long and wonderful
  • May be accompanied by pythons that wrap around her
  • May wear an extremely expensive, ostentatious watch (even as a mermaid, it's waterproof)
  • May have the most expensive cell phone model
  • Makes her presence known via the sound of merry laughter

Original Form

Although now frequently portrayed as a classic mermaid with a fish tail, Mami Waters was originally a snake goddess: her lower half is serpentine. Her iconic images usually feature a snake somewhere in the picture.

Powers and Abilities

  • Brings joy, wealth, success, and prosperity
  • Causes and cures infertility and impotence
  • Heals various illnesses, especially chronic fatigue and venereal diseases
  • Bestows health, beauty, and musical prowess
  • Grants wishes and abundance
  • Responsible for some drowning deaths but also rescues people

How She Signals Interest

Mami Waters typically demonstrates interest in a person via illness, specifically medically inexplicable fatigue and malaise. The person becomes remote and distant. No medical cause can be found. Health doesn't improve until the person contacts Mami Waters, either directly or through her clergy. The person may dream about Mami Waters or her symbols constantly, or just daydream compulsively. Sometimes infertility or the inability to bear a living child indicates Mami Waters' attempts at communication with women.

Relationships with Male Devotees

If Mami Waters really likes a man, she may permit him to be potent with her alone, thus cutting him off sexually from the human community. The men she most adores typically devote themselves exclusively to her, sacrificing other romantic or sexual ties. They achieve positions of tremendous wealth and power, thanks to Mami Waters, but in true fairy tale fashion, must keep their relationship with her a secret or their good fortune will evaporate.

Spirit Classification

This malaise is perceived as a kind of spirit possession but more like that of a Zar spirit than Western demonic possession. Like a Zar spirit, Mami Waters can be transformed into an ally: she can reverse all negative ailments she causes. She does not intend them badly; she may not know a better way to signal someone's attention. She may in fact be a Zar spirit. Some consider her a Simbi, and still others suggest that Mami Waters forms her own family of spirits with many individual paths or avatars.

Syncretism

  • In Latin America, syncretized to Martha the Dominator
  • Elsewhere syncretized to Eve
  • The snake charmer associated with Mami Waters is also identified with Dodder, Martha the Dominator, and various Bori spirits
  • In Haiti, the central snake charmer is identified as the lwa Reine Travaux ("Queen of Works"), with the big snake being Damballah and the smaller inset image being Ayida Wedo

Attributes

  • Attributes: Golden comb, expensive wrist watch
  • Colors: Red, white
  • Creatures: Snake, crocodile
  • Element: Water

Sacred Sites

She has shrines throughout Western and Central Africa, Europe, South and North America.

Rituals

Those called by Mami Waters are encouraged to spend time in water, whether herbal or enchanted baths or immersion in living water (ocean, lake, river, spring).

Iconography

Her image is painted on walls of lottery parlors to bring wealth and luck.

Cultural References

Nigerian author Flora Nwapa's 1966 novel Efuru explores a woman's relationship with Mami Waters. Mami Waters was prominently featured in New York City's American Museum of Natural History's 2007 exhibition on mythic creatures.
