Phinehas ben Yair (pronounced pin-hahs ben yah-eer) stands among the venerated Jewish saints of the second century, remembered for his extraordinary devotion and unconventional practices.

Life and History

This sage maintained strict personal boundaries that puzzled even his contemporaries. After reaching adulthood, he never accepted food in another person's home. This extended even to his own father's table. His community regarded him as exceptionally devout rather than antisocial.

His family connections link him to Simon bar Yohai, the great miracle worker, though sources disagree on the exact relationship. The Talmud identifies Phinehas as Simon's son-in-law, while the Zohar reverses this, naming Phinehas as the father-in-law.

Domains

Modern devotees seek his blessing when facing the end of a marriage or partnership. He has become the advocate for those hoping to part ways without acrimony, ensuring separations conclude with dignity and peace.

Ritual

Those seeking his intercession for an amicable separation travel to his burial place in Safed's Old Cemetery. The traditional practice involves lighting a memorial candle, then circling the grave seven times in a counterclockwise direction while speaking your request aloud.

Sacred Sites

His tomb in the Old Safed Cemetery, Israel.
