Orthodox saint known for her miraculous healing abilities, prophecy, and clairvoyance despite being born blind and later paralyzed.

Life and Legend

Matrona was born around 1885 in Russia's Tula Province to peasant parents. She came into the world without eyes. Her impoverished parents planned to send her to an orphanage, but her mother dreamed of an impossibly beautiful white bird with empty eye sockets landing on her breast. Interpreting this as a divine sign, she kept her blind daughter.

By age six or seven, Matrona demonstrated profound powers of clairvoyance, telepathy, and prophecy. She could read thoughts, perceive sins, and accurately foretell disasters. She diagnosed ailments of body, mind, and soul and manifested miraculous healing powers. People traveled from across the province, transporting sick relatives in wagons and wheelbarrows to receive her healing touch. Rather than being a burden, she became her family's primary provider through the gifts grateful petitioners brought. At seventeen, she became paralyzed and never walked again.

The Revolutionary Years

As a teenager, Matrona predicted the Russian Revolution, warning a local landowner to sell his property and flee Russia. After 1917, now a landless peasant, she wandered with her mother before settling in Moscow by 1925. She lived secretly in the homes of friends and relatives, unable to stay with her Communist brothers due to her devout Orthodox faith, which was then illegal.

The Communists sought to arrest her, but she consistently eluded them through her foreknowledge. One police officer found her only because she waited for him. She told him to return home immediately. When he arrived, he found his wife on fire and rescued her just in time. He never returned to arrest Matrona.

Ministry and Death

Matrona saw as many as forty visitors daily, living an ascetic life of frequent fasting, prayer vigils, and exorcisms. She insisted petitioners wear a cross and encouraged Orthodox marriage over Soviet civil unions. She maintained that she was merely an intercessor, channeling God's power.

She predicted her death three days in advance, accepting visitors until the end. Her final words promised continued intercession: "I'll see you; I'll hear you; I'll come to your aid!" She died 2 May 1952. Her grave became an immediate pilgrimage site. She was glorified (Orthodox canonization) in 1999.

Iconography

Matrona is portrayed with closed eyes, wearing a white scarf, her right hand raised in blessing.

Sacred Site

The Church of the Protecting Veil of Our Lady at Intercession Convent in Moscow houses her relics (translated 1998) and a miracle-working icon she commissioned during her lifetime. Oil from the icon lamps at her shrine is said to have healing properties.

Feast Days

19 April or 2 May, depending on calendar used.
