Shrine to Artemis

Artemis is depicted as a young goddess wearing a horned or lunar headdress, carrying a crescent-shaped bow and arrows, and accompanied by a stag or a pack of hunting dogs. She is the moon goddess and twin counterpart to Apollo, god of the sun. Born to Leto and Zeus, she arrived first and immediately helped midwife her twin brother into the world, in this aspect she is sometimes called Cynthia, linking the moon to women and the act of birth. Artemis is a chaste goddess beholden to only herself. Her mythology holds a message for moon magick practitioners: working in tune with nature, within and without, and dedicating oneself to mastery, can lead like her arrows to hitting any target.

Ancient mistress of wild nature, witchcraft, women's mysteries. Regulates fertility of humans/animals/plants. Guardian of thresholds, hunts, sacred groves. Bear origins, Lady of Beasts. Twin to Apollo, born first, midwifed his birth. Colors white/silver.

Diana: Italian Forest Queen

Indigenous Italian (possibly Etruscan) deity with stronger night/darkness/magick associations than Artemis. Lives in Forest of Nemi with consort Virbius, horned stag spirit subordinate to her.

Goddess-Consort Pattern

Diana and Virbius exemplify original Pagan hierarchy: powerful goddess with male consort whom she initiates and tutors. Goddess is eternal and dominant.

Christian Inversion: Made male devil dominant over female witches, inverting original power structure where goddess ruled.

Devil's Grandmother: Russian/Central/Northern European folklore preserves memory. She taught devil everything, retained secrets, knows tricks he doesn't, legend both diabolized old women and recalled when women's wisdom was respected.

Celtic Worship & Persecution

Worshipped as log in Celtic Europe. Male wolf-shamans/lunar priests served her. Early Christians saw cult as major rival. When Christianity gained power, Diana vilified as Queen of Witches and Satan's bride. A Spanish Inquisitor declared, "Diana is the devil."

Despite 6th-10th century persecution, worship continued. Inquisition called witchcraft "The Society of Diana." Central to contemporary Stregheria.

Midwife Goddess of Life and Death

Artemis is the sacred Greek midwife whose first act upon being born was to help her mother Leto give birth to her twin brother Apollo. This primordial midwife experience established her eternal role as protector of childbirth and midwifery.

Artemis held the ultimate power over birthing outcomes: she was the Greek deity responsible for determining which women died in childbirth and which survived. This divine authority over the threshold between life and death during birth made her both revered and feared by pregnant women and midwives throughout the ancient Greek world.

Midwives in ancient times were affiliated with shrines and temples in Egypt, Sumeria, and elsewhere in Asia and Africa as well as among the Aztecs and Incas. Birth was considered sacred and magickal but also a dangerous experience requiring divine protection.

Role in Hellenic Household Worship

Within the household, offerings to Artemis include cedar, deer tokens, jasmine, myrtle, white blooms, and frankincense (manna) incense. The bow with its arrows and the torch She carries serve as Her tokens. The colour linked to Her is green. Her sacred animals include the deer, the dog, the bear, the partridge, and the quail. Plants sacred to Her include amaranthus, palm, the cypress, peanut, and wormwood.

Notes

Principally a goddess of animals and hunting, but in Greek-speaking Asia she is a mother goddess. Her known period of worship runs from circa 800 BCE and earlier until Christianization around 400 CE, and probably later. Her synonym Potnia Theron means mistress of the animals. Centers of cult included Antioch-near-Pisidia, Delos, Magnesia-on-the-Maeander, Pamphylia, Perge, and Ephesus in Turkey. Cultic statues survive, most notably the multibreasted figures at Ephesus, and her literary sources include cuneiform texts modeled on earlier Asian forms, the Iliad, and the Theogony.

Artemis is a deity of very ancient origins who survived and attracted great popularity in both Asia Minor and Greece into Christian times, when much of her ethos was arguably transferred to the Virgin Mary. Both figures enjoyed major sanctuaries at Ephesus. As an Asiatic goddess she was often drawn winged and standing between wild animals, equipped with boots, a torch, and a pointed cap. She is also a strongly androgynous figure, a feature depicted dramatically in the statue of Artemis of Ephesus. Her temple at Ephesus dates from the fourth century BCE and ranks among the seven wonders of the world. The cult statues were carried in procession on May 25 among a congregation of up to thirty thousand.

To the Greeks she is the daughter of Zeus and Leto, and she was honored in the sanctuary on Delos with its celebrated Horn Altar from circa 700 BCE. In Greek mythology the androgynous aspect was firmly discounted. In her earliest pre-Homeric form the Mistress of Animals suckles the young of every wild creature that roams the fields. As a huntress she uses a bow and arrows. By Homeric times the ferocity of this prehistoric element has waned in favor of a more timid image of a young girl dominated robustly by her stepmother Hera. A contrary character study in the Odyssey pictures her more positively as a virgin goddess chasing and killing boars and hinds over the hills and fields, fleet of foot and in company with a band of nymphs. She presides over nature and over the initiation rituals of young girls. She is also a goddess of blood sacrifice. A cruel element emerged as she threatened any maiden who turned to the role of wife. Paradoxically, and more in keeping with the old Semitic personality, she is also the goddess of birth.

The Artemisia Family

The artemisia botanical family is named in honor of Artemis, who rules magick, fertility, psychic gifts, and wild female nature. Her favorite plants share her nature: they bear profound gifts but are prickly, volatile, and potentially dangerous, and few plants are more closely tied to the magickal arts. The two most famous members of the family are Mugwort and Wormwood.
