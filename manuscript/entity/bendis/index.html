Magickal Properties

  • Primordial Thracian/Dacian/Scythian goddess whose origins are lost in mists of time
  • Intensely lunar deity, even more so than Artemis or Hekate, epitomizes lunar female power
  • Spirit of divine ecstasy and passion, binds different realms together through shamanism
  • Mother of nameless Thracian Rider/Horseman/Hero, brought forth son without male assistance

Ritual Uses

  • Petition exclusively at night during waning and dark moon phases for maximum power
  • Conduct ecstatic dancing, sexual rites, and nightly torch footraces in her honor
  • Request revelation of hidden treasure (material and intangible)

Offerings

Lunar images and images of her sacred animals (fox, deer, leopard, snake)

Favored People

Those seeking fertility, protection, ecstatic spiritual experience, foreigners, outcasts

Petition For

Fertility, successful childbirth, protection, good health, good fortune, revelation of hidden treasures

Warnings

Greeks considered her disreputable as goddess of foreigners who never shed Thracian identity. Eventually Athenian government banned all her rituals except torch races, forcing other rites underground. Petition exclusively at night.

Additional Notes

  • Depicted with peaked Thracian hat, cloak, short tunic, fox-skin boots, often with erect phalli devotees
  • Attributes: Torch, spear, dagger, bow/arrows, cup, staff, phallus
  • Mounts: Doe, leopard, chariot drawn by three winged horses
  • Sacred festivals celebrated at full moons near solstices with ecstatic dancing and sexual rites

Historical Context

Though her Thracian origins reach back into prehistory, Bendis appeared formally in Athens during the Peloponnesian War, when Thracian residents of the city won official recognition for her cult and inaugurated the torchlight procession known as the Bendideia. In her Hellenized guise she was linked stylistically with Artemis as a huntress, sharing the bow, the hunt, and lunar authority. Her signature attributes in Greek art are boots, torch, and pointed Thracian cap.
