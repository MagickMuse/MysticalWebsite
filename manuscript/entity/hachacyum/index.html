Overview

Hachacyum is the supreme creator god of the classical Mesoamerican Mayan tradition of Mexico, remembered especially in the Lacandon lineage of the Chiapas forests. He stands at the head of the pantheon as the ultimate origin, the god from whom the ordered world of gods, land, and people takes its shape. Other Mayan deities are placed in relation to him as his brothers, his messengers, and his agents.

⚠️ Hachacyum is venerated within Mayan Lacandon and Indigenous Mesoamerican traditions. These are closed practices and should only be approached by those within the originating cultures or with explicit cultural permission.

Domains

Hachacyum presides over the office of supreme creator, the ordered world in its wholeness, the making and continued care of the Mayan people, and the extended pantheon that carries out different aspects of his creative and sustaining work.

Ritual

Hachacyum belongs to the living Mayan religious tradition, particularly as carried by the Lacandon of Chiapas. Treat this entry as reference only. Do not attempt to petition him outside the initiatory context maintained by the communities that hold his worship.

Lore

Hachacyum's brother Ac Yanto is credited with the creation of Europeans and their goods, a mythic response to colonial encounter that places even the arrival of foreigners within the frame of the pantheon. Cacoch, the messenger deity, carries word between Hachacyum and the wider circle of gods, and the water lily cosmogony traced through Cacoch belongs to the same creative fabric that Hachacyum oversees. Through these relationships the highest creator remains connected to every layer of the ordered world.
