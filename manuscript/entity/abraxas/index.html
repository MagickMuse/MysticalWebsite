🔺Abracadabra

Abraxas (also spelled Abrasax) is a powerful figure from Greco-Egyptian magick, appearing primarily on carved gemstones and magickal papyri from the Roman period. Part deity, part cosmic principle, part magickal formula, Abraxas represents the fusion of Egyptian, Greek, and Gnostic traditions.

Origins and Meaning

The name ABRAXAS holds numerical significance. In Greek isopsephy (numerology), the letters add up to 365:

  • A (1) + B (2) + R (100) + A (1) + X (60) + A (1) + S (200) = 365

This number represents:

  • The days of the solar year
  • Cosmic completeness and totality
  • The 365 heavens or archons in some Gnostic systems

Basilides, a 2nd-century Gnostic teacher in Alexandria, described Abraxas as the supreme deity ruling 365 celestial spheres. Later church fathers condemned this as heresy, but the name and image spread throughout the Roman world on protective amulets.

Iconography

The classic Abraxas image shows:

  • Rooster head: Vigilance, solar power, announcement of dawn
  • Human torso: Often armored
  • Serpent legs: Chthonic power, wisdom, connection to earth energies
  • Whip in right hand: Authority, driving away evil
  • Shield in left hand: Protection

This composite form combines solar (rooster), human, and chthonic (serpent) elements into a single figure of cosmic power.

Magick Gems

Thousands of carved gemstones bearing the Abraxas image survive from antiquity. These gems were worn as:

  • Protective amulets against evil spirits and misfortune
  • Healing talismans (particularly for digestive ailments)
  • General-purpose magickal objects

The gems often include additional inscriptions: divine names (IAO, SABAOTH), voces magicae (words of power), and requests for specific benefits.

Abraxas in the Greek Magickal Papyri

In the magickal papyri, ABRASAX appears as a name of power invoked alongside Egyptian, Greek, and Jewish divine names. The name was spoken or written to add force to spells, regardless of whether the practitioner held any particular theological view of who or what Abraxas was.

Abracadabra

The magickal word "Abracadabra" likely derives from ABRAXAS or a related formula. It first appears in a 2nd-century CE medical text, prescribed as a cure for fever when written in a diminishing triangle and worn around the neck.

The Knights Templar

The Abraxas symbol was adopted by the Knights Templar, who used it on their seals. No one knows precisely why this image was of particular significance, but the hidden layers within the name itself may provide a clue. In Greek, the seven letters are the initials of the first seven planets in the Solar System.

The Chariot

Occasionally Abraxas appears driving a chariot drawn by four horses. These horses represent the four elements.

Demotion and Duality

Depending on perspective, Abraxas is either an Egyptian Sun God adopted by the early Christian Gnostics, or a demon closely associated with Lucifer. The case for the former is stronger, since Abraxas was not demoted to the ranks of a demon until the Middle Ages. As Ruler of the First Heaven, he held dominion over the cycles of birth, death, and resurrection.

The name "Abraxas" itself may mean "harm me not," adding another layer to the speculation that the supreme magickal word "Abracadabra" derives from it.

Modern Interpretations

Abraxas continues to appear in ceremonial magick, chaos magick, and various esoteric systems as a figure of cosmic totality and magickal power.
