Overview

Vajrapani began as a yaksha so fierce that he eventually attained the status of Chief General of the Yakshas. He is now one of the three principal protectors of the Buddha, alongside Avalokiteshvara and Manjushri, and epitomizes the power of all Buddhas. He is a complex spirit who manifests in many forms: companion and bodyguard of the Buddha, but also a Buddha himself, according to legend destined to be the last Buddha to appear in this world cycle. He is a principal Bodhisattva.

Magickal Properties

When the Nagas approached the Buddha seeking to learn from him, Vajrapani was charged with protecting them from their mortal enemies, the Garudas, and assumed a form resembling a Garuda to confuse and combat them. He is a wrathful spirit of fierce, benevolent compassion. Because of his associations with the Nagas, he evolved into a rain deity, petitioned when rain is too abundant or insufficient. He banishes the worst demons; as a guardian image, he is frequently found near the entrances of Buddhist shrines in Mongolian and Tibetan temples and in the homes of devotees.

Manifestation

Vajrapani's earliest images were based on Greek images of Heracles. In his modern aspect, he is draped in tiger or elephant skin, a snake coils around his neck, and he wears a crown of five skulls. He dances within a halo of flames, symbolic of the energy of the enlightened mind and of fearlessness.

Attributes

Vajra, blue lotus, rope.

Additional Notes

His consort is Sujata. His color is dark blue.
