Magickal Properties

The Dharmapalas or Dharma Protectors are guardians of truth and defenders of Buddhism. Although fierce, wrathful deities, they are simultaneously Buddhas and Bodhisattvas and so beings of sublime compassion and wisdom. They wage merciless war against enemies of Buddhism and evil spirits who prey on their devotees.

There are hundreds of Dharmapalas, but the eight most popular and significant are known as the Eight Dharma Protectors: Begtse, Hayagriva, Kubera, Mahakala, Palden Lhamo, Tsangpa Drakpo, Yama, and Yamantaka. Seven are male; Palden Lhamo is the lone female. They are known as the terrible ones because of their ferocious appearance, but they are terrible in righteousness, not evil.

Ritual Uses

The Eight Dharma Protectors are among the most popular subjects of Tibetan art. Images of them are contained in most Tibetan Buddhist temples. In Tibetan folk religion, the Dharmapalas are considered somewhat akin to guardian angels. Over generations, many families share devotion to specific Dharma Protectors similar to the way generations of a family may venerate saints. Many clans and families claim affiliation with specific Dharmapalas.

Warnings

These are fierce wrathful deities with ferocious appearance. They wage merciless war against enemies of Buddhism and evil spirits. Approach with respect and understanding of their fierce nature.

Additional Notes

The Eight Dharma Protectors are perhaps the most significant subjects of devotion for ordinary Tibetans. Not necessarily one of the eight specifically; there are hundreds of Dharmapalas that families may affiliate with across generations.
