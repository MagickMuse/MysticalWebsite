Overview

Brhaspati, whose name means lord of prayer, is an astral god of the Hindu Vedic, Epic, and Puranic traditions. He is the personification of the planet Jupiter and one of the great figures of the Vedic priestly world. In the Rg-veda he appears as a priest, and in the later development of the tradition he is remembered as the guru of the gods themselves, the teacher whose office secures the ritual and moral order of the pantheon.

In Vedic literature

Brhaspati is called the son of Angiras, taking his place in the great priestly lineage of the Angirasas. In the Rg-veda his hymns celebrate the power of prayer and sacred speech, and he is invoked as the divine chaplain who leads the gods in their rituals. In this office he is sometimes called Brahmanaspati, lord of the sacred word. His authority over the spoken formula gives him a role very close to that of Brahma himself, and the tradition later regards the two as almost identical.

Later tradition

In the Epic and Puranic material Brhaspati stands as the guru of the whole later Hindu pantheon, the counterpart on the side of the gods to Shukra, who is guru of the asuras. His consort is the goddess Tara, and his son by her is Kaca, remembered for his mission to obtain the sanjivani knowledge of resurrection from the enemy camp. Brhaspati is also the presiding deity of the planet Jupiter in Hindu astrology, and Thursday is his day.

Iconography

Brhaspati rides in a chariot drawn by eight horses. His color is golden yellow. His recognized attributes are the arrow, the golden ax, the book, the bow, the rosary, the staff, and the water jar. The book and the rosary mark his office of teaching and recitation; the staff belongs to his priestly authority; the water jar carries the association with ritual purification; and the ax, bow, and arrow express the martial capacities that a chaplain of the gods requires when the pantheon takes the field.

Domains

Brhaspati presides over sacred speech and the recited formula, over priestly authority, over the office of the teacher and the pupil-teacher relationship, over the astral influence of the planet Jupiter, and over solemn ritual as a whole. He is a benign and dignified figure whose blessings extend to any undertaking that depends on clear language, faithful transmission of knowledge, and moral steadiness.

Ritual

Approach Brhaspati with the ritual purity and dignified bearing suitable to the guru of the gods. His worship is particularly appropriate before major study, teaching, oration, or ritual, and on the days and hours associated with Jupiter in Hindu astrology.
