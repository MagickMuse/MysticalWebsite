The Nightly Battle

Apophis dwells in the Duat, the Egyptian underworld. Each night, Ra's solar vessel must pass through this realm. Apophis waits there, seeking to devour the sun and end creation permanently. His movements make the earth tremble.

Ra travels with divine allies who defend him during this perilous crossing. Set, despite his role as Osiris's murderer, stands aboard the solar barque fighting Apophis. The same chaotic force that disrupted the divine order also protects it from total annihilation.

Role in Egyptian Magick

Apophis represents everything Egyptian magick works to defeat. Spells against chaos, disorder, and hostile forces often identify the target with Apophis. The magician assumes the role of Ra or his defenders, channeling divine authority against the serpent.

Defeating Apophis became a metaphor for overcoming any enemy or obstacle. Execration rituals drew on this imagery: destroying a figure inscribed with an enemy's name paralleled the gods' nightly victory over the chaos serpent.

Iconography

Apophis appears as an enormous serpent, sometimes shown being speared, bound, or dismembered by Ra's defenders. These images were themselves magickal, depicting the desired outcome of the eternal battle.

Warnings

Apophis is not a spirit to petition or honor. He embodies annihilation. Egyptian magick positions practitioners as his opponents, never his allies. Working with Apophis imagery means working against what he represents.
