Classification

Unofficial saints.

Overview

The Bethen are a triad of female saints venerated in Austria and Bavaria. Their identity appears rooted in their Pagan alter egos, a triad of goddesses also once worshipped in Bavaria and Austria, and also known as the Bethen. Bethen may derive from a Germanic word meaning "to pray."

Names

The names of the individual Bethen vary depending on region. They are called:

  • Ainpet, Gberpet, and Firpet
  • Ainbeth, Borbeth, and Wilbeth
  • Aubet, Cubet, and Quere
  • Catherine, Margaret, and Barbara

The renaming of the individual members of the Bethen as Catherine, Margaret, and Barbara may be an attempt to identify them with the extremely popular and official Saints Barbara, Catherine of Alexandria, and Margaret, who are sometimes venerated together as a triad.

History

The Bethen are unofficial, or popular, saints. No documentation exists regarding them as historical persons. The earliest surviving depiction of the Bethen in the form of saints dates from an 1127 painting in a southern Tyrolian church. They were extremely popular saints for centuries, until they were eventually overshadowed by increasing devotion to Mary.

Evidence indicates that the Bethen were venerated as individual saints and as a triad.

Powers and Domains

They are invoked to provide fertility.

Colors

Red, black, white.

Sacred Sites

The Bethen have traditionally been venerated at wells, sacred springs, stones, and trees, but there are also shrines associated with them:

  • The Church of Leutstetten in Bavaria, where, according to legend, three noble virgins never married, donating all their wealth toward building the church instead. Their image was enshrined in the church. No evidence documenting this story exists, but a painting of the Bethen in the church dates from 1620.
  • The Church of Saint Vigil in Obsaurs in Austria also features a painting of the Bethen. There is a sacred well nearby.
  • There is also an image of the Bethen at Worms Cathedral.

Traditional Offerings

An 11th-century document suggests that women laid offering tables for the Bethen complete with food, drink, and three knives, one for each of them.

Magickal Properties

The Bethen bless fertility, birth, and good fortune. They spin the secret threads of women's mysteries, rock the cradle, and reveal hidden treasure (both material and spiritual).

Ritual Uses

Lay a beautiful table with food and drink following birth or before a wedding. Propitiate at death vigils when possible.

Warnings

Respect their refined taste and secrecy. Men were historically excluded from rites.

Additional Notes

Their wells, stones, and trees remain potent sites (Bethenbrunnen, Bethensteine).
