Overview

Candanayika is a goddess of the Hindu tradition, honored as a distinct form of Durga and as one of the nine navadurgas, the nine forms of Durga worshipped through the nine nights of Navaratri. Her name means mistress of the fierce, from candi, the feminine of canda meaning violent or burning, and nayika meaning heroine or lady, so that her name carries at once the sense of a leader among the fierce goddesses and a sovereign of the wrathful power that the navadurgas embody.

Classification

Goddess of the Hindu Sakta tradition. Distinct form of Durga, one of the navadurgas.

Domains

Sovereignty over fierce power, the leadership among wrathful goddesses, the ninefold turning of Durga through the nights of Navaratri, the mistress-office within the group of ferocious forms.

Iconography

Depicted in the manner of the fierce forms of Durga, multi-armed and richly armed, in the standard iconography of the navadurga cycle. She is generally shown of dark color, armored, and mounted on the lion of Durga, with the weapons of the goddess distributed across her many hands.

Attributes

Sword, trident, bow, arrow, discus, mace, noose, and the other weapons of Durga carried in her multiple arms, together with the emblems of sovereignty appropriate to a mistress-goddess of her kind.

Sacred Sites

Durga temples across India that observe the navadurga cycle, particularly during Navaratri when each of the nine forms takes her turn on the nightly worship.

Ritual

Invoked during Navaratri as one of the nine navadurgas. She receives offerings appropriate to the fierce forms of Durga: red flowers, red cloth, and the recitation of the Devi Mahatmya and related Sakta hymns.

Lore

Candanayika belongs to a family of Durga forms whose names begin with the root canda, including Canda, Candika, Candarupa, and the compound Camunda. These names sound the note of fierce burning power that Sakta theology understands as the sovereign edge of the great goddess, and Candanayika's title as mistress of the fierce places her at the head of that family within the navadurga cycle.
