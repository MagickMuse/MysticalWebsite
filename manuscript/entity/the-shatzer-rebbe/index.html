Venerated as a Jewish saint, Rabbi Sholom Moskowitz (died 1958), better known as the Shatzer Rebbe, was a Hassidic leader who continues to intercede for petitioners after death.

Life and Legend

Born in the Romanian city of Suceava (known in Yiddish as Shatz), the Shatzer Rebbe was a member of the Shatzer Hassidic dynasty, a renowned Torah scholar and Kabbalist considered holy during his lifetime. He moved to England before World War II and was buried there upon his death.

In his will, the Shatzer Rebbe declared that since he had always tried to help people in life, he intended to continue after death. He offered his services as an intercessor to anyone in need, particularly those seeking recovery from illness. He advised petitioners to visit his grave, ideally on a Friday before noon.

Magickal Properties

Many miracles are attributed to the Shatzer Rebbe, including healings, fertility blessings, restored marriages, and legal victories. He assists both women and men equally. However, he imposes one condition: whoever makes a request must promise to better their way of Yiddishkeit in at least one point. This condition is inscribed on his grave in English, Hebrew, and Yiddish. If this requirement holds no meaning for you, he may not be the right intercessor for your needs. The condition is minor and easily accomplished, but he stipulates that he will be very angry with anyone who deceives him.

Graveside Ritual

A traditional ritual exists for requesting the Shatzer Rebbe's assistance:

  1. Upon arriving at the grave, light one candle for the soul who will announce your presence and summon the Shatzer Rebbe
  2. Light two additional candles for the Shatzer Rebbe's soul
  3. Make your request, identifying yourself by stating your own name and your mother's name (if praying on behalf of another, provide their information instead)

Sacred Sites

His grave at Enfield Adass Yisroel Cemetery (also spelled Adath Yisroel) in England. People travel from around the world to burn candles and leave notes there.
