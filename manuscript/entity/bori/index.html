⚠️ The Bori spirits and the Maguzawa religion belong to the Hausa people of Nigeria and Niger. Bori is a closed initiatory practice; possession ceremonies and healing rites should only be approached within established Hausa lineages and with explicit cultural permission.

Magickal Properties

  • Bori refers to traditional Hausa spiritual system and the spirits within it, also called Iskoki
  • Ancient pre-Islamic spiritual tradition of the Hausa people, particularly in northern Nigeria and Niger
  • Spirits associated with healing, divination, and maintaining balance between human and spirit worlds
  • Each spirit has specific attributes, powers, and requirements for interaction

Ritual Uses

  • Spirit possession ceremonies for healing and divination
  • Traditional drumming and dancing to invoke specific spirits
  • Diagnostic sessions to determine which spirits are affecting individuals
  • Therapeutic rituals to restore harmony between humans and spirit realm

Offerings

Traditional Hausa offerings, divination materials, healing substances

Favored People

Petition For

Spiritual healing, divination guidance, protection from illness, connection with ancestral wisdom

Warnings

Respect traditional Hausa protocols and work with established practitioners when engaging with Bori spirits.

Additional Notes

  • Survived alongside Islam, creating syncretistic practices in many communities
  • Each Bori spirit has distinct personality, preferences, and areas of influence
  • Traditional practitioners undergo extensive training to work safely with these spirits
  • Important cultural preservation practice maintaining connection to pre-Islamic Hausa traditions
  • Spirits often manifest through possession during healing ceremonies

Names and Numbers

Bori are the spirits of Maguzawa, the indigenous pre-Islamic religion of the Hausa people of Nigeria and Niger. These spirits are also called Iska (wind) or Aljan (derived from Arabic Djinn). There are infinite numbers of Bori spirits, with approximately 100 to 300 actively involved with people.

Spirit Behavior and Preferences

Bori spirits live like people in their own city called Jangare, organized into twelve houses divided by family, ethnicity, and occupation. Bori spirits can develop extremely close relationships with people, though they can be demanding. Each Bori prefers specific colors, numbers, offerings, fragrances, rhythms, songs, and dances, and engages in ritual possession. Each Bori possesses a specific song that celebrates and summons them.

Healing Relationship

Bori are traditionally invoked to bless beginnings: new homes, business ventures, marriages, or births. They provide healing through a complex relationship with illness, Bori spirits both cause human illness and misfortune and then provide the remedy. Each Bori is associated with specific ailments which they have power to cure. The healing ritual is simultaneously an initiation, where the newly healed individual becomes a trance medium and active participant in future rituals.

Traditional Offerings

First fruits of the season, metal smith's tools, phallic images, wine consumed from special ceremonial cups. Bori ritual leaders are herbal specialists, so each Bori is associated with specific botanicals.

Who They Affect

Those seeking healing from illness or misfortune, people experiencing general incompetence or clumsiness (signs of Bori affliction), and those called to become trance mediums. Women hold prominent positions in Bori spiritual traditions.

Anger and Repulsion

Bori spirits demonstrate anger by gradually sucking away someone's life-force. The key word is gradually, opportunity is usually left to make offerings, conduct ceremonies, or otherwise appease and propitiate the spirit. Iron repels the Bori, even the word "iron" spoken or written may be sufficient to send them away.

The Twelve Houses of Jangare

The Bori live in an invisible city named Jangare with twelve houses: House of the Chief of Spirits, House of the Chief's Brother, House of Koranic Scholars, House of Lepers and Snakes, House of Fulani Spirits, House of Younger Brother of Fulani Chief, House of Water Spirits, House of Hunters, House of Chief of Pagans, House of Chief of Gwari, House of North Africans, and House of Magickians and Sorcerers. Some Bori are Muslim while others maintain Pagan paths.
