Life

Antonio Mamerto de la Cruz Gil Nunez (c. 1847 to c. 1878), better known as Gaucho Gil, is the most famous and popular gaucho saint. During Argentina's Civil War, Gil was forcibly drafted into a gaucho militia, but deserted rather than kill other gauchos after a Guarani spirit appeared in his dreams.

Gaucho Gil turned outlaw, stealing from the rich and giving to the poor. He survived as a fugitive for years, developing a reputation as a gaucho Robin Hood and miracle worker. Allegedly, he only had to touch animals to heal them. He manifested clairvoyant powers and could hypnotize people with his gaze.

Martyrdom

On 8 January, celebrating the Feast of Saint Balthazar, he was betrayed by a friend. Gil surrendered peacefully, sacrificing himself in a manner devotees perceive as resembling Christ. En route to sentencing, the police officer invoked the "law of flight" and executed him. A firing squad shot but every bullet missed.

As a devotee of San La Muerte, he was invincible due to the amulet worn beneath his skin. He met death willingly, requesting the amulet be extracted. He was hung upside down from a thorn tree and decapitated with his own knife. His last words: "This is an injustice. You are shedding innocent blood." Tragically, Gil had already been pardoned, but paperwork arrived after his execution.

First Miracles

Gaucho Gil began producing miracles immediately. Just before dying, he told an executioner that the man's son was deathly ill, but Gil would intercede in Heaven. When the man arrived home, his previously healthy son was at death's door. He begged Gil's forgiveness and the child miraculously recovered. The sergeant made a cross and erected it in ground soaked with Gil's blood. A shrine was born.

Gil's blood splattered when his throat was cut, landing on the crippled hand of one of his executioners. The hand was instantly healed. Similarly, the failing vision of Longinus, the Roman soldier who pierced Christ's side, was miraculously healed when Christ's blood fell into his eye.

Manifestation

Described as exceptionally handsome with a penetrating, hypnotic, sometimes paralyzing gaze.

Iconography

Only one photograph exists, showing a smiling man with long dark hair and prominent mustache. Statues portray him with red bandanna, red scarf, and red cape.

Attribute

Bolas (indigenous throwing weapon symbolic of gaucho culture).

Feast Day

  • January 8 (features dancing, as he was arrested at a dance)
When offering candles to Gaucho Gil, light them with wicks facing down, because that's the way Gil was facing when executed.

Sacred Site

Principal shrine just outside Mercedes in Corrientes Province. Hundreds of thousands visit. Despite his enormous popular following, the Catholic Church has not canonized him as an official saint.
