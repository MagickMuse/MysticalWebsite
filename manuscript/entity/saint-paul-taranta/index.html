Saint Paul Taranta is a controversial and unofficial path of Saint Paul, closely identified with Tarantism. Depending on perspective, Tarantism may be a vestige of Italian Paganism or a compulsive dance mania similar to Saint Vitus' dance. The dance associated with Tarantism is the tarantella, or the spider dance. Tarantism may be perceived as demonic possession or as a spirit-channeling dance tradition similar to modern East African Zar.

Domains

Saint Paul Taranta is the snake-and-spider saint. It is not clear whether he is the stolid apostle behaving unconventionally or whether some old god has borrowed Paul's name and persona. He both protects against tarantism and inflicts it. He can heal any kind of spider bite or its effects. Those scared of spiders may ask him to keep them away.

The Tarantella

The Tarantella dance phenomenon responded to a condition called tarantism, blamed on a spider's bite. Those bitten were allegedly forced to dance compulsively or die. Rather than dancing to death, they danced to stay alive. The first documented case was recorded in 1370 near the southern Italian coastal town of Taranto. The dance, the condition, this saint, and eventually the name tarantula all derive from this town.

Dancers sometimes speak and behave obscenely, completely out of character. Although initially blamed on spider bites, the condition was contagious, spreading from person to person. Some theorize that tarantism is a vestigial survival of Dionysian rites once popular in the area.

History

In the late 18th century, Saint Paul became associated with Tarantism. He was credited with healing and releasing dancers, provided they first danced for him. Release from the mania occurred when the saint spoke to the dancing victim during the dance. He communicates with people while they are dancing, not only to release them but to impart messages and information.

The Healing Well

According to tradition, Saint Paul visited Galatina in early Christian times, where he received an extremely warm welcome. In gratitude, Paul produced a miraculous healing spring in his host's home, credited with curing all those harmed by venomous creatures. The home became known as the House of Saint Paul.

Paul transmitted his healing power to his host and all future descendants. Healing was accomplished by making the sign of the cross over injuries from venomous creatures, followed by a drink of water from the well. Wandering shamanic healers and snake charmers throughout southern Italy boasted of descent from Saint Paul, calling themselves the San Paolari or Sanpaolari. Proof of ancestry was allegedly established by serpentine-shaped birthmarks beneath tongues, behind knees, or elsewhere on the body.

By the early 18th century, Tarantists sought healing in Galatina. In 1752, a chapel dedicated to Saint Paul was built over the well. The chapel still exists. In June 1959, the Galatina sanitation authority declared the well water contaminated and dangerous. It was bricked up.
