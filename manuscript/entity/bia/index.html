Bia

◇Precautions
Bia is force itself, not persuasion or reason. She is invoked for coercion, not consent, and her use should be weighed accordingly. She stands closest to the divine order when she serves it, and her power is at its most dangerous when turned to purposes that lack the sanction of higher authority.

Bia is the Greek goddess of force. She is the daughter of the underworld goddess Styx and the Titan Pallas, and the sister of Kratos, the god of strength, Nike, the goddess of victory, and Zelos, the god of zeal. Together with her siblings she stands in the service of Zeus, and Styx and her children were the first spirits to support him in the war against the Titans. For that loyalty they were granted permanent places beside his throne.

Bia is the goddess of raw physical compulsion, of the might exerted upon the unwilling. She appears most memorably in the myth of Prometheus, where she and her brother Kratos accompany Hephaistos to hold the Titan while he is bound to the rock in punishment for the theft of fire.

Domains

Force, coercion, the might that constrains, and the enforcement of the decrees of the gods upon those who resist them.

Kinship

Daughter of Styx, the goddess of the underworld river, and the Titan Pallas. Sister of Kratos (Strength), Nike (Victory), and Zelos (Zeal). She and her siblings stand permanently at the throne of Zeus, having been the first spirits to declare for him in the war against the Titans.

Petition For

Compulsion in matters that will not yield to persuasion, the enforcement of a just decree upon the unwilling, and the raw might that binds what resists binding.
