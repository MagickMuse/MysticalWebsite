Samantabhadra, whose name means universal worthy or all-good, is a great bodhisattva of the Mahayana Buddhist tradition. He is one of the four principal bodhisattvas of Chinese Buddhism, paired most often with Manjusri: where Manjusri embodies the wisdom of the Buddhas, Samantabhadra embodies their practice and their vows. In the Avatamsaka Sutra it is the Ten Great Vows of Samantabhadra that mark the culmination of the bodhisattva path, laying out the disciplines by which a practitioner takes up the work of liberating all beings.

Domains

The universal practice of the Buddhas, the great vows through which a bodhisattva's aspiration is carried into action, the meditative concentration by which practice is sustained, and the compassion that extends itself outward to every being without exception.

Iconography

Samantabhadra is characteristically depicted seated on a great white elephant, most often the six-tusked white elephant that marks his particular vehicle. The elephant signifies the strength and gentle steadiness of his practice. He wears the regal ornaments of a bodhisattva and often holds a lotus, a scroll of scripture, or a wish-fulfilling jewel.

Cross-Tradition

In Japan Samantabhadra is known as Fugen; in China as Puxian; in Tibet as Kuntu Zangpo. In the Taoist tradition his figure has a counterpart in Bo Hsian, a wisdom god likewise depicted upon a white elephant.
