Roseline of Villeneuve (1267 to 17 January 1329) is an unofficial saint whose canonization process was begun but never completed. The daughter of Sibylle de Sabrant and Arnaud Villeneuve (Arnold of Villanova), a renowned Catalan alchemist, mystic, and physician, she grew up in Villeneuve Castle in Provence.

Domains

Roseline is credited with miraculous healings, including recovery of sight and mobility. She aids those unjustly imprisoned or held captive.

Iconography

Roseline is portrayed as a young woman bearing roses or with roses at her feet, referencing her bread-to-roses miracle.

Attributes

Asked for advice regarding the best route to Heaven, Roseline quoted Apollo: "Know thyself."

Sacred Sites

Roseline's body lies in the chapel near her family castle in Les Arcs, Provence. After the French Revolution, the site was deconsecrated and had various owners. It is now at the center of a large vineyard, Château Sainte Roseline. The chapel has been restored with art commissioned to honor her, including a mosaic by Marc Chagall called "The Angel's Dinner" and a bronze by Alberto Giacometti titled "The Miracle of the Roses."

Lore

Roseline wished to enter a convent, but her parents refused permission. She secretly slipped away from home to bring food to the poor despite her father's prohibition. When he caught her and demanded to know what she was hiding in her apron, knowing full well she carried bread, Roseline reluctantly opened it. The bread had miraculously vanished and roses spilled out instead. Her parents then allowed thirteen-year-old Roseline to join the Carthusian Abbey, where she was assigned to the kitchen.

Roseline prayed so intensely that she lost track of time and forgot all about cooking until roused by the bell summoning the nuns to the dining hall. Angels miraculously appeared to make dinner and set the table. In 1300, her parents built her an abbey at Celle-Rouboud, where she served as prioress until her death.

Miracles accompanied Roseline throughout her life and did not cease after death. Her brother Helios, a Crusader captured and imprisoned in Rhodes, invoked his dead sister's aid. His Saracen captors abruptly fled, leaving Helios to return home.

Roseline's tomb at Celle-Rouboud became a popular pilgrimage site. Her body remained so incorrupt and her eyes so bright that she appeared alive. Louis XIV, upon viewing her, ordered his physician to stick a needle into her left eye. The mark of that needle remains visible today.
