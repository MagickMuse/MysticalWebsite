Overview

Zephyr is the Greek god of the west wind, the warm breeze that breaks winter and ushers in spring. Among the four wind brothers he is the mildest, the bringer of soft weather, returning flowers, and the first stirrings of growth after frost. He is also a god marked by love and by jealousy, remembered most painfully in the story of Hyacinthus.

Magickal Properties

Zephyr governs the warm currents of air that lift the chill of late winter, dry the earth enough for planting, and carry pollen and birdsong across opening fields. Petition him for favorable wind on a journey, for the gentle weather that lets a garden wake, and for an easy transition from one season to the next. He is also called upon by those who must walk through their own jealousies without doing harm.

The Love of Hyacinthus

Zephyr loved the Spartan youth Hyacinthus, as did Apollo, and Hyacinthus chose Apollo. Coming upon the two of them playing discus, Zephyr was overtaken by jealousy and turned the throw aside on the wind, striking Hyacinthus dead. The flower that sprang from the youth's blood carries that story in every spring bloom, and Zephyr carries the consequence of that grief in his name.

Iconography and Attributes

Zephyr is depicted as a young winged god, often shown in flight with flowers spilling from his hands or his cloak. His instruments are the breeze, the scattering of petals, and the lifting of leaves at the turn of the season.

Colors

Pale green of new shoots, soft blue of spring sky, and the violet of the first hyacinths.

Sacred Sites

He is honored wherever the west wind first stirs the year, especially in groves and gardens that come to bloom early, and at any height open enough to feel his approach.

Ritual

Offer Zephyr the first cut flowers of the season, especially hyacinths, at an open window or in a high garden where the wind can reach them. Light incense and let the smoke be carried away rather than holding it close. When asking his help with jealousy, speak the name of the rivalry aloud and ask that the wind move past it without harm.

Lore

Zephyr is one of the four Anemoi, the wind brothers, alongside Boreas of the north, Notus of the south, and Eurus of the east. Of these, Zephyr is the gentlest, but his story with Hyacinthus is a reminder that even a soft wind can carry weight enough to kill. The Spartans, who kept the grave of Hyacinthus and held the Hyacinthia at midsummer, remembered Zephyr as the unseen third figure in that loss.

Additional Notes

Zephyr's other consorts include his sisters, Chloris and Iris, though he also adores young men. After he raped the Harpy Podarge, she gave birth to Achilles' horses. Zephyr lives in a cave in Thrace.
