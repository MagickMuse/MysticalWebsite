Bhairavi (also called Tripura Bhairavi, the fierce one of the three worlds) is one of the ten Mahavidyas and among the most fearsome of the group. She represents the Devi in her aspect of overwhelming, blood-marked ferocity.

Iconography

Bhairavi has a reddish complexion and wears a garland of severed heads. She holds a rosary and a book in two of her four hands and makes the signs of fearlessness and conferring boons with her other two hands. Her breasts are smeared with blood. The Kalika-purana adds that her eyes roll from intoxication and she stands on a corpse.

The combination of the rosary (spiritual practice), the book (sacred knowledge), fearlessness, and boon-conferring together with a blood-smeared body and garland of heads is characteristic of the Mahavidyas: terror and grace occupy the same form simultaneously.

Nature and Significance

Tripura Bhairavi is the fierce form of Tripura Sundari (Sodasi). Where Sodasi is the beautiful, erotic face of the three worlds, Bhairavi is the terrible face of the same reality. Her power to liberate and grant boons is inseparable from her capacity for destruction and terror.

In the Kalika-purana she is intoxicated and stands on a corpse, features she shares with Kali. This underlines the Mahavidyas' tendency to converge at their extremes: the group's most fearsome members resemble each other because they all express the same concentrated divine fury.

As a Mahavidya

Bhairavi is one of the ten forms Sati assumes when she surrounds and terrifies Shiva in the Mahavidya origin myth. The Mahavidyas assert the Devi's supreme autonomy and the terrifying depth of her power. Bhairavi contributes the overwhelming force of blood, fire, and ferocious grace.
