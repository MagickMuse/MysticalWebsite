Overview

Manat is one of the three pre-Islamic Arabian goddesses mentioned in the Koran (along with Allat and Al-Uzza). Her name means "Time," "Fate," or "Destiny." She is a spirit of destiny and fortune, whether good or bad. Less information about her survives than about her compatriots Allat and Al-Uzza, as her worship was suppressed with the rise of Islam.

Manifestation

Manat manifested as a large black, uncut stone, a sacred meteoric or volcanic rock that served as her cult image.

Domain

Destiny, fate, fortune, time.

Greek Identification

The Greeks identified Manat with Tyche, their goddess of fortune and chance.

Sacred Site

Manat's shrine was located between Mecca and Medina. It was destroyed by order of the Prophet Muhammad as part of the establishment of Islam and the elimination of pre-Islamic polytheistic worship.

Planet

Moon.

Historical Context

Manat was one of the three "daughters of Allah" mentioned in pre-Islamic Arabian religion. These three goddesses (Manat, Allat, and Al-Uzza) were extremely important in the religious life of pre-Islamic Arabia, particularly among the tribes of the region. Their worship represented the old polytheistic traditions that were later replaced by monotheistic Islam.

Symbolism

As a goddess of fate and time, Manat represented the inexorable forces of destiny that governed human lives: the predetermined course of events that could bring either fortune or misfortune.
