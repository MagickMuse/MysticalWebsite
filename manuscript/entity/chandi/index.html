Magickal Properties

Chandi is a powerful tribal and village goddess of birth and death who has been partially incorporated into the Hindu pantheon. Known as the Queen of Dust, she possesses tremendous oracular and prophetic abilities. Her dominion over dust from footprints grants her the power to magically influence people, making her particularly potent in love spells and binding magick. She displays different aspects of herself in various locations, earning titles like Chandi of the River or Chandi of the Grove.

As a disease spirit, Chandi wields dual power over healing and causing ailments, particularly eye diseases and cholera. She holds responsibility for livestock illnesses and must be propitiated for animal health. Her connection to birth and death energies makes her a formidable force in matters of life transitions and spiritual transformation.

Ritual Uses

Chandi is invoked in love spells, especially those involving footprint magick where dust taken from a person's footprint is used to influence them. She serves as an oracular spirit for divination and prophecy, providing guidance on safety, romance, and fertility matters. Practitioners seek her aid in banishing malevolent spirits and protecting against supernatural harm.

Her worship includes placement of round black stones on thrones with painted female faces, representing her tribal origins. Water pitcher rituals are performed for scrying and divination purposes, tapping into her prophetic powers.

Offerings

Round black stones seated on thrones or painted with a woman's face, red sari fabric and garments, garlands of skulls, water pitchers for divination work, offerings appropriate for disease spirits and fertility goddesses.

Favored People

Tribal communities and village practitioners, those seeking romantic love or fertility, people needing protection from evil spirits, practitioners of footprint magick and binding spells, livestock owners seeking animal health.

Petition For

Safety and protection from harm, romantic love and relationship success, fertility and successful childbirth, banishing malevolent spirits and supernatural threats, healing eye ailments and cholera, cattle health and livestock protection, oracular guidance and prophetic visions.

Warnings

Chandi bestows both good fortune and disaster with equal measure, she is not a benevolent spirit but rather an impartial force. Footprint magick involving dust creates powerful binding connections that may be difficult to break. Her disease powers mean she can cause the very ailments she heals. Approach with proper ritual protocol and understanding of her dual nature.

Additional Notes

Chandi is venerated alongside Kali and the Dakinis in the Hindu pantheon. Her title Queen of Dust reflects her mastery over footprint magick and earth-based influence spells. She manifests differently across various locales, adapting her energy to local spiritual needs. Her mount is a lion, emphasizing her fierce protective nature.

Candika (fierce)

Chandi is also known by the Sanskrit form Candika, meaning fierce or burning, and under this name she is honored in a further register as a goddess of desire in the Hindu Epic and Puranic tradition. In this office she is sometimes counted among the seven mothers, the saptamataras, and among the eight mothers, the astamataras, taking her place beside the other fierce matrika goddesses whose worship gathers the ferocious feminine power of the tradition. The name Candika also runs through the family of goddesses whose names sound the canda root: Canda, Candanayika, Candarupa, Candavati, Candogra, and the compound Camunda, all of them forms of Durga or of the terrible mother power of which Chandi herself is one central expression.
