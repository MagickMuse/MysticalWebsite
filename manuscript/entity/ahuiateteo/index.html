⚠️ The Ahuiateteo are sacred to Aztec and Mesoamerican Indigenous traditions. These are closed practices and should only be approached by those within the originating culture or with explicit cultural permission.

Magickal Properties

The Ahuiateteo are the "Lords of Excess," five spirits who embody the dangers and consequences of overindulgence in drinking, gambling, and sex. Each bears a calendar name with the number five: 5 Flower (Macuilxochitl), 5 Lizard, 5 Vulture, 5 Rabbit, and 5 Grass. For the Aztecs, five symbolized excess, one too many.

Ritual Uses

The Ahuiateteo are invoked by fortune-tellers and diviners to help read the writing on the wall and predict consequences of excess. They embody the natural consequences of overindulgence and administer punishment, usually in the form of illness.

Offerings

Traditional Aztec offerings may be appropriate, but these spirits are generally not petitioned for favors. They are more spirits to be wary of than to actively petition.

Favored People

Fortune-tellers and diviners who work with them to interpret warnings and consequences of excess. They assist those who help others understand the dangers of overindulgence.

Petition For

Divination regarding consequences of overindulgence and help reading warning signs of excess. They can provide insight into the natural consequences of going too far.

Warnings

Spirits of punishment who administer consequences for excess in drinking, gambling, and sex. Not benevolent protectors and should be approached with extreme caution, if at all. They enforce natural consequences rather than providing protection.

Additional Notes

When you've had five, you've had too much. Punishment for excess was perceived as natural consequence by the Aztecs, and the Ahuiateteo ensure punishment is administered. Their chief is Macuilxochitl (5 Flower), who is associated with Xochipilli. They are associated with the direction South.
