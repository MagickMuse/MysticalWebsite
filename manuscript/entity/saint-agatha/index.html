Saint Agatha is renowned for healing breast cancer and breast afflictions.

Classification

Venerated by most branches of Christianity.

Life and History

Saint Agatha, a Sicilian martyr, died in approximately 250 CE. Even by the standards of martyrdom, her death was particularly horrific and prolonged.

Agatha, a beautiful, wealthy young woman, secretly converted to Christianity and committed her virginity to Christ. Depending on the version of her story, either she refused to marry an important Pagan official or he attempted to blackmail her, demanding sex in exchange for keeping her Christian identity secret.

Martyrdom

Agatha was steadfast, refusing all sexual advances or offers of marriage. She was sent to a brothel and repeatedly raped. Deprived of food and water, she was racked and beaten and ripped by iron hooks. Her breasts were first crushed, then severed, and she was burned with torches.

Agatha was locked up in a dungeon, bleeding, mutilated, and suffering, when Saint Peter, accompanied by a youth carrying a torch, appeared in a vision of light and healed her. (In some versions, he reattaches her breasts.)

Rather than being awed by this miracle, her captors rolled her naked over live coals mixed with burning embers and sharp, broken potsherds. Agatha, tortured beyond endurance, prayed for release. The area immediately suffered a massive earthquake and Agatha died.

Alternative Version

Another version describes how Agatha, in the throes of pain, commanded the volcano Mount Etna to erupt, which it immediately did. The crowds panicked and begged authorities to stop torturing Agatha. She was returned to jail, where she died. She is believed to have been approximately twenty years old.

Pagan Connections

Early Christian icons depicted Agatha carrying her severed breasts on an offering plate. They were eventually confused for bells, making Agatha the matron saint of bell makers.

Agatha's iconography and attributes (snakes, metal implements, bells, even breasts) bear a powerful resemblance to goddesses once venerated in Sicily prior to Christianity, particularly Isis, Demeter, or Aetna, the goddess of the volcano with which Agatha is closely associated. Many of Agatha's functions overlap with those performed by these goddesses.

Agatha's name, meaning "good," echoes Agathadaemon, the good snake spirit once worshipped in Sicily. Some devotees and scholars suggest that secret veneration of one or more of these deities may hide beneath the saint's veil.

The Bona Dea Connection

The term Bona Dea, literally "the good goddess," refers to a class of goddesses worshipped exclusively by women. A Bona Dea named Agatha whose specialty was healing infertility was worshipped at Mount Etna. She may have traveled in the retinue of the healing deity Asclepius, along with the goddesses Hygeia and Panacea.

The Veil of Saint Agatha

Saint Agatha's veil was taken from her tomb and preserved. It allegedly keeps Mount Etna, Europe's largest active volcano, magically under control. The annual display of her veil during her festival in Catania, Sicily, is accompanied by tambourine performances reminiscent of those once offered to goddesses like Isis or Kybele.

Iconography

Agatha carries her breasts on a plate or holds the palm of martyrdom. She may be wearing her veil. Sacred images typically depict Mount Etna, frequently drawn to resemble a huge breast, threatening to overflow with milk rather than lava.

Attributes

Bell, brazier filled with smoking coals, iron tongs, pincers, and other smith's tools, a pair of severed breasts, long red veil, sword.

Feast Days

  • February 5 (primary feast)
  • August 17 (commemorating the return of her body from Constantinople)

Festivals

The largest annual festival in Catania, Sicily, honors Agatha. Although the festival technically runs between 3 and 5 February, celebratory preparations begin on 1 February. The festival features candlelit processions, fireworks, and poetry and music competitions.

Ritual Food

Special breast-shaped pastries called Saint Agatha's Breasts are eaten on her feast day.

Ritual Note

Drink Holy Water on Agatha's feast day and allegedly snakes will not harm you all year.

Sacred Sites

  • Cathedral in Catania, Sicily (relics and veil)
  • Church of Sant'Agata La Guilla, Palermo (braided hair)
  • Church of Sant'Agata La Pedata, Palermo (footprint in limestone)
  • Church of Saint Agatha of the Goths, Rome (built c. 460)
  • Saint Agatha's Crypt, Rabat, Malta
  • Church of Saint Gadea, Burgos, Spain
  • Church of Saint Agatha, Cremona, Italy
  • Mount Etna
  • Bell foundries
  • Santa Agueda (Half-Moon Mountain), Menorca, Spain

Spanish Veneration as Agueda

The name Agueda is the Spanish variant of Agatha. Although Agueda shares Agatha's feast day and mythos, her veneration in Spain is unique with distinct attributes and rituals.

Like Saint Agatha, Agueda's assistance is invoked for breastfeeding, but she is also invoked against headache and chest pain.

Favored People (Spanish Tradition)

Married women, lactating women.

Attribute (Spanish Tradition)

A silver lamp with which she lights the way for mariners and others who ply or travel the sea.

Sacred Site (Spanish Tradition)

Santa Agueda, also known as Half-Moon Mountain, in Menorca, Spain, is a 254-meter-high mountain once used for military surveillance by the Romans. It was also the last point of resistance for the Moors of Menorca. Women traditionally climb the mountain to offer prayers and petitions to Saint Agueda.

Festivals (Spanish Tradition)

An annual festival honoring her is held in Segovia, Spain, in February. Agueda is celebrated with circle dances. Women play primary ritual roles during her festival, which is unusual for a Roman Catholic festival. There is also an annual festival held for her in Guam.
