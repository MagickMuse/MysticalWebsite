Boudicca is a folk saint and ancestral hero.

Life and History

Boudica (died c. 60 CE) was wife to Prasutagus, a client king who ruled the Iceni tribe of Norfolk, England, under Roman patronage. Client kings maintained local peace in exchange for Roman protection, but these arrangements were personal and dissolved upon death.

When Prasutagus died, his will named his daughters and Emperor Nero as co-heirs, a formality likely forced upon him. He probably never expected Rome to actually claim his throne, perhaps unaware they had absorbed other territories through similar legal pretexts.

Roman Brutality

Nero's financial agent arrived to inventory and seize the kingdom. His manner was aggressive and insulting. Boudica, whose name derives from the Celtic word for "victory," declared herself leader of the Iceni in defiance of Rome, likely on behalf of her young daughters.

Rome's response was savage. They stripped and flogged Boudica publicly, then raped her daughters before her eyes. Romans called in predatory loans they had forced Icenians to accept, seizing property when people couldn't pay.

The Uprising

Roman authorities assumed they had broken Boudica. They were catastrophically wrong. In 60 CE, she assembled over 120,000 warriors from multiple tribes eager to expel the Romans. She rode before them in a chariot with her daughters, releasing a rabbit in an oracular ritual before battle.

Her forces sacked and burned several Roman towns including London. Roman historians report over 80,000 Roman deaths. Governor Suetonius Paulinus had to abandon his destruction of the druid groves on Anglesey to confront her.

Finally trapped and defeated, Boudica chose poison over Roman captivity, as Cleopatra would for similar reasons.

True Ruler?

Described as being "of royal blood," modern scholars theorize Prasutagus ruled only because he married her. Boudica may have been the true hereditary ruler, her marriage to a Roman-friendly king possibly imposed by the occupiers, explaining her fury when Rome denied her daughters' succession.

Modern Veneration

Boudica is embraced by Celtic revivalists and modern pagans. Those of Celtic heritage invoke her spirit, as do all who resist oppression.

Manifestation

Witnesses since at least the 19th century report her apparition riding a chariot through Lincolnshire.

She appeared as a very tall, grim woman with long fair hair falling to her hips. She wore a multicolored tunic beneath a cloak fastened with a brooch, and a great gold torc around her neck. Her gaze was piercing, her voice harsh. She carried a spear.
