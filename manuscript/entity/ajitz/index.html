⚠️ Ajitz is venerated within Mayan and Indigenous Mesoamerican traditions. These are closed practices and should only be approached by those within the originating cultures or with explicit cultural permission.

Magickal Properties

Ajitz is a Mayan word meaning "shaman," "healer," "wizard," or "holy man" and is also the name given to the spirit of a deified shaman. During the Spanish Conquest of Guatemala, this particular shaman disappeared into the jungle carrying a doll in his own image and an axe, symbolizing his power to clear paths and cut through obstacles. He eventually reemerged as the spirit Ajitz.

Ritual Uses

In Guatemala, traditional Spanish dance dramas depicting the conflict between Christians and Moors evolved into the Dance of the Conquest. The dance drama evolved to feature Ajitz in the role of a shaman who neither converts nor dies but survives. The dancers who impersonate him wear red painted masks and red clothes and carry axes and red dolls.

Offerings

Occult tools, botanicals, cigars, and alcoholic beverages. Present offerings that honor his role as a spiritual protector and obstacle remover.

Favored People

Shamans, healers, those seeking spiritual protection, spiritual practitioners working with Mayan traditions, and people needing obstacles cleared from their paths.

Petition For

Banishment of evil spirits, spiritual protection, clearing of obstacles, assistance in shamanic work, and protection during spiritual transitions.

Warnings

A deified shaman who disappeared into the jungle during the Spanish Conquest, carrying a doll in his own image and an axe. His power involves cutting through spiritual and physical obstacles.

Additional Notes

Traditional wooden masks depict him with black eyes and hair, mustache, and beard. Statues are maintained on home altars. He is usually depicted enthroned but may stand, axe in hand, ready to strike. His attributes include an axe, his own image, and scales. His sacred color is red.
