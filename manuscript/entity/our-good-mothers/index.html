The Secret Name

The real name of these Breton birth Fairies is a secret or is forgotten: people prefer to use the euphemism Our Good Mothers to stay on their good side. If you call them your good mother, perhaps that is how they will behave.

Groups of Three

Our Good Mothers appear in groups of three to bestow a baby's fate and fortune, reflecting the ancient triad structure of Fate goddesses found throughout Indo-European cultures (Greek Moirae, Roman Parcae, Norse Norns). Their leader is Béfind, who as the eldest or most powerful coordinates the fate-determination ritual.

Etymology

Fairy derives from Old French fée, from Latin fatua (female seer) and fatum (fate or destiny). In Italian, fata means both "The Fates" and "The Fairies." Our Good Mothers embody this ancient connection, being simultaneously fairies and Fates in Breton tradition. Breton tradition maintains strong Celtic roots while incorporating French cultural elements, reflected in their preference for champagne and elaborate French-style dining.

Sleeping Beauty Connection

The fairy tale of Sleeping Beauty describes actual spiritual rituals performed throughout Europe: offering tables laid for fairies who bestow a baby's fate at birth celebrations. Our Good Mothers are the Breton expression of this tradition, with their preference for elaborate feasts directly reflected in fairy tale banquet scenes.

Offerings

Our Good Mothers enjoy lavish, multi-course meals befitting honored guests: multiple courses of fine food, champagne, whiskey and other strong spirits, quality wine, pastries and elaborate desserts, fresh seasonal fruits, and fine nuts. Set a beautiful table as if hosting nobility. The quality and abundance of offerings may influence the favorability of their pronouncements.

Warning

Do not skimp on offerings. Our Good Mothers expect and deserve lavish hospitality, and poor offerings may result in unfavorable destiny pronouncements. The birth period is sacred and requires proper respect through abundance.
