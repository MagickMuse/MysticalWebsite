Magickal Properties

Ekajata, originally the Bon sky goddess, is now more popularly known as Blue Tara. She is described as wrathful, lustful, and radiant. Ekajata, fierce Lady of Heaven, was among the Bon Spirits subdued by Padmasambhava. He pierced her right eye and banished her until she agreed to serve the Dharma. Ekajata as Blue Tara is now considered a form of Tara. She embodies fierce feminine protective energy and is associated with the sky and celestial realms.

Blue Tara spreads joy and happiness. She transmutes negative emotions into positive. She is the Tara of protection who does more than just banish enemies: she removes your fear of them, too. Blue Tara clears away obstacles that obstruct her devotees' spiritual (and other) paths. She is a spirit of opportunity and good fortune. Blue Tara protects secret mantras: her own is known only to initiates. Her wrathful aspect is balanced by her radiant nature, making her a complex figure of both fierce protection and spiritual illumination.

Ritual Uses

Ekajata is invoked for protection and spiritual transformation. She can be called upon to overcome obstacles and to access fierce feminine power. Work with her through traditional Tibetan Buddhist practices.

Offerings

Incense, lamp light, flowers, disciplined vows, traditional Tibetan Buddhist offerings. No secret mantras without initiation.

Favored People

Those seeking protection, those facing obstacles, secret mantra practitioners, Tibetan Buddhist practitioners, those seeking fierce feminine protection.

Petition For

Protection, spreading joy and happiness, transmuting negative emotions, removing fear of enemies, clearing obstacles, opportunity, good fortune, protecting secret mantras, spiritual transformation, swift removal of obstacles, fierce guardianship.

Warnings

Ekajata was subdued by Padmasambhava who pierced her right eye; now serves Dharma; protects secret mantras; own mantra known only to initiates. She is described as wrathful and lustful. Her energy is intense and should be approached with proper preparation and respect within the context of Tibetan Buddhist practice. Requires proper preparation within Tibetan Buddhist practice.

Iconography

Blue Tara may be portrayed with up to twelve heads. She wears a necklace of skulls, a tiger skin, and flayed human skin. She wears the image of Blue Buddha Akshobya in her hair.

Attributes

Arrow, ax, bell, book, bow, chalice, conch, hook, knife, noose, skull cup (skull bowl), staff, sword, vajra chopper, and curved knife.

Additional Notes

Blue Tara sometimes serves Khadiravani and appears in company with Khadiravani, sometimes in the entourage of Green Tara. Her colors are sky blue and dark olive green. As Blue Tara, she represents the fierce aspect of Tara's compassion.
