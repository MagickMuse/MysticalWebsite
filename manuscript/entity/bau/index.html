Magickal Properties

  • Ancient Sumerian Goddess of Healing and Life, once among the most significant deities of the Sumerian pantheon
  • Intensely associated with dogs and may appear as dog‑headed woman
  • Lady of Life invoked for healing, prosperity, abundance, and emotional and psychic relief
  • Has seven daughters known as the Lukur Girls who served as her priestesses

Ritual Uses

  • Invoke for any sort of healing as well as prosperity and abundance
  • Petition for emotional and psychic relief
  • Honor at altars featuring dog imagery

Offerings

Build altar featuring dog imagery; appropriate gifts include contributions to dog shelters and hospitals or acts on behalf of dogs in need

Favored People

Those seeking healing, prosperity, abundance, emotional and psychic relief

Petition For

Healing of all kinds, prosperity, abundance, emotional relief, psychic healing

Warnings

Her popularity faded as Inanna rose, and the Lukur Priestesses were eventually transferred to Inanna. Approach with respect for her ancient power.

Additional Notes

  • Daughter of Sirius, the Dog Star
  • Sacred animal: Dog
  • Seven daughters (Lukur Girls): Zazaru, Nipae, Urnuntaea, Hegirnuna, Heshaga, Zargu, Zurgu
  • In later Babylonian incarnation as Gula Bau, walks Earth accompanied by pack of hounds
  • Dog and puppy burials found in her temple ruins

Sacred Sites

She was locally worshiped in Lagas, where the ruler Gudea built her a temple.
