The Virgin Mary is renowned for healing any illness or affliction, fertility, lactation, easy childbirth, and intercession for all miracles. She is the patron saint of bicyclists, airline pilots, sailors, mothers, blood donors, coffeehouse owners, and construction workers.

Venerated as a saint by every Christian denomination that acknowledges saints. Mary is the foremost and most preeminent of Christianity's saints, though she transcends that status. Devotion to Mary rivals that of her son Jesus. Many perceive her as the epitome of the feminine divine, described by some as "Christianity's goddess."

Life and Legend

Mary was born to a Jewish family in Judea, possibly in Nazareth. Her parents are Saints Anne and Joachim. Her birth was miraculous, announced by an angel to her aged, childless mother. As a young woman, Mary was betrothed to Joseph when the angel Gabriel announced she would bear the Messiah, conceived by the Holy Spirit rather than human means.

Mary witnessed her son's life, ministry, and death. She was present at the wedding at Cana when Jesus performed his first public miracle, and she witnessed the Crucifixion. Her death is not recorded in scripture. According to tradition, when the Apostles opened her grave years later, they found it empty, concluding her body had been taken to Heaven. The Assumption of Mary is official doctrine for Catholic and Orthodox churches.

Goddess Syncretism

Veneration of Mary was officially sanctioned at the Council of Ephesus in 431 CE, though unofficial devotions existed long before, particularly in formerly Pagan communities devoted to mother-and-son deities like Isis and Horus or Cybele and Attis. Mary assumed roles previously associated with goddesses. As Stella Maris (Star of the Sea), she shares a title with Isis and Ishtar. The church at Ephesus, previously a stronghold of goddess worship, was dedicated to Mary.

Mary's Bean

Seeds from morning glory family vines native to Central America wash up on northern European shores, especially Scotland's Hebrides and Orkney Islands. Called Mary's Beans, Crucifix Beans, or Sea Beans, they bear cross-shaped markings. These beans reputedly bestow luck and spiritual protection. A woman holding a Mary's Bean during labor is allegedly guaranteed easy childbirth.

Magical Properties

The Virgin Mary is often interpreted as a Corn Mother with Jesus as her fruit, cut down in his prime, sacrificed, and then resurrected. In votive imagery, Mary is often depicted holding her infant in the same manner that ancient Corn Mothers hold stalks of wheat. This relationship connects Mary to the ancient tradition of grain deities whose death and resurrection ensures the continuation of the harvest cycle.

Corn Mother Symbolism

This Corn Mother interpretation is insinuated through the sacrament of Holy Communion, wherein Jesus' body is consumed in a wafer made from wheat. The Vatican is adamant that communion wafers be made from wheat, maintaining this ancient grain connection even in modern times. Despite the Inquisition's perception of folk practices as sacrilegious, use of communion wafers in Roman Catholic folk spells were almost always intended for purposes of agricultural benefit or healing—the equivalent of scattering a sacrificed Pagan grain god's ashes over the land.

Astrological Connection

Mary is associated with the astrological sign Virgo, although the concept of the virgin is understood in its modern, literal, physical sense. This connects her to the ancient grain goddesses associated with Virgo, particularly those connected to the harvest season.

Ritual Uses

Invoke Mary for healing, agricultural blessings, maternal protection, and intercession. In folk Catholic practice, communion wafers were used in spells for agricultural benefit—the desire was not to injure Christ but to benefit from the power of his body, continuing ancient traditions of using the grain deity's essence to bless the land.

Offerings

Candles, flowers, prayers, wheat offerings. In folk practice, communion wafers were sometimes used in healing and agricultural spells.

Favored People

Christians, mothers, those seeking healing and agricultural blessings, farmers, practitioners of folk Catholic traditions.

Petition For

Intercession, healing, agricultural blessings, maternal protection, resurrection and renewal, fertility of land and body.

Warnings

Approach with respect for Christian tradition. The Corn Mother interpretation exists alongside orthodox Christian theology. Folk practices using communion wafers were historically condemned by the Inquisition but reflected ancient agricultural magic traditions rather than malevolent intent.

Juniper's Shelter

An Italian tradition of hanging Juniper during the winter holidays traces back to the flight from Bethlehem. Fleeing King Herod's soldiers with the infant Jesus, Mary took shelter among juniper's branches, and the tree concealed them.

Additional Notes

Mary's role as Corn Mother demonstrates the continuity of ancient grain goddess worship through Christian imagery. The symbolism of Jesus as wheat—planted (buried), growing, harvested (crucified), and providing sustenance (communion)—follows the pattern of dying and resurrecting grain gods throughout world mythology. Mary, as the bearer of this divine grain, fulfills the role of the mother who both mourns and facilitates the necessary sacrifice that brings life to her people.
