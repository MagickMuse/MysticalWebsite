Classification

Vietnamese folk saints.

Overview

Trung Trac and Trung Nhi were daughters of the Lord of Me-Linh, a powerful first-century Vietnamese military chief. Born around 10 CE, these noblewomen became legendary for leading the most famous rebellion in Vietnamese history against Chinese Han dynasty occupation.

The Rebellion

Vietnam had been under Chinese rule since 111 BCE. Chinese overlords controlled government positions, and unfamiliar taxation systems replaced traditional gift-giving practices. Discontent simmered for generations.

In 39 CE, a Chinese general attempted to suppress the growing resistance by raping Trung Trac and executing her husband. The plan backfired catastrophically. The enraged sisters organized an army of approximately 80,000 soldiers, both men and women. Thirty-six of their generals were female, including the sisters' own mother.

The rebellion succeeded. The Trung Sisters were proclaimed co-queens of an independent Vietnam, establishing their royal court at Me-Linh (now a suburb of Hanoi). Their kingdom stretched from Hue to southern China and lasted three years.

Death and Apotheosis

In 42 CE, Chinese forces returned. Despite fierce resistance, the Vietnamese army was overwhelmed. According to the most accepted account, rather than face capture, the sisters threw themselves into the Hat-Giang River and drowned. Other traditions say they died in battle, were beheaded (their heads sent to China as trophies), or never died at all but ascended into the clouds.

Manifestation

The sisters appear in dreams and visions. A 12th-century Vietnamese king described them as beautiful women wearing green robes over red garments, with red crowns and sashes, riding iron horses.

Iconography

Typically portrayed riding elephants into battle or over clouds, symbolizing their power over precipitation.

Attributes

Each sister wields a sword.

Sacred Sites

  • Hai Ba Trung Temple, Hanoi: Their most famous shrine, located near Hoan Kiem Lake.
  • Statues, temples, and shrines throughout Vietnam.
  • Historically, Taoist shrines in China (including Canton) also honored them.

Festival

A festival is traditionally held at the Hai Ba Trung Temple on the fifth and sixth days of the second lunar month. Their feast day falls on the fifteenth day of the first Vietnamese lunar month.

Additional Notes

Trung Trac is the elder and is considered the dominant sister; the sisters may be invoked together or separately. Their cult spread even to China by the fifteenth century. Trung Sisters Day is Vietnam's National Women's Day. According to the fifteenth-century historian Ngo Si Lien, prayers to the sisters never go unanswered.
