Magickal Properties

Achelous is king of Greece's river spirits, venerated throughout Greece and southern Italy. His primary role was ensuring successful maturity of young men. He is extremely fertile, father of Nymphs, Sirens, and many sea spirits. He begets bodies of water including streams, rivers, and springs, making him the spirit to petition for new water sources.

Ritual Uses

Honor Achelous together with his daughters, the Nymphs, or surround him with mermaids and Sirens on his altar. Young men traditionally cut their hair and dedicated it to him in exchange for guidance and protection. Create rituals for successful maturation and water blessings.

Offerings

Hair dedication (especially from young men) in exchange for guidance and protection or as fulfillment of vows. Fresh water libations are essential. Include nautical offerings and honor his retinue.

Favored People

Young men seeking successful maturity, those desperately needing new water sources, people working with river spirits, and those seeking fertility and prosperity.

Petition For

Successful maturity of young men, new sources of water (streams, rivers, springs), guidance and protection, fertility, prosperity, and assistance with water-related needs.

Warnings

Always include fresh water libations and honor his daughters the Nymphs when you petition him. Avoid leaving offerings on dry ground or making solitary petitions that exclude his retinue.

Additional Notes

He competed with Heracles for the same woman; during battle, Heracles ripped off one of his horns, which some theorize is the true horn of plenty. Zeus himself instructed that offerings be made to Achelous. He typically appears as a bull with human face or man with bull's face, a horned spirit with one horn broken or missing. Water flows from his long beard like a waterfall.

Lineage

Achelous is the son of Okeanos and Tethys. His daughters were said to be the Sirenes, and his consort was Melpomene.

Rivalry with Heracles

He was a rival suitor for Deianeira, who eventually became the wife of Heracles.

The River Achelous

A river of the same name runs into the Ionian Sea.

Etruscan Reception

In Etruscan tradition he appears as Achlae. Among his attributes, the bull horns are particularly emphasized.
