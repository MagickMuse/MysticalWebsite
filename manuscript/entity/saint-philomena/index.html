Saint Philomena is renowned for healing autism in children.

Saint Philomena is a young virgin martyr whose relics were discovered in the Catacombs of Priscilla in Rome in 1802. Despite being removed from the Roman Catholic liturgical calendar in 1961 due to lack of historical documentation, she remains extremely popular in folk Catholicism and is associated with miraculous healings and intercessions.

She is particularly beloved by practitioners of New Orleans Voodoo and was a favorite saint of Marie Laveau.

Domains

Philomena specializes in desperate, impossible, and forgotten causes. She intercedes for healing (particularly cancer, heart conditions, and infertility), protection of children and pregnant women, and miraculous interventions when all other options have failed.

Iconography

Philomena appears as a young girl around twelve years old, dressed in pink or white. She holds a palm branch symbolizing martyrdom and often carries arrows, an anchor, or a lily. Her image sometimes includes a crown, representing her heavenly reward.

Attributes

Philomena is associated with the colors pink and white. Her sacred number is 14, corresponding to her age at death. The Cord of Saint Philomena, a blessed red and white cord worn around the waist, serves as a healing amulet.

Colors

Pink, White

Sacred Sites

The Sanctuary of Saint Philomena in Mugnano del Cardinale, Italy houses her relics and remains an active pilgrimage destination. Her cultus spread rapidly after her relics were transferred there in 1805.

Lore

When workers discovered her tomb in the catacombs, they found tiles bearing the inscription LUMENA PAX TE CUM FI, which they rearranged to read PAX TECUM FILUMENA (Peace be with you, Philomena). The tomb also contained a vial of dried blood, indicating martyrdom. Her legendary biography, revealed through visions to a nun in Naples, describes her as a Greek princess who refused Emperor Diocletian's marriage proposal and endured multiple tortures before her beheading. Pope Gregory XVI canonized her in 1837, making her the only person canonized solely on the basis of miraculous intercessions rather than historical documentation. When the Vatican removed her from the calendar in 1961, citing lack of evidence for her existence, devotion to her actually increased among folk Catholics and African Diaspora practitioners.
