The Gnostic saints, also called Thelemite saints, are commemorated within Thelema, the religion founded by author and magus Aleister Crowley (1875-1947) in 1904. The Gnostic saints include historical and mythic individuals who are venerated within that religion. The original list of Gnostic saints is found in Liber XV, "Book 15," also known as the Gnostic Mass, written by Crowley in 1913. The saints are invoked during the Gnostic Mass.

The Original Saints

All the original Gnostic saints are male. The list includes:

Spiritual Founders and Deities

Lao Tzu (founder of Taoism), Siddhartha Gautama (the historical Buddha), Krishna, Thoth, Moses, Dionysus, Mohammed, Hermes, Pan, Priapus, Osiris, Melchizedek, Heracles, Orpheus, Odysseus

Classical and Medieval Figures

Virgil, Rabelais, Simon Magus, Basilides, Valentinus, Merlin, Arthur, Charlemagne

Renaissance and Early Modern

Roger Bacon, Francis Bacon, Jacques de Molay (Templar Grand Master), Christian Rosencreutz (founder of Rosicrucians), Dr. John Dee (astrologer, spy, magus) and Edward Kelley, Elias Ashmole (alchemist and antiquarian)

Modern Era

Adam Weishaupt (founder of Illuminati), Wolfgang von Goethe, King Ludwig II of Bavaria, Eliphas Levi (Alphonse Louis Constant), Friedrich Nietzsche, Gerard Encausse (Papus), Algernon Charles Swinburne (author)

Crowley counted himself among the saints.

Later Additions

The list of saints remained as Crowley wrote it for decades:

  • 1997: William Blake was added by virtue of an essay by Crowley describing Blake as a saint
  • 2000: Giordano Bruno was added, commemorating the 400th anniversary of his execution

Related Entities
