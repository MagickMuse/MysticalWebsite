Joseph of Arimathea, venerated as a saint by most branches of Christianity, is the patron saint of aromatherapists, perfumers, and those who prepare bodies for burial.

Life and History

Joseph of Arimathea was a wealthy member of the Sanhedrin (Jewish council) who became a secret disciple of Jesus. After the Crucifixion, he boldly requested Jesus' body from Pontius Pilate and provided his own new tomb for the burial. Along with Nicodemus, he prepared the body with myrrh, aloes, and other precious aromatics according to Jewish burial customs.

Powers and Domains

As one who anointed Christ's body with precious aromatics, Joseph is invoked by aromatherapists, perfumers, and those who work with sacred oils and unguents. He also patronizes funeral directors and those who minister to the dead.

The Grail Legends

Joseph features prominently in Arthurian and Grail mythology. According to medieval legend, he caught Christ's blood in the cup used at the Last Supper (the Holy Grail) and later brought it to Britain, establishing the first Christian church at Glastonbury.

Sacred Sites

  • Glastonbury Abbey, England, where legend says he planted his staff, which took root as the Glastonbury Thorn
  • The Chalice Well at Glastonbury

Feast Days

  • March 17 (Western Church)
  • July 31 (Eastern Orthodox)
