Queen of Snakes; Destroyer of Poison

Overview

Manasa, sister of Vasuki the Naga King, is a snake goddess who is extremely popular among women in parts of India. She is classified as a Naga. She heals, bestows, and enhances personal fertility. She grants wishes, protects against snakes, and provides wealth, abundance, and success. Manasa is a healing spirit whose specialty is infectious and contagious illnesses including smallpox and AIDS.

Mythology: The Abandoned Wife

Once upon a time, Manasa married the sage Jaratkaru and strove to be the perfect wife. One day Jaratkaru fell asleep with his head in her lap. She didn't move for hours lest she disturb him, but finally the sun set and she knew it was time for him to perform important evening rituals. Very, very gently and respectfully, she woke her husband. He was livid, claiming that he would have woken up on his own at the right time. Accusing Manasa of breaking her vow of obedience, he abandoned her.

Personality

Ever since her husband's rebuke and rejection, she has been a touchy, somewhat hypersensitive spirit who demands respect and devotion. She is jealous and may or may not be willing to share devotees with other spirits. In other words, if you develop a close relationship with her, she may prefer that you not venerate other spirits.

Healing Powers

Manasa can allegedly heal any snake bite, even those for which no known antidote exists. Herpetologists who search for unknown species might do well to cultivate a relationship with Manasa, just in case.

How She Signals Interest

Manasa demonstrates interest in people by causing their hair to mat. If she is aggravated or defending a devotee, her preferred weapon is snake bite. Snakes are her messengers.

Manifestation

  • May manifest as a beautiful woman or a snake
  • Rides through the sky in a chariot drawn by snakes
  • May have one eye

Iconography

  • A woman covered or surrounded by snakes

  • Attribute: Lotus
  • Spirit Allies: Nagas; she travels in Shiva's entourage and is sometimes called his daughter, but this may be intended to make her subservient to him instead of a competitor
  • Element: Water

Sacred Time

The Manasa Puja (feast) occurs during the Hindu month of Sravana.

Family

Sister of Vasuki, the Naga King; husband (former) was the sage Jaratkaru.

As a Village Goddess

Manasa is one of the great regional village goddesses of the north, the snake mother whose worship is rooted in local communities and bound up with their everyday survival. Like other village goddesses she is fiercely independent and easily slighted, and her power is felt most sharply when she strikes suddenly and without warning. A snakebite that comes out of nowhere and kills swiftly carries her presence into a household, tearing open the fragile order of ordinary life and reminding people how thin the membrane is between safety and sudden death.

Ambivalence and the Fragility of Life

Her nature is ambivalent in the manner of the disease and disaster goddesses: she both inflicts harm and protects against it, both sends the venom and heals the bite that no other remedy can touch. To attend to a goddess like Manasa is to keep before one's eyes the truth that an ordered, settled world can be overturned in an instant, and so to hold a steadier and less brittle view of life.
