Gabriel of the Sorrowful Mother is a Roman Catholic saint renowned for healing and preventing tuberculosis.

Early Life

Francis Possenti, the future Saint Gabriel of the Sorrowful Mother, was born 1 March 1838 in Assisi. The eleventh of thirteen children, he was baptized in the same font used to baptize Saint Francis of Assisi centuries before. Possenti's mother died in 1842.

Although devoutly religious from childhood, no one predicted in his teen years that Possenti would become a saint. He loved parties, flirted with girls, and was vain about his appearance, spending hours before the mirror primping. His nickname was "the dancer."

Conversion

In 1851, Possenti fell dangerously ill. He vowed that if he recovered, he would adopt a religious vocation. He recovered but forgot or ignored his vow. In 1853, suffering from a serious throat abscess, he again made a similar vow, and this time kept it. He attributed his recovery to the intercession of Andrew Bobola.

Shortly afterward, the older sister who had raised him died during a cholera epidemic. When the cathedral in Spoleto organized a religious procession featuring an ancient Marian icon, young Possenti had a profound spiritual experience as the icon passed. He heard a voice asking why he remained in this world. He resolved to enter the Passionist Congregation. On 21 September 1856, he received the habit and a new name: Gabriel of the Sorrowful Mother.

Death

Possenti died of tuberculosis at age twenty-three on 27 February 1862, before his ordination. On his deathbed, he held an image of the Sorrowful Mother. Just before he died, Gabriel suddenly sat up and reached out his arms, his face radiant, as if he could see something others could not. It is popularly assumed that Mary came for him.

Gabriel was canonized on 13 May 1920, just days before Joan of Arc. He is invoked to heal and prevent tuberculosis. It is traditional for high school students to visit his shrine 100 days before graduation.

Handgunner Patronage Controversy

There is an active campaign to have Gabriel declared the official patron saint of hand-gunners. The basis is a legend that he once used hunting skills to save a town from bandits, but there is doubt as to this tale's veracity since he was already in late stages of tuberculosis at the time.

Iconography

A boy or young man in the garb of a Passionist, sometimes gazing toward Heaven with arms outstretched, bathed in celestial light.

Feast Day

  • February 27

Sacred Site

His shrine in Gran Sasso, near Teramo, Italy, containing his tomb. Gabriel spent the last two years of his short life there.

Related Entities
