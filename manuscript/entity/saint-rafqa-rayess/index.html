Saint Rafqa Rayess is renowned for healing chronic pain, childhood illness, and performing miraculous cures through dreams.

Classification

Maronite and Roman Catholic saint.

Epithets

  • The Purple Rose
  • The Little Flower of Lebanon

Life and History

The woman canonized as Rafqa Rayess lived under many names throughout her lifetime. Born on 29 June 1833 in Hemlaya, Lebanon—coinciding with the Feast of Saint Peter—she was given the birth name Boutrosiya Shabaq al-Rayess. Because of her birth date, she also went by French feminine variants of Peter's name: Pierina, Petronia, Pietra, and Pierrette. Her mother Rafqa (a variant of Rebecca) passed away when Boutrosiya was only six years old.

Between ages ten and fourteen, young Boutrosiya worked as a domestic servant in Syria at the home of a family friend. At fourteen, her future became a source of heated family conflict—her stepmother wanted her to marry the stepmother's brother, while an aunt pushed for marriage to her own son. These competing demands distressed Boutrosiya, who felt drawn to religious life rather than matrimony. Taking matters into her own hands, she entered the Convent of Our Lady of Liberation.

Her father and stepmother arrived to bring her home, but from the moment she crossed the convent threshold, Boutrosiya experienced profound peace and joy. She asked the Mistress of Novices to excuse her from seeing her relatives, and her request was granted. She never saw her family again.

Within the convent, she used the name Pierina until 9 February 1855—the feast of Saint Maron—when she began her novitiate and took the spiritual name Anissa, a variant of Agnes.

In 1871, her order merged with another congregation, giving the sisters three choices: join the new order, transfer to a different order, or return to secular life. Sister Anissa struggled with this decision, praying intensely for guidance. One night, Saint Anthony of the Desert appeared to her in a vision—manifesting as an elderly man with a long white beard, carrying a tau-cross staff. He instructed her twice to join the Baladiya Order (the Lebanese Maronite Order). She obeyed, and on 12 July 1871, at age thirty-nine, she became a Maronite nun and adopted her mother's name, Rafqa—the name under which she would eventually be canonized.

Suffering and Sanctity

In 1885, Sister Rafqa—noting that she had never experienced illness in her life—prayed to Jesus, asking to share in his Passion and suffering. Her prayers were answered immediately. Her health began deteriorating rapidly. She developed a condition causing daily hemorrhaging from her eyes for nearly thirty years, often accompanied by severe nosebleeds. Witnesses described the sensation as resembling a red-hot needle piercing her skull.

Physicians could find no medical explanation and offered no relief—though Rafqa herself did not necessarily seek it. When doctors recommended surgical removal of one eye, she refused anesthesia. Despite her suffering, she continued spinning, knitting, and singing in the choir until 1907, when she became completely paralyzed. A 1981 medical report prepared for her canonization process suggested tuberculosis with ocular localization and multiple bone growths as the most likely diagnosis. She died in 1914.

Miracles

The first miracle attributed to Rafqa occurred just days after her death. Sister Ursula, a fellow nun who had suffered for eighty years from a throat growth that made swallowing nearly impossible, was resting in her cell when she heard a knock and a voice saying: "Take sand from Rafqa's grave. Apply it to your throat and you will be healed." Assuming it was another nun, she returned to sleep, but the experience repeated. This time she responded that she would gather the sand in the morning. The next day, no one admitted to knocking on her door. Sister Ursula mixed grave sand with water, applied the paste to her throat, and the growth vanished instantly.

Numerous other healings have since been attributed to Rafqa, particularly involving children. She continues appearing to devotees in dreams, offering guidance and miraculous cures.

Beatification and Canonization

Pope John Paul II beatified Sister Rafqa on 17 November 1985 and canonized her on 10 June 2001.

Iconography

Depicted as a Maronite nun, often with lilies or purple roses.

Attributes

Lily, purple rose.

Sacred Sites

Her tomb shrine at the Monastery of Saint Joseph at Jrabta, Lebanon.

Feast Day

23 March.
