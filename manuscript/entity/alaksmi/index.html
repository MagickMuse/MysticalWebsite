Lakshmi

Alaksmi is a minor goddess of the Hindu tradition, the dark sister and opposite of Sri-Laksmi. Her name is formed by placing the negating prefix a- before Laksmi, giving her the meaning of misfortune or the absence of auspiciousness.

In Vedic literature

Alaksmi appears in the Sri-sukta, the early hymn to Sri that forms part of an appendix to the Rg-veda. There she is named as Sri's sister and identified with the opposite of everything Sri embodies. Where Sri brings prosperity, beauty, and abundance, Alaksmi takes the forms of need, poverty, hunger, and thirst. The hymn asks Sri directly to drive her away: banishing Alaksmi is presented as the necessary counterpart to inviting Sri in.

Character

Alaksmi represents the inauspicious pole of a paired structure with Sri. She is not a force to be petitioned but one to be repelled. In the later Hindu tradition she is sometimes identified with Jyestha, a goddess of ill omen, misery, and bad luck who is said to dwell in places of filth, quarrel, and neglect. The two sisters are understood to be in permanent tension, Laksmi drawn to purity and order and Alaksmi drawn to squalor and discord.

Cited hymns

Key reference: Sri-sukta 5, 6, 8 (Alaksmi asked to be banished; characterized as need, poverty, hunger, and thirst).
