Ibra Fall (c. 1855 to 1930), a Sufi and Mouride saint known by the epithet "Lamp," was the most ardent supporter, promoter, and disciple of Senegal's saint Sheikh Amadou Bamba. He is now venerated as a saint himself, his image almost as ubiquitous in Senegal as Sheikh Amadou's.

The Lamp

His nickname "Lamp" reflects his role as the light of Sheikh Bamba's Mouride movement. The central minaret of the Great Mosque of Touba is named for him. The word minaret derives from the Arabic manarah, meaning "lamp," creating a meaningful wordplay.

Revolutionary Teachings

Amadou Bamba conferred the honorific "Sheikh" upon Ibra Fall, though his capacities as a Koranic scholar are disputed. His revolutionary act was emphasizing the value of hard work and dedication as a spiritual path.

The Baye Falls

His devotees, called Baye Falls, are recognizable by their dreadlocks and patchwork clothing worn as tribute to their master. The Baye Falls are now considered a subsect of Mouridism with their own unique practices.

Iconography

A famous photograph portrays Ibra Fall standing before a crowd wearing dark robes. This image, in sharp contrast to Sheikh Amadou's vividly white robes, indicates Ibra Fall's dedication to physical labor. He is often portrayed beside Sheikh Amadou as if their photographs were blended.

Musical Tribute

Cheikh Ibra Fall is a musical tribute by Senegalese singer Youssou N'Dour.

Sacred Site

His grave in Touba, Senegal.

Feast Day

9 June (anniversary of death)
