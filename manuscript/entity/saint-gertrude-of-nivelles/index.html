Saint Gertrude of Nivelles is renowned for healing mental disorders, phobias, and fear of rodents.

Classification

Venerated as a saint by most branches of Christianity.

Life and History

Gertrude's father, Pepin of Landen, an ancestor of Charlemagne and high-ranking official serving the Merovingian king Dagobert I, died when Gertrude (626-659) was fourteen. By then, she had already publicly announced she would wed no one but Jesus Christ.

Her mother founded a monastery for monks and nuns in Nivelles (now Belgium). Gertrude joined her there, becoming its first abbess at age twenty. Famed for tremendous hospitality toward pilgrims, she was a visionary who died at thirty-three, the same age as Jesus.

Guardian of Rodents

Gertrude is invoked against rats and mice, as well as to heal and protect those deathly afraid of these animals. She is frequently portrayed in the company of rodents and may be understood as their guardian and controller. She presides over "exorcism" rituals intended to banish rodents.

By extension, Gertrude has become the unofficial matron saint of cats.

Patron of the Recently Dead

Gertrude is considered the matron of the recently dead. According to medieval legend, it is a three-day trek to the afterlife. The dead spend the first night of the journey under Saint Gertrude's protection. She is also the guardian of graves.

Symbolism of Mice

It is unclear whether Gertrude's associations with rodents derive from her miraculous ability to prevent them from destroying harvests and spreading disease, or from her associations with death. In pre-Christian Germanic folk belief, mice can represent souls of the dead.

Other Powers

  • Invoked by travelers seeking hospitality
  • Provides safety at sea
  • Heals and protects against mental disorders and phobias

Year of the Rat and Cat

Because of her associations with these animals, Gertrude can be considered the guardian of those born in the Year of the Rat, as well as the Vietnamese zodiac's Year of the Cat.

Iconography

Portrayed as an abbess, with mice running up her cloak or pastoral staff or sitting at her feet. Sometimes accompanied by a cat or holding a large mouse or rat.

Attributes

Rats, mice, cats, squirrel, distaff, lily, chalice filled with wine.

Sacred Site

The bronze statue of Saint Gertrude on the Gertraudenbrucke (Saint Gertrude's Bridge) in Berlin. The bridge once led to a hospital named in her honor. If you make a wish or say a prayer and rub the heads of the mice, it is said Saint Gertrude will grant your wish, provided you keep it secret.

Feast Day

17 March
