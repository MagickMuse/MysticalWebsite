Venerated as an Orthodox saint, Saint Sava (c. 1175-14 January 1235) is the hero and patron saint of Serbia who founded the Serbian Orthodox Church and served as its first archbishop.

Life and Legend

Described as "the sun of the Serbian heaven," Sava was born Prince Rastko, third son of Serbian king Stefan Nemanja. His parents wanted him to become a ruler, but he sought a spiritual life instead. He ran away to Greece and joined a monastery on Mount Athos, where he received the name Sava. Finding no Serbian monastery there, he founded one. His father eventually relinquished the throne to Sava's brother and joined him in monastic life.

In 1208, Sava returned to Serbia to reconcile his feuding brothers. Fervently devoted to the Serbian people, he served as diplomat and legislator, writing Serbia's first constitution. Folktales describe how he taught the Serbs to plough. An avid traveler who made frequent pilgrimages to Jerusalem, he died of pneumonia while traveling and was buried in the Cathedral of the Forty Martyrs in Turnovo (now Veliko Tarnovo, Bulgaria).

In his lifetime, Sava performed miracles, producing therapeutic springs and commanding wolves. On 6 May 1237, his relics were returned to Serbia and interred at Mileshevo Monastery. When Serbs rebelled against Ottoman rule, they carried banners depicting Saint Sava. As punishment, in 1594, Ottoman ruler Sinan Pasha had Sava's remains dragged to the highest point in Belgrade and burned. Some insist the relics were never truly destroyed, either hidden and substituted, or unable to burn except on a pyre of grape vines, an otherworldly plant in Serbian folklore.

Magical Properties

Sava is a fierce saint who both blesses and curses. The more magical aspects of historical Sava may represent the ancient Slavic deity Dabog in disguise. He continues to perform miracles after death.

Iconography

Traditional icons depict Saint Sava as a monastic figure. The burning of his relics, a devastating experience for the Serbian people, is captured in many paintings.

Sacred Sites

The Cathedral of Saint Sava in Belgrade, the largest Orthodox church building in the world, stands on the site where his relics were burned. Other sites include the Church of Saint Sava in Split, Croatia, and Saint Sava Orthodox Cathedral in Cleveland, Ohio.
