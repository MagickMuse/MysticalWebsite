⚠️ Agwé is venerated within Haitian Vodou and African Diasporic traditions. These are closed initiatory practices and should only be approached by those within the originating cultures or under proper cultural guidance.

Magickal Properties

Agwé is the King of the Sea in Vodou tradition, a military spirit and stickler for protocol who commands the beautiful ship Immamou and is married to the alluring mermaid La Siréne. He receives ultimate credit for chasing off European colonials following the Haitian Revolution. He usually appears as a fair-skinned black man with distinctive sea-green eyes.

Ritual Uses

Formal services for Lord Agwé are held at sea on carefully ornamented wooden rafts and are among the most lavish of all Vodou ceremonials. His presence is requested by blowing a conch shell. He serves as psychopomp, guiding dead souls to Ginen (the Vodou realm of the dead beneath the sea).

Offerings

He has a discerning palate and expects only the finest: champagne, caviar, deluxe pastries, cakes, and desserts. If financially challenged, a small serving of the best you can afford is preferable to something substandard. Traditional offerings include coins, food, flowers on a wooden raft, and anything maritime.

Favored People

Seafarers, those who travel on water, people seeking wealth and prosperity, and practitioners of Vodou traditions.

Petition For

Safe passage on water, wealth and abundance, guidance of lost souls, protection during ocean voyages, and victory in maritime endeavors.

Warnings

Avoid informality; address him as Lord Agwé or Admiral Agwé, salute him, and make voyage offerings at sea or to living water because he is a stickler for protocol and may withdraw protection if treated casually.

Wheel of the Year Connection

Agwé's feast day is December 12, making it perfect to honor him during Yule (winter solstice). As a lwa of the ocean and something of a psychopomp escorting the dead to the abysmal waters, he creates a nice parallel with Papa Legba (also a psychopomp) at the summer solstice.

Celebrations normally happen by the ocean or at rivers and involve elaborate setups of food offerings floated on the water on rafts. His symbols include boats, ships, seashells (especially conch shells), driftwood, and hag stones. His colors are blue and silver. As a representation of the sea, Agwé symbolizes all the abundance the salty waters of life can bring, mirroring the abundance and goodwill of the Yuletide season.

Additional Notes

He controls everything lost at sea or resting on the ocean floor. Those enslaved Africans who arrived safely in Haiti are considered to have done so through his grace. It's traditional to make a small offering when embarking on ocean voyages, promising a larger one upon safe arrival. His sacred colors are blue and white.
