Sarama is a minor goddess of the Hindu Vedic tradition. Her name has been understood to mean "the fleet one," which echoes the pattern seen in Saranyu, whose name carries a similar sense of swiftness.

In Vedic literature

The one significant episode in which Sarama appears in the Rg-veda concerns the recovery of stolen cattle. The thieving Panis have driven away cows belonging to the gods, and Sarama is sent across the cosmic waters to find them. She acts as Indra's messenger, locating the herd and opening negotiations with the Panis for its return.

The Rg-veda gives no further substantial description of her character. Her ability to track the stolen cattle and to cross the heavenly rivers in pursuit of them has been read as an early hint of the canine associations that surface in later literature, where she becomes the mother of dogs and is sometimes called the heavenly bitch. None of that later development is yet visible in the Rg-veda itself.
