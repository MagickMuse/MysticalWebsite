Overview

Aditi shows up across roughly eighty hymns of the Rg-veda, yet she remains one of the more elusive Vedic figures to characterize. Her name almost always shares the invocation with another deity, no single hymn is dedicated to her alone, and she lacks the natural-element identification that anchors most of her contemporaries. Beside the radiant Usas or the substantial Prthivi she can read as oddly abstract, and her body is hardly described at all. What carries through the references is a vast maternal authority: she stands as the originating mother of gods and kings, and as a guarantor of the order that keeps the cosmos coherent.

In Vedic literature

The originating mother

Motherhood is the trait that most clearly distinguishes her. The Rg-veda names her as the source of the Adityas, an assembly of seven or eight deities that includes Mitra, Aryaman, Bhaga, Varuna, Daksa, and Amsa. Beyond that lineage she is also identified as the parent of Indra, of earthly kings, and of the gods as a collective. She carries this maternal identity without a husband; the Rg-veda gives her no male partner at all, which sets her apart from Prthivi, whose own motherhood is bound up with the sky god Dyaus.

Protector and benefactor

Hymns approach Aditi as a parent who shelters those who call on her and supplies them with riches, security, and plenty. Her maternal image extends into bovine symbolism: she is sometimes named a cow or treated as one. In that form she nurtures her devotees, and as the celestial cow she yields soma itself, the regenerative draught of the gods.

She who unbinds

Her name traces to the verbal root da, to bind or to fetter, with the prefix a- inverting the sense. A-diti is therefore the one who is not bound, the free one, and her petitioners ask her to loosen them from whatever holds them, especially the grip of sin and disease. One striking hymn pleads with her to release a worshipper who has been trussed up like a captured thief. This office of binding and releasing echoes that of her son Varuna; the mother who frees and the son who restrains are two sides of one cosmic discipline.

Custodian of rta

That same authority makes her the keeper of rta, the cosmic and ethical order. She is praised as a sustainer of living beings, and her sustenance consists in upholding the rhythms and statutes that separate chaos from form. To free the bound and to enforce rta are, for her, the same maternal labor.

The wide one

She is named far-extended and spacious, the lady of broad enclosures. That language of breadth, together with her cow associations, draws her toward Prthivi, and in the Brahmanas the boundary between the two collapses; the later texts treat them as effectively one. Where Prthivi is the dependable ground underfoot, Aditi is the unbounded maternal expanse that nourishes, releases, and preserves rta across all that walks within it.

Fate in later Hinduism

Unlike Prthivi and Sarasvati, Aditi does not carry forward as a major deity in post-Vedic Hinduism. Her name continues to appear in genealogies and in passing references, but she is rarely found in the later texts as a goddess approached on her own terms. The maternal authority and rta-keeping office that the Rg-veda assigns her are largely diffused into other figures, including Prthivi, with whom the Brahmanas already begin to merge her.
