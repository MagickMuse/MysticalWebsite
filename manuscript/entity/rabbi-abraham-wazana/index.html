Overview

Rabbi Abraham Wazana, renowned sage, scholar, Kabbalist, and miracle worker, is the founding patriarch of the Moroccan-Jewish Wazana clan, a family celebrated for producing many powerful saints across generations. He is known as Abraham El-Kebir (Abraham the Great) to distinguish him from his grandson, another Rabbi Abraham Wazana, who fathered the legendary Jacob Wazana.

The Night at the Spring

Once while traveling toward the city of Ouarzazate on a Friday as twilight and the Jewish Sabbath approached, Abraham Wazana decided to stop overnight beside a spring. He sent his escort ahead to announce his arrival the next day. The Jews of Ouarzazate were dismayed to learn he planned to spend the night at this particular spring, notorious as a haunt of bandits, wild beasts, and djinn. They underestimated Wazana.

Bandits did approach, intending to rob the lone traveler. As they drew near, they found themselves paralyzed, able only to move away from Wazana, never toward him. The thieves fled. Wazana was left alone to prepare his meal and pray.

At nightfall, beasts approached: hyenas, lions, tigers, and snakes. These had been sent by the Prophet Elijah to guard Wazana while he slept. Elijah himself, armed with a rifle, stood vigil all night on a hilltop overlooking the spring.

The next day, the animals kept Wazana company while he prayed and studied. When evening fell, a lion politely requested that he depart so they could eat. Wazana explained he had to wait for his escort from Ouarzazate. The lion offered to bring him there himself.

Rabbi Abraham Wazana arrived in Ouarzazate riding a lion and accompanied by an entourage of other lions, only to discover people mourning him. They had presumed he had died at the spring. Wazana directed that the lions be fed Sabbath leftovers. After they ate, he uttered the Ineffable Name of God and the lions vanished into thin air. His reputation as a miracle worker was assured.

Death and Continuing Miracles

On the night Wazana died, he invited 200 scholars to feast with him, requesting they read from the Book of Psalms until midnight. At midnight, all the scholars instantly fell sound asleep, so no one witnessed his death. He appeared to two people in dreams, his favorite student and a righteous woman, telling them where to find his corpse. He was discovered in the cemetery at Ait Budiel, mysteriously washed, wrapped in a shroud, and ready for burial.

Miracles continue at his tomb-shrine. He appears frequently in dreams to grant requests. He is particularly renowned for healing infertility. During a severe drought, he appeared to a dreaming woman, telling her that when she awoke there would be water in a dry riverbed. The next morning, clean, fresh water was there.

Sacred Sites

  • Ait Budiel, Morocco: His shrine. A stream nearby is renowned for magical and healing powers. According to legend, all attempts to divert this stream have resulted in instant death.
  • Netanya, Israel: Celebrations are held on his feast day.

Feast Day

3rd of Tevet (usually Capricorn).
