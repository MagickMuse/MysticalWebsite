Classification

Venerated by Jews, Lutherans, and the Spiritual Churches of New Orleans.

Life and History

Queen Esther, the heroine of the biblical Book of Esther, was a Jewish woman married to the King of Persia, who was unaware of her religion. Advised to keep her identity secret, she used her proximity to the throne and her bravery to save her people from disaster.

Patron of the Hidden

Queen Esther was secretly venerated by the Crypto-Jews of Spain, Portugal, and their Western Hemisphere colonies, who prayed that she protect them as she had protected her ancient people. Because they too were obliged to hide their true religious identity, many Crypto-Jews identified deeply with her travails and felt she would understand and sympathize with their plight.

Spiritual Churches Connection

Esther is a heroine of the Spiritual Churches of New Orleans. She was among the personal spirit guides of Mother Leafy Anderson (1887-1927), widely considered the founder of this tradition. Mother Anderson introduced Esther's veneration, where she has evolved from personal spirit guide to full-fledged saint.

Powers and Domains

Esther is invoked to help individuals, but especially to assist communities in need. In life, she was a female member of a persecuted people who successfully opposed oppressive authority. She serves as proof that even the weakest can effect profound change.

Invoke Queen Esther when your community is under attack.

Order of the Eastern Star

Symbolizing fidelity and courage, she presides over the third degree of initiation in the Order of the Eastern Star.

Etymology

The name Esther may mean "star." It derives from the Mesopotamian goddess Ishtar, Queen of the Morning and Evening Star, a warrior and savior. Her birth name was Hadassah, meaning "willow" in Hebrew.

In Art

A favorite subject of paintings, films, novels, and operas, Esther became queen by winning a beauty contest and is invariably portrayed as beautiful and alluring.

Colors

Pink, white.

Sacred Sites

  • Esther is reputedly buried in Hamadan, Iran, beside her uncle Mordecai. The present mausoleum (13th century) remains a major pilgrimage site.
  • Another tradition places her tomb in the Galilean village of Bar'am, Israel. Pilgrimages are documented since the 13th century, though no trace of this shrine remains.

Feast Day

24 May (Lutheran Church)

Festival

The Jewish holiday Purim, celebrated on the 14th of the Hebrew month Adar. The Fast of Esther occurs from dawn to dusk the previous day, commemorating her fast as she gathered courage to approach her husband.
