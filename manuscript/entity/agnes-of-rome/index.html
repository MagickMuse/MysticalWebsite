Agnes of Rome, venerated by most branches of Christianity, is renowned for healing skin ailments and diseases. Her name, meaning chaste, resembles the Latin agnus (lamb), which became her most popular attribute.

Life and History

Very little is actually known about Saint Agnes. Although she allegedly died in approximately 304, her legend was not written down until over a century later.

Agnes was a very young (twelve or thirteen years old) Roman girl from a patrician, but secretly Christian, family during Diocletian's reign (284-305). She was beautiful, noble, and wealthy: a fine marital prospect.

Betrayal and Persecution

A prominent Pagan suitor proposed and was rejected. Agnes may have consecrated her virginity to Christ or the suitor may simply have been unsuitable to her family, or both. The suitor, aware that the family was Christian, betrayed Agnes to authorities, who punished her by sending her to labor in a brothel.

Miracles

  • As Agnes was dragged naked through the streets, her hair miraculously grew long enough to cover her modestly.
  • Those in the brothel who approached her sexually were instantly struck blind and knocked down by an angel invisible to all but Agnes.
  • One of the blinded men begged Agnes for mercy; she prayed for him and his sight was instantly restored.
  • She was thrown into a fire but remained unharmed.
  • She was thrown to lions, who did not harm her.
  • A man (possibly the rejected fiance) attempted to goad the lions into attacking Agnes; instead they killed him.

Martyrdom

Agnes was beheaded by a sword on 21 January, singing hymns while an executioner hacked at her neck. Miracles were attributed to her almost immediately. She was buried in catacombs beyond the city walls.

Apparition

On her birthday, 28 January, an apparition of Agnes appeared to her parents mourning at her grave, advising them that she was fine.

Iconography

  • A young girl holding a lamb
  • A young girl with a sword pointed at her throat
  • A young naked girl covered by her long hair
  • A young girl naked beneath a long white cloth held over her by an angel

Attributes

Lamb, palm branch symbolizing martyrdom, crown of thorns.

Colors

White, light blue, red and white.

Feast Days

  • January 21 (primary feast)
  • January 28 (birthday, secondary feast)

Sacred Day

Friday.

Saint Agnes' Eve

The night preceding her feast day (January 20) is traditionally dedicated to divination and magick rituals, usually intended to predict a young girl's future marital prospects. This tradition is commemorated in poems by John Keats ("The Eve of Saint Agnes") and Alfred Lord Tennyson ("Saint Agnes' Eve").

Sacred Sites

  • Sant'Agnese in Agone (Saint Agnes in Agony), Rome, built on the site where Agnes was killed
  • Saint Agnes Outside the Walls, Rome, where her bones reputedly rest
