The Face That Launched One Thousand Ships

Origin: Greece

Overview

Helen of Troy is described as the most beautiful woman in the world, possessing supernatural beauty and allure. She was never entirely mortal. In the most famous version of her origin, Helen is the daughter of Zeus, who transformed into a swan to rape her mother Leda, a mortal queen. This makes her the immortal sister to her mortal twin, Clytemnestra.

Mythology & Origins

Birth Legends

Primary Version: Leda simultaneously conceived two children by Zeus and two by her mortal husband. Two children were mortal; the other two immortal. Helen was the immortal sister.

Alternate Version: In a more obscure myth, Helen is fully immortal. Zeus pursued the resisting Nemesis, who transformed into various creatures to escape, first a fish, then finally a goose. Zeus, in the guise of a swan, raped her. Nemesis laid and abandoned an egg the color of blue hyacinths. The egg ended up with Leda, who hid it until Helen hatched from it.

The Abduction & The Judgment

When she was a child, the hero Theseus saw her dancing for Artemis, abducted and raped the child. Helen's twin brothers, the Dioscuri, rescued and returned her.

Helen's supernatural beauty later made her the prize in the Judgment of Paris. When Trojan prince Paris was forced to decide which of three goddesses deserved a golden apple labeled "for the fairest," Aphrodite promised him the world's most beautiful woman in exchange for choosing her. When Paris picked Aphrodite, she gave him Helen, her devotee.

The Trojan War

Helen is blamed as the cause of the Trojan War, ostensibly fought to return Helen to her husband Menelaus, King of Sparta. In one version of her myth, Helen was never in Troy—growing bored with Paris even as they eloped, she jumped ship in Egypt, where a pharaoh kept her safe and hidden.

As Goddess

Helen is the original sex goddess, the lady of irresistible allure. This isn't meant only metaphorically. She was worshipped as a goddess with various shrines throughout Greece:

  • Sparta: She had a shrine that may have contained the original egg from which she hatched (or a replica)
  • Memphis, Egypt: Where she allegedly entertained the pharaoh
  • Rhodes: Venerated as a tree goddess

The Rhodes Mystery

After the death of Menelaus (with whom she reconciled after the Trojan War), Helen's step-son banished her from Sparta. She went to visit Polyxo, Queen of Rhodes, whom she thought was a friend. In truth, Polyxo secretly hated Helen, blaming her for her husband's death during the Trojan War.

Polyxo dressed servants up as Erinyes. They stripped Helen naked, scourged her, and hung her from a tree, killing her. Some understand this as a lynching; others interpret "hanging from a tree" to mean crucifixion. Perhaps to atone or appease Helen's ghost, veneration of Helen was then instituted on Rhodes.

Powers & Domains

Helen is an erotic goddess petitioned for:

  • Romantic and domestic happiness
  • Fertility and conception assistance
  • Protection and vengeance for victims of sexual violence and exploitation

Special Knowledge: Helen knows the secret formula for nepenthe, the potion that banishes grief and suffering and provides healing oblivion.

Connections & Syncretism

  • Jewish folklore identifies Helen as an avatar of Lilith
  • Simon Magus allegedly discovered Helen of Troy (either her immortal self or reincarnation) laboring in a brothel in Tyre (modern Lebanon). He rescued her, proclaiming her his Sophia (Shekhina, Ennoia); they were eventually venerated together
  • Saint Helena may contain vestiges of Helen, being profoundly associated with romance and love magick in Christian folk traditions
