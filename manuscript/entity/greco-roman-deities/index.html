Greco-Roman Deities

Mount Olympus in Greece is a sacred mountain archetype and the home of the mighty beings known collectively as the Olympians, or the Dodekatheon: the twelve Gods. Twelve is a sacred number, belonging not only to the solar months of the year but to the concept of fulfilment itself. Although more than twelve beings were said to dwell on the mountain, the twelve principal ones are:

  1. Zeus: The Father. Like Odin, a God of Thunder and King of all the Gods; his kingdom is the sky itself. Dispenser of justice, his word is law.
  2. Hera: The Mother. Consort of Zeus, Queen of the Gods, personification of the maternal aspect, and ruler of marriage and motherhood.
  3. Poseidon: The God who rules over the Sea, with dominion also over horses and earthquakes.
  4. Ares: Not to be confused with Aries, Ares is the God of War and bloodshed. The two are sometimes conflated, particularly since Mars, the planet of war, rules the astrological sign of Aries.
  5. Hermes: The Messenger of the Gods, whose distinctive attributes are his winged sandals and the caduceus. Scribe and recorder to the Gods.
  6. Hephaestus: Ruler over fire and forges, the blacksmith of the Gods, crafting magickally empowered weapons.
  7. Aphrodite: Goddess of Beauty, love, and sexual desire.
  8. Athena: Goddess of Wisdom, often accompanied by an owl as a sign of her sagacity and access to hidden knowledge.
  9. Apollo: The glorious Sun God, governor of light, music, poetry, and beauty.
  10. Artemis: Goddess of the Hunt, often depicted with a quiver of arrows and a bow. Artemis is also the Moon Goddess and the Goddess of Virgins.
  11. Demeter: Goddess of the Harvest, agriculture, and fertility.
  12. Hestia: Goddess of the Home and the hearth.

Family Relationships

The family connections of the Olympians are labyrinthine. Zeus, Poseidon, Hera, Demeter, Hestia, and Hades were all siblings. Hades, as God of the Underworld, does not figure among the Olympians proper, even though he is a key deity; his realm is the world of the dead, which lies in the nether regions rather than atop a mountain. Persephone spends part of her time among the other Gods but also belongs to the Underworld, where she resides for four months of the year. Hermes, Hephaestus, Artemis, Apollo, and Ares are children of Zeus by various mothers. Athena was born from the forehead of her father Zeus, and Aphrodite was born from the castrated phallus of the sky that existed prior to the Creation.

Greek and Roman Correspondences

The Romans adopted many of the Greek Gods and their qualities, adding them to their own deities to form a Greco-Roman pantheon.
