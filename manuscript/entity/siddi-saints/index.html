The Siddi saints derive from the African diasporic communities of India and Pakistan. The word "Siddi" comes from the Arabic "Sayid" meaning "Lord" or "Master," a respectful honorific. On the Asian subcontinent, however, it specifically refers to people of African descent. The alternative name "Habshi" makes this connection explicit, deriving from the Arabic word for Abyssinian (Ethiopian): Habash.

Classification

A subdivision of Sufi saints reflecting the distinct synthesis of Muslim, Hindu, and East African spiritual traditions characteristic of Siddi folk religion.

Origins

The Siddi community derives from multiple migrations, both voluntary and forced. Historically, inhabitants of coastal East Africa were kidnapped by Arab traders, converted to Islam, and sent to labor throughout the Muslim world. Enslaved Africans were brought to the subcontinent as recently as the 19th century, mainly employed as soldiers, bodyguards, domestic servants, healers, and herbalists.

The most beloved Siddi saints are the siblings Baba Ghor, Baba Habash, and Mai Mishra. According to legend, Baba Ghor was a military commander in Abyssinia who received a spiritual directive to defeat a "dangerous female demon" in India. He assembled an entourage that traveled with him, and the Siddi community claims descent from this group. Many Siddi saints are simultaneously ancestral spirits.

The Female Saints

Seven female Siddi saints are traditionally acknowledged: Mai Mishra, Mai Miriam, Mai Goma, Mai Ratani, Mai Dukhri, Mai Kheini, and Mai Ghumli.

Ritual Practices

Women play significant roles in Siddi saint veneration, not only as supplicants but as respected ritual specialists. The shrines of female Siddi saints are cared for by women. Mai Mishra is served by female ritual specialists. Both Baba Ghor and Mai Mishra communicate through male and female spirit mediums.

Siddi rituals may include dance and drumming, reflecting indigenous African spiritual traditions.

Sacred Site

The shrine complex in Ratanpur, Gujarat, contains the tombs of Baba Ghor, Mai Mishra, and Baba Habash. It is famed for miracles of exorcism.
