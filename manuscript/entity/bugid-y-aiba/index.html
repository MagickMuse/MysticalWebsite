⚠️ Bugid Y Aiba belongs to Taino spirituality, the tradition of the Indigenous peoples of the Caribbean including Puerto Rico and Haiti. Taino practice is a closed tradition and should be honored only by those within the originating culture.

Bugid Y Aiba is a god of war of the Taino peoples of Puerto Rico and Haiti. He is counted among the zemis, the sacred spirits and ancestral powers who are venerated across the Taino cosmos, and he holds among them the office of the war god through whom martial strength is asked and received.

Domains

War and martial strength, the augmentation of the warrior's body, the augmentation of endurance in battle, and the restoration of failed sight.

Bestowal of Strength

The Taino peoples have long held that Bugid Y Aiba can bestow strength upon those who honor him. When his devotees smoked in a ritual ceremony held in his honor, their arms were said to increase in size, a tangible sign of the strength the god poured into them and a token of the enlargement of warlike capacity that would carry them through the coming conflict.

Restoration of Sight

Bugid Y Aiba is also invoked for the restoration of failed eyesight, and in this role he attends to a very different edge of the warrior's need: the clarity of vision that is required as much as the strength of the arm. Where the god of war is often imagined only in the register of force, this Taino figure gathers both the enlargement of the body and the return of clear seeing under the same divine hand.

Classification as Zemi

As one of the zemis, Bugid Y Aiba belongs to the wider order of Taino sacred beings that includes the mother goddess Atabey and the cassava-and-sea lord Yucahu. The zemis were both spirits and the carved and shaped objects that housed them, and offerings to them formed the central ritual practice of Taino religion in the islands of the Caribbean.
