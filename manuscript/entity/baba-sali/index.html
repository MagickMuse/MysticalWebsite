Overview

Baba Sali is the name by which the beloved saint, scholar, Kabbalist, and miracle worker Rabbi Israel Abihatsira is popularly known. A scion of the illustrious Abihatsira family, he may be the most popular modern Jewish saint. He is famed for his refusal to curse; he only blessed.

Life

Born in Tafillalt, Morocco, in 1890 on Rosh Hashanah, he demonstrated tremendous religious devotion in childhood. His spiritual powers were quickly apparent. Even in his youth, people flocked to him for blessings, earning him the title Baba Sali, literally "Father of Prayer." He immigrated to Israel in 1964, where he died on 8 January 1984. He was a humble, ascetic man who lived modestly. He was also known for blessing water to heal the sick.

In Israel, Baba Sali seldom left his home, which contained a synagogue and ritual bath. He dressed in traditional robes, leaving little more than his face exposed, giving him the appearance of a biblical prophet.

The Famous Miracle

Baba Sali's most famous miracle involved a young Israeli soldier whose legs were paralyzed from an injury during the 1973 Yom Kippur War. Medical solutions having failed, he sought Baba Sali's help as a last resort. Baba Sali told him that God gives us limbs to serve Him and, if the soldier would promise to become more observant, Baba Sali would intercede for a full recovery. The soldier kissed Baba Sali's hand, and Baba Sali's wife told him to stand up and walk. And he did.

Feast

Baba Sali is commemorated on the fourth day of the Hebrew month Shevat, corresponding to Aquarius. Hundreds of thousands visit his gravesite in Netivot.

Sacred Site

His tomb in Netivot, Israel.
