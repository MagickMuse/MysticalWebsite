Volcano and Water

Waiau tends the sacred pond named for her on Mauna Kea. She is both a volcano goddess and spirit of water, one of Pele's principal rivals. Lake Waiau is considered an entrance to the Underworld.

Origin

Waiau and her sisters, Poliahu, Lilinoe, and Kahoupokane, daughters of Haumea and Kane, were born fully formed on Mauna Kea, now a dormant Hawaiian volcano; their father lived with them there for a while. Before departing for further adventures, he created the sacred pond and named it Waiau in honor of the daughter who tends it. The sisters bathe in the pond and drink its water, which is charged with protecting it from desecration.

Magickal Properties

Waiau is considered one of Pele's principal and most formidable rivals; do not place them on the same altar. Lake Waiau, known as the bottomless lake, is traditionally considered an entrance to the Underworld, and Waiau presides over this portal.

Sacred Sites

Lake Waiau, in the Pu'u Waiau cinder cone on Mauna Kea, Hawaii.
