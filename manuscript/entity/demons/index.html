Overview

The idea of a creature that personifies the forces of evil is an ancient one, and incantations to repel such beings reach back as far as the first millennium BCE, though they were not yet called demons. In a world built of opposing forces, of male and female, black and white, good and bad, it makes a certain sense that the good should have its shadow.

Origins of the Word

The demon as commonly understood today is a malicious, ungodly creature, the very epitome of evil, yet the word once meant something quite different. The Greek daemon was a divine being with its own energies and powers. Each person carried such an aspect within the soul, joined to the Great Spirit and able to spark sudden flashes of inspiration or enlightenment, literally a person's genius. This essential spirit helped its human reach toward a higher consciousness. Places too had their daemons, seen for example in the spirits of the elements: the salamanders, sylphs, nymphs, and undines.

Demonizing the Old Gods

Many of the pre-Christian deities of every nation held these creative, daemon-like qualities. Where such ancient beings were not absorbed and renamed as saints, they were heavily discredited by the early Church and recast as evil entities, part of the effort to replace the old pagan gods with the single patriarchal God of Christianity. Described in countless ways, these so-called demons came to include fairies, pixies, elves, goblins, and many more. To demonize a thing or a person is to represent it as a demon, that is, to discredit or contaminate it.

Lilith

Lilith, named as the first wife of Adam, is often called an arch-demoness. She had already existed in another form as the ancient goddess Astarte, who is linked to Venus and Aphrodite, but Lilith's refusal to take the submissive position, among other defiances, saw her cast out and replaced with the seemingly more compliant Eve. Ever after she was charged with all manner of diabolic misdeeds.

Possession and the Shadow Self

One tradition holds that demons are born of angels mating with mortal women, another that they are angels who rejected their own god-like nature. Such beings were thought able to possess human people and work their will through them. Read psychologically, the demon has come to stand for the difficult part of the personality or psyche that a person must face and reconcile in order to find balance and peace of mind.
