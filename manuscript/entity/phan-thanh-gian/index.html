Phan Thanh Gian (1796 to 1867) is a Vietnamese folk saint, a tragic hero whose veneration remains controversial. As Vietnamese prime minister, mandarin, and distinguished scholar, he was the diplomat involved in events leading to French domination of Vietnam.

Life and History

On 31 August 1858, French naval forces attacked the Vietnamese port city of Da Nang, claiming persecution of Roman Catholic missionaries as justification. After several years of military conflict, Phan Thanh Gian advised surrender to avoid further bloodshed, not because he wished to do so, but because he perceived the Vietnamese cause as hopeless. He described attempts to battle the militarily superior French as like a fawn attacking a tiger.

The Treaty of Saigon, which Phan Thanh Gian signed on 5 June 1862, formally ceded three rich, rice-growing southern provinces and the city of Saigon to France. A year later, he traveled to Paris as the first Vietnamese ambassador in Europe, attempting to redress the situation and reclaim the territory. The effort failed, and by 1867 France had taken over the rest of the country.

Suicide and Enshrinement

Blaming himself for the shame and loss, Phan Thanh Gian committed suicide in 1867 by drinking poison. He was enshrined in Vinh Long, his hometown. Miracles attributed to him attracted devotees.

Domains

Phan Thanh Gian is reputedly an extremely responsive spirit who fulfills petitioners' requests quickly. His temple was refurbished by the Republic of Vietnam (South Vietnam), but following Communist victory, he officially fell from favor. The Communist Party despised him as the one who signed away Vietnam. His temple was closed for years but reopened in the 1990s, albeit on a very limited schedule. His identity as a scholar is now emphasized, not his political career.

Iconography

His votive image depicts a distinguished mandarin dressed in red robes and flanked by storks.

Sacred Sites

Phan Thanh Gian Temple, a Confucianist temple in Vinh Long, is his primary shrine. He is also enshrined in Le Van Duyet's mausoleum.
