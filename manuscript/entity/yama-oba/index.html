Mountain Crone

Yama Oba, Old Mountain Woman, is a master herbalist and renowned healer who also knows about poison. She was originally a mountain goddess. Like Baba Yaga, she is a dangerous initiatrix celebrated and defamed by gruesome stories.

Classification

Depending on the version, Yama Oba may be a benevolent spirit, a cannibal ogress, a demon, or some or all of the above. She is sometimes classified as a witch; whether she is seen as positive or negative depends on the myth and the beholder.

Nature and Behavior

According to malicious folk tales, she sometimes manifests as a sweet old lady proffering invigorating health potions that are really the equivalent of date-rape drugs used to immobilize her victims.

Mountains are revered as sacred places in Shinto, and Yama Oba may be the name of one specific goddess, the name for a species of mountain spirits, or one great spirit presiding over a host of local ones. The Mountain Mother lives in caves or little huts deep in forested mountains; she may be old but is still fertile, and is said to give birth to various spirits, possibly including the Tengu. Although she can be harmful and is a favorite subject of horror stories, she also sometimes bestows blessings and good fortune. She is the mother or foster-mother of the folk hero Kintaro, subject of famous Noh dramas.

Legends of Yama Oba date back to at least the Heian era (794-1185 CE).

Manifestation

She is usually described as an old hag with long, disheveled hair, but she is a shape-shifter and may appear as a much younger woman. According to folklore, her hair transforms into snakes when she wishes, and she may have a hidden mouth on top of her head.

Correspondences

Color: red. Creature: snake.

Additional Notes

Many artists have been inspired to create portraits of her, including Hokusai and Kitagawa Utamaro.
