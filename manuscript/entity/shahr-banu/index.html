Venerated as a Zoroastrian saint, Shahr Banu bridges Persian and Islamic spiritual traditions as the daughter of the last Sassanid emperor and wife of the third Shia imam.

Life and Legend

Shahr Banu was the eldest daughter of Yazdgird III, the last emperor of Persia before the Islamic Conquest. When Arabic forces captured her, she was brought to Medina. There she married Hussein ibn Ali, grandson of the Prophet Muhammad and third Shia imam. Legend holds that she recognized him upon meeting because she had already seen him in a dream.

In 658 CE, Shahr Banu gave birth to Ali Zayn al Abidin, who became the fourth Shia imam. She died shortly afterward from complications of childbirth, joining Persian royalty to the line of Shia spiritual leadership.

Sacred Sites

Shahr Banu's shrine stands in Ray, now a suburb of Tehran. The mountain bearing her name, which contains her shrine, may originally have been dedicated to the ancient Iranian goddess Anahita. Zoroastrians consider Anahita an angel, and this connection suggests the site's sacred significance predates Islamic veneration.
