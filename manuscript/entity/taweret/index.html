Overview

Taweret is a hippopotamus goddess of fertility, conception, childbirth, vengeance, and the underworld. Her earliest incarnation was as a sky spirit who helped midwife the sun. She is extremely ancient and may be an importation from sub-Saharan Africa; images of her have been displayed in households since prehistoric times. Both feared and revered, her importance in the official state cult diminished over time, but she remained an immensely popular folk goddess until the Roman conquest.

Manifestation

Taweret's body is comprised of the fiercest creatures: hippopotamus, crocodile, lion, and pregnant woman. She usually manifests as a pregnant hippopotamus standing on her hind legs, displaying large, pendulous, womanly breasts, sometimes with the back of a crocodile, complete with tail, and the arms and legs of a lion. In her avenger aspect, she has the body of a hippopotamus and the head of a lion, brandishes a dagger, and has a crocodile slung over her shoulder.

Magical Properties

Taweret's image was found all over ancient Egypt. Pregnant women wore amulets of her. Images were carved on beds and headrests to guard sleepers against snakes, scorpions, and spiritual dangers. Homes had murals of her, and vases molded in her shape were filled with milk, poured through the nipples, to guarantee conception, facilitate childbirth, and protect mother and baby. Her image was also engraved on ivory magic wands.

Her image was intended to produce two effects. First, it was meant to be terrifying: the guard dog or Rottweiler of the spirit world, she is a fierce protector of pregnant women and children, scaring away evil spirits by being scarier than they are. Second, it was meant to never let one forget fertility: her saggy breasts and huge belly indicate her present fertility and past successes, demonstrating that she can conceive, bear, and nurse, and that she provides human women with these same abilities.

Attributes

A torch, to drive away demons and purify the atmosphere; the sa, a protective instrument in the stylized shape of a life preserver worn by ancient Nile travelers; and sometimes a knife, for defense or as a midwife's tool.

Sacred Sites

She had temples at Thebes, Karnak, and Deir el-Bahri, but she was predominately a family guardian whose primary residences were within people's homes.

Partnered With Bes

Taweret and her husband Bes may have been the most beloved deities of ordinary Egyptians. She has a woman's breasts on a hippo's body and, seen from behind, resembles a Nile crocodile. Either may be called alone, but the couple is at full strength when paired, offering protection while inspiring bravery and courage. They recognize the placement of their images as a call for protection.
