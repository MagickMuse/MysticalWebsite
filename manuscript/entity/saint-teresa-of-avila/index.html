About

Saint Teresa of Avila (1515–1582) was a Spanish Carmelite nun, mystic, and Doctor of the Church. Born Teresa Sánchez de Cepeda Dávila y Ahumada in Ávila, Castile, she entered the Carmelite Order at age twenty and went on to become one of the most celebrated contemplatives and reformers in the history of Western Christianity. She also founded the Discalced Carmelite order, a reform branch of the Carmelites.

In 1970, Pope Paul VI declared her a Doctor of the Church, one of the first two women (alongside Saint Catherine of Siena) to receive this title.

Mystical Life

Teresa experienced dramatic mystical phenomena throughout her religious life, including:

  • Levitations, which she reportedly found embarrassing and asked others to hold her down during
  • Visions of Christ and the saints
  • Ecstatic prayer states, described in detail in her autobiography, The Life of Teresa of Jesus
  • The Transverberation of the Heart, a mystical experience of being pierced by an angel's spear, the subject of Bernini's famous sculpture

Holy Water

Teresa was a strong advocate for holy water as protection against evil spirits. She wrote:

"I know by frequent experience that there is nothing which puts the devils to flight like Holy water."

She described holy water as a powerful tool for spiritual protection and purification, accessible to any Christian.
