Magickal Properties

This old hag who lives in the grain fields of Ukraine is sometimes explicitly identified as a witch. She has pendulous iron breasts and wields an iron hook. The Iron Woman captures children who wander into the fields and throws them into her iron mortar to grind them up and eat them. She bears a strong resemblance to Baba Yaga; if they are not one and the same, they are cut from the same cloth.

Ritual Uses

The Iron Woman is not invoked for positive magickal work. She represents the dangerous, devouring aspect of the harvest, the grain spirit who must be appeased and avoided rather than petitioned. Her presence in folklore serves as a warning to keep children safe from the dangers of agricultural work and the liminal spaces of grain fields.

Offerings

None - this is a dangerous entity to be avoided rather than propitiated.

Favored People

None - she is a dangerous entity that specifically harms children.

Petition For

Protection from The Iron Woman, not petition to her. Protective magick and warnings to keep children safe from grain field dangers.

Warnings

Dangerous hag who lives in grain fields with iron breasts and iron hook. Captures children who wander into fields and grinds them in iron mortar to eat them. Bears strong resemblance to Baba Yaga; they may be one and the same or closely related spirits.

Additional Notes

The Iron Woman represents the dangerous, consuming aspect of the Corn Mother archetype. While many Corn Mothers are benevolent providers, she embodies the threatening side of harvest spirits who devour rather than nourish. Her iron implements (breasts, hook, mortar) emphasize her connection to both agricultural tools and her indestructible, implacable nature.
