Hotra is a minor goddess of the Hindu Vedic tradition, grouped with Ila, Bharati, and Mahi among the deities tied to the sacrificial cult of the Rg-veda. She is most often summoned together with Sarasvati in the formulas that call the goddesses to attend the rite.

In Vedic literature

Hotra is rarely the subject of an extended hymn. Her appearances fall almost entirely within the list of sacrificial goddesses who are summoned to take their place on the sacrificial grass before a rite, and the hymns offer little independent description of her nature. Some readings treat her as a personification of one or another aspect of the offering, but the surviving material is too sparse to confirm such a reading, and apart from the partial exception of Ila the proposal remains a guess.

Why these goddesses are so consistently invoked alongside Sarasvati is also unclear. None of them is tied to a river in the Vedic material, and Sarasvati herself plays no major part in the sacrificial ritual, so the grouping is not easily accounted for.
