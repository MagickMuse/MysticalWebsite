Origins & Name

Heracles' origins are mysterious. His name means "Hera's Glory" but until his deification, she is his mortal enemy. Alternative myths suggest she may really have been his mother, but classical Greek mythology identifies him as the son of Zeus and Alkmene, a mortal princess.

Hera's Persecution

Hera does all she can to prevent the birth; later sending snakes to assassinate the baby. Heracles, already incomparably strong even in the cradle, strangles the snakes instead. Hera periodically strikes Heracles with violent madness so that he harms innocent people and those he loves. (Or at least Hera is blamed.)

Heracles' saga is a cycle of heroic acts and acts of expiation for the most terrible crimes.

Nature of the Hero

Heracles is a hero, the strongest man on Earth, capable of holding the entire Earth on his shoulders, the subject of numerous legends and entertainments. But he is also:

  • A ruthless killer and assassin
  • Champion of the Olympian order
  • A contract killer whose assorted labors involve eliminating sacred beings of earlier or alternative pantheons

Scholars interpret his labors as indicating the victory of terrestrial spirits over an older, more aquatic pantheon.

Ancestral Spirit & Deification

Heracles is an ancestral spirit. Many royal lineages traced their descent from him. (He once lay with a king's fifty daughters, all in one night, impregnating each one.)

Although Heracles was mortal, after his death, Zeus deified him and brought him to Olympus, where he reconciled with Hera and married her daughter, Hebe.

Regional Variations

Heracles was venerated differently in different places:

Greece

Venerated alongside Zeus and the Nymphs, invoked for:

  • Prosperity, victory, healing, and good health
  • Associated with healing springs and thermal spas
  • "Herculean baths" indicated naturally hot or artificially heated waters

Rome

Hercules evolved into men's deity venerated by merchants, traders, travelers, soldiers, and military personnel. He provides:

  • Protection, success, and good fortune
  • Blessings of vigor, good health, stamina, physical strength, and endurance
  • Hercules was the male counterpart of the Bona Dea, who refused to accept males into her presence
  • Hercules similarly rejected female devotees

Celtic Regions

Hercules evolved into a spirit of healing. Some theorize that Celtic Hercules is really a Roman name for Celtic deities like the Dagda, Ogmios, and Borvo. It's theorized that heroic Hercules was considered a fighter against disease or disease demons.

Phoenician-Ruled Areas

Hercules is likely to be Melkart, whom the Greeks called Tyrian Heracles.

Attributes & Correspondences

Manifestation: A huge, muscled bearded man draped in a lion's pelt (and usually nothing else)

Attributes: Club, lyre (Chiron taught him how to play; he clunked another music tutor over the head with it, killing him)

Consort: Hebe (Hera's daughter), whom he marries after his death and deification

Sacred Dates:

  • 30 June: Dedication day of the Roman temple of Hercules and the Muses (179 BCE)
  • 12 August: Men offer sacrifices to Hercules Invictus ("Invincible Hercules") at the great altar near the Circus Maximus (women are excluded)

The Maiden Who Became the Water Lily

The Greeks tell of a young woman who was madly in love with Heracles. She died of jealousy and was transformed into the Water Lily.

Cerberus and the Birth of Aconite

In the last of his Labours, Heracles dragged Cerberus up from the Underworld into the daylight. The hound, confused and disoriented by the brightness, began to foam at the mouth. Wherever that spittle struck the earth, the deadly aconite plant sprang up. Through this passage Heracles becomes the unintended midwife of one of the most dangerous plants in the witch's garden. See Cerberus and Aconite.

Sacred Sites

  • Shrines along the entire Mediterranean coast
  • Cadiz (now Spain)
  • Ancient Phoenician city of Lixus (now Larache, Morocco), allegedly the site of the Garden of the Hesperides
  • Mountain shrine atop Mount Oite in Greece (scene of his death and funeral pyre)
  • The Via Herculea (Herculean Way), the ancient road that ran from Rome to Cadiz

Wine Connection

By the Fifth Dynasty, if not before, Egyptians were producing white and red wines known as the Left and Right Eyes of Horus, respectively.

Offerings & Worship

Heracles likes to drink. (He once lost a drinking contest with Dionysus. As payment, he spent time dancing in Dionysus' processions.) He prefers wine but will not rebuff anything stronger. He likes extreme drinks such as over-proof liquors, but be careful not to serve him too much. Even as a spirit, he's not a good drunk: too much liquor and he becomes unreliable.

Roman Traditions

According to Roman tradition, Hercules accepts any food or drink offerings but only from men. True devotees usually tithe or offer him a percentage:

  • Merchants offer percentages of proceeds earned
  • Soldiers of all ranks offered a percentage of booty won
  • Money may be spent on public feasts in Hercules' honor
  • Animal sacrifices had to be shared and eaten in their entirety
  • Lavish dinners were typically held in his honor with food shared by all attendees

The Gambling Priest: The High Priest of his Roman temple used to gamble with Hercules. If the priest won, Hercules granted him favors. If Hercules won, the priest procured courtesans for the deity.

Heracles is not quite so exclusively associated with men in the other regions where he was worshipped, although they were his primary devotees.

Related Entities

Role in Hellenic Household Worship

Within the household pattern of offerings, Heracles receives wild olive, walnut, red roses, and frankincense incense. His tokens are the club and the bow. His colour is gold, and the lion stands as His sacred animal.
