Overview

Minakshi, the fish-eyed goddess, is the great goddess of Madurai and one of the most powerful temple goddesses of the Tamil south. She is worshipped as a form of the Great Goddess and as the divine queen of her city, where she reigns alongside her consort, a form of Shiva. In her the warrior-queen and the bride are held together in a single figure.

The Warrior Queen

Minakshi is born to a king with three breasts, and her unsettled parents are told to raise the child as they would a son. Brought up as a warrior, she takes her father's throne at his death and sets out to conquer the whole world. She and her armies prove invincible, sweeping through every direction undefeated, until her campaign brings her at last to the mountain of Shiva.

Meeting Her Consort

When she catches sight of Shiva her whole nature changes. Her third breast vanishes in that instant, the sign that she has met the one destined to be her husband, and the conquering queen grows shy and modest before him. She returns to Madurai, where their marriage is celebrated as the central sacred event of the city. Yet even as a wedded goddess she remains the sovereign of her temple and her city, the ruling presence to whom her consort is joined.

In Magick

Minakshi is called on for sovereignty and rightful authority, for victory in struggle and the courage to lead, for protection, and for the blessing of marriage and union. Her pattern joins fierce, world-conquering strength to the fulfillment of union, and she is suited to workings that ask for power held with grace and for the meeting of an equal.
