Classification

Venerated as a saint by most branches of Christianity.

Life and History

Saint Sylvester (died 31 December 335) was pope when Emperor Constantine made Christianity the state religion of the Roman Empire. He served as counselor and spiritual advisor to Emperor Constantine. Born in Rome, Sylvester became pope on 31 January 314.

His feast day commemorates the anniversary of his death but also coincides with secular New Year's Eve in the Western World. In many parts of Europe, the name Sylvester is synonymous with New Year's Eve and has entered the popular vocabulary - people of all religious persuasions may eagerly await "Sylvester" rather than speaking of New Year's Eve.

Etymology

Although Pope Sylvester was a devout Christian, whoever named him may not have been. The name Sylvester derives from sylvan, a Latin word meaning "wooded" or "wilderness" that also echoes the name of the Pagan deity Sylvanus, the spirit of wild nature.

Folk Traditions and Magick

Saint Sylvester is widely venerated in German and Central European folk tradition, but his nature does not correspond to that of the historical pope:

  • He is no somber cleric, but a spirit of ecstatic revelry and intoxication
  • Sylvester Night is intensely associated with magick, witches, and divination
  • The Wild Hunt, that parade of marauding nocturnal spirits and specters, rides out on Sylvester's night
  • Witches journey to mountain peaks to dance and revel
  • Sylvester Night - the night of 31 December and the early hours of 1 January - is considered among the most powerful nights for love magick, especially for spells intended to force fidelity

Sylvester Night Rituals

Juniper Smoke: Branches of fragrant juniper are burned so that the resulting smoke will drive away lingering misfortune from the old year and ward off danger in the new.

Elder Wheel Talisman: Elder branches are soaked, then bent into wheels and kept in the home as talismans to provide protection and good fortune until next Sylvester.

Pear Broth Protection: The Central European death goddess Madame Death sits perched in pear trees. At midnight on Sylvester Eve (when 30 December becomes 31 December), a broth prepared from wild pears was poured over thresholds to prevent Madame Death from entering a home that year - combined with a prayer to Saint Sylvester. As wild pears are not accessible to all, it may be worth experimenting with a glass of Poire William or another beverage containing real pears as a substitute. Perhaps a champagne cocktail blended with a pear liqueur and dedicated to Saint Sylvester will do the trick.

Iconography

Sylvester is depicted trampling the dragon of Paganism.
