John Coltrane (1923 to 1967), a saint of the Saint John Coltrane African Orthodox Church, was a saxophonist, composer, musical innovator, and spiritual seeker. His transformative journey from the depths of drug addiction to spiritual transcendence made him an icon of redemption through art.

The Church

After witnessing Coltrane perform in 1965, Archbishop Franzo King and Reverend Mother Marina King were moved to found San Francisco's Saint John Will-I-Am Coltrane African Orthodox Church in 1971. Archbishop King, raised Pentecostal, recognized divine power in Coltrane's music, describing the concert as a "sound baptism."

The church is socially active, running outreach programs for the poor and needy.

Iconography

An icon of Saint John Coltrane depicts him holding a scroll and the "Holy Ghost horn," a saxophone from which flames emerge.

Sacred Sites

  • His grave at Pinelawn Memorial Park and Cemetery, Farmingdale, New York
  • The Saint John Coltrane Church, San Francisco
