Ah Kin XocHachacyum

⚠️ Ah Kin is venerated within Mayan and Indigenous Mesoamerican traditions. These are closed practices and should only be approached by those within the originating cultures or with explicit cultural permission.

Ah Kin, "he of the sun," is a Mayan sun god of ambivalent personality: the young suitor of the moon goddess Acna, and also the aged sun god in the sky. He is feared as the bringer of drought, but also protects mankind from the powers of evil associated with darkness. In the Lacandon tradition he is known as Acan Chob, a name distinct from the Yucatec Mayan wine god Acan.

Underworld Passage

He is said to be carried through the underworld at night on the shoulders of the god Sucunyum.

Worship

Ah Kin is prayed to at sunrise and rituals include the burning of incense. He is invoked to cure illness and to bring wives to bachelors.

Iconography

Attributes include a square third eye subtended by a loop, a strong Roman nose, a squint, and incisor teeth filed to a T-shape.
