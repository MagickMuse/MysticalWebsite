Saint Margaret of Antioch is renowned for healing and protection during pregnancy and childbirth, hemorrhagic bleeding (especially uterine), and kidney ailments.

Satan manifested to Saint Margaret of Antioch in the form of a dragon or gigantic snake. Mouth gaping open, he swallowed her whole, but Margaret's goodness and purity were so potent and distasteful to him that she was immediately spat out unharmed and on her way to sainthood. She is now the matron saint of pregnancy and childbirth.

The Unofficial Version

Snakes are the creatures most associated with reproduction and female reproductive health, traditionally envisioned as women's spiritual and magical teachers, especially in the region from whence Margaret comes. (Antioch is now Antakya, Turkey, near the Syrian border.) Maybe she was not disgorged because she was too good to die. Maybe she emerged because she passed her test. Only by facing and surviving her spiritual trial by dragon was she able to acquire the needed wisdom and secrets to perform her new role.

Margaret bridges Christian and Pagan symbolism: the dragon who swallows and releases her stands for women's own power and prowess in birth, and for the healing, protective power of women's blood.

The Official Legend

The episode with the snake was merely a prelude. According to her official legend, Margaret, the daughter of a pagan priest, secretly converted to Christianity. A Roman prefect fell in love with her, but she rejected him. He had her tortured, hoping she would reject Christianity instead, but his plan failed.

Margaret was bound with chains and placed inside a cauldron of boiling oil, but a miraculously timed earthquake shook her chains loose and she emerged unscathed. She was killed by beheading but, just before her death, she made public promises:

  • She vowed to protect women who prayed to her before childbirth
  • She promised that any fearful woman who called out to her during childbirth would receive her blessings
  • She declared that anyone naming a church for her or who burned candles dedicated to her would obtain anything their hearts desired
  • She promised that those who invoked her on their deathbed would be saved from Hell

Domains

  • Fertility and protection during pregnancy and childbirth
  • Against hemorrhagic bleeding of all kinds, especially uterine bleeding
  • Protection against kidney ailments
  • Rescue from natural disasters, especially earthquakes, storms, and floods
  • Exorcism of demons and malevolent spirits
  • One of the Fourteen Holy Helpers

Controversy

Margaret's death is variously dated from the late third century to the early fourth, but there is no historical documentation for her whatsoever. Although incredibly popular for centuries, she has always been a somewhat disreputable saint, perceived as bribing devotees with extraordinarily lavish promises. She is among the saints most criticized by Protestant reformers.

As early as 494, Pope Gelasius I declared her legend apocryphal. Unlike Saint George, who is always depicted killing the dragon, Margaret is frequently portrayed simply in the dragon's company, sometimes leading it on a leash as if it were her pet. Her attributes, like a cauldron, are indistinguishable from those of Pagan fertility goddesses. Her name evokes the sea rather than Christian virtues. (The Eastern Church calls her Saint Marine or Marina; Margaret means "a pearl.")

Pagan Origins?

Suspicion has existed for centuries that Margaret may be a Pagan goddess like Aphrodite attempting to slip beneath the radar in the guise of a saint. Antioch was among the first areas dominated by militant Christianity. Her devotees may have sought to continue worshipping her discreetly. Her promise that they would not go to Hell has added resonance in that context. The Vatican officially suppressed Margaret's cult in 1969 for lack of historical evidence, but she continues to perform miracles.

Balkan Traditions

In the Balkans, where she is called Saint Marina, her Pagan affiliations are more blatant. She is the Mistress of Snakes who protects people from snakes but also protects and liberates the snakes. She heals and inflicts snake bite. She is associated with holy springs and wells, where people deliver offerings to her.

Manifestation

In the Balkans, Margaret/Marina appears on full-moon nights as a beautiful woman with long, flowing hair.

Iconography

A beautiful long-haired woman, dressed in a magic girdle, stands beside or upon a huge dragon, with a cauldron nearby.

Attributes

Dragon, cauldron, magic girdle

Sacred Sites

Margaret was once especially popular in England, where over 250 churches are dedicated to her, including Saint Margaret's Anglican Church in Westminster Abbey, the parish church of the House of Commons. Another significant site is the Basilica of Saint Margaret in Santa Margherita Ligure, near Genoa, Italy. She may also be venerated with Saint Marina the Monk; the two once shared a grotto, Mar Marina in Tripoli, Lebanon, north of Beirut.
