Atea

Culture
PolynesianHawaiian
Gender
Male
Type
Deity
Also called
Avatea, Vatea, Wakea

Atea is a supreme god of the Polynesian tradition, the father of the gods. He is depicted as a hybrid, his body divided vertically, with the left half fishy and the right half of human form.

In the tradition of the Hervey Islands, Atea is the firstborn son of the primordial mother Vari-Ma-Te-Takere. After a short existence low down in the world coconut, living immediately above his mother, he moved to the opening of the upper world. He is largely comparable to Tane, the god of light. He is also called Avatea, Vatea, and Wakea.

Encyclopedia Source Notes

In the Hervey Islands tradition, Atea is classified as a moon god and is the firstborn of the great mother Vari-Ma-Te-Takere, the elder sibling of Tinirau. Vari-Ma-Te-Takere plucked a piece from her right side to engender Atea, who is half man and half fish, divided vertically with his left side fishy and his right side human. He is the father of gods and humankind, and is said to live in the coconut of the world. After his temporary period low down in the shell, he was assigned to the opening of the upper world, immediately above the home of Tinirau. He is also called Avatea, Vatea, and Wakea (Hawaiian).

⚠️ Practices attributed to Polynesian peoples are sacred to those traditions and should only be performed by those within the originating culture.
