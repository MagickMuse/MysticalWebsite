Classification

Roman Catholic saint.

Life and History

Bathild (c. 627-680), whose name means "Bold Battle," was related to Ricberht, East Anglia's last pagan king. After his defeat by the Christian Sigabert, young Bathild was sold into slavery and ended up serving in the household of Erkenwald, palace mayor to King Clovis II of the Merovingians. She rose to become head housekeeper before catching the king's eye. In 649, she married Clovis and became Queen of the Franks, using her position to advance Christianity.

Reign as Regent

Widowed in 655, Bathild ruled as regent and immediately outlawed enslaving Christians. While official accounts emphasize her charity and monastery-building, she was in reality a formidable and ruthless ruler, implicated in several political killings.

Later Life

She deeply devoted herself to Saint Eligius. After his death, an apparition of the saint convinced her to abandon her wealth and live simply. When her son Clotaire turned fifteen, she surrendered power and entered a convent. All three of her sons eventually became kings.

Before dying of intestinal disease on January 30, 680, Bathild experienced a vision of ascending a golden ladder to Heaven accompanied by angels. She rests at the Abbey of Chelles near Paris, though some relics may have reached Glastonbury.

Powers and Domains

Bathild heals, particularly children, and aids kidnapping and abduction victims. She exorcises stubborn evil spirits from children. Her own journey from aristocrat to slave to queen makes her ideal for petitions involving overcoming seemingly impossible obstacles.

Iconography

A crowned nun distributing alms.

Attribute

Ladder.

Historical Note

In 2003, a gold seal ring was discovered in East Anglia bearing Bathild's portrait and name on one side, with an intimate image of her and Clovis embracing on the other. Scholars believe she used the formal side for official correspondence and the private side for personal letters to her husband.
