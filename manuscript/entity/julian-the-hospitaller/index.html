Julian the Hospitaller, a Roman Catholic saint, is the patron of circus performers, carnival workers, clowns, jugglers, innkeepers, and travelers.

The Prophecy

Julian, the sixteen-year-old only child of a French duke, was out hunting one day. While pursuing a stag, he became separated from the rest of his party. He came upon the stag and shot it with an arrow but, before dying, the animal suddenly spoke, prophesying that one day Julian would kill his own parents.

Following in the footsteps of Oedipus, he ran away, attempting to escape his fate. He wandered, praying, and wound up in Rome, where the Pope ordered him to spend two years "over the sea," possibly Outremer, the Crusader States. Julian traveled to Syria, enlisted in the Knights Hospitallers, fought in the Crusades, and was knighted.

The Fulfillment

French pilgrims advised Julian that his parents had died of natural causes. Relieved, he returned to Europe, fought in Spain against the Moors, and married a countess who owned a castle. But his parents were still alive and had never stopped searching for him.

They arrived at the castle while Julian was hunting. His gracious wife put them to bed in her own room. Julian arrived home late, saw two forms in his bed, and sliced both bodies in half with one strike of his sword. His wife rushed in and explained all.

Penance

Julian vowed to do penance. The Pope ordered him to establish a hostel for travelers at a place of perilous passage. Julian and the countess built a roadhouse near a treacherous stream and he became a ferryman.

After many years, Julian ferried a leper who asked for the countess for the night. Julian refused, but she consented. When she approached the stranger's bed, he had vanished, but his voice announced they were absolved from all sins. Seven years later, thieves killed them in the exact same way Julian had killed his parents.

Historical Note

Julian's story is found in The Golden Legend, a 13th-century compilation now considered not to have much historical credibility. There is no documentation that Julian ever existed. His legend was spread by troubadours, who considered him their patron saint.

Spiritual Ally

Basilisse, his wife, is not officially a saint but is sometimes venerated along with Julian.

Sacred Site

Julian is particularly beloved in Malta, home of the Knights Hospitallers, where a town, San Giljan, is named in his honor.
