Bhaga, whose name means "the dispenser of fortune," is a minor Hindu sun god of the Vedic and Puranic traditions. In Vedic times he was understood as the incarnation of a woman's good fortune in marriage, the divine power that grants a happy match, marital wealth, and the blessings that flow from the wedded state. He is one of the six Adityas, the solar sons of the great mother goddess Aditi, and takes his place among the celestial administrators of dharma and reward.

Domains

Good fortune in marriage, the just distribution of blessings, wealth and prosperity as sanctioned by the gods, and the solar radiance that ripens undertakings into success.

Parentage and Consort

Son of the goddess Aditi, brother to the other Adityas including Surya. His consort is the goddess Siddhi, personification of accomplishment and attainment.

Iconography

Bhaga is depicted with two lotuses, a prayer wheel, and a trident, marking him as a solar dispenser of blessings whose gifts turn like the wheel of prayer and whose light touches all who seek him.

Petition For

Good fortune in marriage and partnership, the just receipt of what is owed, and the ripening of endeavors into their rightful reward.
