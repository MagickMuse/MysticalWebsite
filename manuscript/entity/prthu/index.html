Prthu is the legendary first human king in Hindu mythology, credited with making the earth habitable and productive. He is the archetypal sacred king whose ritual and moral authority draws forth the fertility of the earth for the benefit of all living creatures.

Overview

Prthu appears in the Mahabharata, the Puranas, and Vedic literature as the prototype of sacred kingship. Before his reign the earth was inhospitable — her terrain impossible to cultivate and her fertility untapped. Prthu leveled the earth's mountains and hills to make her fit for agriculture (Mahabharata 7.69) and is described as milking the earth like a cow, drawing forth from her interior the treasures and abundance needed to sustain civilization.

The King and the Earth

Prthu's myths express the ancient Indian understanding that the king stands in a generative, quasi-marital relationship with the earth. The earth, though fertile and potent, does not yield the abundance of her interior without being stimulated, activated, or "milked" by a heroic, royal figure. Conversely, the king's reign will not be fruitful unless he can draw forth the richness of the earth.

This "marital relation of the ruler to the earth" is directly expressed in the Sanskrit word Bhupati — "lord of the earth" — a standard title for kings. Prthu is the legendary model for this royal function: the civilizing hero who enters into a sacred relationship with the land, provoking her latent fertility and making her productive.

Significance for Sita Mythology

Prthu's mythology provides essential background for understanding Sita as a goddess of the plowed earth. The pattern of the sacred king activating the fertility of the earth — seen in Prthu's leveling of the terrain and milking of the earth, and in King Janaka's ritual plowing that brought Sita to light — reflects a fundamental and ancient layer of Indian religious thought about sovereignty, fertility, and the earth.

Source
