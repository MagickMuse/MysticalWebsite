Martinian (died c. 422 CE) is venerated as a saint by most branches of Christianity. Born in Caesarea (now in modern Israel), he became a hermit at eighteen, moving to a remote location in Cappadocia (now modern Turkey). He lived there in solitude for twenty-five years, fasting, praying, wrestling with temptation, and earning a reputation as a wonder worker.

A harlot named Zoë tested his resolve. Approaching his cell on a dark, stormy night, she begged for shelter lest wild beasts devour her. Once inside, she tried to seduce him. Martinian responded by kindling a fire and stepping into it, choosing to burn his body rather than sin. Zoë was so shocked that she converted to spiritual life. Martinian, severely burned, took a long time to recover and garnered unwanted attention for his self-immolation.

Seeking solitude, Martinian found a small, isolated island. Even there, he could not avoid women. Photina, the sole survivor of a shipwreck, landed on his beach. Martinian walked into the sea rather than risk temptation. A dolphin carried him to shore. He lived the rest of his life as a wandering hermit, never trusting any one place to provide safety.

Domains

Martinian is invoked to resist temptation of any kind and to meet goals. He performs feats of healing and rescue.

Iconography

Icons of Saint Martinian may be available. If not, the Hermit tarot card or male images of the Anima Sola may substitute.

Lore

Martinian is venerated together with Saints Photina and Zoë, the two women whose encounters shaped his legendary life.
