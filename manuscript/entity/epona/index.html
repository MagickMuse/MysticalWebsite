Magickal Properties

Epona is a great and important Celtic goddess, guardian spirit of horses and their riders. Her name derives from Epos, a Celtic word meaning horse. She is an extremely complex deity who was once widely venerated throughout Europe. Epona is a spirit of abundance and plenty, healing, and death. She is a goddess of peace and the matron of soldiers. Her shrines incorporated therapeutic healing springs. Epona may be a psychopomp, leading souls to the Afterlife. The ancient Celts perceived horses as oracular animals more closely attuned to the will and desires of spirits than human beings. Epona is likely to have been an oracular spirit.

As with virtually all Celtic goddesses, little documentation regarding Epona exists. She was popular throughout virtually the entire ancient Celtic world. Roman soldiers encountered her in Gaul and adored her literally. Uniquely for a Celtic deity, Epona was brought to Rome, enshrined, and assigned her own official feast day, indicating her official recognition as part of the Roman state religion. Epona was especially popular during the first through fourth centuries CE. She was venerated by a wide spectrum of Romano-Celtic society. Epona was beloved by Roman cavalry officers who adopted her as their own guardian goddess.

Ritual Uses

Petition Epona for protection of horses and their riders. Invoke her for abundance, healing, safe passage through difficult terrain, and prosperity. She can be called upon for oracular guidance and as a psychopomp to guide souls safely to the Afterlife.

Offerings

Cakes, fruit, and something for her horses.

Favored People

Horse breeders, horse traders, horse whisperers, horse doctors, equestrians, cavalry.

Petition For

Protection for horses and their riders, abundance, healing, safe passage, prosperity, oracular guidance, and safe passage to the Afterlife.

Warnings

Epona is the matron of soldiers. She has dominion over horses, donkeys, and mules. Those who care for them may consider themselves under Epona's dominion. Those who abuse them should probably avoid attracting Epona's attention.

Additional Notes

Epona is never represented without a horse. A typical image portrays Epona riding side-saddle on a mare. A foal sleeps beneath its mother, suckles from its mother, or eats from a plate that Epona offers. Alternatively Epona is portrayed sitting between several horses that approach her in homage, or she sits between male and female ponies and feeds them. She is occasionally portrayed as a mermaid on horseback. Her attributes include a key, cornucopia, basket of fruit, and the cloth once traditionally used to start horse races. The Mothers are Epona's sometime companions. Sacred animals include horses, dogs, and ravens. Her sacred day is December 18, her Roman feast.

The Uffington Horse

Cut into the chalk hillside at Uffington in south-east England, near an ancient Iron Age hill fort, is the great figure of a white horse visible for miles. It has been proposed that this landmark was made in honour of Epona or her near-equivalent Rhiannon. The two goddesses are so closely aligned in character, symbol, and function that they may in fact be the same deity, known by different names in the Roman and Welsh corners of the Celtic world.
