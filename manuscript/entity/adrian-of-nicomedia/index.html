Adrian of Nicomedia, Patron of Soldiers and Protector Against Plague, is a Roman Catholic saint venerated by most branches of Christianity including Orthodox and some Protestant traditions. He is the patron saint of arms dealers, soldiers, smiths, butchers, and brewers, and is renowned for healing and protection against bubonic plague and other infectious diseases.

Life and History

Adrian of Nicomedia (died c. 306 CE) was a Roman soldier from Cappadocia (modern Turkey) who served as an officer of the Herculean guard under Emperor Galerius Maximian.

Conversion

While overseeing the persecution and torture of Christians, Adrian was so moved by their courage and faith that he asked them what reward they expected from their God. When they replied with Scripture about the rewards awaiting those who suffer for Christ, Adrian declared himself a Christian and had his name added to the list of those to be martyred.

Martyrdom

Adrian was seized and imprisoned. His wife Natalia, secretly a Christian herself, visited him in prison and encouraged him to remain steadfast. According to legend, Adrian was martyred by having his limbs broken on an anvil. During his execution, a violent thunderstorm arose that scattered his executioners and protected his body from desecration.

Saint Natalia

His wife Natalia is also venerated as a saint. She accompanied him through his imprisonment and martyrdom, and after his death she preserved his relics and eventually died while praying at his tomb.

Iconography

Adrian is typically depicted as a Roman soldier, often shown with:

  • An anvil (the instrument of his martyrdom)
  • A sword (symbol of his military status)
  • A helmet (military attribute)
  • A palm branch (symbol of martyrdom)

Attributes

Anvil, sword, helmet, palm branch, lion at his feet.

Sacred Site

Basilica of Sant'Adriano al Foro, Rome (built over the site of the Roman Senate building in the Forum).

Feast Days

  • March 4 (Roman Martyrology)
  • August 26 (Eastern Orthodox)
  • September 8 (Western calendar, shared with Nativity of Mary)

Fourteen Holy Helpers

Adrian is counted among the Fourteen Holy Helpers, a group of saints venerated together in Roman Catholicism because their intercession is believed to be particularly effective against various diseases and difficulties. He is specifically invoked against plague and infectious diseases.
