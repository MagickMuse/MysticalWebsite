Buk is a river goddess of the Nuer peoples of the Upper Nile floodlands in the region of what is today South Sudan. She stands within the broader Nuer religious sphere as a protective female power tied to the great rivers and the creatures that dwell within them, and her name is spoken most often when the danger of the water is felt closely, above all when crocodiles threaten those who wade, fish, or draw water from the banks.

Domains

Guardianship of the rivers, safe passage across and along the water, protection against the attack of crocodiles, and the safety of women and children whose daily work brings them to the water's edge.

Iconography and Attributes

Buk is known by the poetic title daughter of the fireflies, a name that connects her to the small, flickering lights that hover in clouds over the water and the reed-beds at dusk. This title places her among the luminous, elusive spirits of the river-margin rather than the deep waters themselves, and she is understood as a female power close to the everyday world of the Nuer riverine village.

Ritual

Buk is invoked by the sacrifice of a goat, offered to secure her guardianship against crocodile attack. Such offerings are made on behalf of an individual, a household, or a party about to travel or work upon the river, and the animal's blood and body are given over to the goddess as a plea for her intervention in the water. The rite belongs to the wider Nuer sacrificial tradition, in which cattle and small stock are the recognised offerings by which mortals address the powers of above and below.

Lore

As a river power Buk is felt to move among the fireflies of the reed-banks and to travel with the currents. To pass her by unaddressed is to risk her averted gaze; to invoke her by name and by the proper offering is to walk under her cover. She is one of the female divinities of Nuer belief through whom the household is bound to the living water and to the animals within it.
