Tante Arie

Tantairie, Aunt Arie

Magickal Properties

It's unclear whether Tante Arie from the Jura canton of Switzerland and Montbéliard in France is a witch or a fairy or both. She manifests in the form of a snake or giant and lives in a cave filled with treasure chests brimming with gold. She wears a diamond crown, has iron teeth and goose's feet, like a combination of Baba Yaga and Mother Goose. Tante Arie has dominion over spinning and spinners.

Ritual Uses

Work with Tante Arie for spinning magick and mastery of the spinning craft. Invoke her on Christmas Eve when her power is strongest. She rides through the region on Christmas Eve on her donkey, distributing gifts to those deemed deserving but punishing disobedient children.

Offerings

Obedience and proper behavior, especially for children. Respect for the craft of spinning and those who practice it.

Favored People

Deserving and obedient children, spinners and those who work with thread and yarn, those who demonstrate good behavior throughout the year.

Petition For

Spinning mastery, Christmas gifts and blessings, access to treasure and abundance, rewards for good behavior and obedience.

Warnings

Tante Arie punishes disobedient children. Her iron teeth and goose's feet suggest a dangerous, otherworldly nature. Like Baba Yaga, she may be as dangerous as she is helpful. Her manifestation as a snake or giant indicates shape-shifting powers and unpredictable nature.

Additional Notes

Tante Arie shares characteristics with multiple witch and fairy figures: Baba Yaga's iron teeth, Mother Goose's goose feet, Befana and Perchta's Christmas Eve gift-giving. Her cave filled with treasure chests of gold suggests ancient origins as a guardian of wealth. Her dominion over spinning connects her to fate and destiny, as spinning is traditionally associated with the Fates who spin the thread of life.

Spinning Fate in the Mountains

Tante Arie is a fairy-witch goddess of the Jura Mountains and Franche-Comté who spins Fate in her mountain sanctuary. Singing and spinning are said to lure her from her caverns, suggesting the power of these combined sacred arts to call forth the divine. Her connection to spinning identifies her clearly as a fate-spinning goddess rather than merely a gift-giving figure.

Protector of Pregnant Women

Beyond her role with children and spinning, Tante Arie protects pregnant and laboring women. This connection between spinning and childbirth reflects the ancient association of spinning goddesses with birth. The Parcae's name itself derives from "to bear or give birth," and many spinning goddesses oversee both the spinning of fate's thread and the birthing of new life.
