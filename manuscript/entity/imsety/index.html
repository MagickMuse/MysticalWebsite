Imsety is one of the Four Sons of Horus, a funerary spirit who guards the liver and ensures bodily wholeness in the afterlife.

The Four Sons of Horus

Imsety belongs to a group of four minor gods who protect the organs removed during mummification. Each son guards a specific organ and is associated with a cardinal direction and a protective goddess.

Imsety, the only son depicted with a fully human head, protects the liver. His direction is South. The goddess Isis watches over him.

Role in Egyptian Magick

During mummification lasting seventy days, embalmers removed and separately preserved the stomach, lungs, liver, and intestines. Canopic jars held these organs, their lids shaped like the heads of the Four Sons. Imsety's human-headed jar contained the liver.

Amulets depicting the Four Sons were placed over the mummy's abdomen during wrapping. These images ensured the organs remained protected and that the deceased maintained bodily integrity in the afterlife.

Iconography

Imsety appears as a mummiform figure with a human head. He is painted on coffin midsections and represented on canopic jar lids.

Associations

  • Direction: South
  • Organ: Liver
  • Protective goddess: Isis
  • Material: Often rendered in faience or stone for amulets and jar lids
