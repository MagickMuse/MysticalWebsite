The Two Lords

In life, the Taungbyon Brothers were carousers, drunks, and seducers. They are now the subject of Myanmar's most beloved annual Nat Pwe (festival). The brothers heal illness and infertility, reveal the future, and provide prosperity and gambler's luck.

Origin Myth

The two brothers, Shwepyingyi and Shwepyingge, are at the center of a complex myth involving eating a magician's corpse to gain supernatural powers. They were killed by the king, who had their testicles crushed, and were transformed into the Taungbyon Nats. They appeared to King Anawratha and so terrorized him that he built a shrine in their honor and ordered an annual festival, which remains one of the most popular in Myanmar.

Worship

The Taungbyon Brothers are served by female shamans who engage in sacred marriages with the brothers, as well as by cross-dressing male shamans. They are channeled via spirit mediums to heal illness and infertility, reveal the future, and provide prosperity and gambler's luck.

Feast

Their festival lasts a week, beginning on the eighth day of the waxing moon in the Burmese month of Wagaung and ending at the full moon (typically in August). It is held in Taungbyon Village, twenty miles north of Mandalay.
