Satyabhama is one of the Ashtabharyas, the eight principal queens of Krishna, and is considered an avatar of Bhudevi (the earth goddess). She is known for her fierce devotion to Krishna, her strong will, and her role alongside him in battle.

Overview

Satyabhama is the daughter of the Yadava chieftain Satrajit. She is one of Krishna's eight principal wives and among his most beloved queens. Unlike the gentle, self-effacing devotion of Rukmini or the aching separation of Radha, Satyabhama's love for Krishna is bold, passionate, and demanding. She is described as beautiful, proud, and spirited — not above testing Krishna's love and asserting her claim on him.

Satyabhama accompanies Krishna into battle on his chariot, notably during his defeat of the demon Narakasura, from whose captivity thousands of women were rescued. She is also associated with the famous episode of the Parijata tree, in which she insists that Krishna bring the celestial flowering tree from Indra's heaven and transplant it in her garden, prompting a confrontation with Indra.

As Avatar of Bhudevi

Satyabhama is identified with Bhudevi, the earth goddess. This pairing connects her to a deep strand of Hindu cosmological thought: just as the earth is potent and generous but requires activation by a divine king or cosmic hero, Satyabhama's strength and vitality are matched and awakened by Krishna's divine energy. As Bhudevi, she represents the fertile, generative power of the earth itself, eternally paired with the divine.

Relation to Radha

In the Radha-Krishna mythology, Satyabhama represents one of the many women in Krishna's life whose claims on him are superseded, in the devotional imagination, by Radha's selfless and unconditional love. The adulterous Radha, who has no legal claim on Krishna and therefore loves him freely, is held by Bengal Vaishnava theologians to surpass even the lawful queens in the quality and purity of her love.

Source

Kinsley, David. Hindu Goddesses: Visions of the Divine Feminine in the Hindu Religious Tradition. University of California Press, 1986.
