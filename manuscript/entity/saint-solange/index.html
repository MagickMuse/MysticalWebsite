Roman Catholic saint, a French shepherdess martyred in approximately 880 CE.

Life

Solange belonged to a family of peasants who labored on the estate of the Count of Poitiers. She became a shepherdess in childhood and simultaneously began demonstrating spiritual powers. Her presence alone was reputedly sufficient to exorcise demons. According to legend, she vowed herself to Christ at age seven.

Martyrdom

Solange was a beautiful, charismatic young woman who caught the eye of Bernard, the count's son. He attempted to rape her, perhaps presuming that as his family's tenant, her body was his by right. She resisted. Bernard murdered her in the struggle, beheading her with his sword.

Solange, headless, stood up, picked up her head, and carried it to the next town. There she preached and described what had happened to her, though there had been no witnesses. Only then did she rest in peace. The town was renamed Sainte-Solange in her honor.

Miracles and Veneration

Miracles were immediately attributed to her. Her head was preserved as a relic. In 1281, an altar was erected for her, and the church was renamed in her honor.

Her story bears extraordinary resemblance to ancient Celtic veneration of severed, oracular heads, such as that of Bran the Blessed. She belongs to a category of saints known as cephalophores, those who carry their own heads after decapitation.

Iconography

Solange is depicted as a young girl kneeling before a cross, accompanied by her sheep.

Feast Day

10 May.
