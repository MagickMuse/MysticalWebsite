Classification

Unofficial saint.

Life

Eva Maria Ibarguren Duarte Peron (7 May 1919-26 July 1952), a poor illegitimate Argentinean country girl, came to Buenos Aires to become a movie star. She became a successful radio hostess and local fashion icon, marrying Colonel Juan Peron shortly before he became president of Argentina.

Eva was the subject of a cult of personality. She worked tirelessly for the poor, establishing a foundation to help find jobs and distribute funds. She was also accused of funneling funds to Swiss bank accounts. In general, poor and working people loved her; the wealthy and military elite despised her.

Death and Veneration

Like Jesus Christ, Eva died at age thirty-three. Devotees compared her to the Virgin Mary and Joan of Arc. At the news of her death, 700,000 Argentineans took to the streets in public mourning. Sixteen people died among the crowds; 3900 ended up in the hospital.

In a speech, Eva said, "I left my dreams by the wayside in order to watch over the dreams of others." In her last speech she vowed, "I will be with my people, dead or alive."

Orchestrated Sainthood

Unofficial saints tend to spring up despite authority, but Evita's veneration was officially encouraged. Five days after her death, a formal request for canonization was made to Pope Pius. The Vatican received over 100,000 requests, many crediting her with miracles.

  • Official postal boxes were maintained so letters to dead Evita could be mailed
  • Children's books described Evita wearing a halo or as a star in the sky
  • A children's magazine published a prayer beginning "Evita, our love who art in Heaven..."
  • Official first-grade texts featured "Ave Eva" with illustrations of Eva among angels

The Incorrupt Body

The attempt to keep her body incorrupt cost over $100,000, embalmed by superstar Spanish pathologist Dr. Pedro Ara. The plan was to display her corpse in the world's largest mausoleum, but Peron was overthrown first.

Underground Veneration

For sixteen years (1955-1971), it was illegal to possess Eva's photograph or say her name. Her statues were removed; copies of her autobiography burned. Her corpse was kidnapped and transported several times, ending up buried in Milan under the name Maria Maggi. Returned to Argentina, she is now buried fifteen feet deep in a specially armored vault in Buenos Aires' most exclusive cemetery.

Powers

Home altars are maintained for Santa Evita. She performs healings, locates jobs, and provides cash to devotees in need. Some anticipate a second coming.

Manifestation

Some devotees see her profile in the moon.

Iconography

Countless photographs exist. Some use images of the Virgin Mary to represent her. A popular icon features Evita's face superimposed over Our Lady of Sorrows.

Feast Days

  • May 7 (birth)
  • July 26 (death)
  • October 17 (renunciation of vice-presidency)

Sacred Site

La Recoleta Cemetery in Buenos Aires.

Related Entities
