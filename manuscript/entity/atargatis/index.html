Magickal Properties

Atargatis is the proud Great Fish Mother and Syrian Lady of love, fertility, protection, abundance, and the sea. She safeguards devotees, bestows prosperity and health, and guards mariners and sacred fish and doves.

Ritual Uses

Petition for abundance, health, protection, fertility, and safety at sea. Venerate at her water shrines and ponds of sacred fish.

Offerings

Votive fish in gold or silver. Feed and protect doves. Maintain ornamental fish ponds; offer care for sea creatures and waters.

Favored People

Those seeking fertility, protection, prosperity, and sea‑safety.

Petition For

Abundance and prosperity, health, fertility, protection, and safe passage by water.

Warnings

Devotees traditionally refrain from eating doves and fish of her ponds.

Additional Notes

Primary shrine: Hierapolis in Syria; also venerated in Philistine Ashkelon, Cyprus, Crete, and carried widely by Phoenicians. Sometimes identified by others as Dea Syria or Derceto.

Encyclopedia Source Notes

Mother goddess of the Northern Syrian tradition. She enjoyed major cults at Khirbet Tannur, where she is depicted as the vegetation goddess in nine separate variations, and at Khirbet Brak, where she is associated with dolphins. She often carries a cornucopia linking her with the goddess Tyche of fortune, and may commonly be flanked by lions. She sometimes carries a rudder or wears the mural crown of a city guardian. There are hints of sky affinities in some depictions, with a sign of the zodiac or a nimbus-like veil. Her earliest consort is Dusara, but in later times she is linked with the Syrian storm god Hadad. At Dura and Hierapolis, where she is known as Hera-Atargatis, she tended to overshadow Hadad. Atargatis is also a fish goddess depicted like a mermaid, and in most of her cult centers she enjoyed a sacred lake stocked with fish. Statues of Hadad and Hera-Atargatis were carried in twice-yearly processions to the sea from Hierapolis, and by the third century BC her cult had reached Egypt. Greek writers of the Hellenic period describe her as a radiate goddess, suggesting some links with sun symbolism. The name Allat is also applied to her.
