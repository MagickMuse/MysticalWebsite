Saint Lucy is the patron saint of the blind, those with eye ailments, and clairvoyants. She is among the most beloved saints in all of Christianity, venerated by most branches. She heals afflictions of the eye, restores sight, bestows clairvoyance, and averts and removes the Evil Eye.

Official Histories

In one story, Lucy was a beautiful young Sicilian noblewoman who secretly converted to Christianity and made a vow of chastity. When a marriage was arranged for her, Lucy literally removed her own eyes, betting the groom would retract his proposal. He fled. God, impressed with her determination, restored her eyes and vision.

In another version, Lucy stalled the marriage for three years, praying to her intercessor, Saint Agatha. Agatha cured Lucy's mother's hemorrhagic bleeding disorder, and her mother canceled the wedding. The rejected suitor reported Lucy to Roman authorities. She was arrested, tortured, and sentenced to a house of prostitution, but miraculously became too heavy for even oxen to transport. Her eyes were gouged out but her vision was restored. Attempts to burn her alive failed. Finally, she was stabbed in the throat and died.

Unofficial Histories and Folk Magic

Less officially, Lucy ranks among the most beloved saints of folk magic, closely associated with witchcraft and the Wild Hunt. The Church acknowledges her hagiography is built on legend and folklore. Different female spirits may operate under her name, including Juno Lucina, Freya, and Hulda.

Before the Gregorian calendar reform of 1582, her feast fell on the winter solstice. The name Lucy derives from Latin for "light." Lucy the Lightbringer rules the longest night, considered an optimum time for magic, divination, and spiritual activity. In Austria, "Lucy's Light" is a folk name for second sight.

Hungarian Traditions

Hungarian folk tradition acknowledges two saints named Lucy: the official saint and a "strange being." Women were forbidden from traditional women's work on her feast, spending the day reveling instead.

Saint Lucy's Eve is considered among the most effective times for divination. Her feast day marks the opportunity to begin constructing a Saint Lucy Chair from nine different woods without nails, to be completed by Christmas Eve. Standing on this chair during Midnight Mass allegedly reveals secret witches by horns on their heads. The chair's maker must then flee home scattering poppy seeds, which pursuing witches are compelled to pick up. At least one such chair is displayed in the Ethnographic Museum in Budapest.

Other Traditions

In German tradition, Saint Lucy's Eve is notorious for upsurges in spirit activity, especially the Wild Hunt. In Switzerland, she accompanies Father Christmas, giving gifts to girls. In Bohemia, she hunts scoundrels on her eve, carrying them to subterranean caverns.

Iconography

A young woman holding a plate with her eyes on it. Sometimes she has eyes in her head; sometimes empty sockets. She may be portrayed praying at Saint Agatha's tomb or with a dagger at her throat.

Charms Against the Evil Eye

A Tuscan Charm:

  1. Craft a wreath from rue and red ribbons
  2. The afflicted person spits through the wreath three times while begging Saint Lucy for protection

A Germanic Verbal Charm:

Drive away the Eye!

Lore

The poet Dante credited Saint Lucy with healing his eyes after weeping for Beatrice. She appears in his Inferno and in the Dante's Inferno video game. Eyes painted on Mediterranean fishing boats are often called Saint Lucy's eyes.

Los Ojos de Santa Lucia (Saint Lucy's Eyes or Little Eyes) are eye-shaped amulets that ward off the Evil Eye, typically given to babies and children.

Swedish Traditions

The Feast of Saint Lucy is celebrated with young girls embodying the saint. The eldest or youngest daughter rises before dawn to fix breakfast, serving ritual foods including saffron buns and lussekatter (Lucy's Cats). She wears a white dress and a crown of lit candles.

Ritual

Lucy once saved Syracuse from famine when a grain ship appeared while people petitioned her. Some say Lucy herself appeared at the helm. Communities serve wheat dishes or semolina pasta on her feast, while others avoid milled grains. Saint Lucia Pudding (cucci) is made from wheat berries.

Sacred Sites

  • The Basilica of Saint Lucy, reputedly built over the site of her martyrdom
  • The Church of San Geremia in Venice houses her relics (stolen in 1981, restored that December)
