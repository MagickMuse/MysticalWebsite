Brahma

Satarupa, whose Sanskrit name means she of a hundred forms, is a Hindu goddess of the Puranic tradition, honored as the daughter and, in the same telling, the first consort of the creator god Brahma. Her myth belongs to the cosmogonic literature of the Puranas, where she stands as the primordial feminine formed from Brahma's own self at the beginning of creation.

Domains

The primordial feminine formed from the creator's own being, the multiplicity of created forms, and the archetypal first pairing from which the ordered generations of beings descend.

The Myth of the Four Heads

One of the great Puranic accounts describes how the beautiful goddess Satarupa was formed from half of Brahma's own self. To prevent her father from looking on her with desire, she circled around him, and Brahma, unwilling to lose sight of her, generated a new head in each of the four directions so that he might behold her from every side. His four heads resulted from this act of pursuit. A fifth head is said once to have existed, which Shiva decapitated with the thumb of his left hand.

The Puranic tradition notes that this transgression is partly the reason Brahma receives comparatively little devotional worship, despite his supreme office as creator.

Iconography

Satarupa is depicted as a beautiful young woman, sometimes shown in flight around Brahma, and sometimes as the seated consort of the creator god in his cosmogonic aspect.
