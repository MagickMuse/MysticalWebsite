Magickal Properties

Coatlique ('She of the Serpent Skirt, Mother of the Heavens') is mother of the moon and stars who was keeper of the sacred shrine at Coatepec (Snake Mountain). When a ball of down feathers fell from the sky and she tucked it in her bosom, she became mysteriously pregnant with Huitzilopochtli, Lord of War. She's a fierce spirit of fertility with tremendous transformative power.

Ritual Uses

Invoke Coatlique for major life transformations and spiritual rebirth. She excels at fertility work and protection during vulnerable times of change. Work with her when facing opposition or attacks, her child Huitzilopochtli emerged fully formed and armed when she was threatened. She can grant spiritual warrior strength.

Offerings

Serpent imagery and snake representations, earth-based offerings, items honoring motherhood and fertility, things that represent transformation and rebirth, coral snake imagery (her sacred animal).

Favored People

Mothers and pregnant women, those undergoing major life transformations, spiritual warriors facing opposition, people working with earth and fertility magick.

Petition For

Fertility and abundance, protection during vulnerable transformation periods, spiritual warrior strength, successful childbirth, earth-based magick and grounding.

Warnings

She's a fierce spirit of fertility, her power is intense and transformative. Her appearance can be frightening (skull head, serpent skirt, talons). Approach with respect for her primal earth power.

Additional Notes

Manifests with human skull or snake head (sometimes double snake head), necklace of human hands and hearts, skirt of entwined serpents, taloned feet. Associated with the number 5, coral snakes, and the earth element. Tepeyac (site of her great temple) was a major pilgrimage destination. Some consider her a precursor to the Virgin of Guadalupe, whose shrine now stands at the same site.

Mother of the Gods

Coatlique holds one of the most significant positions in the Aztec pantheon. Her name means "She of the Serpent Skirt," and she is often titled the Mother of the Gods — a fitting epithet for a goddess said to have given birth to the stars and the Moon. She is the great Earth Goddess of the Aztec tradition: creator and destroyer both, much as the Indian goddess Kali is at once the force of creation and of dissolution.

The Divine Conception

Coatlique was, by all accounts, an exceptionally pious figure. One day, while she was sweeping the temple, a small bundle of hummingbird feathers drifted down and settled on her. She tucked it away, and shortly afterwards found herself expecting a child. The bundle was understood as a message from the gods, and the child's divine origin was never in doubt. This story has drawn comparisons to the conception of the Virgin Mary.

Iconography

Coatlique is depicted wearing a skirt woven from writhing serpents and a necklace strung with human hearts and skulls. These are not signs of cruelty but of her dominion over the full cycle of existence: birth, death, and the continual return of life from within the earth itself.
