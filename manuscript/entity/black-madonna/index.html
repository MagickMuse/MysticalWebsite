The Black Madonnas are renowned for healing miracles, fertility, protection, and resuscitating dead babies. They are among the greatest miracle workers in the world.

Magickal Properties

  • Cult of miraculous images understood to be animated by divine spirit, among greatest miracle workers in world
  • Approximately 250 surviving Black Madonna images worldwide, greatest number in France, Italy, Switzerland, Spain
  • Many have mysterious provenance, appeared miraculously in wells, ghost ships, trees, caves
  • Demonstrate supernatural power: make themselves light/heavy, refuse to stay where placed, choose shrine sites

Ritual Uses

  • Standard offering is pilgrimage to home shrine if they produce miracle for you
  • Reproductions of images also radiate power and may be invoked anywhere
  • They identify where hidden via apparitions and dreams
  • Many brought to Europe by returning Crusaders from Middle East/North Africa

Offerings

Standard offering is pilgrimage to home shrine if miracle granted

Favored People

Pilgrims, those seeking miracles of healing/fertility/protection

Petition For

Miracles of healing, fertility, protection; they produce miracles of healing, fertility, and protection

Warnings

Extremely controversial, many refute their existence claiming just unusual Mary images darkened by candle smoke. Nothing can be assumed about their true identity.

The Meaning of "Black"

  • Not all Black Madonnas are literally black; some are gray, brown, silver, or other colors
  • Some were once black but have since been whitewashed
  • The word "black" may be understood in context of the Shulamite in the biblical Song of Songs, who describes herself as "black but comely" and may be the Queen of Sheba
  • Also understood in context of ancient Egypt: the name Egypt derives from Greek, but Egyptians called their country KMT or "the black land"
  • Black in Egyptian cosmology was the color of fertility and life everlasting, the color of good rich soil without which there is no survival
  • "Black" refers to literal dark color and metaphysical/metaphorical description, not modern racial terminology

Identity Theories

  • Virgin Mary: Many are affronted at the suggestion they could depict anyone else
  • Mary Magdalene: Jesus as father of the child she holds, child interpreted as daughter (sacred bloodline theory)
  • Hidden Pagan Goddesses: May mask secret veneration of forbidden goddesses like Athena, Kybele, Demeter, Persephone, Rosmerta, Aphrodite, Astarte, and especially Isis (ancient Isis images nursing Horus served as prototype for Holy Mother-and-Child imagery; often carved from black stone)
  • Originally Pagan Images: May have been crafted to represent Pagan goddesses, hidden during Christian banning of Pagan religions, then rediscovered centuries later and reinterpreted
  • Vessels of the Sacred Feminine: Regardless of original intent, they embody the sacred feminine and divine mother

Legendary Behaviors

  • Often choose their own shrine sites
  • Disappear and mysteriously reappear at certain sites no matter how many times moved, even from locked containers
  • Sometimes become too heavy to lift from where they first appeared, then instantly become light once a shrine is built there
  • Identify where hidden via apparitions and dreams (example: Black Madonna of Kazan appeared in a girl's dream to reveal her hiding place)
  • Sometimes Madonna is black and child is white

Miracles and Powers

  • Healing
  • Preventing disasters
  • Providing fertility
  • Resuscitating dead babies (at least long enough to be baptized)
  • Safety for travelers
  • Release for those imprisoned

Shrine Associations

  • Images of certain saints like Saint Roch frequently appear in Black Madonna shrines
  • Various esoteric theories suggest hidden political and spiritual histories may be revealed by analyzing these shrines, their motifs, and their saintly inhabitants

Additional Notes

  • Famous examples: Avioth, Candelaria, Chartres (two), Czestochowa, Einsiedeln, Guadalupe (Spain), Hal, Orcival, Regla, Kazan
  • Size: Most are small, comparable to home altar statues, hand-carved and unique
  • Many destroyed during French Revolution and France's religious wars; others hidden in church vaults, private collections, or unidentified in museums
  • Within conventional Christian iconography, Black Madonna imagery is odd, containing subversive elements that may be creative license or intentional messaging
