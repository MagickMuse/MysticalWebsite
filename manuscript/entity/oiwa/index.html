Japan's Most Famous Ghost

Oiwa, also known as the Yotsuya Ghost or Lantern Ghost, is Japan's most famous ghost and a living presence somewhere between vengeful spirit and goddess.

The Betrayal

Oiwa was poisoned by her husband Iemon, who disguised the poison as medicine. The poison disfigured her: her hair fell out, her face became distorted, and one eye drooped. She died of a broken heart on 22 February 1636.

Haunting and Vengeance

After her death, Oiwa haunted Iemon to madness through mental torture, appearing within burning lanterns. Her relentless vengeance against the man who betrayed her made her the archetype of the wronged woman who returns from death to claim justice.

Wishes and Warnings

Oiwa grants wishes to respectful visitors at her grave but afflicts the disrespectful with a swollen right eye. She is protective when honored at her shrine.

Theater Hauntings

Oiwa hovers around theaters: productions about her are often beset with bad luck unless cast members visit her grave and make offerings. It is traditional for casts to pay respects before mounting any work involving her story.

Cultural Legacy

Oiwa inspired the 1825 Kabuki play Yotsuya Kaidan, over thirty films, and annual television appearances. She appears in the carvings of netsuke artists and in prints by Hokusai, Yoshitoshi, and Kuniyoshi. The character Sadako in the Ring films combines Oiwa (face) with Okiku (well).

Manifestation

Long hair amid a partially bald head, contorted eyes with one drooping, and a disfigured face. She often appears in or behind burning lanterns.

Sacred Site

A shrine in Yotsuya, Tokyo maintains her veneration.
