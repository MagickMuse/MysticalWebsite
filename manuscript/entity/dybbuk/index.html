Magickal Properties

According to Jewish tradition, Dybbuks are souls so wicked or who have committed such grievous sins that after death, neither Paradise nor Hell will accept them. Evil angels refuse to allow these souls to rest, lashing them with fiery whips and driving them endlessly all over Earth. Sometimes these souls escape and attempt to hide by jumping into the nearest object or living being, with human beings being their ideal hosts.

Ritual Uses

Dybbuk possession requires exorcism, not invocation. Exorcism must be performed by shamanic rabbis who can negotiate better afterlife terms for the Dybbuk, either shortening their stay with pursuing angels or arranging for expiation of crimes so the Dybbuk can enter Paradise. Certain magickal passages or biblical verses may force the Dybbuk to leave.

Offerings

No offerings are appropriate. Professional exorcism is required, not propitiation.

Favored People

None. Dybbuks possess people against their will, often targeting young women as their victims.

Petition For

Exorcism services, protection from possession, safe removal of Dybbuk from host without harm, spiritual protection against wandering evil souls.

Warnings

Dybbuk possession is uniformly negative. There is no such thing as positive or benevolent Dybbuk possession. Exorcism must not be done by amateurs because if done incorrectly the host can be harmed or killed. The Dybbuk is usually forced to leave through the nail on the big toe to cause least damage to the host.

Additional Notes

Some Dybbuks try to live silently within hosts while others assert their presence by speaking through their hosts' mouths or displaying knowledge the host previously lacked. The most powerful may resist several exorcists. Central and Eastern European Jewish communities had a Dybbuk panic concurrent with the European witch panic, with most victims being young women and exorcists being male.
