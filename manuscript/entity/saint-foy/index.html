Saint Foy is renowned for healing, fertility, and exorcism of evil spirits.

Classification

Roman Catholic and Anglican saint.

Life and Martyrdom

Local tradition suggests that Saint Foy was born in December 290 in Agen, Aquitaine, now part of modern France. Her family was wealthy, noble, and Pagan, but Foy's nanny was a secret Christian who converted her young charge. "Foy" (meaning "faith") was her baptismal name; her birth name is now unknown.

The name Foy translates into English as "faith," and so this child martyr is often called Saint Faith. However, she is not related to the young sister saints Faith, Hope, and Charity. There are two completely distinct saints named Faith.

Persecution

When Foy was twelve, organized persecution of Christians began in Agen. Her nanny fled to the mountains. Foy was deliberately left behind, considered safe due to her wealthy family. Either Foy revealed herself to authorities or her father turned her in, hoping to scare her into obedience. He misjudged her.

Defiance and Death

Brought before Roman authorities, they attempted to bribe the exceptionally beautiful, charismatic child, promising a fine husband if she renounced Christianity. Foy proclaimed Jesus was the only spouse she desired. Ordered to sacrifice to the goddess Diana, she refused. She was bound to a brazier and roasted, then beheaded. Onlookers who expressed pity were summarily beheaded too.

Veneration and Relics

Foy's body was rescued by Agen's hidden Christians. Two centuries later, a basilica was built on the site of her martyrdom. She became associated with tremendous miracles: healing, fertility, exorcism. Her relics were brought to Conques on 14 January 866. Whether stolen or brought by a monk acting under her orders remains contested.

Conques was on the pilgrimage route to Compostela, and Saint Foy became extremely popular. Her head was placed in a beautiful, bejeweled, gilded, enthroned doll-shaped reliquary resembling a Pagan idol. Under the anglicized name Saint Faith, she also became very popular in England.

Personality

As befitting an eternal twelve-year-old, Foy has a reputation as a prankster saint who sometimes plays tricks on devotees. She appreciates sparkly offerings, especially jewelry, and has little patience with those who fail to deliver promised offerings promptly.

Iconography

A beautiful, regal young girl wearing a crown, or depicted being tortured.

Attributes

Gridiron, brazier, rods, sword (instruments of torture), palm branch of martyrdom.

Spiritual Ally

Saint Lawrence (also tortured by roasting).

Feast Day

  • October 6

Sacred Sites

  • Sainte-Foy Abbey Church in Conques, France (UNESCO World Heritage Site 1998)
  • Saint Faith's Chapel in Westminster Abbey, London (13th century)
  • Saint Faith's Chapel in Saint Paul's Cathedral crypt, London

Related Entities

Faith, Hope, and Charity; Saint James the Greater; Saint Lawrence
