The Crocodile God

Sobek is the crocodile god—his name literally means "crocodile." He is a particularly ancient Egyptian deity, with active veneration continuing until the forced abolition of traditional religion. Sobek was adored by some and feared by others, for crocodiles were killers and the primary danger in the Nile.

Role in Egyptian Magic

Friend and Foe

Crocodiles in Egyptian magic occupied a borderland similar to hippopotami. Moving between water and land, they could be friend or foe. Crocodiles are extraordinarily dangerous—even a young adult can kill a human in minutes. In tomb paintings showing vulnerable cattle crossing a canal, a crocodile often lurks threateningly below the water line.

The Water Spell and Magical Gesture

Herdsmen employed a magician to protect cattle from crocodiles. He would make a specific magical hand gesture (two fingers pointing out, two folded into the palm) while reciting a water spell to protect against the "marsh-dweller" by rendering the crocodile blind. This same spell could be invoked for any water crossing.

Crocodile Images as Protection

Turn that murderous power to your own ends through magic, and crocodiles became useful. Bringing an image of Sobek or any crocodile into a birthing room allegedly eases labor pains and keeps predatory spirits at bay. If unavailable, any crocodile image or Egyptian hieroglyph is sufficient.

Sacred Crocodile Mummies

Like cat mummies, young mummified crocodiles served as sacred offerings at temples. Priests of Sobek raised crocodiles in temple quarters, feeding them by hand. In the Fayum, a crocodile was enshrined as a living manifestation of Sobek known as Petsuchos ("Son of Sobek"), who lived a life of luxury and was mummified like a pharaoh when he died.

Mythology and Powers

Lord of Dark Waters

Sobek rules the most powerful and dangerous aspects of water. He protects those he loves and menaces others. According to one myth, the Nile emanates from his sweat.

The Osiris Myth

In the saga of Isis and Osiris, Sobek personally retrieves Osiris's dead body from the river and carries it to shore on his back—an act of service that shows crocodile power channeled toward cosmic restoration.

Deity of Fertility

Sobek is a deity of personal and vegetative fertility, an erotic spirit and epitome of virility. He bestows sexual prowess to men and fertility to women. He can be invoked to help women in childbirth.

Origin Myths

Son of Neith (may have self-generated with no father), or alternatively the father of Set. Another myth says he emerged alone from the primordial dark waters of chaos and himself created the ordered world.

Manifestation

Sobek appears as a Nile crocodile or as a man with a crocodile's head.

Attributes

  • Was-scepter: Symbol of power
  • Ankh: Symbol of life

Consorts

Associated with Hathor, Renenet, and Taweret. May be Khnum's father.

Primary Shrines

  • Shedet (Crocodilopolis): His oldest shrine, in the swamps of the Fayum oasis west of the Nile. The Greeks renamed it "Crocodile City."
  • Kom Ombo: A large sanctuary near Aswan, now a prime tourist attraction

During the Middle Kingdom, pharaohs paid attention to the Fayum and brought Sobek to national attention. He was particularly revered during the 12th and 13th dynasties—eight pharaohs were named Sobekhotep. Sobekneferu (1799–1795 BCE), last ruler of the 12th dynasty, was the first documented female pharaoh.

Warnings

Sobek is intensely associated with crocodiles and acts as their guardian. If you have harmed crocodiles—hunted them, eaten them, worn their skin—it may be best to avoid contacting him.
