Rune Storage & Care

How to Store Runes

A set of runes is a small collection of highly magickal objects requiring care and accounting. Treat them as a single entity to avoid incomplete sets.

Multiple Sets Recommended:

  • A sacred set for individual use
  • A travel set you can part with more easily
  • Allows spontaneous "check-in" pulls without risking your primary set

Progression: Start with Witch runes (pictographic, self-explanatory, no transliteration needed), then advance to more esoteric systems like Norse.

Storage by Material:

  • Stone: Store flat in a single layer (box) to prevent scratching. Line lids with soft fabric.
  • Painted staves: Avoid friction to prevent wearing.
  • Witch runes: Small number, usually painted on stones. Cast in a lidded box.
  • Ogham runes: Carved/burned on sticks, require larger surface area for spreads.

Pouch Storage:

  • Drawstring pouches work well. Choose ample size (5" x 7" for most sets).
  • Personalize by embroidering your name in runes (right to left). This creates a bond and accelerates learning.

Environmental Care:

  • Store away from direct light and heat.
  • Sunlight charging is fine occasionally but degrades color saturation over time.
  • Ask sellers if stones are natural, dyed, or heat-treated.

Altar Storage:

  • Arrange runes on altar surface, cover with dedicated cloth.
  • Covering shows respect for the oracle.
  • Do not consult too casually.

Caring for Your Runes

Treat runes as magickal tools, not kitchen utensils.

By Material:

  • Stone: Wrap individually to prevent abrasion and scratching.
  • Wood: Keep clear of candles and oils. Prevent dust accumulation.
  • Metal Runes: Store airtight to prevent oxidation. Dark patina is not necessarily undesirable.

General Practice:

  • Consult runes in ritual (need not be elaborate).
  • Enter expanded consciousness with intentional receptivity.
  • Consider: cloth pouch color, box type, storage location. All impact effectiveness.

Treatment Cautions:

  • Essential Oils: Can dissolve painted staves. Discolors wood permanently. Only use on carved/burned staves with awareness of effects.
  • Salt/Salt Water: Corrosive. Rinse briefly rather than soak. Never submerge wood (warps, splits, damages).
  • Sunlight/Smoke: Will impact appearance over time.
