Runic Codes & Numerical Symbolism

Runic Codes and the Ættir System

One of the most compelling runic practices is the concealment of magickal formulas through intricately devised codes. These codes served a dual purpose: making the messages more secret (and therefore more magickally effective) and keeping them incomprehensible to the uninitiated.

The basis of all runic codes is the numerical value of each rune. Both the Elder and Younger Futharks are divided into three groups called ættir (families). Through this system, any rune can be identified by a twofold numerical formula indicating its ætt and its position within that group. For example, the formula 1:2 identifies the second rune of the first ætt.

These cryptic systems reached their highest development during the Younger Futhark period, but they were certainly known and used in the older period as well, since ancient representations of the Elder row are also clearly divided into ættir. Two particularly remarkable methods are:

  • Branch runes (ON kvistrunir): A single glyph can portray a single rune. Strokes to the left of a vertical line indicate the ætt, while strokes to the right reveal the runic position. A change of word in the formula is indicated by the upward or downward direction of the ætt stroke.
  • Tent runes (ON tjaldrunir): Read in a clockwise direction beginning on the left, each segment encodes an ætt and position number.

The ninth-century St. Gall MS 270 portrays several additional cryptographic systems of rune writing.

Numerical Symbolism

Numerical criteria play an important role in the loading and working of rune tines and are often critical in other types of rune magick. In the ancient inscriptions the vitki sometimes sacrificed linguistic clarity for numerical or ideographic potency by leaving out staves (especially vowels) or by adding and doubling them. Number is one of the three keys to each rune, and much of the interpretation of individual runes is drawn from numerical criteria. The H-rune (9) connects to the Nine Worlds of Yggdrasill, and the J-rune (12) to the twelve months of the solar year.

The numerical values of inscriptions, tines, and magickal formulas place the power of the runes in various spheres of working while drawing on the power inherent in that number. A general principle holds: the more levels of meaning packed into the least amount of space, and the more cryptic the form, the more effective the magick. This applies equally to the shaping and interpretation of talismans.

Runic number formulas are analyzed in two ways. The first is the rune count, the number of staves in the formula. The second is the runic total, the sum of the numerical values of each rune (similar to gematria). These numbers are then broken down into their multiples to further analyze their powers. The formula luwatuwa, found on ancient talismans, demonstrates both systems: a rune count of 8 (2 x 4) and a runic total of 66 (6 x 11), forming an incantation of great ideographic, phonetic, and numerological power.

For the rune count, the numbers 1 through 24 are all powerful and imbue the formula with the force of the rune of that number. Using any twenty-four runes in an inscription provides a broad base of power and invokes the force of the whole rune row. To a twenty-four-fold rune count, the number eight and its multiples (and indeed twenty-four and its multiples) may be added to maintain the whole harmony while intensifying force. The multiple of the rune count also modifies the runic potency in subtle ways. These are common patterns in ancient inscriptions.

On the level of the runic total, the sums 1 through 24 indicate the sphere where that particular rune is at work. Prime numbers are especially powerful and express a tremendous amount of will. Whatever the runic total might be, it is through its multiple factors that the root force of the number is revealed:

  • Multiples of 3 and 9 are powerful in operations dealing with magickal forces working on many levels at once, including the earthly realm
  • Multiples of 10 are especially forceful when the intent is to cause a change in the manifest world of Midhgardhr
  • Multiples of 12 are also potent in this regard but produce a more prolonged and enduring effect
  • 13 and its multiples are the most universal numbers of power. A vast number of runic inscriptions manifest this numerological pattern. The number is indicative of universal potency and contains the mystery of Eiwaz as the world-tree (9) and the three realms (3) in the ontological oneness of Ginnungagap (1). The number by which this master number is multiplied further modifies and directs the overall force of the formula according to its runic nature

All of these principles may be used when constructing rune tines and rituals, but they need not dominate the form of the operation. Intuition and natural inclination should be the guide, and vitkar may totally dispense with numerical considerations without diminishing results. The correct use of runic numerology is an art in itself, one that needs to be supplemented with a deep grounding in Nordic lore to be completely effective.

It should be noted that the old Germanic attitude toward number differed considerably from that of their southern neighbors. To the Pythagorean and Gnostic mystics, number came to be the rule of all things. To the vitkar, number was only one among three equal expressions of the same holy mystery embodied by a rune. Where the Gnostics and Pythagoreans tended to see number as a way of measuring and distinguishing one thing from another, the vitkar saw numbers as points of connection and interrelation in a cosmos forever in a state of ebb and flow.
