Rune Grimoire

🌙Witches Alphabet

Type
Magical Alphabet
Also called
Theban Script; Alphabet of Honorius

The Theban script, also known as the Alphabet of Honorius, is most often called the witches' alphabet because of its popularity in Books of Shadows, where it is used to encode magickal spells. It was first described by Johannes Trithemius in a work on cryptography published in the early sixteenth century. Cornelius Agrippa, a student of Trithemius, also set it down in his writings on occult philosophy, published in Antwerp in 1531. The Honorius of the alternate name is Honorius of Thebes, who wrote a learned book on magick.

Trithemius was a striking figure. Made a Benedictine abbot at the age of twenty-one in 1483, he also earned a reputation as a magician, borne out by a coded work of his on the use of spirits as a means of long-distance communication. His writings became a cornerstone of the Golden Dawn society almost five hundred years later.

The witches' alphabet may have begun as a Latin cipher used by early tenth-century alchemists to keep their discoveries secret. Its twenty-six characters are substitute symbols for the letters of the Latin alphabet, so anyone fluent in the script, most likely another witch or magician, could translate it with little trouble, which makes its value for genuinely disguising spells or charms rather doubtful.

Its true age is disputed: some practitioners consider it a relic of Atlantean times, while others link it to the Cabalistic Enochian Script associated with the Elizabethan astrologer and magician Dr. John Dee. Whichever account is favored, it is widely treated as ancient, and witches have long used it as their standard alphabet for writing spells and inscriptions, including inscribing a witch's own magical name onto tools and jewelry.
