↗Symbols1

Overview

This Arabic script is first mentioned in a ninth-century book (AD 855) by an Arabic scholar of magical lore. Also called Dambudi, the Davidian script is named for King David of Israel, whose son Solomon was renowned for his wisdom. Even now, thousands of years after his death, Solomon is held in reverential awe by Arabs and many others as the greatest magician who ever lived.

By association, the alphabet is imbued with Solomon's powers. Said to be the father of Freemasonry, Solomon was a polymath whose gifts were held to include understanding the language of birds. Stylistically the script derives from Hebrew letters, and its characters were believed to be so charged with occult meaning that they were the very embodiment of mystical power. The Arabs who used it called it simply rihani, meaning "magic."
