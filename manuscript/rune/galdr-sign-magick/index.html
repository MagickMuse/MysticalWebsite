Galdr & Sign Magick

A galdr is a magick song in which runes are sung. Giving voice to something amplifies it, creating a vibrational link between the witch and the rune while engaging with the space-time continuum to affect outcomes.

Galdr (Magick Song)

Historical Origins

Also called a shield song, galdr was historically used to affect battle outcomes. It was meant to be amplified, a sound intended to carry, whether to inspire, intimidate, bind, or defeat.

The technique involved singing at very high pitch and loud volume. Warriors positioned shields over their mouths to create resonance as sound waves bounced off metal surfaces, dramatically increasing projection.

Traditional Uses

  • Battle: Shifting conflict energy into victory
  • Healing: Chanted repeatedly at the bedside of the afflicted
  • Cursing: Directing harmful energy toward targets
  • Divination: Summoning spirits who could bring knowledge of the future

The Hand-Cupping Method

Hold your hands together, slightly cupped, about 2-3 inches from your mouth. This creates a resonating chamber that directs sound back to your own ears. The power may be surprising at first.

This technique works well in coven settings, those hesitant to engage with their power publicly can benefit from the modesty that covering the mouth allows.

How to Perform a Galdr

  1. Choose your rune: Select from your cast or choose one aligned with your intended outcome
  2. Position: Use the hand-cupping method or hold your altar charger in front of your nose and mouth
  3. Face your altar and close your eyes
  4. Visualize: Envision the best possible outcome for everyone involved
  5. Sing: Voice your rune with as much power as you can muster

The Nature of the Sound

The song does not have to be pretty. It is not a performance. It can be primal, guttural, or transcendent, whatever your spirit needs in that moment. The sound may waver from a cry to a shriek to a cackle.

There is no wrong way to sing a galdr as long as you put your heart and soul into it and make certain the sound is loud and clear.

Why It Works

Using a galdr is a form of invocation. Invoking powers to come to your aid increases energy and improves outcomes. Bringing this practice from the distant past and reinterpreting it for modern use is a form of reconstruction, the human need for protection, healing, and manifestation remains unchanged.

Sign Magick (Signingagaldr)

Signingagaldr blends the techniques of signing and sending with galdr (incantation). This form of magick is the most difficult because it requires the greatest concentration and visualization to be maximally effective. Once the techniques have been mastered, however, it promises to be the most direct and effective form of galdr available to the vitki.

The main difference between signing in this context and that used in the loading of tines is that here the signs are directed toward and melded with already living systems, whether wights or dynamistic fields of runic force.

The Principle

The vitki cuts runes into the living fabric of the multiverse, melding rune might with a symbolic "target" formulated by the hugr through concentrated visualization, bringing about a change in that target. This is only one of many ways to use sign magick techniques. The process may be carried out in the hugauga (totally by meditative visualization) or by external ritual work. In both cases the techniques are the same, though the vitki may wish to begin with visualization and work into more difficult ceremonial performance.

The Three Steps of Signingagaldr

  1. Formulation of and/or concentration on a target
  2. Formulation and projection of the willed rune might
  3. Melding of the previous two into a single field of force such that the second influences the first in the desired way

Formulating a Target

To formulate a target for the projected magickal force, the vitki sets off a "target area": a framework within which the visualized target is held, so that the rune might may be directed into it. This may be rectangular or triangular in shape.

In full ritual work, a frame may be constructed from wood and suitably painted or decorated. The frame may contain a symbolic form of the target (a picture, for example), but most often the vitki will magickally build the form of the target image within the framework using powers of concentration and visualization.

Projection and Melding

Once the target is firmly established and bound within the frame, the vitki begins to formulate the willed rune might in the center of the body. This power is then projected into the target area and signed in the manner of sending and signing. The galdr and/or formali that is sung or spoken gives intention and refined form to the runic power. As the vitki signs the rune, the force of the sign should feel as if it is actually being infused into the living fabric of the target. The vitki will immediately perceive the change in the target once the melding of forces is complete.

The Role of Galdr in Sign Magick

Both sound-formulaic and poetic forms of galdrar may be used, but the vitki should always aim for the more complex poetic form because of the degree of flexibility and exactness it affords. These magick songs are found throughout old Germanic literatures. For the ancient Germanic peoples, the art of poetry grew out of the power of magick.

The galdr serves to aid in the invocation and evocation of the rune might and in its formation within the vitki. It also aids in projection and linkage of that force to the target through its principal quality: vibration.

Breath and Concentration

The breath should be the focal point of concentration throughout the galdr. During inhalations, the vitki concentrates on the flow of rune might into the personal center. There it is momentarily held for exact formulation, and then on exhalation, the portion of the galdr to be projected is intensely concentrated on.

Most galdrar require several breaths to perform completely. One line of the incantation may be performed with each breath. In the case of poetic galdrar, the vitki should "runically" concentrate on each letter-sound of every word in the incantation, feeling their force rather than thinking about their meaning. If well composed, the galdr will seem to do its own work. What is important is the flow of force, its formulation, and its projection in a smooth concentrated pattern that becomes almost unconscious to the accomplished vitki. A unity is built between the rune might, the vitki, and the target.

Sound and Form

The main magickal unity on which the vitki must consciously concentrate is that of the sound (of the incantation) and the form (of the stave and the beam of light through which it is projected), all within the common "runic vibration." Meditational exercises will greatly aid in this process.

Incantations may be sung either out loud or within the hugr. When spoken aloud, galdrar may be sonorously and boomingly sung or softly whispered. The former seems more effective when the vitki performs in solitude or in the company of fellow vitkar, while the latter is often more powerful when non-vitkar are present.

Preparation

Because this form of galdr comes so close to an unconscious, meditational approach, it is essential to memorize all aspects of the rite before performance. A successful work of signingagaldr depends on a magnificent blend of all segments of the vitki's psychosomatic complex. The unexpected mystical results of these operations are often astounding.

On Composing Poetic Galdrar

Poetic galdrar may certainly be composed in the vitki's native language. Much thought should go into their composition as to both form and content. The dedicated vitki will undertake the study of Old Norse and/or other old Germanic dialects (Old English, Gothic, Old High German), for these languages vibrate with magicko-mythic qualities that are difficult to conjure in modern tongues. Most important is that the vitki finds a magickally potent language and poetic form that speaks with the voice of the unconscious realms.
