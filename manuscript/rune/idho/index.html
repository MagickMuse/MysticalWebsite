Idho (also spelled Iodhadh or Ioho) is the fifth few of the Fourth Aicme of the Ogham alphabet. According to Old Irish myth, Ogham takes its name from Ogma, the god of speech, and each symbol represents a particular tree and its characteristics.

Divination Meaning

Idho of the yew tree (Taxus baccata) is a rune of longevity. With regard to a situation, it is a culmination of a long journey that could signify a significant transformation and possibly even a change of identity or death. Culminations are not the same as endings, because the message of Idho is one of endurance and continuance. Yew reminds us to see both sides of any given situation, for everything has a light and a dark energy.

Keywords

Duality, mystery, and awakening.

Traditional Uses

Yew trees often grow in graveyards, maybe because they are thought to safely usher the souls of the dead to the next world. They're very long-lived—some are thousands of years old—and they're also quite toxic. When yew trees droop so low that they touch the ground, they are capable of rooting, so perhaps they are viewed as regenerative as well as guardians of the dead. Magically, yew pertains to anything involving other realms or astral travel.

Working with Idho

Draw on Idho when working with death and rebirth, seeking transformation, or exploring connections to the otherworld. This rune supports those navigating major life transitions and those who work between worlds. It favors those who can hold the tension of duality—light and dark, life and death, endings and continuance.
