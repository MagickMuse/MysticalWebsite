Rune Grimoire

Choosing Your Path

Selecting a runic system is a personal journey. You may resonate with one specific system or use different ones for various purposes. While ancestral connection is a common starting point, it is not the only criterion, especially for those without access to family histories. Runes are fluid constructs that transcend time, useful for navigating both the past and the present.

Exploring Ancestral Roots

Genealogy can be revealing but also challenging, potentially uncovering generational trauma. However, confronting these histories can open pathways to healing.

  • Reclaiming: For those whose lineages were disrupted or who feel disconnected, reclaiming a system can be empowering. It offers a sense of self and connection to the migrations that led to your current life.
  • Language & Naming: Understanding the origins of names and language impacts your interaction with the oracle. Just as marginalized groups reclaim language as an act of defiance, choosing a runic system can be an act of claiming personal power.
  • DNA Testing: While not 100% accurate, DNA tests can reveal general migration patterns. Comparing these with family stories can provide insight, though ancestry never prohibits you from using a system that calls to you.

A Starting Place: The Present Moment

If family records are lost, connect with your present environment.

  • The Land: Acknowledge the history of the land you occupy.
  • Migration: Consider your own life's movements. Frequent movers might resonate with the exploratory energy of Norse runes. Those deeply rooted in one place might find kinship with the tree-based Ogham runes.

The Vegvísir

This eight-rayed symbol, often associated with Vikings, is likely a modern reconstruction from the 1800s (appearing in the Huld manuscript). Despite its debated ancient origins, it has taken root in the collective consciousness as a powerful symbol of protection and guidance through "storms," both literal (climate change) and metaphorical.

Practical Experimentation

To determine which system calls to you, try writing your surname or magickal name in different scripts.

  1. Norse: Write phonetically from right to left along a horizontal stave.
  2. Ogham: Carve vertically from bottom to top along a central stave.
  3. Theban: Transcribe directly using the cipher alphabet.
  4. Witch Runes: Select symbols that feel poignant to your identity.

Observe which form pleases your eye and resonates with your soul.

Cultural Associations & Elemental Correspondences

Understanding the cultural "flavor" of a system can help align it with your personality or zodiac sign.

Norse Runes (Earth, Fire, Water)

  • Vibe: Powerful, mutable, transformational.
  • Best for: Those attracted to themes of creation, destruction, distant travel, and overcoming obstacles.
  • Elements: Volcanic fire (creation/transformation) and icy water (endurance/travel).

Ogham Runes (Air, Earth, Spirit)

  • Vibe: Cyclical, nature-based, reverent.
  • Best for: Those who see death as a rite of passage and are deeply connected to the earth and seasons.
  • Elements: Earth (inspiration/devotion) and Air (rapid change/growth).
  • Context: Druidic culture emphasized the cyclical nature of life and the connection to universal consciousness.

Witch Runes (Visual/Psychic)

  • Vibe: Intuitive, symbolic, direct.
  • Best for: Visually oriented practitioners and those developing psychic abilities.
  • Usage: Excellent for psychometry, talismans, and quick fortune-telling. Since they are non-alphabetic, they rely on pure symbolism rather than linguistics.

Understanding Energetic Properties

Before engaging with different energetic properties, you must first reclaim your power and understand your receptive abilities. Once you are self-aware, you can interpret the subtle energies inhabiting both the rune's material and its symbol.

Elemental Properties

  • Air: Ranges from gentle breezes of new growth to formidable winds of transformation and destruction.
  • Fire: Can be the illuminating candle flame or the raging wildfire that reduces substances to their purest form. Represents change through redirection or destruction.
  • Water: Manifests as nourishing rain or deep, unknowable mysteries and complex emotions.
  • Earth: The life-giver and repository for generations, sustaining all life and absorbing it back in death.

Runes and Divination

Divining is subtle work requiring active engagement. Like thermodynamics, energy is transferred from the rune maker to the object, and between the petitioner and the source.

Source Points:

  • Spiritual: Deities (Goddess/God)
  • Ancestral: Spirits of ancestors
  • Secular: Elemental confluences or the subconscious self

The Magickal Force: Your magick is the "outside force" (inertia) that sets things in motion.

Energetic Principles

Identify your energetic source to establish a clear connection.

1. Spiritual Energy:

  • For witches on an initiatory path.
  • Involves invocations to deities, directions, and elements.
  • Practice: Dedicate rune sets as formal pathworking or explore pantheons (Norse, Celtic, Greek) to find resonance.

2. External Energy:

  • For witches focused on wellness, weather, and environment (Green/Hedge/Kitchen witches).
  • Based on elemental and ancestral energy rather than formal deity worship.
  • Practice: Use runes in the presence of elemental representations (candle, water, salt) to establish a link.

3. Internal Energy:

  • For secular or solitary witches relying on intuition.
  • Requires high levels of self-examination.
  • Practice: Engage in meditation to clear the mind. Treat the oracle as a trusted confidant or ancestor. If needed, seek a like-minded partner for objectivity.

Pathways and Choice

Modern practitioners (Heathens, Druids, Wiccans) all use runes for divination, drawing from reconstructed traditions or personal gnosis.

Gnosis: Empirical spiritual knowledge gained through personal experience rather than books or lore. When multiple practitioners receive the same messages independently, it validates without requiring published sources.

Three Pathways:

  1. Solitary Secular Practitioner:
    • Read for yourself or others for personal guidance.
    • Rely on intuition and instinct.
    • Choose your system by preference, affinity, or how you wish to be perceived.
  2. Tradition, Craft, or Practice:
    • Use runes ritualistically alongside psychometry, meditation, and astrology.
    • System chosen through elder knowledge, ancestral origins, or cultural affiliation.
  3. Initiatory Path:
    • Runes as conduits to higher spiritual powers.
    • Incorporated into coven work with reverence for deity, nature, and oracle.
    • Techniques: Grounding and centering before readings, cleansing runes before and after, invoking guiding powers.

Conclusion: Consciously choosing your energetic properties and pathway empowers you as a rune-master. The depth of your practice depends on your preferences, needs, aptitude, and ability. The only wrong way to use runes is as pure entertainment with disregard for their origin, historical application, and modern power.

Reclaim Your Power as a Witch

Runes bring the past alive while empowering the present. Reclaiming runes is a valid exercise for any witch, regardless of tradition. Whether you follow an initiatory path or an unconstrained spiritual practice, there is no wrong way to experience magick.

  • Historical Context: Witches were once feared and persecuted for their perceived threat, healing abilities, or nonconformity. Using runes connects you to this lineage of power and divination, a staple of occult practice for millennia.

1. Declare Your Power

The first step is acknowledgment. Even in initiatory paths, true initiation comes from the Gods and personal transformation. The outward trappings merely honor the internal shift. Acknowledge yourself as a creator of magick. No one else can declare when you have awakened.

2. Own Your Power

Be true to yourself as an ethical learner. Tap into your sources of power by attuning with moon phases and practicing mindfulness. Be honest about your psychic strengths, whether empathy, clairvoyance, or other abilities, and embrace them.

3. Use Your Power

Power is multifaceted. Approach it as an expression of your highest aspiration for the good of all. Never confuse "empowerment" with "power over others."

  • Responsibility: Extend your seeking of autonomy to all beings. Avoid creating chaos or imbalance.
  • Safety: If celebrating fire festivals, take precautions. Understand your strength in both the physical and metaphysical worlds.

The New Burning Times

The witch hunts of the 16th and 17th centuries persecuted many who were not actual witches. As your understanding of humanity grows, so should your acceptance of "otherness." Acknowledge modern equivalences of persecution based on actions or appearance as part of your path.

Witch Runes

Witch runes differ significantly from stave runes (like Norse and Ogham) in both form and symbolism. They consist of thirteen pictographic symbols, often painted or inscribed on round tiles or stones. Along with the Theban alphabet, they are one of the two systems of writing and divination most strongly associated with modern witchcraft.

Origins and History

While their exact origins remain unknown, this system of folk magick was popularized in the 1970s, largely due to Dana Corby and her work The Witches' Runes. Interest continued through the late 20th century with works like Susan Sheppard's A Witch's Runes.

  • Theories of Origin: Suggestions range from Eastern Europe to Northeastern Scotland.
  • Pictish Resemblance: The forms bear similarity to early carvings by the Scottish Picts.
  • Ancient Connections: The rudimentary designs resemble symbols found in Egyptian hieroglyphs, Slavic light symbols, and early Bronze Age carvings.

Symbolism and Associations

Witch rune symbols are pictographic, meaning their form often directly reflects their meaning (e.g., the Eye looks like an eye, Rings look like rings). This directness removes immediate secrecy but retains occult depth through context.

  • Celestial: Sun, Moon, Star.
  • Elemental: Flight (Air), Wave (Water), Harvest (Earth), Romance (Fire).
  • Polarity: Man and Woman reflect the duality seen in many traditions (Goddess/Consort), though gendered polarity is not a prerequisite for modern practice.
  • Protection: The Eye serves as a talisman and ward.
  • Fate: Scythe, Crossroads, and Rings connect heavily to predictive magick.

Why Use Witch Runes?

Modern witches appreciate Witch runes for their adaptability and aesthetic appeal. They are among the most modern occult motifs, offering:

  • Versatility: Can be drawn on paper for charms/jars or inscribed on candles.
  • Simplicity: Easier to interpret than alphabetic systems because they are pictographic (cannot spell names).
  • Directness: Simple forms allow for quick understanding and visual parity.

Divination Methods

Psychometric Divination (Pendulum Work)

  1. Setup: Arrange all 13 runes in a 6-inch circle (Sun at 12 o'clock).
  2. Connect: Ground yourself and hold a pendulum over the empty center. Establish "yes" and "no" movements.
  3. Ask: Speak your question/intention aloud to create vibrational energy.
  4. Probe: Move the pendulum over specific runes to gauge positive or negative influence based on the pendulum's response.

Holding Space (Casting)

  1. The Vessel: Cup your hands (palms not touching) with runes inside to create an energetic opening.
  2. The Focus: Instead of a direct question, state the situation (e.g., "The issue is my relationship").
  3. The Breath: Repeat the issue three times while shaking the runes. Exhale deliberately into your hands, then drop them.
  4. Interpretation:
    • Dominant Rune: The first one you notice.
    • Guide Rune: The one farthest away.
    • Combinations: Runes touching each other modify meanings (e.g., Scythe + Flight = fast ending; Eye + Wave = healing/forgiveness).

Uses in Magick

  • Divination & Psychometry: Used for readings and reading object energy, though spreads are often less complex than those for Elder Futhark.
  • Spellwork: Ideal for casting spells, scribing candles, and emblazoning charms.
  • Grimoire Work: Used to add context and embellishment to magickal records.
  • Secrecy: Historically, esoteric symbols allowed witches to record spells and communicate safely during times of persecution, acting as ciphers for the craft.

Theban Runes

The Theban alphabet, also known as the Runes of Honorius, originated in the medieval period. While its precise roots remain unclear, it is attributed to Honorius of Thebes and first appeared in Book Three of Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa's Three Books of Occult Philosophy. Agrippa's work was a cornerstone of Renaissance discourse, inextricably linking magick and religion.

Agrippa's Feminism

Agrippa was a revolutionary voice against the misogyny of his time. His 1529 publication, Declamation on the Nobility and Preeminence of the Female Sex, challenged the deep-seated patriarchal belief in the inferiority of women, establishing him as a significant early feminist figure.

Aesthetics and Modern Usage

Next to Norse runes, the Theban alphabet is the most widely used system in modern witchcraft. Its graceful curves and complex patterns contrast with the rudimentary staves of earlier rune systems, showing unmistakable Indo-European influences.

Application in the Craft

Theban runes are prized for their elegance and their utility in maintaining secrecy. They are frequently used in grimoires to:

  • Label and consecrate tools
  • Create and record spells and charms
  • Record memories
  • Dedicate objects for special use

Ogham Runes

Druidic Mystery and Oral Tradition

The Druids, a Celtic class of priests and mystics, left no written manuscripts. They revered nature and passed traditions orally. Consequently, modern Druidry is a reconstruction. This evolution does not invalidate the practice; like language, tradition fluxes and refines over time.

History of the Ogham Runes

Theories on Ogham origins vary. Some suggest it arose after the biblical fall of the Tower of Babel. Others propose early Irish Christians created it to conceal communications from Latin-reading rivals, or simply as a symbol of Irish distinctiveness.

While some scholars argue Ogham was purely written, poet Robert Graves suggested it had phonetic equivalents and functioned as a spoken language. This aligns with the oral nature of Druidic mystery.

Structure and Meaning

Ogham script consists of hash marks or tallies etched along a central line.

  • Fews (Feda): The individual characters.
  • Aicme: Families of five letters, united by a common motif.
  • Tree Ogham: Each letter is named for a specific tree, imbuing it with occult significance.
  • Calendar: The system functions as a calendar, with thirteen months associated with specific trees.

Robert Graves also theorized a system of hand gestures where finger parts corresponded to letters, creating a secret sign language.

Archaeological Evidence

Ogham dates back to at least the fourth century. Surviving examples appear on tombstones and in manuscripts like the Book of Ballymote.

  • Inscriptions: Often indicate possession or lineage (e.g., "Stone of [Name], son of [Name]").
  • Orientation: Early stone carvings read vertically from bottom to top. The Book of Ballymote presents them horizontally.

Modern Usage

Modern witches use these cipher-like symbols for divination, celebrating love and family, and marking property.

Using Ogham for Divination

Creating an Ogham Set:

Make an Ogham set by burning or painting the markings onto larger twigs, popsicle sticks, or pieces of dowel rod. These become your casting sticks. Toss them onto a casting cloth and interpret their positions and relationships.

The Casting Cloth:

A useful design features a circle divided into different sections, with each section pertaining to a different area of life: Health, Happiness, Relationships, Work, Home, and other areas meaningful to your practice.

Interpreting the Cast:

  • Sticks outside the circle: Remove first. Not relevant to the matter at hand.
  • Sticks touching other sticks: Most important. Their connection indicates a significant relationship between their meanings.
  • Sticks half in and half out: May reveal what is entering or leaving the current situation, energies in transition.
  • Sticks face down: Of minor input, present but not dominant influences.

Beyond Divination:

Ogham may also be used for sigil creation, talismans and amulets, and as markers around your space for protection or encouragement. Get to know the fews and Ogham sticks, familiarize yourself with their timing and the trees they represent, and they will soon become a magickal tool that is portable and profound.
