Signing, Sending & Protection

Signing and Sending of Runes

The practice of making runic gestures or signs was well known in ancient times. The Norse godhar (priests) would make the "sign of the hammer" (Old Norse hamarsmark) over goblets before drinking. This rite was established well before the coming of Christianity.

How to Sign a Rune

A rune may be traced in the air with:

  • The palm of the right hand
  • The right index finger
  • The right thumb
  • The rune wand

Some staves may be signed with both hands in a smooth and aesthetic gesture.

Visualization and Projection

The vitki should project the image of the stave from a sphere of brightness in the center of the body, along a shaft of red light to the point where the rune is intended to appear. Once the beam has reached this distance, trace the form of the stave from the substance of light. The color may be red or some other symbolic hue.

Breath Rhythm

  1. Inhale as the arm is raised, concentrating on the intake of önd
  2. Exhale, sending and signing the stave while singing the name and/or galdr of the rune

After the Signing

The invoked force may be:

  • Reabsorbed into the personal sphere of the runester
  • Infused into an object as an act of loading or "charging"
  • Sent to do work elsewhere

This is one of the most powerful techniques available to the vitki, but one that must be mastered with extremely strong concentration and visualization.

Rituals of Protection

Before beginning any ceremonial rune work, the vitki should perform a rite that banishes all forces detrimental to the work at hand. These forces are not "evil," merely disadvantageous to the operation.

Three formulas serve this purpose:

  1. The Hammer Rite (Hamarssetning): The strongest, providing maximal protection and isolation
  2. The Hagalaz Rite: Provides the most potent magickal atmosphere and potential
  3. The Elhaz Rite: Strikes a balance between protection and magickal potency

A rite of this type may be practiced every day and should be used in conjunction with an opening ritual to begin all ceremonial work.
