Rune Grimoire

👁️Enochian Script

Type
Magical Alphabet
Also called
Adamical; Angelical; Celestial Speech

Overview

Many magical scripts found in grimoires and manuscripts, used to keep their contents secret, are really no more than ciphers: they swap in different symbols for the letters of an existing alphabet, so that even when Roman or Hebrew characters appear, the underlying language is still English or Hebrew. There are a few true exceptions, however, and the Enochian tongue is one of them.

In the sixteenth century John Dee, a renowned mystic and astrologer to Queen Elizabeth I, together with Edward Kelley, claimed that angels had imparted both the script and the language to them. Dee was a deeply learned scholar, fluent in many languages and fascinated by the angelic realms. A tireless diarist, he noted the angels God was said to have sent to speak directly with the prophets, and he longed to find his own, reasoning that knowledge of their language would be the most powerful tool any magician could possess. He pursued this with the help of Kelley, an alchemist and clairvoyant "seer," who saw the letters in a vision while scrying with a crystal ball. When Kelley complained that he could not make them out clearly, the angels are said to have caused the characters to appear on paper, where he could trace them before they faded.

Dee himself did not call the script "Enochian." He named it "Angelical," "The Celestial Speech," or sometimes "Adamical," for the angels told him the language had been taught by God to Adam, who used it to speak with both God and the angels and to name all things. The label "Enochian" arose because Dee believed the biblical patriarch Enoch had been the last person to understand it.

The tongue was said to have been lost when Adam was banished from the Garden of Eden, a myth with a powerful symbolic charge: if humankind can no longer speak with a divine creative spirit, then we are truly cast out and lost. Adam's faint memory of the language is held to have produced an early form of Hebrew. Enoch alone could still fully understand it, but the book he wrote describing it was lost in the Great Flood. The idea of a divine language existing before the Deluge was a popular one in Dee and Kelley's day.

There are twenty-one characters in this arcane language, and, like other angelic scripts, it is written from right to left, though the letters are often used as a left-to-right cipher. It was once proposed during the Second World War that Enochian be used to expose a Nazi conspiracy within Britain, but the plan came to nothing.

A case might even be made for teaching Enochian today, since practitioners of Enochian magic claim to use it to summon and work with angels. There is a darker side, though: the spoken and written language was used extensively within Aleister Crowley's Golden Dawn circle until it was judged too dangerous and abandoned.
