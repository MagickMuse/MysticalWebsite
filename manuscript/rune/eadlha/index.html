Eadlha (also spelled Eadhadh) is the fourth few of the Fourth Aicme of the Ogham alphabet. According to Old Irish myth, Ogham takes its name from Ogma, the god of speech, and each symbol represents a particular tree and its characteristics.

A Note on Traditions

Sometimes Eadlha is associated with the white poplar tree (Populus alba), although aspen (Populus tremula) does seem to be more prevalent in most traditions. Both trees share the characteristic of leaves that tremble in the slightest breeze.

Divination Meaning

Symbolizing courage, Eadlha is connected to the quaking aspen with its bright leaves dancing in the breeze. The poplar is somewhat delicate, and drawing Eadlha may indicate vulnerability. It is a sign to gather strength in order to overcome what lies ahead.

Keywords

Tranquility, oneness, and fearlessness.

Traditional Uses

Aspen is used in magic pertaining to healing, for protection against theft or loss, and for releasing doubts and apprehension. The trembling leaves were said to whisper secrets and connect the practitioner to subtle energies. The tree's ability to reproduce through root suckers creates vast connected groves—symbolic of oneness and interconnection.

Working with Eadlha

Draw on Eadlha when you need to release fear, find tranquility in uncertain circumstances, or connect with the subtle whispers of intuition. This rune supports those who must face vulnerability with courage. It favors those who can find stillness even when everything around them trembles.
