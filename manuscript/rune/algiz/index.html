Modern Relevance

Elhaz signifies the elk, representing material comfort and spiritual satiety. It focuses on stability, protection, and meeting individual needs to build a strong foundation for higher attainment.

Protection, Valkyrjur, and the Splayed Hand

This rune is historically perhaps the most complex of all from a symbological perspective. The Proto-Germanic form algiz means "protection," and its stave form is perhaps derived from the basic sign of defense and protection: the splayed hand.

The concept of the valkyrjur has been connected to this stave through the interpretation of the name as "swan." The valkyrjur are protective, life-giving beings who often fly by means of magickal cloaks made of swan feathers. They are protectors, power givers, and a mode by which Odin communicates with chosen heroes. The sign is often found carved into spears for protection and victory.

The Cosmic Harts and the Yew

Elhaz, meaning "elk," refers to the four cosmic harts that constantly bite at the needles of the World-Tree. The yew again enters the symbol complex through the Old Norse word ihwar, which occurs only in runic inscriptions. This term means either "yew tree" or "yew bow" and is later rendered by the word yr.

The Gothic word alhs (sanctuary) has also been related to this rune: a protected grove or enclosure dedicated to the gods. The Z-rune contains an aspect of the protective power of the divine twins. Alcis, the name of the twins reported by Tacitus, may indeed be related to the rune name. In their aggressive, warlike aspect, the twins are visualized as harts, while in other aspects they are represented as horses.

Bifröst and the Connection Between Gods and Humanity

Elhaz is the power of human life and spirit striving toward the world of the Aesir. It is the rune of the connection between gods and humanity, the force that draws the consciousness of mortals toward the realm of the gods.

The Z-rune is the three-colored bridge of shimmering light, Bifröst, the "Rainbow Bridge" of Norse mythology. This bridge connects Asgardhr, Midhgardhr, and Hel. It is another mode for the consciousness to traverse the worlds, the curved path of the branches and roots rather than the straight path of the trunk, a symbol of the magickal power of the hamingja.

The Z-rune is the force used by Heimdallr in his aspect as guardian of Asgardhr. Elhaz is a rune of consciousness and awareness (a hugrunin). It is over Bifröst that the rime-giants and fire-giants destroy the worlds of mortals and gods.

The stadha postures of the Z-rune are the traditional postures in which the Germanic peoples communicate with the gods. This shape was later employed as the M-rune in the Younger Futhark.

Rune Character

This rune is used for protection. It is also used in consecration and the banishing of negative energies. Excellent for the operator to wear when performing dangerous rituals as it protects against negative energies.

Stadha

I. Stand upright with arms stretched out upward and to the sides.

II. Keep arm position the same as above, but kneel, sitting on the heels. Keep torso vertical with the head slightly tilted back.

III. Same arm position with the right knee on the ground and the left foot out in front, thigh parallel to the ground.

Magickal Workings

  1. Protection and defense
  2. Mystical and religious communication with nonhuman sentient beings
  3. Communication with other worlds, especially Asgardhr and the cosmic wells of Urdhr, Mimir, and Hvergelmir
  4. Strengthening of hamingja (magickal power and luck) and life force
  5. Consecration and banishing of negative energies

Constructive Applications

Used for protection, consecration, and banishing negative energies. Excellent to wear when performing dangerous rituals as it shields against negative energies.
