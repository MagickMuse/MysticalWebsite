🔳Pigpen Cipher

Type
Magical Alphabet
Also called
Masonic Cipher; Freemason's Cipher

Overview

The oddly named pigpen cipher takes its name from the grid, resembling a pigpen, in which its letters are arranged; each letter is swapped for the symbol formed by the section of the pen it occupies. Also called the Masonic or Freemason's cipher, it was devised by Freemasons in the seventeenth century to keep their accounts and correspondence private.

To the uninitiated it can look like a simple graphic design. A fine example survives on the gravestone of one Thomas Brierley, buried in 1785, which carries other Masonic symbols as well; though the stone has weathered over the years, the script appears to read "Holiness of the Lord."
