Rune Grimoire

Tools, Attire & Timing

The "external tools" of rune magick symbolize internal forces. These are the traditional tools and techniques of runagaldr, which aid in the manipulation of the rune streams. For detailed entries on individual tools, see the Materials grimoire.

Attire

The magickal attire of the vitki roughly corresponds to the everyday dress of an early medieval Northman, with special symbolic features. The main advantage of liturgical vestments is the magickal effect of setting oneself apart from everyday life.

An ideal set of ritual attire includes:

  • A hooded cloak or frock of deep blue or black as the outermost piece
  • Bright red pants, a special sign of the vitki in ancient times
  • Black or natural-color heelless leather shoes (though many rites, especially outdoors, should be performed barefoot)
  • A pullover-type tunic of white, blue, or red worn under the cloak, fitting loosely and girdled by a belt of leather or deerskin
  • A sheath for the knife attached to the belt, and a pouch for other magickal instruments

Runes may be represented in the vitki's attire via a white headband embroidered with runes in bright red, and a bracteate engraved with the Futhark row and other magickal symbols (bronze, gold, or silver).

Generally, male and female vitkar dress very much alike; however, the female usually goes bare-legged or wears a long red skirt. Ritual nudity is also practiced according to the nature and aim of the rite. In all matters of magick, let intuition be the primary guide.

Magickal Time

The timing of runic rites is important but not as rigid or complicated as traditions more influenced by zodiacal astrology. The most important criteria are:

  1. Season
  2. Moon phase
  3. Solar position (time of day)

The most auspicious times are dawn, midday, evening, and midnight. For increase of power the waxing moon is desired, but for constriction of force its waning phase is used. The best general time for any undertaking is in the nights of the new moon or just after, or the full moon or just before.

Time and space are considered aspects of one another and both are measured by the mjötvidhr (the measuring tree, Yggdrasill).

Other Ritual Instruments

Several rites require further instruments beyond the primary tools. The cloth, leather thong, and other minor implements are introduced in their pertinent ritual sections.

All major tools needed for the performance of rune work should be portable and so well concealed that no one would even notice their presence. The equipment of the rune vitki is characterized by its mobility.
