Rune Grimoire

What is a Rune?

The word rune does not simply mean "letter." Across every ancient Germanic dialect, the term carries a primary meaning of "secret," "mystery," or sacred hidden knowledge. This concept parallels the use of arcana in the Tarot: each rune is first and foremost a holy idea that must be approached with reverence and worked with in concealment.

The modern reduction of runes to mere alphabetic characters strips away this foundational meaning. Understanding that a rune is a mysterium, not a letter, is the first step in genuine runic practice.

Etymology Across the Germanic Languages

The word rune is native to the Germanic language family and appears in every major ancient dialect:

DialectWordMeaning
Old NorserúnSecret, secret lore, wisdom, magickal signs, written characters
GothicrúnaSecret, mysterium (used by Wulfilas in his fourth-century Bible translation to render the Greek mysterion)
Old EnglishrúnMystery, secret council
Old SaxonrúnaMystery, secret
Old High GermanrúnaMystery, secret

The modern German cognate raunen means "to whisper." The word also appears in Old Irish (rún) and Middle Welsh (rhin), both carrying the same meaning of mysterium, likely borrowed from Germanic languages. The deepest root traces back to Proto-Indo-European \reu-*, meaning "to roar."

Letter Runes and Glyph Runes

Not all runes were used for writing. The runic tradition contains two distinct categories:

Letter runes received phonetic values and were organized into the Futhark system by the magickal guilds of the time. Their standardization followed specific numerological and conceptual criteria, not arbitrary convention.

Glyph runes remained in the purely ideographic realm. They carried meaning through symbol rather than sound, functioning as visual containers for magickal concepts.

Both categories are equally runic. The letter runes simply gained an additional function (phonetic representation) on top of their original ideographic and magickal nature. The symbol came first. The phonetic value came later. The sound never replaced the secret.

The Vitki

A vitki is a rune magickian. Where scholars see ancient writing symbols that bore religious or magickal significance, the vitki sees something deeper: complex hieroglyphs that encode processes of force and energy. Through investigation of both intellectual and magickal realms, the vitki recognizes runes as ideographs expressing the flow and state of power across self, planet, and multiverse.

The Threefold Nature of Runes

Every rune holds a threefold essence. These three aspects together constitute the full secret contained within each symbol:

  1. Form. The visual ideograph and its associated phonetic value. This is the rune as it appears when carved, drawn, or inscribed.
  2. Idea. The symbolic content the form represents. Each rune encodes a distinct world-concept, a specific pattern of meaning that exists independent of its physical shape.
  3. Number. The rune's position and dynamic relationship to other runes. This reveals how each rune interacts with and relates to the rest of the system.

Runes describe energy flows and states at every scale, from the personal to the planetary to the cosmic. Each one summarizes and graphically expresses a separate world-concept that can serve as a focal point for magickal and mystical operations, both individually and in combination.

The Origin of the Runic Forms

The mysteries encoded in the runes are timeless. They came into being alongside the emergence of the Nine Worlds from Ginnungagap, the magickally charged void. The runes are not products of creation but agents within it, preceding the arising of animate beings across the Worlds.

The physical shapes of the runes, however, can be placed in historical context. These forms originate from holy signs conceived by Bronze Age priests and magickians, and likely much earlier. They are abstract graphic expressions of the innermost content of religious and magickal teachings, found in abundance among the oldest rock carvings of Scandinavia. In this earliest period, these pre-runic signs functioned as pure ideographs, carrying meaning without representing sound.

From Ideograph to Phonetic Symbol

When Germanic peoples came into contact with Mediterranean cultures, the concept of representing language phonetically through symbols gradually entered the northern territories. Some scholars place this introduction in the second century BCE, when the Cimbri and Teutones invaded the Italian peninsula and encountered the North Etruscan and Latin alphabets. Others argue the runes were formulated by the Goths in the first and second centuries CE while they inhabited the Baltic coast.

When the Germanic peoples selected a cultic sign to represent a phonetic sound, they typically chose a shape that resembled the corresponding Etruscan, Latin, or Greek character. This resemblance played a significant role in the development of galdrar (number formulas) and their associations with specific runic forms.

Critically, the internal structure, ordering, naming, and symbolic content of the runes remain entirely unaffected by Mediterranean influence. A magickal deep-structure governs these elements. The priests and magickians of the Germanic cult knew this deep-structure intimately and transmitted the runic ideology across tribal boundaries through preexisting cultic channels. This ancient guild of runemasters maintained the system's internal integrity across countless tribal lines over several centuries. By the first century CE, Tacitus was already describing runes (which he called notae in Latin) being used in fully developed divinatory rites.

Odin and the Runic Initiation

Mythically, it is through Odin, the God of Magick, that gods and mortals receive rune wisdom. Odin was the first being to undergo full initiation into the runic mysteries, extracting the wisdom directly from its source and formulating it within his being so that it could be communicated to others. It is through the Odhinic force that the runes may be perceived most clearly.

This initiatory myth appears in the Elder (Poetic) Edda, in the song called the Hávamál ("the Sayings of the High One"). Stanzas 138 and 139 describe the ordeal:

I know, that I hung
on the windy tree
all of the nights nine,
wounded by spear
and given to Odin,
myself to myself,
on that tree,
which no man knows,
from what roots it rises.

They dealt me no bread
nor drinking horn,
I looked down,
I took up the runes,
I took them screaming,
I fell back from there.

This passage describes an initiatory process in which the initiate passes through the Nine Worlds of the World-Tree, descending to the realm of Hel (Death) and momentarily entering her sphere. In that instant, the entire body of rune wisdom is received and etched permanently into the initiate's being. The initiate then returns to Midgard with the knowledge encoded and ready for use and communication.

A Note on the Tarot and Runes

Some investigators have proposed that the runes are the origin of the Tarot's Major Arcana. Whether or not this is true, there may have been a point at which the two systems came into contact. The runes possess a documented past that reaches much further back than any existing documentation for the Tarot. Correspondences between the two systems do reveal interesting parallels, and each vitki is invited to explore these connections and draw their own conclusions.
