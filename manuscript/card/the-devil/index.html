When The Devil shows up in a reading, he recommends re-evaluating your relationship to people and material things that keep you "chained." It's time to let go of old fears, hang-ups, inhibitions, and ways you manipulate others to satisfy your needs. The Devil tells you to figure out what's true and what's false, at least for you.In a reading about money, this card can advise you to confront fears about financial security, social status, and material success. Are you too attached to money and material things? Are you taking advantage of others for personal gain? Are you "selling your soul to the devil" in order to profit?If the reading is about your work, The Devil may indicate you're chained to your job and neglecting other areas of life. Perhaps you're a workaholic or crave the status your position gives you. This card warns against engaging in questionable practices to get ahead.In a reading about love, The Devil asks, "Is an abusive, obsessive, or harmful relationship adversely affecting your life?" Sexual desire may cause you to overlook character flaws in a partner. Self-inflicted bondage is preventing you from moving ahead.
The Devil invites an honest look at what still feels chaining, whatever fears, habits, or manipulations are being used to get needs met. Sorting out what's actually true from what's simply comfortable is the real work here.Financially, this card can point to indulgence that's outpaced what actually supports a sense of security, or to profiting from something that doesn't sit right internally. It's worth asking whether money has become tangled up with self-worth.At work, ambition or a desire for status may be pulling focus away from the rest of life. Watch for situations that ask for compromise on integrity in exchange for getting ahead.In relationships, physical attraction or comfort can sometimes mask deeper concerns, whether an unhealthy dynamic, temptation to stray, or simply falling into routine. Any decision made here deserves an honest look at the potential fallout.Health related questions under this card often point to habits that have quietly become a lifestyle rather than an occasional indulgence, things like caffeine, alcohol, smoking, or painkillers that have grown difficult to go without. Vacations and treats are one thing, but when moderation disappears entirely there's cause for concern. Bringing a hidden habit into the light is often the first real step toward addressing it.

Archetypal Meaning

The Devil represents awareness of one's own negativity and darkness. It can also signal an encounter with negative energies.

The Fool's Journey: Trappings

Eventually the material world calls, and the traveler meets the Devil's stage: trappings. Seductive attachments to the material bind the traveler here, often without full awareness of the pull.

Imagery and Symbolism

The Devil card shows a winged figure bearing both male genitalia and a woman's breasts, with fingers and toes that end in claws and a strange headdress that includes a pair of antlers or horns. His right hand is raised while his left holds what looks like a sword that is all blade and has no hilt. He stands on a small plinth resembling an anvil, joined by a rope whose ends are knotted around the necks of a male figure to the right and a female figure to the left. These two are not what they first seem, for they wear antlered hats, have the pointed ears of animals, long tails, and cloven feet.

The Devil is an archetypal figure of evil of great antiquity, present long before the Christian Church demonized the old Gods and spirits. He often appeared as a destructive spirit carried on the disease-ridden and pestilential winds of the Mesopotamian deserts, and it made sense for our ancestors to personify such a natural force in order to feel they could control it. The familiar image of the Devil that still resonates is of an androgynous hybrid creature whose animal nature is at war with the spirit, which gives rise to the card's most basic reading: ill-gotten gains, surrender to the desires of the flesh, and corruption. At its simplest the card reminds us that humankind is shackled to the material world and can be corrupted, reverting to a raw animal state, by those very chains.

The Devil is also often depicted seated in a cavern of darkness, with a goat headed appearance drawn from mythological creatures and the various religious depictions of a "big bad." Despite appearances, neither chained figure is bound against their will, the chains loop loosely enough around their necks to be slipped off entirely. Their choice to remain tied to the material world, even while chasing after something that isn't serving them, is the card's real confrontation, an invitation to face whatever inner motivations keep a person tethered and to acknowledge the fears they'd rather avoid.

Astrology

The Devil is connected to the Earth sign Capricorn, ruled by Saturn, a pairing associated with restriction, discipline, and challenge. This astrological connection emphasizes themes of materialism, ambition, and the potential for obsession with power and control. Capricorn's influence highlights the struggle between pursuing material success and maintaining spiritual and emotional integrity, reflecting the shadow side of its tenacity and drive, where the pursuit of status and achievement can lead to ethical compromises or neglect of deeper personal connections. This card asks whether you'll remain chained by ambition and societal expectation, or find the courage to integrate your shadow and build a more lasting kind of success.
