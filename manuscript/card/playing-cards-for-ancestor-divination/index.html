Playing Cards for Ancestor Divination

Playing cards can be dedicated to communing with ancestors and used as a simple, powerful yes-or-no divination system. This page covers the three-card draw method, how to phrase questions well, how to use divination to assess offerings, and the key caveats for working responsibly with this system.

The Three-Card Draw

When asking a yes-or-no question, draw three cards. Red suits (Hearts and Diamonds) are read as positive omens. Black suits (Clubs and Spades) are read as negative omens.

ResultReading
🔴🔴⚫ Two red, one blackWeak yes. The outcome may be attained, but stay alert. Additional work may be needed.
⚫⚫⚫ Three blackStrong no. A cleansing to reset your energy field is recommended after this response.

Always end a session on a yes. If you receive a weak or strong no, continue asking questions about how to improve your circumstance through prayer, offerings, or practical action until you receive a yes.

This method works with any binary system beyond playing cards, including flipping a coin. Advanced readers may add nuance to the yes/no by further interpreting each card according to their full cartomantic system.

Phrasing Questions

The most important skill in structured yes/no divination is phrasing the question correctly:

  • The answer must be yes or no.
  • The "yes" must be the positive outcome.

Example: Asking "Will my boss fire me?" and pulling three black cards gives you a no, which sounds good, but the energy is overwhelmingly negative. Rephrase so the desired answer is yes: "Will I keep my job?"

The No Loop

Sometimes every question returns a no, no matter what is asked. This may indicate unseen forces at work. Consult a professional diviner. A useful short-circuit question: "Should I consult a professional diviner about this?"

Avoid "Should" Questions

Some diviners refuse questions that begin with "should" (e.g., "Should I quit my job?"). These questions often mask a desire to hand off responsibility for a decision, or a wish to know how to avoid pain. Pain is an inevitable part of life, and ancestors, who no longer experience pain or pleasure, are not well-positioned to advise on avoiding it.

Ask questions that help you make decisions with greater confidence, not questions that make decisions for you. Use divination when you are blocked, confused, or partially perceiving something and need confirmation or more detail. Divination is medicine.

Divining for Offerings

The three-card draw can also be used to ask ancestors whether they desire specific offerings.

Example question: "Would you like a slice of red velvet cake as an offering, in exchange for helping me attract good people to go out with?"

ResultReading
🔴🔴⚫ Two red, one blackWeak yes. The offering can be given, but other things would build better communion.
⚫⚫⚫ Three blackStrong no. The offering must not be given; the idea to ask may have come from the wrong motivations.

You are never forced to give ancestors what they request. But receiving a strong yes and ignoring it may feel discourteous. Start with simple, low-cost offerings and escalate gradually:

  1. More of what you've already given (more incense, another candle)
  2. Something you haven't given but already have on hand (food or liquor from the pantry)
  3. Something cheap and easy to acquire (flowers from a nearby shop)
  4. Something moderately expensive or harder to find (a specific liquor; a party in their honor)
  5. Something extra (a tool, a statue, a pilgrimage)

Caveats

1. Know Who You're Talking To

Before relying on this system, be confident you are actually talking to your ancestors and not another spirit or random noise. This confidence grows over time. Once you have kept an ancestor altar for a while, notice the energy it carries: how your body feels, what genre of thoughts arise. Check whether that same energy is present during divination, or whether something feels different.

2. Don't Ask Dumb Questions

Don't ask questions you already know the answer to. Don't ask about inconsequential things. If you are agnostic or skeptical about divination, that may be a sign to wait. Don't ask questions as tests. It erodes trust on both sides of the relationship.

3. Don't Let Divination Run Your Life

You are the ancestor who is alive right now. You hold the responsibility and privilege to make decisions. Divination is a tool to help you make them better, not a mechanism for avoiding them.

Dedicating a Deck

To establish a stronger bond, consider dedicating a specific deck of cards solely to ancestral communication rather than using a general-purpose deck. One traditional method: cleanse a new deck with benzoin at the new moon, then wrap it with a piece of black tourmaline in silk. Keep it on your ancestor altar.
