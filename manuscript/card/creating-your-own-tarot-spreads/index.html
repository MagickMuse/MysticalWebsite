Creating Your Own Tarot Spreads

Designing your own tarot spreads gives you clear, focused readings tailored to whatever question or situation is at hand, rather than trying to force a borrowed spread to fit. An existing spread, including any of the ones already documented here, can also be adapted to better suit a specific circumstance if starting from a foundation feels more comfortable than building from scratch. Some readers love creating original spreads, while others are perfectly happy relying on the thousands already available.

When designing an original spread, a few key factors keep it practical, meaningful, and aligned with its purpose.

Be Clear and Intentional

Define the spread's purpose before starting to design it. A spread can be built around something very specific or something broad. Getting clear on the question or issue being explored, and the outcome the reading is meant to support, comes first.

The Number of Cards

A three card spread can carry just as much power as a fifteen card one. Too many cards too quickly can become overwhelming, so experimenting with different counts and positions helps find the right balance. A complex issue may call for more cards to explore different angles, while a simpler question often only needs two or three.

Relevant Position Assignment

Each position should carry a meaning tied directly to the question being explored, so the positions work together toward a clear answer. Like a well told story, a spread benefits from a satisfying, connected structure from start to finish, giving the querent both an answer and something empowering to act on. A spread that only surfaces challenges without also reflecting strengths and blessings misses a chance to bring balance to the reading. When assigning meaning to each position, it helps to ask whether the spread itself would feel worth using, then refine from there.

Test Run Your Spreads

Once a first draft exists, put it to use. Shuffle the deck and see how the spread performs in practice, staying open to adjustments if certain positions fall flat or fail to produce useful insight. Refining a spread through testing is part of building real experience as a reader, and preferences around style will keep evolving with practice.

Example: Creating a Spiritual Growth Spread

Here is one way a spread focused on spiritual growth might come together:

  1. Intention: Understand the current state of one's spiritual self and identify areas for further development.
  2. Number of cards: Five cards, one for each growth area: strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, challenges, and potential outcomes.
  3. Positions and assigned meanings:
    • Card 1, Strengths: the spiritual strengths currently present.
    • Card 2, Weaknesses: the weaknesses that may be slowing spiritual growth.
    • Card 3, Opportunities: the opportunities available for spiritual development.
    • Card 4, Challenges: the challenges that need to be overcome to grow.
    • Card 5, Potential Outcome: the most likely outcome if this reading is put into practice.
  4. Layout: The cards could be arranged in a star pattern, with strengths at the top, weaknesses and opportunities on either side, challenges at the bottom, and the potential outcome in the center.
  5. Testing: Use the spread for an actual reading, reflect on how each position resonates with the current situation, and adjust positions or meanings as needed.
