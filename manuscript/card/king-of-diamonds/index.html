Court Cards

Court cards, also called face cards, depict human faces. Though influenced by elemental associations like the rest of the deck, court cards almost always represent people who can influence the querent directly through their actions and decisions. The elemental influence of the suit shapes the personality and energy of the person the card represents.

Kings often represent mature people fulfilling the warrior, protector, or provider archetype, regardless of gender. For younger querents, Kings indicate older, more experienced individuals. For querents around forty or older, a King can represent any peer of similar age, since by that point most people have settled into established life patterns. The concept of maturity is relative to the querent's perspective.

While Kings tend to indicate those embodying traditionally masculine roles, a King in a reading can refer to anyone serving as the warrior, protector, or provider in the querent's life.

Further distinctions are a matter of perspective and vary from one reading to another. It is up to the reader to determine how a court card should be interpreted based on who they are and who they are reading for.

In Readings

The King of Diamonds indicates an adult, often someone in a position of power due to wealth, reputation, or both. This person carries a dignified demeanor.

Like all Diamond court cards, this person has a hidden or secret agenda. Interpretation requires care and context from surrounding cards. The secret could be something the querent needs to worry about, something the querent is already in on, or even something involving the querent directly.

If no one with a secret comes to mind, the card advises watching for a congenial, dignified person who has a plan or agenda that may conflict with the querent's goals. Let intuition guide the interpretation whenever any of the Diamond court cards appear in a reading.
