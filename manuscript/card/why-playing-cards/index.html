Card Grimoire

Why Playing Cards?

Playing cards are among the most accessible and historically grounded tools available for divination and spellwork. Long before mass-produced tarot decks lined bookstore shelves, everyday people reached for what they had on hand. European peasants practiced magick with kitchen knives, local herbs, and still water, not ritual swords or exotic imports. A deck of cards fit that same tradition perfectly. Even before modern manufacturing, playing cards were easier to obtain than any tarot deck, and today they can be found at gas stations, grocery stores, and dollar shops.

A Legitimate Magickal Tradition

Playing cards carry a history as deep as the tarot. Their four suits, Hearts, Clubs, Diamonds, and Spades, encode elemental symbolism that has been used in sortilege (the drawing of lots) for centuries. Cartomancy with playing cards can be traced to at least the 1540s, predating most of the illustrated tarot decks popular today.

Playing cards are as valid a magickal tool as any other modern convenience adopted into practice. A witch who brews potions on an electric range rather than a cauldron over an open fire applies the same principle when picking up a deck of cards for divination.

The Power of Imageless Divination

One of the greatest strengths of playing cards is what they lack: prescribed imagery. Modern tarot decks feature detailed illustrations on every card, guiding (and sometimes limiting) interpretation. The Ten of Swords in the Rider-Waite-Smith deck shows a figure impaled by blades, which leaves little room for nuance. The Ten of Spades, by contrast, is simply ten spade pips. The reader must rely on intuition, psychic faculty, and personal experience to draw meaning.

This makes playing card divination closer in spirit to fire gazing (pyromancy) or crystal gazing (crystallomancy) than to illustrated tarot. The meanings are more open-ended, and interpretations can be deeply personal. A reader might find that the Three of Hearts carries a significance never mentioned in any book, one that shifts and evolves as the reader's own life changes.

A Language of Their Own

Every divination system speaks in its own dialect. Runes address different dimensions of a question than ogham symbols, and both differ from tarot. Playing cards have their own distinct voice. Many readers find that playing cards tend to speak about external events and the people surrounding the querent, while runes and tarot lean more toward internal, personal transformation.

Playing cards are a parallel system to tarot with their own strengths, history, and magickal depth.
