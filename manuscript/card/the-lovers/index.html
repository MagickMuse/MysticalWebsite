Archetypal Meaning

The Lovers speak to the awareness of opposites, the relationship between opposites, and the ability to balance what appear to be different aspects of the self.

The Fool's Journey: Relationships

Growing less self-centered and craving balance, the traveler reaches the Lovers' stage: relationships. Union with another becomes possible here, along with the first real lessons about choice, since decisions now visibly affect other people.

Imagery and Symbolism

Esoteric Imagery

Often misread as a plural scene, the card in fact shows a single lover, a man who must choose between two women, which makes it a card of choice that echoes the forked path of the Y of Pythagoras. For the first time in the sequence the central figure appears as an ordinary human being, with no mystical or magickal attributes, facing a wholly human dilemma. Above his head, unseen by him, hovers the winged figure of Cupid with bow and arrow poised, a sign that the choice may be shaped by outside forces beyond the man's control, the powers of destiny at work.

The two women represent very different aspects of the feminine. One wears a headdress and seems more dignified, touching his shoulder to suggest a more spiritual bond, while the other, a loose-haired temptress, lets her hand hover above his heart. The lover is drawn between reason and intellect, the head, and the passions of sexuality, the heart, and his head turns toward the woman on his right while the rest of his body leans to the left. Six is the number of sexual union, shown perfectly in the six-pointed star as the Shatkona, the union of opposites. The young man may need to reconcile both sides of the Divine Feminine within himself to become a fully rounded individual.

Classic Imagery and Body Language

In many representations of the Lovers, a couple stands amid an idyllic scene, looked over by an angel in the sky. Sunlight pours from behind this being as if blessing the union. In many decks, the couple is naked, representing that they have nothing to hide from each other. They're out in nature, a call to our connection to the natural world. Often, there is a snake, apple, and tree in the card, linking older versions of this card to classic Christian iconography and the book of Genesis.

Astrology

The Lovers is connected to the Air sign Gemini, ruled by Mercury. Gemini's intellectual curiosity, duality, and gift for communication mirror this card's themes of connection and choice, while Mercury's influence underscores the importance of clear, open expression in every partnership.

Major Arcana Name Card

If The Lovers is your Major Arcana Name Card: You're deeply attuned to relationships and choices, often finding meaning in connections with others. This card suggests you value harmony and alignment between your actions and your heart. You may face decisions that test your commitment to your values.
