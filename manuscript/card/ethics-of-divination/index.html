Card Grimoire

Ethics of Divination

When interpreting cards for another person, the reader looks beyond the ordinary exterior that person presents to the world. There is an intimacy to divination. For this reason, it is essential to set boundaries, both for the reader's protection and for the querent's. Readers who ignore the ethics of divination are likely to make enemies and develop bad reputations.

Personal Ethics and Boundaries

Tarot readers work without an international governing body or a shared code of ethics. There's no oath comparable to the Hippocratic Oath binding readers to a universal standard, so shaping a personal ethical framework is left to each individual. While no reader can control what a querent does with a reading afterward, understanding the weight that words carry within a reading remains a real responsibility.

Not every reader chooses to cover the same ground. Some feel comfortable answering any question a querent brings, believing it's their role to read whatever comes up. Others draw lines around specific topics, such as declining to speak to a medical diagnosis and instead referring the querent to a doctor while still exploring how they can build a healthier relationship with their body. Keeping a referral list of trusted professionals, lawyers, doctors, therapists, financial advisors, can help when a querent's needs fall outside what a reading can responsibly offer.

Suspected affairs are one of the more delicate situations a reader will eventually face. Consider in advance how a wrong read on that kind of question could affect a querent's relationship, and how you'd feel if it did.

Third-party readings, where a querent asks about someone who isn't present, such as an ex's current activities or a new partner's pregnancy, raise their own questions worth thinking through ahead of time.

Reading for children brings its own considerations. Most parents ask out of love and a genuine wish to know their child is okay, but it's worth deciding in advance how you feel about reading for an older teenager on their own, and whether you'd want a parent present for that kind of session.

These boundaries aren't fixed forever. As a reader's knowledge and experience grow, their sense of what they will and won't do often shifts too. As long as a reader stays aligned with their own inner compass and feels good about how they show up in a reading, that's the real measure of ethical practice.

Consent

Never read for anyone without their consent. Reading for someone is not the same as reading about someone. Even in solo readings, other people will appear in the cards. If the Five of Hearts and Seven of Spades suggest a new romance, someone else is obviously involved. In a twelve-card layout, at least a third of the cards almost always represent other people.

Drawing cards for absent people as a practice exercise is acceptable, but the reader is ethically bound not to share anything seen in those cards with others. The reading exists solely for skill development.

Likewise, if a personal reading reveals something about another person, that information should not be disclosed. If the cards suggest a partner is considering leaving, the reader can pay closer attention or have a conversation about the relationship, but cannot cite a card reading as the source of suspicion. People who understand divination know that a reader might pick up on things, but nobody wants to feel surveilled. Furthermore, not everyone wants to know what the future holds. Some find it uncomfortable, and others object for personal or spiritual reasons.

The Power of Words

Words carry weight. When giving a reading, the reader must avoid manipulating the querent in any way. Everyone has a personal agenda, things and outcomes they prefer over others. The ancient Delphic teaching to "know thyself" is crucial here. An ethical reader is mindful of their own biases and takes care not to let them color the reading. The goal is to help the querent, never to steer them.

Using a reading to pursue personal interests, such as implying a romantic connection with a querent, is a severe breach of ethics that accomplishes nothing beyond damaging the reader's reputation.

Influences, Not Destiny

There is a world of difference between saying "your marriage is doomed" and saying "you may be facing some marital challenges in the near future." The first implies an unalterable fate, a futile existence without free will. The second simply suggests possible influences ahead.

Speaking in absolutes can become a self-fulfilling prophecy. Telling someone their marriage will fail does not help them. It may actively contribute to the failure. But overly optimistic absolutes are equally dangerous. Telling someone they will receive a promotion might lead them to stop putting in the effort required to earn it. There is a world of difference between "you are going to get a promotion" and "I see some very positive influences concerning your career."

The future is constantly unfolding from the present, shaped by actions and decisions. What divination reveals are probabilities, not certainties. An ethical reader always ensures the querent understands they have control over their own destiny.

Handling Difficult Readings

Sometimes the cards show what appears to be a crisis. In a twelve-card reading, the Four of Clubs in position 2 (resources) could indicate the querent is oblivious to what is happening around them. The Six of Spades in position 7 (partner) might show depleted energy in the relationship. The Queen of Diamonds in position 8 (partner's resources) could suggest another person with an agenda has a significant presence in the partner's life.

Superficially, this looks like infidelity. But the ethical reader does not blurt out that conclusion. First, there is the inevitable margin of error; the interpretation could be wrong. Second, expressing it bluntly could become a self-fulfilling prophecy, leading the querent to take actions that destroy a salvageable relationship. Third, the querent has the right to shape their own destiny.

Instead, suggest that the querent be mindful of the relationship and perhaps talk with their partner about how to strengthen it. Frame the other person's presence as something the querent can address. Express what the cards show as influences rather than fate, empowering the querent to take action rather than feel helpless.

It is equally important not to lie. If the cards clearly show something difficult, pretending everything is fine betrays the querent's trust. Honest framing, expressing challenges as influences that can be navigated, respects both the truth and the querent's autonomy.

The Margin of Error

Divination is not infallible. Sometimes predictions are completely wrong. Maybe the cards were interpreted incorrectly, or circumstances shifted. This margin of error is actually a good thing. It confirms that the future is not set in stone. If a reading shows challenges to a marriage, the querent can take steps to overcome them. A failed relationship is not a foregone conclusion.

If there were no influence over destiny, divination would be pure voyeurism. The entire point of reading cards is to illuminate the path so the querent can navigate it with greater awareness.

When to Decline a Reading

There is never an obligation to read for someone. Reasons to decline include:

  • The person is a skeptic who views divination as a joke or intends to disprove the reader's ability
  • Something about the person creates an uncomfortable feeling, even if it cannot be identified
  • The cards produce a result the reader does not feel equipped to share responsibly

In any of these cases, lay out the cards and then explain that no impressions are coming through. This exits the situation gracefully. If the person has paid for a reading (such as at a charity benefit), return the payment when declining.

If choosing to read for a skeptic, follow the same ethical guidelines as with any other querent.

Never Offer to Change the Future

As a card reader, the role is that of an observer. Never offer to help the querent alter their future through magickal means. This applies to querents who are not close friends or family. Anti-divination laws were originally enacted because of individuals who posed as readers and then "helped" their clients avoid misfortune, a scheme that inevitably involved relieving the client of a substantial sum of money. Offering spellwork to querents risks association with that kind of activity, regardless of intent.

Exercise Eight: Your Tarot Reading Ethics

These scenarios work as case studies for examining personal ethical standpoints on tarot. Each one presents a hypothetical situation or question posed by a pretend querent during a reading. Consider each question and reflect on how it would be handled.

Scenario One

Querent question: "My boyfriend recently broke up with me. I want to know what my current ex is doing and if he's with anyone else."

Scenario Two

Querent question: "I just interviewed for my dream job, and I know it's competitive. I want to know how I can get this job."

Scenario Three

A client arrives for a reading and brings her sister along. The sister isn't receiving a reading of her own, she simply wants to listen in. The client seems reserved, though, giving the impression that she may not want her sister present. How would this situation be handled?

Scenario Four

A querent explains that they've lost a family heirloom somewhere in their apartment and asks whether the tarot cards can help locate the missing item. What would the response be?

Scenario Five

Querent question: "Can the tarot cards tell me the winning lottery numbers?"

Scenario Six

Partway through a reading, a querent turns out to be seventeen years old. Would the reading continue? What minimum age requirement would apply going forward?

Scenario Seven

Querent question: "Am I going to get pregnant this year?"
