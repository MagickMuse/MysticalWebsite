Card Grimoire

Keeping a Tarot Journal

A tarot journal tracks how a reader's relationship with the cards and their deck changes over time. Because a single card can reveal new layers of meaning between one reading and the next, keeping a written record surfaces patterns and recurring lessons that would otherwise go unnoticed.

Why Keep One

Describing a card's artwork and the feelings it brings up trains intuition and builds familiarity with the deck. A journal also creates a reference to revisit later, so a reader can measure how accurate a reading turned out to be and notice where a question could have been phrased more clearly. Both newcomers and experienced professionals benefit from this practice, since writing through symbols and possible meanings reinforces what has been learned.

Beyond formal readings, a journal is also a place to note intuitive impressions tied to the tarot, such as thoughts that surface during meditation with the cards, dream imagery connected to a specific card, or an unrelated moment that suddenly calls a card to mind.

Common Obstacles

Reluctance to begin usually traces back to a handful of concerns: disliking one's own handwriting, feeling short on time, or not wanting to mark up an appealing notebook. Each has a workaround. Drawing or typing can stand in for handwriting, short recordings can stand in for written entries, and any tool eventually earns its keep simply by being used. A practice picked up in bursts, set aside for weeks, then returned to, still counts as progress. Consistency matters less than continuing to show up for it.

Choosing a Format

There is no single correct format, only one that gets used: a fresh notebook, a well worn binder already on hand, or a digital option such as a blog or voice log. Above all, a tarot journal is a private space intended purely to support a reader's own exploration and growth, with no expectation that anyone else will ever see it.
