Archetypal Meaning

The World represents a celebration of the dance of life and a time of completion of a major cycle. The World card contains every preceding card, just as a soul is the sum of every step taken along the path. It is a time of fulfillment and joy.

The Fool's Journey: Completion

Walking through the World's stage as an integrated being brings wholeness and fulfillment: completion. Meaning found along the way is now ready to be shared with others, and the traveler stands ready to begin the journey again, returning to the Fool archetype.

Imagery and Symbolism

This is the last of the Major Arcana, and the number itself marks a coming of age, the moment when the world is handed over symbolically, as a key once was, on reaching one's twenty-first birthday. The card shows a young woman whose sex is concealed by an artfully placed scarf, her legs forming the figure four in common with the Hanged Man's posture, looking to the left with a wand in each hand. She is enclosed in a laurel wreath shaped like the vesica piscis, and around her are the four Tetramorphs, winged human and hybrid figures that rule the elements and the four corners of the Earth. Working clockwise from the upper left they are the man, the eagle, the lion, and the ox.

This is the card of victory and rebirth. The vesica piscis from which the girl emerges is a magickal doorway between the spiritual world and the material one, and here the wreath seems made of two halves joined at top and bottom, so that it also represents the egg, itself a symbol of creation. The laurel leaves stand for victory, awarded to great heroes, and the Tetramorphs both witness that victory and signify the four corners of the world that now belong to the girl. The two wands imply a perfect balance of opposites, conscious and unconscious, matter and spirit, the harmony of the Universe.

In some decks, this card instead shows a dancing figure encircled by a wreath, its shape suggesting a yoni, with the dancer looking outward in a gesture of victory, success, and positivity. Two red ribbons form infinity symbols at the top and bottom of the wreath, representing life force and success flowing both above and below the figure. The four fixed zodiac signs in the corners appear more fully formed here than in the Wheel of Fortune, having integrated the knowledge gained since that earlier card, and the central figure embodies the balance of opposites needed to achieve what they desire.

Astrology

The planet Saturn, with its themes of achievement, responsibility, and the culmination of long-term efforts, is connected to the World, along with the grounding Earth element. Often considered the taskmaster of the zodiac, Saturn brings structure and discipline, mirroring the World's message of concluding cycles with wisdom and maturity. The card encapsulates a moment of cosmic alignment and harmony, reflecting the Saturnian rewards that result from perseverance and patience. While there is success to be had and well-earned moments of synergy, the World is also a reminder that when one cycle ends, another begins.
