Tarot-Numerology

Tarot-numerology combines two divinatory systems to provide quick guidance on decisions, timing, and life situations. By calculating the numerical value of a date, name, or situation, you connect that number to its corresponding Major Arcana card for insight.

Numerology assigns meanings to numbers that offer insight into your life, relationships, and the lives of the people you'll read tarot for. It commonly draws on the numbers in a birth date to calculate a Life Path or destiny number, and those same birth-date numbers can build out a full numerology chart, much like an astrology chart, offering insight into character, likely future events, and decision-making. The same techniques used for dates can also reveal the numerical vibration of a name, both yours and the names of people in your life.

Exact techniques, methods, and interpretations vary by numerology school, and any school can be paired with tarot to enrich your understanding of both the cards and the numbers themselves. Two schools are most commonly used alongside a deck: the Pythagorean and the Kabbalistic.

The Pythagorean school, also called Western numerology, traces back to the teachings of the Greek mathematician and philosopher Pythagoras. It centers on the numbers 1 through 9, applying a numerical value to each letter of the alphabet, along with the master numbers 11, 22, and 33, considered numbers of particular power and influence when present in a chart.

The Kabbalistic school is rooted in Jewish mysticism and the Kabbalah, with techniques and interpretations built on the Hebrew alphabet. Each letter carries a numerical value, and the emphasis falls more heavily on spiritual and mystical interpretation, deepening the connection between personal development, the divine, and the cosmic archetypes of the tarot.

This page uses the Pythagorean school throughout, since it's a popular and accessible starting point for weaving tarot and numerology together.

The Method

Calculate a number from a date, name, or relevant situation. Reduce multi-digit results to a single digit (except master numbers 11 and 22). Find the corresponding Major Arcana card, then interpret using both the number's meaning and the card's symbolism.

This system traditionally uses the Tarot of Marseilles, which places Justice at VIII and Strength (Force) at XI. Modern decks swap these positions. Either ordering works, but remain consistent with your chosen deck.

Calculating a Name

  1. Find the number for each letter
  2. Add all numbers together
  3. Reduce to a single digit (unless you reach 11 or 22)

Example: ROSE = 9 + 6 + 1 + 5 = 21 → 2 + 1 = 3 (The Empress)

Calculating a Date

Add all digits of the date. You can calculate two ways:

  • Month + day only gives one card
  • Month + day + year gives another card

Both calculations offer valid insight. The first focuses on the day's energy; the second incorporates the year's influence.

Example: January 15, 2026

  • Without year: 1 + 1 + 5 = 7 → Card VII (The Chariot)
  • With year: 1 + 1 + 5 + 2 + 0 + 2 + 6 = 17 → 1 + 7 = 8 → Card VIII (Justice)

Number Meanings

Each number carries positive and negative aspects. Positive suggests alignment with that energy; negative indicates imbalance or warning.

Positive: Creation, self-assertion, independence, new cycle beginning

Negative: Laziness, imitation, dependence, selfishness, unhelpful new beginnings

Weaving Numerology into Your Tarot Readings

Repeating numbers or sequences within a spread can highlight specific themes or challenges. A spread dominated by cards numbered two, for example, may draw attention to relationships, partnerships, balance, and the reciprocation of energy. A rising sequence, such as the Three, Five, and Nine of Cups, signals a progression of experience and energy: if your querent is working toward a goal, this kind of build tells you their momentum is likely to continue. A cascading, or descending, sequence can instead point to regression, an unwillingness to face the future or current challenges, or something unresolved from the past that needs attention before progress becomes possible.

For readers who enjoy some on-the-spot arithmetic, try reducing the sum of every number in a reading down to a single digit to find a core number representing the reading's overall essence or theme. It can take a little time, or a calculator for larger spreads, but it's a fun way to see the main energetic thread running through a reading.

The balance of even and odd numbers across a layout is also worth a look, since it tends to set the reading's general tone. A spread weighted toward odd numbers tends to bring energy of initiative, action, and movement, while one weighted toward even numbers brings energy of balance, patience, and divine timing.

Numerology skills built this way carry over across tarot systems, so if you eventually explore the Thoth or Marseille decks, the numerical foundation you've already built will transfer directly. As your reading practice grows, so will your grasp of numerology, each reading revealing new nuances and each number taking on richer meaning over time.

Practical Applications

When to Use This Method

  • Before making important proposals or decisions
  • Considering education or career changes
  • Moving to a new home
  • Starting a new job or project
  • Major purchases or financial decisions
  • Community involvement decisions
  • Any situation requiring quick guidance

Reading the Results

  1. Note whether you land on a single digit, double digit, or master number
  2. Consult both the number meaning and the corresponding Major Arcana card
  3. Consider whether the positive or negative aspects apply to your situation
  4. If you calculated a date two ways, compare both cards for a fuller picture

The method offers quick insight without requiring a full tarot spread. Use it alongside traditional readings or as a standalone decision-making tool.

The Power of Numerology in the Minor Arcana

Numerology is vital to understanding the minor arcana. Each number from ace through ten carries a distinct vibration and message that weaves through the four suits. While the suits add their unique elemental essence to these numbers, the numerological core remains consistent. The suits show us how the core of the number is being expressed in a card, and thus experienced in your life or your querent's life. Below you'll find overviews of how numerology manifests through the minor arcana suits.

Aces

Represent beginnings and potential. They are the seed from which all growth emerges.

  • Ace of Wands: The spark of inspiration and creativity. It embodies the fire of ambition and the courage to start something new.
  • Ace of Cups: Symbolizes emotional renewal and love. It speaks of the potential for deep connection and spiritual awakening.
  • Ace of Swords: Cuts through confusion, offering clarity and truth. It signals a mental breakthrough or the beginning of an intellectual pursuit.
  • Ace of Pentacles: Represents tangible opportunities, such as financial or career prospects. It is the seed of material growth and prosperity.

Twos

Express an energy of duality and balance. It's reciprocation, exchange, and connection. Twos also signify choices and partnerships.

  • Two of Wands: Highlights planning and decision-making. It's about envisioning the future and weighing options.
  • Two of Cups: The card of partnerships, reflecting mutual respect and harmony in relationships.
  • Two of Swords: Speaks of indecision and stalemates. It reflects the challenge of balancing opposing thoughts, ideas, or mental states.
  • Two of Pentacles: Represents juggling priorities and adapting to change while maintaining equilibrium.

Threes

Represent growth and collaboration; when you add something to the balanced twos, there is an opportunity to expand. Three is also the number of creativity.

  • Three of Wands: The card of expansion and foresight, symbolizing the results of past planning and the anticipation of the future.
  • Three of Cups: Celebrates community and joy, reflecting emotional growth through shared experiences and being around like-hearted people.
  • Three of Swords: Conveys heartbreak and lessons learned through pain, a mental growth process that shapes resilience.
  • Three of Pentacles: Represents teamwork and mastery, where collaboration leads to great progress and recognition.

Fours

Bring the energy of stability and structure. It's the number of form and order, a safe place to pause.

  • Four of Wands: Celebrates stability in creativity and relationships, symbolized by milestones like weddings and achievements.
  • Four of Cups: Reflects emotional stagnation, a time of reassessment to rebuild emotional stability.
  • Four of Swords: Signals rest and recovery, emphasizing the need for mental peace and rejuvenation.
  • Four of Pentacles: Shows financial stability and security but warns against hoarding or fear of loss.

Fives

Represent conflict, challenge, and change as well as the energy of instability. It's the number that reminds us that disruption is necessary for growth.

  • Five of Wands: Represents competition, conflict, and the need to test your skills. It also emphasizes the need to find creative ways of solving problems.
  • Five of Cups: Reflects sorrow, loss, emotional hardship, and instability in the process of grieving.
  • Five of Swords: Signals conflict, often with ethical dilemmas or hollow victories.
  • Five of Pentacles: Shows financial or material challenges and a need to change sources of support.

Sixes

Represent harmony and restoration and the rejuvenation of energy after upheaval. These cards are also connected to communities.

  • Six of Wands: Celebrates victory and public recognition, symbolizing restored confidence.
  • Six of Cups: Reflects nostalgia and emotional healing, reconnecting with joy and innocence.
  • Six of Swords: Represents a journey toward mental clarity and peace, leaving turmoil behind.
  • Six of Pentacles: Signifies generosity and balance in giving and receiving, fostering material harmony.

Sevens

Signal growth, especially spiritual and personal. It's the number of introspection and overcoming challenges.

  • Seven of Wands: Speaks of defending one's position and overcoming external challenges.
  • Seven of Cups: Highlights imagination and choice, urging clarity amidst illusion.
  • Seven of Swords: Symbolizes strategy and cunning but warns of dishonesty.
  • Seven of Pentacles: Reflects patience and long-term planning, reviewing progress with an eye on future rewards.

Eights

Represent movement, momentum, and power. This is the number of mastery and application of energy.

  • Eight of Wands: Represents swift action and forward momentum, a burst of creative energy.
  • Eight of Cups: Signals emotional growth, often through leaving behind what no longer serves.
  • Eight of Swords: Reflects mental entrapment and the need for self-empowerment to break free.
  • Eight of Pentacles: Represents skill development and hard work, mastery through consistent effort.

Nines

Indicate attainment and fulfillment. This number expresses maturity and the energy of nearing completion of tasks or cycles.

  • Nine of Wands: Reflects resilience and the final push toward achievement despite challenges.
  • Nine of Cups: Symbolizes emotional satisfaction and personal happiness.
  • Nine of Swords: Reflects anxiety and mental anguish, highlighting the compounding energy being felt full force.
  • Nine of Pentacles: Represents self-sufficiency and material comfort, the rewards gained for hard work.

Tens

Represent completion, the end of a cycle, and the destination of a journey or cycle. It's the number of wholeness in the minor arcana, both positive and negative.

  • Ten of Wands: Symbolizes burdens and responsibility and work that needs completion requiring great effort.
  • Ten of Cups: Represents emotional fulfillment and harmony, the ultimate realization of love and connection.
  • Ten of Swords: Marks an ending that's often painful but necessary for new beginnings.
  • Ten of Pentacles: Reflects legacy and material abundance, the culmination of generational effort and success.
