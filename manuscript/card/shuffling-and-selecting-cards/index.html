Card Grimoire

Shuffling and Selecting Cards

Many people worry about shuffling incorrectly, but the message comes through regardless of technique or polish. Even experienced readers occasionally send cards flying across the room. Practice builds confidence over time, and the methods below offer a starting point.

Shuffling Techniques

The riffle shuffle. Split the deck in half, angle the corners toward each other, and let the cards fall together in an interlocking pattern. This method can wear cards down over time, so it suits sturdier decks better than a treasured one meant to stay pristine. It works especially well with oversized, square, or round decks, and can look impressive once mastered.

Over and under shuffling. Hold the deck in the nondominant hand and transfer cards from back to front, or the reverse, with the dominant hand. This familiar method suits standard sized decks well.

The washing machine technique. Spread the cards face down across a table and mix them by hand. This relaxed approach works well for smaller hands or unusually shaped decks and doubles as a way to infuse personal energy into the cards.

The piles technique. Divide the deck into a random number of small piles, then gather them back together. More piles mean a more thorough shuffle, making this method helpful for anyone still building confidence in their shuffling.

Shuffling machines. For anyone who struggles to shuffle by hand, such as due to arthritis, a shuffling machine offers a practical alternative. Most machines on the market are built for playing card sized decks, though tarot decks in that size are available.

Selecting Cards for a Reading

Once the deck is shuffled, the next step is choosing which cards to use. As with shuffling, there is no single correct approach. Some readers prefer not to let others touch their cards, while others invite the querent to select their own. Remote readings require the reader to lay the cards out alone. Whatever the arrangement, the right cards tend to find their way into the reading regardless of who selects them. The number of cards drawn depends on the spread chosen or the reader's intuition, decided before the shuffle begins.

Fan, scan, and pick. After shuffling, fan the cards out for the querent. In person, the querent can run a palm across the fan and pull whichever cards call to them, either by feel or by sight. In a remote reading, the reader can run a palm across the cards while the querent calls out when to stop, and that becomes the chosen card. This process repeats once for each position in the spread, with the cards laid out in the order selected.

Pick a pile. After shuffling, cut the deck into piles, most commonly three, and ask the querent which pile draws their attention. The reading is then laid out from that pile. If the reader is comfortable with their deck being handled, the querent can shuffle and divide the piles directly.

Selecting by image. This method skips shuffling entirely. The querent views the card images and chooses based on directed prompts, which works particularly well for counseling or healing focused sessions where resonance matters more than randomness.

Roll the dice. After shuffling and setting an intention, roll a die and count through the deck to the corresponding card. Dice with more than six sides, such as a twelve sided or twenty sided die, work best for this playful, chance driven method.

Shuffling and selecting cards is ultimately a personal art. Whether following these methods exactly or adapting them, the intention behind each action matters more than the technique itself.
