🔹Diamond

The suit of Diamonds reflects the element of Earth. The keyword for this suit is fortune. Gemstones and precious metals are physical manifestations of this cool, dry element and the concept of fortune.

Throughout most of history, diamonds were valuable largely because they were so difficult to find. This changed in the 1800s when extensive diamond mines were discovered in South Africa, though diamonds remain widely regarded as symbols of wealth and luxury. Diamond is also one of the hardest known substances, which further contributes to its perceived value. In the first decks of European playing cards, Diamonds were represented as Coins, a symbol still used in some tarot decks.

The specific symbol matters less than what it represents. Different cultures measured prosperity through different means. In northern Europe, wealth was more often counted in cattle and land than in stones or metal disks. The suit of Diamonds represents fortune in the broadest sense, not necessarily money as any single culture defines it.

In Readings

Diamond cards point to material and substantial matters. Wealth is the most common association, though this could refer to another person's wealth or to a loss of it. The specific message depends on which card appears and which cards surround it. Diamonds can also address reputation and social standing, as these both influence and are influenced by material fortune.

The Diamond suit often relates to career and business interests. A Diamond card might describe work conditions, the general economy, stocks, or investments.

Fortune extends beyond money. Diamonds can indicate success, achievement, recognition of effort, or the attainment of a goal. Anything that affects personal fortune in the broadest sense falls under this suit. Diamonds frequently point to luck and destiny.

Diamonds can also refer to physical condition, since a healthy body is certainly a fortunate asset.

Court Cards

Diamond court cards represent people who carry their own agendas, which may not align with the querent's interests. They are not necessarily untrustworthy, but their ambitions come first. Depending on position in the layout, a Diamond court card might indicate an ambitious person who could prove a valuable ally.

Some readers associate Diamond court cards with blond individuals. This is not a universal tradition and may vary by region and social context.

Tarot Equivalent

Diamonds align with the suit of Pentacles (or Coins) in the tarot, which shares the same Earth element.
