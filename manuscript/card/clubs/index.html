Card Grimoire

Clubs

◇Suit
Clubs
◇Keywords
Fire, transformation, personal responsibility, control, warm and dry
↗Correspondences1
Fire
↗Symbols1
Club

The suit of Clubs reflects the element of Fire, warm and dry. In its natural state, fire is often a dangerous, devastating force, but it is also an element routinely harnessed and used. Its power is to transform. Fire changes wood, cloth, paper, and other combustibles into ash. It clears away old growth in a forest to make way for new green saplings.

Fire is similar to the element of Air in that it creates changes that can be either beneficial or destructive. But whereas Air represents movement, Fire represents transformation. The suit of Clubs speaks of personal changes more often than external events. While surrounding circumstances can influence thoughts and actions, Clubs emphasize the querent's reactions rather than the circumstances themselves.

The querent has much more control over their own reactions than over external events, and in this way Clubs are good to see in a reading. When a Club appears to present a bad omen, think of it as a warning, offering advice to avoid potential problems as or before they arise.

In the earliest card decks, those that eventually evolved into tarot cards and playing cards, the Clubs were represented as polo sticks. In tarot decks, they retain a stick-like imagery and are often pictured as wands, staves, or rods. The Clubs in a deck of playing cards bear little resemblance to these images, but they can still wield all the power of a wizard's wand when it comes to personal transformation.

The keyword for this suit is transformation.

In Readings

Clubs and Spades both point toward chaos, but from different sources. Spades indicate external forces often beyond direct control. Clubs indicate situations where the querent is the cause of the problem or holds significant influence over it. This means the querent is in control of the situation, even when that control is not immediately apparent. The querent's own actions and choices determine the outcome.

A lack of awareness is often at the root of situations described by Clubs. Recognizing the problem and adjusting attitude and choices is frequently enough to resolve it. When personal responsibility is not accepted, the situation tends to worsen.

Court Cards

Club court cards do not point to transformation itself, but to people who have already undergone it. These individuals have conquered challenges and gained wisdom and resilience through hardship. Club court cards tend to represent valuable allies who are sympathetic to the querent's needs, more so than court cards in the other three suits.

Tarot Equivalent

Clubs align with the suit of Wands (or Batons) in the tarot, which shares the same Fire element.
