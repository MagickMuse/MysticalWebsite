Archetypal Meaning

The Moon symbolizes creativity, nurturing, and family. It also marks the surfacing of subconscious negativity, brought up so it can be healed and transformed.

The Fool's Journey: Dreams

Awareness of illusion grows at the Moon's stage: dreams. Embracing dreams here means understanding that everything is reflected, both the good and the bad, though navigating this realm poorly can stir up fear and anxiety.

Imagery and Symbolism

This image is built in three layers. At the lowest level lies a square-edged lake with a crayfish in it. Above it are two animals, two dogs or perhaps a wolf and a dog, raising their open jaws toward the Moon as though howling, with the corners of two slightly different buildings to either side of them, one roofed and the other open to the sky and reminiscent of the tower struck by lightning in the sixteenth card. At the top of the card hangs the full Moon, its face turned to the left and ringed by a halo of rays like moonbeams, with teardrop shapes around it that seem either to fall from the Moon or to be drawn back into it.

The dogs recall the hounds that accompany the Moon Goddess, and dogs also serve as psychopomps, guardians of souls in the spirit world. There is a nightmarish quality to the card, for the surrounding land is barren, with only two small plants in a sort of no-man's land, and it represents the dark night of the soul. Even so, the preceding card promises hope, and the Moon offers the light it reflects from the Sun to illuminate the way ahead, a sign that guidance will come from above.

Some depictions show the Moon eclipsed rather than full, a detail that reinforces the card's themes of hidden things and blocked insight rather than a straightforward reveal. The dog and wolf beneath it read as two sides of the same self, the wolf howling with wild, instinctive energy while the dog waits with more patience and restraint. Even in versions where the Moon shines full, shadow and darkness still dominate the landscape, leaving open the question of whether there's really something to fear in that darkness, or only something imagined.

Astrology

The Moon is connected to the Water sign Pisces, a dreamy sign that brings themes of intuition, dreams, mystery, and the unconscious to this card. Pisces invites a dive into the deeper waters of the psyche, reinforcing the card's ties to fantasy, illusion, and the hidden depths of the mind. This influence suggests a period of heightened emotion and intuition, one that calls for exploring the subconscious and acknowledging inner feelings and fears rather than looking away from them.

The Crayfish in Traditional Imagery

Older versions of The Moon tarot card traditionally feature a creature emerging from water that appears to modern eyes like a lobster or poorly drawn crab. However, these older images actually depict a crayfish, the lunar animal par excellence in Central and Eastern European magical traditions, particularly those influenced by the Romany.

The confusion between crayfish, crabs, and lobsters in tarot imagery reflects different regional lunar traditions:

  • Crayfish: Freshwater crustaceans sacred to the moon in Romany-influenced magical practices
  • Crabs: Mediterranean lunar creatures, sacred to Cancer (the moon's astrological sign) and to sea goddess Amphitrite

Many modern tarot decks have replaced the traditional crayfish with more realistic crabs, but the original symbolism points to the crayfish as the creature of moon magic.

Lunar Symbolism

Crayfish shells were traditionally dried and preserved as fertility amulets, reflecting the ancient belief that fertility is the moon's magical gift transmitted through lunar creatures.
