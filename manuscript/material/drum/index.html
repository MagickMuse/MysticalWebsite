Construction

The witches' drum is built from a box or broad hoop of wood with tightly laced animal hide or vellum stretched across the face. The drumhead is painted with an eight-pointed star formed by two interlaced squares. A line divides the face: one half is stained red, representing summer, male force, and life; the other half is stained blue, representing winter, female force, and death.

Divination Use

Devil's-apple (datura) seeds may be thrown onto the drumhead surface for divination. The position in which the seeds come to rest, relative to the painted divisions and star points, is interpreted to yield an answer.
Music and magick are inseparable. The earliest ritual practices involved music and dance, and these traditions continue in healing, trance, and exorcism ceremonies throughout the world. Spiritual communication itself is frequently accomplished through music.

The Heartbeat of Magick

The drum is among the oldest magickal instruments, its rhythmic beat echoing the pulse of the earth and the human heart. Drumming raises positive energy and disperses negative vibrations while connecting practitioners to primal rhythmic power.

Shamanic Journeying

Drums are essential tools in shamanic traditions worldwide. The steady, repetitive rhythm of the drum facilitates altered states of consciousness, enabling the practitioner to journey between worlds. The drum is sometimes called the "shaman's horse" because it carries the spirit traveler.

Types of Ritual Drums

Frame drums: Circular drums with a single head stretched over a shallow frame. Often associated with goddess worship and feminine mysteries.

Hand drums: Smaller drums played with the hands rather than sticks. Portable and intimate for personal ritual work.

Djembe and similar: African-derived drums that produce a range of tones and are excellent for group energy raising.

Ritual Uses

Drums are used to:

  • Raise energy for magickal workings
  • Induce trance states for journeying
  • Signal ritual transitions
  • Connect to earth energies
  • Call spirits and ancestors
  • Disperse negative vibrations
  • Divination

Drum Divination

Drums serve not only for spirit summoning, psychic enhancement, and shamanic journeying but also for direct divination. The most celebrated divining drums in the world belonged to Saami shamans. Beautifully decorated, ritually prepared, and consecrated, these drums were core ritual equipment on the same level as another tradition's wand or athame. During forced Christian conversion campaigns, the drums were banned and destroyed. A small number of originals survived and are now preserved in Swedish museums.

In this divinatory method, visual elements are as important as the sound. Magickal designs were painted onto the stretched drumhead, with each shaman's drum unique to their own vision and spiritual practice alongside shared traditional motifs. Small objects were placed on the drum and as it was beaten, accompanied by divining songs, the objects danced across the surface and revealed the oracle through the patterns they made.

The Saami and Hungarian peoples share Finno-Ugric ancestry. Until recently, a closely related drum oracle was used by Hungarian country practitioners and Romany women, typically without the full shamanic ceremonial framework. The core method is adaptable and can be improvised freely.

Basic drum divination method: Paint three black circles and three white circles onto a frame drum. Chant and beat the drum, and in the process toss shelled cranberry beans onto the drumhead. Read the oracle from the patterns the jumping beans form in relation to the painted circles.

Deity Associations

Bes, the Egyptian protective spirit, is almost never depicted without his frame drum. Percussion is central to his mythology.

Cybele (Kybele), considered among the most ancient deities still familiar today, carries a frame drum. Percussion is likewise central to her worship.

Drums as Women's Instruments

Contrary to the modern association of percussion with masculine energy, frame drums and tambourines were historically identified as women's instruments and formed a sacred element of many women's spiritual traditions.

African-Derived Traditions

Drumming is central to African-derived spiritual practices. Each orisha and lwa has their own specific rhythms and songs, which are used to summon and open communication with the spirits. Percussion instruments are foundational to these traditions, not supplementary.

Flutes

Flutes are among the most ancient instruments and appear throughout myths and fairy tales across cultures. Greek Pan and Athena each played different forms of the instrument. Fairy tales are full of stories of magick flutes capable of summoning spirits, allies, animals, or loved ones. The Pied Piper is only the most widely known example.

Flutes carry distinct regional traditions:

  • Native American cedar flutes are used for romance and seduction
  • Chinese bamboo spirit flutes invoke a specific spirit, whose name may be carved directly into the bamboo
  • European elder-wood flutes played at midnight in a remote and isolated location are said to summon spirits

Any instrument can be incorporated into spell-work. Instruments may be played live, or recordings may be used in ritual and spellcraft.
