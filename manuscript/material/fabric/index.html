Fabric in Magick

In many spells, components must be encased in fabric. The fabric itself becomes incorporated into the working, making it an active part of the spell rather than merely a container. Many traditions identify specific fabrics as sacred, and in a number of cases the techniques used to create those fabrics, such as batik or ikat, originate from magickal or spiritual practice. The most commonly used fabrics in spell-work are red silk and red flannel.

Embellished Fabric as Spell

Spells are cast through the embellishment of fabric. Many traditional needlework arts are deeply intertwined with spiritual traditions. The elaborate embroidery motifs crafted by Baltic, Hungarian, Romany, and Slavic women are not purely decorative: these patterns are designed to confer blessings, power, protection, and fertility upon the wearer.

Flags and Banners

Among the most significant descendants of magickal fabric are flags. Flags are used:

  • To honor and summon spirits
  • As amulets
  • As protective devices

The use of flags and banners in religion and heraldry is rooted in this magickal origin. The sacred quality attributed to many flags, including national flags, derives from this history. The strong emotional response people have when a flag is treated with perceived disrespect also traces back to these roots.

Symbols incorporated onto flags are understood to provide protection or to project a threat. Pirate flags were not limited to the Jolly Roger. The most feared pirates ensured their specific flag announced their presence and potentially provided protection for the bearer.

Among the most powerful examples of fabric as a magickal tool are Haitian sequined flags, known as Vodoun flags. Sigils, symbols, and images of the lwa or their affiliated saints are rendered onto fabric using sequins, creating objects of devotion and invocation.
