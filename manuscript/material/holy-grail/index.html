Holy Grail

◇Notes/Warnings
The Nanteos Cup story has no forensic evidence. The Grail parallels the alchemists' Philosopher's Stone as an emblem of elusive spiritual wholeness.
↗Category1
Containers

Overview

To say a search is like seeking the Holy Grail means it is for something treasured and elusive that may never be found. Like the Philosopher's Stone, a genuine Grail has kept its hard to reach status.

Pre-Christian Roots

The legend links directly to two mystical pre-Christian objects: the magickal Cauldron of the Celtic gods that never emptied and kept everyone satisfied, and the magickal Chalice that represents spiritual authority and kingship.

The Christian Grail

Received wisdom holds that the physical Grail is either the Cup Christ drank from at the Last Supper or the vessel that caught his blood at the crucifixion. The sacred vessel later went missing.

The Nanteos Cup

Rumor says a fragment of the true Grail, the Nanteos Cup, is hidden somewhere in Wales. Made of olivewood, it is said to have been brought to Glastonbury by Joseph of Arimathea and kept by the monks of the Abbey. When the Dissolution of the Monasteries in the sixteenth century saw monasteries abolished and their treasures seized by the Crown, the relic had to be moved, and allegedly ended up at Nanteos Mansion near Aberystwyth. Though now only a small fragment of wood, water drunk from it is claimed to hold healing powers. There is currently no forensic evidence for the story.

Symbolism

The Grail Legends of the Arthurian tales also stand for the quest for something beyond reach. The knights, spurred to find this object of desire, come to realize they seek far more than a cup. Because the shape of the grail is a feminine symbol and a powerful emblem of the spirit, Jung read it as the inner wholeness that people have always sought. In this it closely parallels the Philosopher's Stone of the alchemists, and shares its elusive nature.
