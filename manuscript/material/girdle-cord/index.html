Overview

Also called the cingulum, the girdle cord is most often red and serves several practical purposes: it cinches the tabard or ritual robe, and its knotted length is used to measure the diameter of a cast magic circle.

As a Ritual Rosary

Some witches also use the cord's knots as a counting aid, like rosary beads, when performing spellwork built on repeated phrases.

Manufacture

Ideally, spin your own flax or gather river rushes for the cord, though a ball of red ribbon works just as well for most modern witches. Measure off three lengths of six feet each. During the waxing moon, exorcise the strands with charged water and fire, then knot the three ends together and begin braiding, chanting the following words with each twist to bind in the magical light:

Made to measure,

wrought to bind,

blessed be

thou cord entwined.

When the braid is finished, tie a large firm knot at the loose ends to keep them from unravelling, and close with the words So mote it be!

Finally, add five more knots down the cord's length: at three feet six inches, four feet, four feet six inches, five feet, and five feet six inches from the first knot. These mark the triple radii used when the cord serves as a compass for casting a magic circle, one larger set of measurements for coven work and a smaller set for solo or small group spells.

Binding and the Cable Tow

Beyond measuring circles, the cord is also used more generally for binding work, and in some initiation rites, as a cable tow. On rare occasions it's even used to bind the practitioner's own body as a ligature.
