💀Kapala

Overview

The kapala, or thod pa, is one of the charnel ground implements used in the sacred rites of Tibetan Buddhism. It is a bowl or chalice fashioned from a human skull, often richly decorated and highly valued. The practice may reach back to far older skull traditions, and within Tibetan ritual the kapala stands as a symbol of the triumph of good over evil and a reminder of impermanence.

Use and Symbolism

Each skull is carefully chosen, washed, and sanctified before use, and tradition placed great importance on the purity of the vessel. The kapala is usually held in the left hand, and as a charnel ground implement it is paired with other reminders of mortality, turning the matter of death toward spiritual liberation.

Related vessels in this grimoire include the Chalice and the Cup.
