Whisky is a distilled grain spirit with a long history of use in Hoodoo and folk magick — both as a practical medium for infusing roots and herbs, and as a ritual libation or offering to spirits.

In court case workings, whisky is most famously called for in High John the Conqueror victory formulas, where High John roots are steeped in the spirit to create a wearable magickal cologne. The alcohol both preserves the root and draws out its magickal qualities over time.

Whisky is also an appropriate offering to spirits associated with legal protection and crossroads work — particularly those of African-derived traditions who prefer spirits (alcohol) alongside other offerings.

Magickal Uses

  • Root and herb infusion: Steeping High John and other roots to create wearable condition formulas
  • Ritual libation and offering: Poured or offered to spirits petitioned for legal victories
  • Preservation medium: Extends the shelf life of botanical formulas
