Poppet magick is one of the most commonly used forms of sympathetic magick, operating on the principle that "like creates like." If you create a poppet of a person, anything done to the poppet will affect the person it represents. This practice has been in use for thousands of years and remains a staple among practitioners today for healing, protection, prosperity, and other needs.

A poppet is, at its core, simply a doll. The word "doll" derives from similar roots as "idol," while "poppet" is related to "puppet." The stereotypical image of a witch's doll stuffed with pins to inflict suffering is only one narrow application of a much broader and older tradition. Modern candle magick, particularly the use of figure candles, is a direct outgrowth of doll magick. Both involve creating a physical representation of a person to direct magickal intent.

Materials and Construction

Poppets can be made from nearly anything. Be creative. Some common options:

  • Cloth poppets (sewn and stuffed)
  • Paper poppets (herbs, photos, and taglocks glued on)
  • Clay poppets
  • Wax poppets (can be difficult to work with)
  • Yarn poppets
  • Aluminum foil poppets

Wax dolls have been recovered from ancient Egypt, establishing their long history, but magickal dolls may also be crafted from fabric, botanicals, wood, clay, and bone.

Stuff the doll with whatever aligns with the purpose of the working: grass and leaves, herbs, grave dust, taglocks, runes, or anything else that serves the intent. The doll should resemble, or at least remind you of, the intended target. Adding taglocks or photos strengthens the connection. There is no wrong way to make a poppet.

Positive Uses

Spell dolls may be used for:

  • Healing disease
  • Inducing love
  • Reuniting the estranged
  • Promoting and enhancing fertility
  • Protection
  • Prosperity

Dark Magick Applications

Constructing for Baneful Work

For best results, incorporate a personal belonging of the target that carries their energy. A photo, a small article of clothing, car keys, personal possessions, hair, fingernail clippings, sexual fluids, or even dirt taken from a footprint can be used. The possibilities are endless, so long as the article has a close connection to the target.

Wax and clay were the traditional materials, though wax can be difficult to work with. If you plan to inflict serious damage, construct the image to be sturdy rather than soft. The poppet should be built to take abuse.

The Working

What matters most is that the right amount of focus and intensity goes into the working.

  • Driving a nail into the head causes insanity
  • Driving a nail into the heart with sufficient focus, intensity, and auric power can cause death
  • Burying the image in the ground causes the person to waste away as the poppet rots

Take whatever personal items you have obtained and use them in construction. Clothing may need to be cut to size; save the leftovers for the ritual itself. Wax, clay, or similar material can be molded and blended with pieces of the target's belongings.

While constructing the image, chant the enemy's name aloud or silently, over and over, for the entire duration of the build. This should be a mantra fueled by hatred. Focus intensely on the wrongs they have committed and your anger toward them.

The Ritual

When the poppet is complete, write or engrave the target's name upon it. For workings intended to completely destroy the enemy, perform this during a waning Moon.

Conduct a full ritual. At the height of the working, present the poppet and read aloud (or silently) the wrongs the enemy has committed against you and the curse you wish to inflict. Burn the written curse in the flame of a black candle.

While the paper burns and for some time afterward, focus all of your anger and hatred on the enemy. Chant their name repeatedly. Curse their name. This is the time to drive nails into the image, roast it over the fire, twist it, and mutilate it however you see fit. Go over every offense. Get all of the anger out until you are completely spent.

Disposal

Once the working is finished, the poppet can be burned, ideally outside in a secluded area. Bury the ashes.

If you cannot burn it, throw it in the trash. If you have a cat and only non-toxic herbs were used in the construction, you may let the cat destroy it.

Warning: Only let a cat destroy the poppet if it is completely safe for the animal. No toxic herbs, no nails, no glass, no harmful materials.

Etymology and Cross-Cultural Notes (merged from duplicate "Dolls" entry)

The French word for doll is poupée. Puppetry derives from sacred ritual and remains used for this purpose in traditional Indonesian practice.

Historical Evidence

Among the evidence brought against Bridget Bishop at the Salem Witch Trials was the discovery of several rag poppets in her former residence, pierced with hogs' bristles and headless pins.

Oracular Functions

In Japan and elsewhere in East Asia, dolls serve as oracles. Legends describe special dolls that literally communicate prophecies.

Fertility Magick

Dolls were once used to stimulate pregnancy virtually around the world, including indigenous North American traditions, Italy, and China. The tradition remains vital in sub-Saharan Africa. The most famous African fertility doll is the Ashanti Akua'ba, so prominent it has been featured on Ghana's postage stamps.
