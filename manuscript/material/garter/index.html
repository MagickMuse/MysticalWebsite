Sign of Identification

Beyond knot magick and love divination, the garter is also carried as one of a witch's identifying jewels: a wearable sign of Craft, sometimes marked in runic script with the wearer's name, coven symbol, or rank. Because it stays mostly hidden and is revealed only at Sabbat gatherings, it works less as a tool of "Fascination" (the reflective, trance-inducing use associated with rings and pendants) and more as a private badge of belonging and a talismanic link to the coven's collective power.

Styles and Materials

Garters come in several styles. Bright red is the traditional color, though black, blue, and green versions exist. Women's garters are typically velvet backed with silk, while men's favor snakeskin, crocodile, or soft leather backed with blue silk. Gold or silver gilt buckles fasten them, sometimes with small bells attached, echoing the bells worn by English Morris dancers.

Markings and Placement

The outer surface carries the witch name, coven symbol, and coven rank, sometimes alongside the same signs inscribed on the Athame. Garters are worn above the left knee, and only at coven gatherings or while casting spells, never as everyday wear.
