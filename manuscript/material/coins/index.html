Coin Divination

Coins have been used as oracular tools since antiquity and are among the most accessible forms of divination available. Most divinatory systems are better suited to nuanced, open-ended questions, but sometimes the only answer needed is a clear yes or no, or a choice between two specific options. When that is the case, a coin toss is a legitimate magickal tool, not a lazy shortcut. Assign one outcome to heads and the other to tails, formulate the question precisely, flip, and accept the result.

Coins carry longstanding associations with metal magick and have served as ritual objects across many cultures. While any coin from your pocket will serve, many practitioners set aside a single dedicated coin for divination and treat it with the same care given to a tarot deck or set of runes. I-Ching coins are a popular choice, as are antique coins. Religious medals, gambling chips, or any small, flat disc with two visually distinct sides may function as a substitute.

For workings that benefit from added potency, invoke blessings from entities associated with metal and smithcraft: Brigid, the Dactyls, Hephaestus, Ogun, or Scathach are among those traditionally associated with these energies.

Coin divination is not limited to binary questions. More complex decisions can be approached using a set of three coins tossed simultaneously, sometimes multiple times in sequence. The reading is based on how many coins land face up versus face down. As with all divination, the question and the method of interpretation should be established clearly before the coins are thrown.
