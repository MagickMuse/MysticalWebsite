Herbal Lore

Historians have traced fermentation's lineage to before 9000 BCE. The debate centers on whether barley's use in bread or fermentation led to civilization's establishment. By growing barley and other cereal grains, early people domesticated cattle and developed codes of conduct.

By 2900 BCE, civilizations like ancient Sumer were brewing beers and dedicating poems to goddesses like Ninsaki, the goddess of brewing. In 2400 BCE, the straw was invented to drink beer more efficiently. Established guides for brewing beer and how to serve it appear among the oldest written laws—the Code of Hammurabi, dated to 1760 BCE.

Since beer is made with malted (sprouted) barley, the first beers were likely accidental. Barley left in wet conditions sprouts readily. Fermentation is a by-product of natural yeasts in the air and on living things.

Magickal Uses

Love Drawing

Barley's Venusian associations make it perfect for attracting the right lover. On a Friday under the moon, ask for Demeter's blessings. List qualities needed in the perfect person, then open the beer. Anoint the forehead for thinking of the ideal person, the heart for heart connection, the hands to receive their love, and your feet to lead you to them. Pour the remaining beer on the ground in thanks to Demeter.

Money and Prosperity

On a Thursday for prosperity, place a candleholder in a bowl and pour beer around it. Use green for fertility or brown for stability. Draw a $ on the candle and write your name. Bless with olive oil for prosperity and let it burn down. Dispose in nature.

Sober Substitution: Use dry barley from the grocery store instead of beer. Add water to keep it fire safe and dispose in nature before it ferments.

Fertility

When trying to conceive, barley aids both healing and sexual potency. Partners can bathe separately or together (rosemary infusion makes an excellent bath for this work). At the end of the bath, each pours a cup of beer over their head—it also removes product buildup and leaves hair weightless and shiny.

Banishing Poverty

On a Saturday (endings), gather a handful of barley. Name each grain with the source of your debt—"joblessness," "medical bills." Envision debt crumbling to dust. Cast the barley into fire and watch it disappear.

Offerings

Both beer and barley have been offerings to gods since before recorded history. Speak from the heart, then take the grain into nature or leave a custard cup on your altar for three days before casting outside.

Funerals

Associated with death and the dead, beer and barley honor life well lived. Opening a beer for a loved one who has crossed the veil can sit for up to three days before the spirit has gone out of the drink and can be poured outside.
