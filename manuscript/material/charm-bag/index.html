A charm bag is a magick spell contained within a bag. It is filled with one or more power items and may function as a miniature spell, a portable altar, or an ever-evolving collection of power objects. Charm bags are the single most popular method of carrying magickally charged items across cultures worldwide.

Names and Traditions

This spell form goes by many names: conjure bag, medicine bag, medicine hand, mojo bag, mojo hand, gris-gris bag, ouanga bag, dilly bag, amulet bag, and for the scholarly, phylacteries. These are only the English names, reflecting the global reach of this practice.

Simplicity and Complexity

Charm bags range from extremely simple to highly elaborate. A Moroccan spell recommends filling an amulet bag with earth taken from a three-way crossroads and wearing it around the neck to ward off the Evil Eye or to find and maintain true love.

At the complex end, the Brazilian patua is crafted from leather or cloth and might contain a danda root shaped into a figa (the fig hand), placed between leaves of rue and mucura. Garlic and cloves may be added along with prayers written in special ink and sewn into the bag.

Traditions Around the World

Different traditions approach charm bags in distinct ways:

  • Native North American tradition initiates a medicine bag with an activating agent such as a pinch of tobacco, pollen, corn kernels, sweetgrass, white sage, or a bit of earth, tied into a piece of red flannel
  • Orthodox Muslim tradition permits only Koranic verses as amulets, with a separate pouch required for each
  • African nomadic practitioners may be covered in leather and metal talisman cases
  • Arabic and Tibetan amulet bags are finely crafted from metal and sometimes adorned with jewels

Materials and Construction

The container itself becomes part of the spell, and materials are carefully chosen. The drawstring bag is the most familiar form. Hoodoo recommends red flannel, while Romany tradition suggests red silk. Other bags may be as simple as a knotted handkerchief.

Hands vs. Sachets

Technically, a "hand" is a closed bag rather than an open one. The distinction between a sachet and a hand is drawn by the fabric (sachets use muslin, hands use flannel) and the contents (a sachet contains only botanical material, while a hand may contain a variety of items including botanicals).

Historical Forms

Early African-American mojo hands, from the post-slavery period onward, were sewn red squares with the material enclosed inside, resembling an isolated single quilt square. The traditional British mojo hand is similar: two pieces of red flannel cut into a heart shape, stuffed, sewn together, and the outside decorated.

Wearing and Carrying

Charm bags are carried in the pocket, worn around the neck or waist. They may also be hung on a wall like an amulet or kept discreetly inside a drawer or cabinet. Bags may be sewn into clothes, or individual items sewn into clothing Romany style, for privacy and added contact with the body.

Charging the Mojo

In some traditions, including Hoodoo and Wicca, the mojo hand is charged before use:

  1. Light a match and plunge it into the bag, extinguishing it (take care not to ignite the contents, especially dried botanicals or volatile essential oils)
  2. Spit in the bag and pull the string tight

Charm Vials

An alternative form popular in Central and South America uses miniature glass bottles instead of fabric, metal, or leather. These charm vials are filled with power objects, creating a visible talismanic and protective display. Tiny bottles, often recycled medical ampoules, are fitted with yarn or cord for wearing or hanging. They typically include layers of charms suspended in oil: seeds, botanicals, minerals, tiny carvings, or votive image cards.

Gris-Gris

The word gris-gris comes from West Africa. Today it is often used as just another name for a conjure, charm or mojo bag, but it originally meant what the Portuguese called fetishes: handmade magickal objects. The original West African gris-gris were often dolls. Modern New Orleans gris-gris are usually somewhere between a mojo bag and a Vodou paquet Congo, although doll shaped gris-gris still exist.

For those who care about the finer points, there is a subtle difference. Mojo, also from an African word, usually implies drawing success, whether in love, gambling, money, work or legal matters. Gris-gris implies protection, so a gris-gris bag can range anywhere from a gentle protective charm to a hex.
