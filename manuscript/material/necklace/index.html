Overview

Worn primarily by women coven members, and generally reserved for Sabbats and Esbats rather than daily wear. Like the girdle cord and garter, it likely began as a coven identification piece. Tradition also treats it as the one jewel a female witch is expected to wear, even though the rest of the set remains optional.

Mythic Origins

Two origin stories circulate among witches:

  • A link to Brisingamen, the elven necklace belonging to Freya, the Norse goddess of love.
  • A connection to the cult of Diana of Ephesus, whose devotees pictured her crowned with an acorn-shaped coif, which may explain why some necklaces use acorns as beads.

Construction

  • Bead counts traditionally run in multiples of nine or thirteen.
  • Beads can be metal, stone, or wood, so long as they're substantial rather than delicate.
  • Amber, turquoise, and jet are especially favored materials.
  • Many witches string their own necklaces, exorcising each bead with fire and water first, then charging it under their witch name, the same process used for other magical tools.
