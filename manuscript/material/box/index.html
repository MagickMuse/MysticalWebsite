Boxes serve a variety of purposes in magickal practice, from storing ritual tools to functioning as self-contained spells or altars.

Cache Boxes

Cache boxes secure ritual tools and small magickal items. The size of the box depends on its purpose: storing swords or wands may require a full-sized chest, while smaller items call for more intimate containers.

Among the most powerful cache boxes, listed in descending order of power:

  • Iron boxes radiate protective power and function as a battery, continually recharging the contents stored within
  • Spice boxes crafted from cinnamon and cloves, as originally made in Indonesia
  • Wooden or leather boxes embellished with magickal designs drawn in alkanet, indigo, wood, or henna (note: while henna is a temporary stain on skin and hair, it is a permanent dye on objects)

Spell Boxes

A spell box is a constructed enclosed altar or tableau. It is filled with power items chosen to achieve a specific goal, though specific spells may provide their own instructions. A spell box may be a cumulative working: items are gathered one by one over time and added to build the box's power incrementally.

Sometimes the box itself is chosen because it resonates with the magickal goal. The box can become an intrinsic part of the spell through:

  • Attaching power items directly to the box's surface, such as charms, shells, beads, and feathers
  • Decoupaging images on the outside or inside of the box to correspond with the spell's goals
  • Embellishing with sigils or other fortuitous symbols using magickal ink, henna, or another natural dye

Shadow Box Altars

An enclosed altar offers discretion and privacy that open altars cannot. Certain spirits, such as dark moon goddesses or some protective spirits, may actively prefer an enclosed altar. Open altars disperse their power outward through an area; an enclosed altar concentrates its power within. Each form carries distinct advantages.

The shadow box altar has its roots in medieval Roman Catholic religious cabinets. These altars range in size from full-sized standing cabinets to miniature altars small enough to fit inside a matchbox.
