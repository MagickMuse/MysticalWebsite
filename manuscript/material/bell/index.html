Traditional Uses

Bells are common in various magickal traditions. Generally smaller hand bells with handles are used. The sound of a bell ringing, especially a metal bell, is believed to exert a purifying influence and serves multiple magickal purposes.

Spirit Work

Bells are used for summoning and banishing spirits. Malicious spirits allegedly flee from the sound of bells, making them effective protective devices.

Protection and Warding

Bells function as protective devices and amulets. When strategically hung from chains or incorporated into mobiles, they serve as the equivalent of a magickal guard dog and allegedly ring spontaneously when needed. Bells are attached to clothing around the world to repel malevolent spirits and the Evil Eye.

Cleansing and Purification

The sound of ringing bells is used for cleansing spells. Bells are used to vanquish and remove the Evil Eye.

Healing Magick

Bells are tools for magickal healing. Ringing bells facilitates healing, and sometimes healing potions are drunk from magickal bells in the belief that the cup adds potency to the brew.

Fertility Magick

Bells are used in fertility spells. This connection derives from ancient sacred imagery where the bell's body represents the vulva while the clapper represents the penis. An alternative interpretation suggests the bell's body represents the womb while the clapper represents the child within. Unlike other images deriving from sacred genitalia, the bell's two components cannot be separated. A bell is not functional unless the clapper is retained within.

The fertility imagery is sometimes enhanced by crafting the body of the bell to resemble a woman. The handle is crafted to resemble her head and torso while the round bell is her skirt.

Preferred Materials

Bells are crafted from many materials, but silver and iron are considered the most auspicious, particularly for protection, fertility, and healing. Bells intended to serve double duty as amulets may be crafted in symbolic shapes: pinecones, cats, and frogs are common forms, each carrying its own additional magickal associations.

Recycled Bell Metal

In some Asian traditions, bells that have been well-used are melted down and the resulting metal is used to create other magickal tools, such as ritual cups and plates. The accumulated energy from the bell's use is believed to transfer into the new object.

Historical Uses

Bells were attached to the ritual clothing of priests who served the Jerusalem Temple. Devices from Pompeii and elsewhere in the Roman Empire combined bells with phallic imagery.

During the European witch-hunt era, it was commonly believed that the sound of church bells ringing repelled witches and caused them to fall off their brooms if flying through the sky.

Church Bell Grease

Grease scraped from church bells is a common component of Goofer Dust, the Hoodoo magickal powder whose primary ingredient is graveyard dust. Slavic witches have traditionally used church bell grease to make similar concoctions. Grease scraped from church bells is allegedly a primary component of the flying ointment favored by Sweden's Easter witches.

Nightmare Dispersal

Keeping a genuine silver bell at the bedside allows the sleeper to ring it upon waking from a nightmare, scattering residual energy and clearing the room's atmosphere.

Mysticism of the Bell

There is a mysticism surrounding the bell that far transcends its mundane use. The sound of the bell is universally accepted as a way of communicating with the spirits, or as a herald for the arrival of a supernatural, holy power.

Across Traditions

The analogy of the bell occurs in language too, used to symbolize something of sacred origin. In Islam, the "reverberation of the bell" describes the sound of the revelations of the Quran. In Buddhism, the "sound of the golden bell" is an analogy for heavenly voices. The sound of a bell is a reminder that, like the sound, the world may be experienced but not possessed.

Pagoda roofs sometimes have hundreds of tiny bells hanging from them, symbolizing the concepts of Buddhist laws as well as frightening away malicious entities. For the same reason, the church bells of Christian churches were at one time pealed not only during processions or as notice of a ceremony, but also during thunderstorms to chase away demons.

Sacred Object

The bell is a sacred object in its own right. In the form of the Buddhist Drilbu or the Hindu Ghanta, it symbolizes the illusory world because of the fleetingly resonant nature of its sound. It is the feminine principle paired with the masculine vajra.

The use of these Eastern bells largely influenced their European symbolism and use. The sweet reverberation of a bell, rung three times in the silence of a large stone church, has a quality of calming the atmosphere, attracting the attention of worshippers, welcoming in the spirits, and setting the scene for the ritual that follows.

The Magickal Bell

The power of the bell as a way of spiritual communication is carried one step further in the magickal bell made of an amalgam of the seven sacred metals ruled by the planets. This bell, engraved with the Tetragrammaton and the planetary seals from alchemy, allegedly has the power to summon the spirits of the dead. However, this spell calls for the bell to be put into a grave for seven days and seven nights before it will work properly.
