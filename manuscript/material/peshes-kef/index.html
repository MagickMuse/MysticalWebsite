Peshes-kef

◇Notes/Warnings
Flint tool with a split blade used by midwives to cut the umbilical cord. Its bifurcated shape was ideal for cutting through tubes or cords. Became part of the ceremonial equipment for the Opening of the Mouth ritual performed on statues and mummies. Connected the private act of giving birth to the cosmic act of being reborn after death.

✂️Craft Tools

The Cord-Cutting Tool

The peshes-kef is a flint tool with a split (bifurcated) blade at one end. Midwives used it to cut the umbilical cord, separating the newborn infant from its mother's body.

Design

The tool's forked shape was ideal for cutting through tubes or cords. Flint was the traditional material, connecting it to Egypt's most ancient stone-working traditions.

Opening of the Mouth

The peshes-kef became part of the ceremonial equipment for the Opening of the Mouth ritual that sem-priests performed on newly-made statues and on wrapped mummies. This rite made statues and the deceased fully alive and sentient.

During the ritual:

  • The priest touched the mouth with the netjery ('divine') tool, shaped like two small fingers (all that would fit into an infant's mouth)
  • The peshes-kef represented the cutting of the cord
  • Two jars called the breasts of Isis and Horus represented the nourishment of breastfeeding

Birth and Rebirth

The midwife's action of clearing mucus from a baby's mouth may have inspired the Opening of the Mouth ritual. Through these gestures and recitations, the private act of giving birth was transformed into the cosmic act of being reborn after death.

This connected pregnancy and motherhood to one of the greatest supernatural concerns running throughout Egyptian society: the continuation of life beyond death.
