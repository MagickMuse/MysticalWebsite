Vajra

Overview

The Vajra is the double-headed scepter seen in the hand of many Buddhist figures, held opposite the bell, or ghanta; together they are male and female. The Sanskrit word means "diamond-like" and "the hard and mighty one," though its sense runs deeper into the metaphysical. It is also called the Thunderbolt, since it destroys ignorance.

Structure and Meaning

The hollow center of the Vajra marks the bindhu, the dot that seems least significant yet may be the most important of all, standing for the sphere of actual reality. On either side of the bindhu sit balanced lotus flowers, one for the material world and one for the spiritual, each with eight petals. The lotus itself speaks to the human condition: born into the mire of the material world, yet reaching spiritual realization by rising beyond those origins.

The Prongs

From the lotus flowers spring flames or spokes, usually five to a side and always symmetrical. The five stand for the five Buddhas with their consorts and their combined energies, and for the five wisdoms: the wisdom of individuality, mirror-like wisdom, reality wisdom, the wisdom of equanimity, and all-encompassing wisdom. Taken together, the ten prongs recall the ten perfections (generosity, proper conduct, renunciation, insight, diligence, tolerance, truthfulness, determination, kindness, and serenity), the ten steps toward enlightenment, and the ten directions.

Peace and Wrath

Where the prongs curve together to a point they evoke the holy mountain, Mount Meru, and the closed ends make the Vajra a symbol of peace. Open ends instead signal the wrath of the deity who wields it, the kind of wrath that destroys illusion and negativity, and they are sometimes shown with flames shooting outward. The Indian town of Darjeeling, famous for its tea, takes its name from this concept, corrupted from the original Dorje Lingam.
