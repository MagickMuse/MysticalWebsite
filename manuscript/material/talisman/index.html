A talisman is an active object created and consecrated with magickal power toward the achievement of a given purpose. The term comes from the Arabic tilsam, derived from the Greek words telein (to consecrate) and tetelesmenon (that which has been consecrated).

Characteristics

Unlike amulets, talismans are active objects. The forces embedded in a talisman can be invoked at will by the magician who created it. A talisman radiates magickal energy that has been customized to create the specific change desired by the magician.

Purposes

Talismans can be charged for anything from:

  • Protection
  • Attracting good fortune
  • Health
  • Planetary energies
  • Favorable circumstances

Placement and Use

Talismans can be worn or carried in a wallet or pocket, but many are kept in the temple or in a space where they can best achieve their assigned goal.

Key Distinction

While an amulet is a passive magickal object of general protection, a talisman is charged with a powerful divine force toward the achievement of a specific goal.

In short: Talismans draw things toward you.

Two Forms

Talismans, like amulets, generally take one of two forms:

  • Written talismans -- inscriptions, sigils, or sacred texts that channel specific fortune
  • Independent objects -- charged physical items that radiate their intended energy

Magick spells are frequently required in order to create effective talismans.

Norse Tradition

In Old Norse there are three principal words for talisman or talismanic magick:

  • Teinn refers to a piece of wood or twig fashioned into a talismanic object. The modern English word "tine" reflects this root.
  • Hlutr may be any object used for talismanic or divinatory purposes. The English word "lot" derives from this term.
  • Taufr means both talisman and magick in general, though in its original sense it refers specifically to talismanic magick.

All three terms describe different aspects of talismanic runecraft and reflect the centrality of this practice in the Norse magickal tradition.

Types of Runic Talismans

Runic talismans are typically fashioned from wood, bone, stone, or metal, though paper or parchment may also serve. They fall into several distinct categories:

  • Dedicated tines are purely magickal objects with no utilitarian function. These are what is traditionally meant by a tine.
  • Utilitarian objects such as belt buckles, tools, or weapons may be inscribed with runes and endowed with hamingja. This imparts success or protection in the areas where the object is used, continuing the ancient tradition of warriors inscribing their weapons and shields with runes for victory.
  • Stationary talismans are fixed objects turned into runic focal points. Trees, large rocks, and houses are examples. A stationary taufr may also be a card or stave placed in the vitki's room, or a tine positioned near the person to be affected. These are used to influence a particular place or the people who regularly occupy it.
  • Internally applied talismans are a further category in which the runic force is directed inward rather than into an external object.

The techniques of tine magick may be applied in operations of every type and should be followed, in one form or another, when fashioning any tool to be used in the runic art.

Planetary Timing and Construction

The surest talismans tilt fortune toward their aim by obeying a firm set of rules. The maker matches the working's purpose to a day of the week, to that day's governing angel and planet, and to the planetary hours in which the chosen influence runs strongest. By custom the work falls in the first, eighth, fifteenth, or twenty-second hour of the day, and the finished piece is ringed within a circle before it is worn or tucked into a purse or bag.

Take a talisman for health: it is made on a Sunday, the Sun's own day, the Sun being the planet of fire, vigor, and the life force. The preferred material is gold, the Sun's metal, and where gold is out of reach a golden hue will stand in its place.
