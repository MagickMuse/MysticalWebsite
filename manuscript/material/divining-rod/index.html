Overview

A divining rod is a forked twig or branch used to locate hidden things beneath the earth's surface: water, buried treasure, minerals, ley lines, and lost objects. The diviner holds one fork of the branch in each hand, with the single end pointing forward. The rod dips or twitches when it passes over the target.

Traditional Materials

Hazel is the classic and most highly recommended wood for divining rods. Hazel twigs and forks cut on Midsummer's Eve are considered especially potent for divining purposes. Other woods have been used historically, but hazel remains the gold standard.

Technique

  1. Cut a forked hazel twig on Midsummer's Eve for maximum potency.
  2. Hold one fork in each hand, with the single end pointing straight ahead.
  3. Walk slowly and steadily over the area to be searched.
  4. The rod will dip, twitch, or pull downward when it detects what you seek.

Connection to Moses

The divining rod has ancient associations with Moses, whose staff famously struck rock to produce water. Some traditions view the divining rod as a descendant of that miracle-working staff.
