Magickal Eye Makeup

Kohl, the eye makeup of ancient Egypt, was made from magickally powerful materials. Besides its health and beauty benefits, it protected the wearer from many spiritual dangers, including both receiving and casting the Evil Eye. Egyptian men and women alike wore it from the earliest days of their civilization to its end, and it is almost impossible to find an image of an ancient Egyptian without it. Originally, eye makeup was designed as much for magickal protection as for looks.

Real Kohl

Kohl is still in use today. Some modern cosmetic pencils are sold as kohl, but true kohl is a loose powder applied with a stick. Its magickal roots show clearly when it is sold in a drawstring bag, just like any other magick powder kept in a conjure bag, and kohl powder gives a measure of protection simply by being carried.

Kohl Formulas

The simplest kohl may be nothing more than antimony powder or soot, while more elaborate magickal formulas are prized too. Vendors in the traditional markets of North Africa and the Middle East still sell their own blends. Be cautious: some vendors put magick ahead of health, and certain formulas contain substances that must never go near the eye, though they may still be carried as talismans.

The Cosmetic Palette

The most common and most ancient Egyptian amulet is the cosmetic palette, a flat grinding stone, something like a mortar and pestle, used to grind the minerals for eye makeup. It appears in the oldest prehistoric graves and was consistently the one tool buried even in the simplest grave.
