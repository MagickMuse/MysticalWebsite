Spear of Destiny

Notes/Warnings
A possibly mythological relic of Christ's crucifixion, the lance Longinus used to pierce his side. Counterpart to the Holy Grail, masculine to its feminine, and carrying largely dark associations. Its most famous relic, the Hofburg Spear in Vienna, was briefly seized by Hitler.
Also called
Holy Lance, Lancea Longini, Spear of Longinus, Hofburg Spear
Category1
Blades
Entities1
Longinus

The Spear of Destiny, also called the Holy Lance, is a relic of Christ's last hours whose very existence may be legend, and in that it keeps company with the Holy Grail as a sacred object reputed to lend extraordinary power to whoever possesses it. The two make a matched pair, the Spear the masculine counterpart to the Grail's feminine, yet their tempers differ: where the Grail gathers bright associations, the Spear gathers dark ones, which suits a blade remembered for confirming that Christ had died.

Longinus

Scripture mentions the weapon a single time, in the Gospel of John. It is a later source, the fourth-century Gospel of Nicodemus, that supplies a name for the soldier who drove it into Christ's side: Longinus. From him the lance takes its alternate title, the Lancea Longini.

Provenance

As with the Grail, every appearance of the Spear, or of something put forward as the Spear, trails a history that cannot be confirmed. The relic seems to have multiplied since it was first noted in Jerusalem, and Rome, Armenia, Paris, and Vienna are among the places that have claimed to hold it. The Viennese example, known as the Hofburg Spear, fell into Adolf Hitler's hands and was sent back to Vienna once the war had ended. That short spell of possession hardened its name as a thing of malign power, and the tales attached to it have gone on to feed novels, comics, and films.
