Menstrual blood is widely considered the most powerful magickal substance in existence. Reduced to its most primal elements, magick rests on two foundations: menstrual blood and iron, which some traditions view as Earth's own menstrual blood.

Power and Protection

Menstrual blood functions as an ultimate controlling mechanism in spellwork. It provides spiritual protection, repels demons, and extinguishes fires. A single drop is said to cause others to fall madly in love with the bearer.

Menstrual blood is the ultimate protective agent. The magickal power inherent in the menstruating woman and her "wise-blood," rather than any supposed uncleanness, is the root of menstrual taboos and of the forced isolation of menstruating women. It protects against the Evil Eye, evil spirits, and people with evil intentions, and it creates a boomerang effect on magickal tricks. Its shock value is so strong that it may literally repel dangerous people too.

Coral and iron share the magickal powers of menstrual blood because, metaphysically, they are understood as the solidified remnants of the menstrual flow of the Ocean and the Earth respectively.

Door Guardian

Smear menstrual blood on lintels, thresholds, and doorknobs to create a protective boundary against malevolence of all kinds.

The Strongest Magickal Substance

Menstrual blood is considered the strongest magickal substance on Earth. Some scholars argue that the historical oppression of women stems not from perceptions of menstruation as "unclean," but from fear of women's power. Menstruating women were isolated in many cultures specifically because of their perceived potency. In parts of Thailand, women were historically discouraged from riding the upper level of double-decker buses, as placing their genitals above men's heads was believed to destroy male power along with the efficacy of their amulets. Some practitioners hold that all magick ultimately stems from menstrual and lunar mysteries, and that all human civilization stems from cultural responses to menstruation.

Moon-Time and Dragon-Time

Menstruation is sometimes called a woman's moon-time, reflecting the connection between menstrual cycles, pregnancy, tides, and lunar phases. It is also known as dragon-time, with the dragon serving as a metaphor for menstruation, a perspective that opens new interpretations of dragon-slaying myths, particularly those involving the rescue of virginal princesses.

Historical Availability

Menstrual blood was once commercially available. In ancient Italy, spiritual goods merchants sold used menstrual rags to those who could not provide their own.
