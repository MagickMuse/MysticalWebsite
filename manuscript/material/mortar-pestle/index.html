Mortars and pestles are ancient, primal tools used for grinding. The modern mortar and pestle is now most commonly used to grind herbs, whether for cooking, healing or spell-casting, but it was once also used to prepare flax for spinning.

Why the Mortar and Pestle is Irreplaceable

More sophisticated grinding tools such as food processors have largely replaced the mortar and pestle for the purposes of cooking, but for magickal purposes the mortar and pestle is irreplaceable for two reasons:

Manual Connection: The manual act of grinding puts one literally in touch with the spell-casting materials. By simultaneously concentrating on one's desired goal and visualizing its accomplishment, one is able to insert one's intentions and desires into a spell in a way that merely pressing a button will not afford you.

Sacred Symbolism: The act of grinding with the mortar and pestle metaphorically reproduces sexual intercourse. What is a magick spell after all but an act of magickal creation, the birth of a new reality or new outcome? Mimicking the literal, physical process further empowers the spell as well as imbuing it with sacred male and female energies.

If one lacks a mortar and pestle, it is preferable to substitute manual methods for grinding materials rather than automated ones. Place the material between folded wax paper and use a rolling pin or hammer as a pulverizing tool.

Materials and Variations

Mortars and pestles come in all sizes, from huge to miniscule, and are crafted from various materials: stone, terracotta, glass, brass, and marble. Usually made from marble, granite, or another hard stone, mortars and pestles are used to grind herbs into smaller pieces or a fine powder. They're useful for making incense and charm bags, as well as mixing herbs of different textures for charms and spells.

When searching for this valuable tool, don't be tempted by cheaper wooden versions; they are too soft to grind resins and will absorb oils, leading to rancid smells. Best to stay with stone or coffee grinders.

The molcajete, the traditional Mexican variant on the mortar and pestle, is created from volcanic rock.

Coffee Grinders as Alternatives

Coffee grinders serve as an effective modern alternative to traditional mortars and pestles for grinding herbs and resins.

The Hermes Myth

The sexual symbolism of the mortar and pestle is made explicit in a lesser-known Greek myth. The more famous account of the discovery of fire credits its theft to Prometheus, but in an alternate version, Hermes, the Opener of Ways, first creates fire by vigorously grinding the pestle in the mortar. The act of creation is thus embedded in the tool itself.

Witches' Transportation

Mortars and pestles were once visualized as witches' transportation devices, perhaps a subtle reference to the use of psychoactive herbs in witches' flying ointments. The ointments would likely have been prepared using mortars and pestles.

Baba Yaga: Baba Yaga flies in a mortar and uses the pestle as her steering device.

Maritime Travel: Witches and goddesses, according to various legends, convert mortars into boats and travel across the sea in them.

Flying Tools: Witches were sometimes envisioned riding pestles like others ride brooms.

Moon Magick

While not the first tool most moon witches reach for, a mortar and pestle becomes genuinely useful as lunar practice deepens. Typically constructed from stone, it pairs a heavy bowl with a rounded, club-shaped pestle designed to grind resins and dried plants into fine powder or blended mixtures. As you begin crafting your own lunar incenses, herbal powders for candle dressing, or custom blends for charm bags and sachets, the mortar and pestle earns a permanent spot on your altar. Grinding by hand also creates a direct physical connection to your materials, allowing intention to flow into the working with every motion.
