Symbolism and Sacred Union

Brooms represent the perfect union of male and female energies: the stick represents the male force plunged into and attached to the female straw. This symbolism parallels sacred images found in Himalayan shrines where stone phallus and vulva unite, but the broom is a symbol anyone can spontaneously craft by simply attaching straw to a branch or stick.

According to Rhiannon Ryall's West Country Wicca, "broom" was old English country slang for women's genitals. "Riding the broom" was thus slang for intercourse, and "Riding the witch's broom" referenced ritual copulation.

Origins and Ritual Purpose

Although brooms are now associated with housecleaning, they may originally have been invented for magickal and spiritual rites. The act of sweeping was a ritual act. The chore remained after the spiritual aspects were suppressed or forgotten. Depending on direction, sweeping over a threshold manipulates energy in or out, inviting or repelling.

Midwifery and Protection

In ancient Greece and Anatolia, brooms were the professional emblem of midwifery, similar to modern pawnbrokers' balls. Midwives were expected to magickally supervise the birthing chamber, keeping it free from malevolent spirits and negative spiritual debris. Magickal protection rituals often incorporated sweeping, especially sweeping over vulnerable thresholds.

The broom was among the sacred attributes of Hekate, Matron of Midwives and Witches. In recent years, the broom has evolved into an emblem of witchcraft, displayed as a badge of pride and a device to memorialize the Burning Times.

Agricultural and Fertility Rites

Brooms were used in agricultural fertility rites: women danced on brooms, men on pitchforks. This connection to fertility magick demonstrates the broom's role beyond simple cleaning.

Male Associations

Brooms are men's tools too, although generally without the long broomstick. Herne, Faunus, and other horned gods carry short brooms, usually switches or whisks made from birch branches. In Europe, Santa Claus' dark helpers, like Krampus, usually carry this type of switch or broom. Older images of Santa Claus sometimes depict him wielding a whip.

This birch whisk remains a popular tool in the sauna and Russian bathhouse and may derive from shamanic roots.

Soul-Journeying and Flight

The most famous thing witches do with brooms is ride them. One theory suggests brooms originated as a shamanic tool for soul-journeying. The witch's broom may have originated as a shamanic spirit horse. A hobbyhorse is essentially a broomstick with a horse's head instead of a broom head.

In many witchcraft traditions, a broom alone is insufficient for flight: incantations and especially flying ointments may also be necessary components. The connection of the broom with soul-journeying may not be merely metaphoric. It is widely believed that the broomstick was a traditional tool used for topical applications of witches' flying ointments.

Baba Yaga flies in a mortar and steers with her pestle, but she uses a broom to sweep away her traces, protecting herself from foot-track magick.

Aztec and Central American Traditions

In Mexico and Central America, brooms and the act of sweeping are symbolic of ritual purification. Central Mexican codices display grass brooms placed beside crossroads, the traditional place for depositing spiritually dangerous or potentially contaminating items.

The broom is the emblem of the Aztec midwife-witch goddess Tlazolteotl as surely as it is that of her Eurasian counterpart, Hekate.

Types of Brooms

Various types of brooms may be used in magickal practice:

  • Single-use ritual brooms are loosely assembled from botanical material and taken apart and dispersed immediately after use
  • Special ritual brooms are reserved exclusively for ritual work, as beautifully carved and crafted as any magick wand or sword. These are particularly popular among modern pagans and Wiccans
  • Household brooms are the plain, everyday broom used to sweep the floor, and the staple of Hoodoo banishing spells. Spiritual and mundane cleansings may be accomplished simultaneously through the use of ritual floorwashes

To Sweep or Not to Sweep

In some traditions, such as modern paganism and Wicca, a ritual broom may never touch the floor, let alone dust anything. Brooms are reserved strictly for ritual use. Hoodoo, on the other hand, is practical magick: a household broom serves both mundane and magickal purposes, often simultaneously. Floorwashes accomplish two goals at once: a magick spell is cast and the floor is cleaned. The act of cleaning effectively casts the spell.

This distinction does not necessarily reflect a split between European and African-derived magickal systems. Hoodoo's extensive use of the broomstick may derive from its European roots. However, maintaining an extensive collection of expensive tools reserved solely for ritual use carries economic implications. One needs space to store these items as well as privacy to practice safely. For those without such luxuries, mundane activities can camouflage magickal ones. Spells may be discreetly yet consistently cast during everyday activities like sweeping or scrubbing the front steps.

Construction

The most basic witch's broomstick is constructed from an ash stick, which provides consistent magickal protection, bound with birch twigs meant to entangle low-level malicious spirits. An alternative choice is willow twigs, as willow is the tree belonging to Hekate, Dark Moon Goddess and supreme teacher of magick (Circe and Medea rank among her finest students).

Altar Brooms

Small handcrafted brooms are designed to be placed and maintained on altars. These miniature brooms should be embellished to coincide with their purpose. For instance, a small broom dedicated to sea spirits might be decorated with sea glass and shells.

Protective Magick

Purification and protection are closely linked: brooms are also used for protective magick. The footprints one leaves behind are believed particularly vulnerable to malevolent magick. An old spell suggests dragging a broom behind you to sweep away your traces so no enemies can work magick on your footprints.

Love Magick

In Spanish witchcraft, brooms are used in love spells, sometimes dressed up as women. There are legends of witches who could make these brooms dance.

Marriage Customs

Jumping the broomstick once indicated a marriage unsanctioned by the Church. It was a British folk custom used by Romany people. Slaves in the former British colonies were married by jumping the broomstick. The tradition has regained popularity among African-Americans as well as in Wiccan and Neo-Pagan handfastings.

Protective Traditions

Other traditions include:

  • Never step over a broom (considered unlucky; the antidote is to step back over it backwards)
  • Lean a broom against or across a door to keep enemies away. They will be unable to cross your threshold
  • Place a broom across a doorway at night to keep witches, ghosts, and spirits away
