Overview

Fans serve a variety of magical purposes across many traditions, both as substitutes for other tools and as powerful instruments in their own right.

As Magical Substitutes

A folding fan, held closed, may substitute for a magic wand. Fans may also substitute for broomsticks. In the Arabian Nights, an enchantress reveals herself by riding a fan.

The Sandalwood Fan

Chinese magic favors the sandalwood fan, especially for protective purposes. The fan creates a personal psychic and spiritual shield. Sandalwood possesses sacred, benevolent, and protective qualities that may be enhanced through ornamentation: fans are frequently embellished with magical images and words of power.

Maintenance

Strengthen and maintain the fan's power by periodically passing it through sandalwood incense smoke.

Care and Storage

As with wands or staffs, fans are not tools to be shared. When not in use, the fan should rest discreetly hidden, wrapped in fabric and placed in a magic box.

Peacock Feather Fans

The powerful Yoruba orisha Oshun counts peacocks among her sacred birds. Devotees keep peacock feather fans in her honor, using them in rituals and keeping them on her altar when not in use.

Peacock feather fans are also appropriate for use with other spirits, including the Hindu Lakshmi and Hera (Roman Juno).

Feather Fans in Native Traditions

Fans crafted from various types of feather, or from an intact bird's wing complete with feathers, are used in Native American and Native American-influenced traditions as a device for wafting incense, especially for cleansing purposes.
