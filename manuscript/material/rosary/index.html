A rosary is a string of beads used to count repetitive prayers. Most commonly associated with Catholic devotion, it structures meditative prayer through cycles of repeated words anchored by muscle memory, freeing the conscious mind to enter a receptive trance state. The standard Catholic rosary consists of five decades of ten beads each, with larger beads marking the boundary prayers.

In magickal practice, the rosary has been widely adapted. Practitioners have made rosaries from bones, hag-stones, semi-precious beads, and found objects. The prayers can be changed to suit the tradition: goddess-focused practitioners have replaced patriarchal language with matriarchal equivalents, and spirit workers use bead strings to count offerings or invocations.

The rosary functions as both a physical offering and a trance induction tool. Its repetitive, rhythmic use bypasses the conscious mind and supports sustained communication with spirits, ancestors, and the divine.
