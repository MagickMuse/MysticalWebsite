Magick Belts

This category of magickal tool encompasses anything crafted with magickal intent that circles the waist or hips, from the Wiccan cingulam to traditional waist beads. The term "girdle" was once widely used for this category, carrying strong connotations of seduction and magickal power before falling out of fashion.

Aphrodite and Ishtar both wear love-inducing belts or girdles that they have been known to lend to others in need. The belt is primarily associated with women's magick, though not exclusively so. Even as a women's article, it extends far beyond love and seduction. Aphrodite and Ishtar both possess martial aspects, and belts are associated with the powerful female warrior Orisha, Oya. Belts also symbolize that one's loins are girded, and they appear in fierce protective women's power spells.

Historical Background

Magick belts were once far more prevalent than they are today. In Cro-Magnon graves, women were buried wearing cowrie shell belts positioned across the abdominal area, indicating their connection to fertility. Amulets and mojo bags were hung from these belts.

Ishtar's magick belt finds its descendants in the spangled belts of belly dancers. The most powerful belt material may be shed snakeskin, used for enhanced fertility and easier childbirth. The word "shed" is essential here: the skin must not be taken by force. The cooperation of the snake's power is required, with fertility understood as the snake's gift.

Waist Beads

In traditional African practice, waist beads were considered highly erotic and seductive, and therefore private. To some extent, beads shifted to the neck in certain contexts to offset this aspect. Belts and waist beads may also serve public and communal rituals beyond their intimate associations.

The Cingulam

The cingulam is the traditional Wiccan belt: a silk cord nine feet in length. Traditionally employed in symbolic binding rituals, it may be a single red braided cord or three cords braided together in red, black, and white.

The cord is also used to measure and define the circumference of a coven circle:

  1. Hold one end of the cord at the center of the circle
  2. Mark the center with a stone, crystal, salt, chalk, or similar marker
  3. Rotate the other end around the perimeter, using the cord as a compass
