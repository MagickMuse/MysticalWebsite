The Mirror as Magickal Object

The ancient Egyptian word for mirror is a pun for life. This connection is made explicit by the shape of the handle-hand mirror, which echoes the ankh, the symbol of life carried by all Egyptian deities with the sole exception of Osiris, Lord of the Dead. The visual echo of this relationship survives today in the akua'ba, the fertility-affirming doll from Ghana whose distinctive shape also recalls the ankh.

Although the mirror is a reflective tool, it is also believed to possess powers of absorption. Like a crystal, mirrors absorb and store information. Anything reflected in a mirror over an extended period is believed to be retained within it, accessible for future use. For this reason, the mirror is a primary tool for drawing down lunar and solar energy. It also carries a reputation as a soul catcher, a use that may be positive or harmful depending on the intent of the practitioner.

The earliest mirrors were not made from glass but from natural materials polished until they showed a reflection.

What is Scrying?

Scrying is divination by means of gazing into something reflective, such as pooled water or a dark mirror, until images can be seen. Cauldrons are traditional vessels for this. It generally takes a lot of patience and practice to become good at scrying.

Modern mirrors are commonly crafted from glass but ancient mirrors were usually created from smooth metal, usually copper, highly polished to be reflective.

Magickal Uses

Magick mirrors are popular in many traditions including ancient Egyptian, Chinese, Western Ceremonial, Aztec, and Italian folk magick. They are used for the following purposes:

Divination and Scrying: Mirrors serve as portals for viewing hidden knowledge and future events.

Love Magick: Used to attract romance and enhance beauty.

Lunar Magick: Connected to feminine mysteries and moon energy.

Protective Spells: Repel malevolent forces and the Evil Eye.

Spirit-Summoning: Facilitate communication with spiritual entities.

Archaeological Evidence

Mirrors are frequently found among the remains of Scythian priestesses and/or queens, indicating their importance to people of high status. Mirrors were also found among the grave goods of women at Çatal Hüyük, one of the world's oldest known settlements.

Deity Associations

Hathor

Hathor, among the most primordial of Egyptian deities, presides over beauty, love, sex, fertility, romance, cosmetics, magick, and copper, the material from which ancient Egyptian mirrors were crafted. She and copper share the same essence: to hold a mirror in your hand is to hold Hathor.

This was made explicit in ancient Egyptian mirrors, which very frequently incorporated an image of Hathor's face and characteristic flip hair-do into the mirror's handle. To hold a Hathor mirror and gaze into it is to absorb a little of the goddess' own beauty, power, and essence.

Tezcatlipoca

Tezcatlipoca is known as the Lord of the Smoking Mirror. The omniscient, all-knowing Aztec Lord of Sorcery, Tezcatlipoca observes everything in his obsidian mirror. He is the equivalent of the All-Seeing Eye. Tezcatlipoca was the sponsor of Aztec shamans and sorcerers.

Pre-European-conquest Aztec wizards used magick mirrors for divination and spiritual communication; the practice remains popular in Mexico and Central America. The most famous of the Aztec obsidian mirrors belonged to Dr. John Dee and is now in the collection of the British Museum.

Aztec Mirrors

The most prized Aztec magick mirrors were crafted from obsidian, a rare natural volcanic glass. Obtaining sufficient quantities of suitable quality was difficult, so obsidian mirrors were reserved for spiritual use exclusively. More common Aztec mirrors were made from iron pyrite.

The obsidian mirror is explicitly linked to Tezcatlipoca, the Smoking Mirror, an omniscient and sometimes malevolent deity who observes all things through his mirror. A preserved idol from the British Museum's collection depicts Tezcatlipoca made entirely from polished obsidian, carrying a mirror of polished gold.

Because of the widespread destruction of Aztec culture, the full scope of how these mirrors were used may never be known. What remains clear is that they were employed for divination (scrying) and interactive spiritual communication, a practice that remains alive among traditional occultists in Mexico and Central America. Following the Spanish conquest of Mexico, obsidian mirrors arrived in Europe; the most famous belonged to Dr. John Dee and is now in the British Museum.

Chinese Magick Mirrors

Chinese occult traditions employ mirrors more consistently and inventively than virtually any other. One historical account holds that a man became Emperor of China through the use of a magick mirror. The favored Chinese magick mirror is a small or moderately sized circular mirror attached to a handle, crafted preferably from gold, though other precious metals may be used. As with the Italian mirror, its power is enhanced by decorating the back with auspicious power symbols.

Mirrors in Chinese tradition are used to capture and focus energy. While mirror magick originally derived from lunar practice, these mirrors may also be employed to channel the power of the sun.

Modern Practice

Magick mirrors remain popular with witches, spiritualists, and Pow-Wow artists.

Feminine and Lunar Associations

Because magick mirrors derive from the tradition of scrying in water, they are associated with feminine, lunar mysteries and energy. Ancient mirrors were most commonly crafted from copper, traditionally considered a feminine metal. Copper is identified with the planet Venus and with powerful goddesses of love and beauty like Aphrodite, Hathor, and Oshun.

This feminine identification applies strongly to modern glass mirrors as well, which bear even stronger resemblance to the moon or to a body of water like a lake.

Solar Charging

⚠️
Safety is essential. Do not look into the mirror while it faces the sun. Keep eyes averted. Do not perform this near dry paper, leaves, botanicals, or anything flammable. Ensure no one is nearby who could be blinded or burned. This ritual must not be attempted by children.

Solar charging is among the simplest yet most potent methods of empowering a mirror. The sun is so powerful that only seconds of direct exposure are needed:

  1. On a bright, sunny day, hold a small mirror in the palm of the hand, tilted to catch direct sunlight
  2. Maintain this position for no more than a few seconds; nine seconds is the absolute maximum
  3. The mirror is now charged
  4. Reserve this mirror exclusively for magickal use. If it is accidentally used for another purpose, it must be cleansed, reconsecrated, and the process repeated

Common Uses for a Magick Mirror

Any mirror may be converted into a magick mirror. Reproductions of ancient Egyptian, Italian, or Chinese mirrors may be used, or a practitioner may create their own. Common uses include:

  • Divination and scrying
  • Spiritual communication
  • Spell-work
  • Romantic or protective amulets
  • Banishing spells

For spells, spiritual communication, or amulet use, a small and discreet mirror is preferable. For divination, whether personal or professional, a larger mirror may be used openly.

Spirit Detection

Mirrors can function as detectors for negative spiritual entities, operating something like a metaphysical smoke alarm. In theory, any large and powerful mirror is capable of detecting spirits. An antique wall mirror works well for this purpose. Chinese tradition advises that a mirror designated for spirit detection should not be used for any other purpose, and should be kept covered when not in use.

Making a Magick Mirror

Basic Western Ceremonial Mirror

A good all-purpose mirror, built from scratch. A round, concave piece of glass is required.

  1. Coat the back (convex side) with matte black paint, applied thickly but smoothly and evenly
  2. Allow the paint to dry completely
  3. Mount the mirror on cardboard or similar backing
  4. Place in a picture frame and embellish as desired

Grimoire-Based Mirror

Best suited for spell-casting, this method begins with a commercially manufactured mirror.

  1. Purchase a mirror
  2. Inscribe angelic formulas on the back (the backing may need to be carefully removed). Formulas can be found in classical grimoires such as The Key of Solomon or The Black Pullet. Dragon's blood ink is a suitable substitute for the traditionally recommended blood ink
  3. Bury the mirror at a crossroads under the full moon, marking the spot for retrieval
  4. Dig it up after three days

Pow-Wow Mirror

Most frequently used for scrying.

  1. Obtain clear glass in the desired size
  2. Construct a three-sided frame so the mirror can be easily slid in and out
  3. Cleanse and consecrate the glass
  4. Make a strong infusion by pouring boiling water over chamomile, dragon's blood, and eyebright
  5. Let the infusion cool, then use it to paint one side of the glass with a brush; allow to dry fully
  6. Paint over the same side again with black paint, resulting in one black side and one unpainted side
  7. Slide into the frame with the unpainted side facing up

When scrying with this mirror, point it toward the physical direction of whoever or whatever is being sought. Tradition varies on whether anyone else should ever look into a personal magick mirror: some hold that no one else should ever see inside it, while others hold that an animal familiar should always have the first look.

Consecrating a Mirror

  1. Add three spoonfuls of sea salt to a dish of spring water
  2. Stir gently until dissolved
  3. Using a new cloth or natural sponge, cleanse the mirror's surface with this solution
  4. Gently wipe dry with another new cloth

Truth and Enlightenment

The Buddhist Mirror of Dharma reveals the causes of past actions, connecting the mirror to enlightenment as well as truth. For Tibetan Buddhists the Wisdom of the Great Mirror teaches, much like Plato's cave of shadows, that reflected things are only another aspect of the Void. In Japanese myth the mirror of the goddess Amaterasu draws light from a dark cavern and beams it back into the world.

Omens and the Mirror of the Soul

A broken mirror is a symbol of doom said to bring seven years' bad luck, though burying the glass in thick cloth was thought to limit the harm. The eye, called the Mirror of the Soul, is itself a reflective surface believed to reveal what lies hidden within.

Reflective Cousins

Related reflective charms include the Witch Ball and the Bagua Mirror, both used to turn unwanted influences away.

Protective Uses

Mirrors frequently serve as protective amulets as they are believed to repel the Evil Eye. Small mirrors are sewn onto clothing and furnishings. They are hung from the rear view mirrors of automobiles.

Mirrors repel the malevolent forces that Feng Shui terms "poison arrows." The ba gua is an octagonal mirror placed outside the house to repel these dangerous forces and provide safety, security, and stability for the inhabitants.

A mirror is a reflective surface used in folk magick, witchcraft, and ceremonial traditions for scrying, protection, and deflection of harmful energy. Mirrors are considered liminal objects that stand between the seen and unseen worlds.

Magickal Uses

Mirrors are used to reflect the Evil Eye and malevolent spirits away from a home or person. Placed facing outward at a door or window, a mirror turns negative energy back toward its source. They are also used in scrying, divination, and as protective amulets hung near cradles and sleeping areas.
