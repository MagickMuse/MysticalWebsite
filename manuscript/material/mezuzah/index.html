📜Mezuzah

Notes/Warnings
A required fixture on the doorposts of Jewish buildings under religious law.
Also called
Mezuza
Category1
💎Adornments

Overview

In Hebrew, mezuzah means "doorpost," but the word commonly refers to the small case fixed to the doorposts of Jewish buildings, whether homes, offices, or shops. Inside is a piece of parchment carrying two verses from the Book of Deuteronomy. Jewish religious law requires a mezuzah on every doorpost.

The Name of God

On the reverse of the parchment is written Shaddai, one of the names of God, understood here as a notarikon (an acronym) for a phrase meaning "Guardian of the Doors of Israel."

Protection at the Threshold

Because a doorway marks a passage between two states of being, or between this world and the next, it calls for protection. A mezuzah made correctly and fixed with due reverence is a powerful protective tool. Many Jewish people touch or kiss it as they pass by.
