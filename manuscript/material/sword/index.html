Magick swords have historically played a role in Chinese, Japanese, Jewish, and Persian magickal traditions. They remain popular in East Asian magick, High Ceremonial Magick, and modern Wicca.

Form and Materials

Magick swords may be actual functional swords complete with sharp blades or ceremonial replicas. Swords invoke primal metal magick although wooden swords also exist, particularly in East Asia. Swords are powerfully associated with the primordial magickal traditions of metalworking.

Energetic Qualities

Swords radiate masculine energy; the scabbard is its feminine partner.

Elemental Associations

Swords are most commonly associated with the element air[1], however this is controversial; some also identify swords with fire. Swords do not fit neatly into elemental categories being the product of all elements. The raw material for swords is dug from Earth and the process of crafting a sword involves the interplay of air, fire, and water.

Individualized Swords

Historically, each sword was crafted to order and measured to suit its bearer, as precisely as a magick wand. A fine sword was regarded as much an individual as a person: each had a name, distinctive qualities, and was believed to project a personality of its own. A legendary sword of this kind can be observed in the film Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon.

Sacred Crafting

Crafting swords was a secret, magickal operation. Spell-casting and spiritual invocation was once involved in the creation of powerful magick sword (and in some cases it still is). Rumors periodically circulated that blood sacrifice, including human sacrifice, was required to forge magick swords. Vestiges of these legends survive in Japanese mythology.

Chinese Sword Traditions

In Chinese tradition, the most valued magick sword is one inherited from a renowned and consistently successful warrior, even if the new owner intends to use it solely for magickal purposes. When such a sword is unavailable, the next best option is a peach wood or iron blade consecrated in the name of a famous sword it is meant to represent. Willow and mulberry wood are also favored materials.

Small Chinese amulet swords offer an accessible alternative. Constructed from coins and red silk cord and decorated with a complex series of knots and tassels, they are readily available through feng shui and Chinatown markets. These coin swords function as protective devices and as talismans for balancing and improving finances.

Western Sword Traditions

In Western magick, swords once belonging to famous warriors carry their own powerful associations. Weapons formerly owned by the Knights Templar, that elite knightly order disbanded and condemned in part due to accusations of occult practice, are considered priceless magickal tools.

Magickal Embellishments

Swords may be engraved with runes, sigils, Names of Power, Kabalistic inscriptions or other magickal embellishments.

Ritual Uses

They are used for casting circles and for various magickal practices including:

Protection spells: Warding and defensive magick

Exorcisms: Banishing unwanted entities

Spirit-summoning: Invoking spiritual presences

Banishing spells: Removing negative energies or influences

Childbirth protection: Drawing a full circle around a laboring woman to defy lurking spirits

Anti-Lilith crib ward: Placed beneath an infant's crib or laid across the top to protect the newborn from Lilith

Weapon Protection

Before weapons were mass produced, smiths customarily performed protective rituals on a blade before handing it over to its owner. To protect a sword or a gun, bathe it in water blended from three or seven separate wells.

Storage

When not in use, swords are kept wrapped in fabric, particularly red silk.

Tarot Connection

Swords are among the four Tarot suits, corresponding to the playing card suit of Spades.
