Ritual Bowls

Practitioners typically maintain three separate bowls for ritual work: one for salt, one for water, and one for the libation offering. Each serves a distinct purpose in ceremony and should be kept exclusively for magical use.

Purpose and Placement

The salt bowl holds consecrated salt representing the Earth element. The water bowl contains purified water for blessing and cleansing. The libation bowl receives offerings of wine, mead, or other beverages poured in honor of deities and spirits during ritual.

Materials

Bowls may be crafted from ceramic, wood, metal, glass, or stone. Many practitioners prefer natural materials that resonate with the element the bowl represents. The size should be practical for the intended use while fitting comfortably on the altar.

Moon Magick

Because the moon governs the element of water, a bowl is one of the most natural lunar tools to keep on hand. Use it to create Moon Water by filling it and setting it out under the full moon, or to hold water for other ritual uses throughout the lunar cycle. For water scrying, a black bowl is especially effective; gazing into still, dark water to receive visions and psychic impressions is a practice found across many cultures and traditions. Bowls also serve the lunar altar beautifully as containers for salt, consecrated water, or libation offerings during esbat and moon rituals.
