Prayer Flag

◇Notes/Warnings
⚠️ Sacred to Tibetan Buddhism. The five colors represent the elements: blue (space), white (water), red (fire), green (air), and yellow (earth). As sacred items they should never touch the ground.

Buddhabodhiprabhavasita

Prayer flags of the Tibetan Buddhist tradition are lengths of colorful cloth that bear images, mantras, and prayers. They take two forms, either horizontal strings of many flags or single vertical flags, and both traditionally mark the sacred landscape, fluttering among the mountains, temples, stupas, and monasteries of Nepal and Tibet, though they now appear far more widely.

Colors and Designs

A typical set comes in five differently colored cloths printed from woodblocks, each color standing for one of the five elements. The order and meaning runs blue for space, white for water, red for fire, green for air, and yellow for earth. The designs carry mantras and auspicious symbols such as the Triratna.

Belief

Tibetans hold that the prayers printed on the flags are carried to the gods, which is why they are raised in high places. As the colors fade, the prayers are believed to become a permanent part of the universe. Fresh strings are hung alongside the faded and tattered older ones, an image of the continual renewal of life.

⚠️ Prayer flags are sacred to Tibetan Buddhism. In common with other sacred items, they should never touch the ground. Treat them with the respect owed to a living devotional practice rather than as decoration.
