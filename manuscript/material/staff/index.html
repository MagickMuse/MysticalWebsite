The distinction between staffs and wands often comes down to size. The staff is longer, thicker, and often doubles as a walking stick. A staff should be long and sturdy enough to lean on comfortably. Thus a tall person requires a longer staff than a shorter person.

Wizards and Divine Associations

Staffs are now identified primarily as wizards' tools. The most famous modern image derives from J.R.R. Tolkien's Lord of the Rings cycle, whose wizards Gandalf and Saruman do battle with their staffs. Staffs are also closely identified with the biblical Moses[1] and his opponents, powerful Egyptian magickians.

Deities identified with staffs include Odin[2] and Eshu-Elegbara[3]. When Hermes[4] isn't carrying his caduceus, he substitutes a shepherd's staff instead.

Practical and Magickal Uses

Like most magickal tools, staffs have other uses besides magick, being associated with walking sticks and shepherds' staffs.

The staff is not a subtle tool: it's a big piece of wood. When walking sticks, and perhaps walking in general, fell from fashion, so did the magickal staff. Its niche has somewhat been filled by the smaller, and thus more subtle and versatile, magick wand.

Obeah and the Staff of Moses

Magick staffs are now most profoundly identified with Obeah, the African-derived magickal and spiritual traditions of the British West Indies. The Obeah or Obi Stick is a carved wooden staff, frequently ornamented with a serpentine motif. The simpler ones are carved so that a snake-like groove encircles the staff. The more elaborate Staff of Moses usually features a snake carved from bottom to top.

Nordic Traditions

Staffs were once also powerfully identified with Nordic spiritual and magickal traditions. Staffs were engraved with powerful runes to further empower and direct their inherent energies. Many traditions recommend hollowing out a staff and filling it with botanicals, amulets or other magickally empowered materials.

Sacred Energy and Symbolism

Staffs radiate male magickal energy; they are the direct descendants of sacred phallic poles, especially those carried in Dionysian processions. Wooden staffs radiate the power of the type of tree they were crafted from.

Tarot Connection

Staffs, also called staves, are among the Tarot's four suits[5], corresponding to the playing card suit of Clubs.

Forms of the Staff

The staff takes many shapes beyond the wizard's tool. The augur carried the curved lituus, the bishop bears a crozier that recalls a shepherd's crook, and the Buddhist monk's khakkhara supports his steps in the manner of a pilgrim's staff. The magick wand is a smaller and more subtle relative of the same family.

World Tree and Authority

The staff that Joseph of Arimathea is said to have pushed into the ground at Glastonbury, which then sprouted into the mystical Glastonbury Thorn, shows the staff as an image of the World Tree and so of the Axis Mundi. As a phallic symbol it joins Heaven to Earth through the person who wields it and directs its power, which also makes it a symbol of authority. In this elemental reading the staff is associated with fire.
