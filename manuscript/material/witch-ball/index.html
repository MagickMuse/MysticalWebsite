Overview

A witch ball is a large, hollow glass sphere, often finished with a mirrored or otherwise reflective surface. The earliest recorded use dates to 1690, and the charm grew popular through the eighteenth century, hung in windows to repel evil forces, who were said to flee at the sight of their own hideous reflection. Its bright, mirrored surface turns the malevolence of unwanted spirits back upon them, working on the same principle as the Bagua Mirror and charms used to repel the Evil Eye. It survives today as both a protective and a decorative charm.

How It Works

Some witch balls held strands of thread inside the hollow interior. These were meant to absorb an evil spirit, which would then be ensnared among the strands and unable to escape. In this way the witch ball serves much the same purpose as the Native American dreamcatcher, trapping harmful energies that arrive in disguise.

Legacy

The witch ball is most likely the ancestor of the brightly colored reflective balls now hung on Christmas trees.
