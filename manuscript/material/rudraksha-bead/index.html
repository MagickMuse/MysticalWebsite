Rudraksha Bead

The rudraksha bead is the fruit of the Rudraksha tree, Elaeocarpus ganitrus, which grows across the northern reaches of India and Pakistan. The fruit shrivels to a hard, woodlike texture and is revered as sacred, said to be formed from the tears Shiva shed for humanity. Legend holds that the first tear to fall to earth became the first rudraksha tree. The name is read either as red eyed or as Rudra, meaning Shiva, joined with Aksha, meaning eye, so the bead is understood as the Eye of Shiva and worn as a powerful guard against the Evil Eye.

Mukhti and Value

A bead's worth is judged in part by its number of mukhti, or facets. The rarest and most prized is the Ek Mukhti, the single faceted bead, which is flat and carries the oval eye shape that deepens its meaning.

Wearing and Use

The beads are sacred ornamentation governed by many rules of how, when, and by whom they may be worn; it is held improper, for example, for a menstruating woman to wear hers. Each bead is cleaned, blessed, and charged with power before use. Rudraksha are also strung into the japa mala, the sacred prayer strings of Hindu monks, which carry 108 beads after the sacred number of the Dharmic religions.
