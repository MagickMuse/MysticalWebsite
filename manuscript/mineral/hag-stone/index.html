Appearance

Pebbles or stones containing natural perforations (holes). The holes must be created by nature through erosion, water action, or other natural processes. Can be any size, color, or composition.

Origin

Found worldwide, typically along beaches, riverbeds, and shorelines where natural erosion creates holes through stones.

Attributes

Hag stones are among the most magical and powerful naturally occurring talismans. Their natural perforations make them exceptionally valuable in occult practice, as they cannot be artificially created. Boring a hole through a pebble is insufficient. The magic resides in nature's creation of the opening.

These stones bestow protection, enhance fertility, and possess the power to grant wishes. The hole serves as a portal between worlds, allowing beneficial energies to flow while blocking harmful influences.

Occultists consider hag stones valuable talismans that should be treasured when found. The discovery of a hag stone is considered fortuitous, as they reveal themselves only to those meant to find them.

Traditional Uses

As Personal Talisman: String on a cord and wear around the neck for continuous protection and to carry the stone's wish-granting power with you.

For Home Protection: Hang on a wall, over doorways, or above the bed to protect the household and its inhabitants. The hole allows positive energy to enter while blocking negative influences.

Wish Magic: Hold the stone, peer through the hole, and speak your wish into the opening. The hole acts as a conduit between the material and spiritual realms.

Fertility Magic: Worn or carried by those seeking to conceive, as the opening symbolizes both the womb and the gateway of new life.

Additional Notes

The naturally occurring hole is what distinguishes a hag stone from an ordinary pebble. This perforation must be created entirely by natural forces: water erosion, weathering, or the action of other stones. Any human modification negates the stone's magical properties.

Also called holed stones or holey stones in various traditions. Some cultures refer to them as Odin stones, fairy stones, or witch stones.

Spellworking

In the Eddas, Odin transmuted himself into a worm and slipped through a hole in a rock to steal "the mead of poetry." Perhaps because of this myth, holed stones were known as "Odin stones."

Stones with naturally occurring holes produced by erosion, wind or wave action, sea creatures, and other means have long been prized as protective objects.

Dream Enhancement

Holed stones are as effective for dream work as precious gemstones, and often more so. A naturally perforated stone provides protection, enhances clairvoyance, and supports all aspects of the dream process, including clarity, vividness, and psychic reception during sleep. When you find one, treat it as a significant gift. To access its dream properties, wear it on a cord around the neck before sleep, attach it to the headboard or bedpost, hang it on the wall above the bed, or tuck it beneath the pillow.

Protection Against Nightmares

Holey stones were hung on the bedpost to prevent nightmares. In England, they were tied with red ribbon and hung over the bed for the same purpose within recent years - a true survival of ancient magic.

General Protection

As a magical protectant, holey stones were worn around the neck, placed in the house, or hung from the front door. Hanging one near where a pet sleeps guards it.

Healing Ritual

To assist the body's healing processes, charge a holey stone to absorb the disease. Place this stone in a tub of warm, salted water, and soak in it for several minutes. Repeat once a day for a week. Cleanse the stone after this and repeat as necessary.

In England, wise women employed holey stones in healing rituals for children. The wise woman rubbed the sick child's body with the stone, magically removing the disease because the stone absorbed it. This rite was also performed on adults to maintain health.

Psychic Sight

In a wild and lonely place, preferably by moonlight, hold a holey stone up to one eye. Close the other one and peer through the stone. You may see visions, ghosts, or nonphysical entities.

Improving Eyesight

Looking through holey stones, in broad daylight, even at home, is said to improve eyesight.

Evil Eye and Vitality

Hag stones serve as powerful shields against the Evil Eye, one of the oldest and most pervasive forms of spiritual attack. The natural perforation disrupts harmful intent directed at the bearer, scattering the energy before it can take hold. Combined with their capacity to repel malevolent spirits and ill-willed individuals, this makes hag stones among the most reliable protective talismans available.

They also bolster physical vitality, restoring energy reserves and reinforcing the body's natural resilience. Wearing one on a cord around the neck or suspending it above the bed allows its restorative power to work steadily, even during sleep. These are gifts from the Earth herself, and they cannot be replicated by human hands. When one appears in your path, recognize it for the rare offering it is.

Folklore and Symbolism

A hag stone can form in any mineral, though in Britain the flint hag stone is the most common type found. Country tradition held that these stones could protect cattle from witches, who were said to take the animals out at night and ride them, leaving the farmer to find them filthy and exhausted by morning. Threaded with string or ribbon and hung above a doorway, the stone also kept milk from curdling. The belief ran so strong that some European farmers held a hag stone while milking so that the milk poured through the opening.

Part of the stone's protective power comes from its formation by water, since magick cannot work in running water and the stone keeps the resonance of that ancient idea. Stones with several holes were regarded as powerful tools for spell casting by the very witches they were named for, and a stone with three holes was especially potent because the number evoked the triple Goddess.

In the East the symbolism shifts. Camels were protected by hag stones, and because the holed stone resembles an eye it was used to guard against the evil eye. Looking through the hole was said to reveal another world, the realm of the fairies or of the spirits. The solid body of the stone represents the material world and the hole at its center the spirit world, the void, the nothing. For this reason the hoop shaped wreath is often used as a floral tribute at funerals.
