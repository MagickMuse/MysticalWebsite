Lore

Titanium was first discovered in 1795 by the German chemist Martin Klaproth, who named it after the Titans of Greek mythology for their legendary fortitude and power. As strong as steel but significantly less dense, titanium quickly became an ideal metal for use in aircraft, spacecraft, and missiles. Its remarkable resistance to corrosion by ocean water made it a popular choice for protecting the hulls of ships and submarines. Titanium dioxide is a common ingredient in house paint and sunscreen due to its ability to shield against UV light. In the metaphysical world, titanium is prized for its role in the aura treatment process, where it is bonded to quartz and other minerals to create the iridescent rainbow coatings seen on aura crystals, infusing them with the full spectrum of light energy.
