Sugilite is a cyclosilicate mineral in the Hexagonal crystal system with a Mohs hardness of 6 to 6.5. First discovered in 1944 on Iwagi Island in Japan, it is named after petrologist Ken-ichi Sugi who first identified it. Also known as Wesselite after the vibrant purple pockets found in the Wessels manganese mine in South Africa, Sugilite is regarded as one of the most potent healing crystals in modern practice. Its manganese content gives it a dense, rich purple coloring, and the stone's intense demand has driven prices dramatically upward over recent decades.

Appearance

Sugilite presents as opaque, lightly banded specimens, though rarely translucent pieces appear. Available in all sizes, it is often found tumbled. Coloring ranges from vibrant purple to deeper purple-black tones heavy with manganese. The deepest purple specimens have been largely mined out, leaving the darker, manganese-heavy stones more commonly available. Even lower-quality pieces carry potent healing energy.

Care

Sugilite sits at Mohs 6 to 6.5, making it moderately durable. It is both sun safe and water safe. Cleanse with running water, moonlight, smoke, or by placing on a selenite plate. Even smaller or lower-quality pieces carry strong healing energy, so do not discount specimens based on appearance alone. Store carefully to avoid scratching from harder stones.
