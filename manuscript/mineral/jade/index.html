Appearance

Translucent (Jadeite) or creamy (Nephrite), somewhat soapy feel. All sizes.

Origin

United States, China, Italy, Myanmar, Russia, Middle East.

Attributes

Jade is a symbol of purity and serenity. Much prized in the East, it signifies wisdom gathered in tranquility. Jade is associated with the heart chakra and increases love and nurturing. It is a protective stone, which keeps the wearer from harm and brings harmony. It is believed to attract good luck and friendship.

Psychologically, Jade stabilizes the personality and integrates the mind with the body. It promotes self-sufficiency. Mentally, Jade releases negative thoughts and soothes the mind. It stimulates ideas and makes tasks seem less complex so that they can be acted upon immediately.

Emotionally, Jade is a dream stone. Placed on the forehead, it brings insightful dreams. It aids emotional release, especially of irritability.

Spiritually, Jade encourages you to become who you really are. It assists in recognizing yourself as a spiritual being on a human journey and awakens hidden knowledge.

Physically, Jade is a cleansing stone, aiding the body's filtration and elimination organs. It is the stone par excellence for the kidneys. Jadeite and Nephrite have the same healing properties but individual colors have specific attributes.

Healing

Jade treats the kidneys and supra-adrenal glands, removes toxins, rebinds cellular and skeletal systems, and heals stitches. It assists fertility and childbirth. It works on the hips and spleen. Jade balances the fluids within the body and the water-salt/acid-alkaline ratios.

Position

Place or wear as appropriate. The Chinese believe that holding Jade transfers its virtues into the body.

Additional Notes

Blue/Blue-Green Jade symbolizes peace and reflection. It brings inner serenity and patience. It is the stone for slow but steady progress. It helps people who feel overwhelmed by situations beyond their control.

Brown Jade is strongly grounding. It connects to the earth and brings comfort and reliability. It aids in adjusting to a new environment.

Green Jade is the most common Jade. It calms the nervous system and channels passion in constructive ways. Green Jade can be used to harmonize dysfunctional relationships.

Lavender Jade alleviates emotional hurt and trauma and bestows inner peace. It teaches subtlety and restraint in emotional matters and sets clear boundaries.

Orange Jade is energetic and quietly stimulating. It brings joy and teaches the interconnectedness of all beings.

Red Jade is the most passionate and stimulating Jade. It is associated with love and letting off steam. It accesses anger, releasing tension in such a way that it can be constructive.

White Jade directs energy in the most constructive way. It filters distractions, emphasizing the best possible result, and aids decision-making as it pulls in relevant information.

Yellow Jade is energetic and stimulating but with a mellowness to it, bringing joy and happiness. It teaches the interconnectedness of all beings. It aids the digestive and elimination systems of the body.

Spellworking

Jade has been used to create musical instruments, including xylophones, gongs, and wind chimes. When struck, it produces a resonant tone. Such instruments were used in ritual in China, throughout Africa, and by the Hopi Indians.

Jade was and is a sacred stone in China. Altars of the Moon and Earth were fashioned of jade, as were images of Buddha and various deities. The stone was often included in grave goods because it was thought to lend vitality to the deceased. Jade carved in the image of two men was exchanged between males as a token of friendship.

In New Zealand, the Maori carve nephrite into images of ancestral figures called hei tiki, usually set with mother-of-pearl eyes, worn on ceremonial occasions.

Jade is believed to have power over the weather. It was thrown into water with great force in order to bring mist, rain, or snow.

Love

Jade is an ancient love-attracting stone. Carved into a butterfly, in China it is worn to draw love, or given to another in the hope of obtaining love. It was a frequent engagement gift from woman to man. Jade is also presented by a man to his bride before their wedding.

Healing

The soothing green color of jade is healing. Wearing the stone helps the body heal itself while working through nonphysical problems that manifested the disease. It is particularly helpful for kidney, heart, and stomach complaints. The ancient Mayans wore jade amulets to guard against kidney disease and bladder problems.

Longevity

The Chinese sensed in jade the power to prolong life. It was carved into images such as bats, bears, and storks, and worn for this purpose. Jade bowls were used in meals because the Chinese believed the stone's energy permeated the food.

Gardening

A piece of jade worn while gardening improves the health of plants. Four pieces buried along the garden's perimeter are also effective.

Money and Business

Wearing jade can bring money into your life. Charge a jade pendant with money-attracting energies, then wear it. When contemplating a business deal, hold jade in your receptive hand to be infused with prosperous energies.

Wisdom

Jade is worn, carried, or placed against the third eye to receive wisdom, not just knowledge, but assimilated knowledge rightly applied or withheld. Jade strengthens the mental faculties and helps reasoning.

Protection

Jade guards against accidents and mishaps that proper attentiveness can avert. It is placed on the altar with purple candles or worn during defensive magic.

Heritage and Symbolism

Jade occurs as two distinct minerals, jadeite and nephrite. The two are difficult to tell apart, though jadeite is the harder and rarer of the pair. While jade is thought of as green, it also forms in yellow, white, pink, mauve, and black. Apple green jade is the rarest shade. It was prized so highly that an Empress of China decreed that all carvings of this color belonged solely to the Imperial Collection, and so it became known as Imperial Jade.

Jade carries a long association with midwives. Kept near a pregnant woman, it was believed to ensure an easy birth. This belief may trace back to the descent of the Babylonian goddess Inanna-Ishtar into the Underworld. At each of the seven gates she surrendered an item of clothing, and at the fifth gate she removed her jade girdle, an object said to assist with childbirth.

Jade was also used to ease stomach pains, a remedy made famous by the Greek physician Galen. Nephritic jade shares a color close to that of the spleen, which was taken as a sign of its power to heal that organ, an association known as the Doctrine of Signatures. In the same way, dark red jade, like other red stones, was thought to support the flow of blood. In China a tonic of powdered jade was believed to soothe the nerves and act as a general restorative. Color guided the cure, since the greener the jade the more effective the treatment was held to be, green being the color of spring and renewal.

The physical qualities of jade shaped its symbolism. Its polish stood for knowledge and education, its smoothness for kindness, its strength for righteousness, and its rarity for purity.

Maori Jade

⚠️ The carving and use of jade within Maori tradition is a closed practice, sacred to the Maori people, and should be honored only by those within that culture. Maori practitioners regard jade as a stone of magickal power. Its protective force is said to grow stronger when the stone is carved into the forms of deities, as with the Manaia.
