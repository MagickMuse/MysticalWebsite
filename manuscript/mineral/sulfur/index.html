Appearance

Yellow, powdery or smallish translucent crystals on matrix.

Origin

Italy, Greece, South America, volcanic regions

Attributes

Sulphur has a negative electrical charge and is extremely useful for absorbing negative energies, emanations, and emotions. Placed in the environment, it absorbs negativity of any kind and removes barriers to progress.

Volcanically produced, this is an excellent stone for anything that erupts: feelings, violence, skin conditions, and fevers. It can also be useful for bringing latent psychic abilities to the surface.

Psychologically, Sulphur ameliorates willfulness and aids in identifying negative traits within the personality. Mentally, Sulphur blocks repetitive and distracting thought patterns.

WARNING: Sulphur is toxic and should not be taken internally. The gem elixir is best made with the crystalline form by the indirect method and applied externally only.

Healing

Sulphur is extremely useful for conditions that flare up, such as infections and fevers. Placed over the site of the swelling, it reduces fibrous and tissue growths. Placed in the bathwater, or as an essence, Sulphur alleviates painful swellings and joint problems. It can be applied externally to heal skin conditions.

Position

Place or hold as appropriate (the crystalline form is better for this, as the powdery form is messy). It is traditional that Sulphur that has been placed over growths be buried afterward. If not thoroughly cleanse before reusing. Make the elixir by the indirect method and use externally only.

Spellworking

Magickal Lore

Sulfur is a yellow mineral that, when burned, emits a powerful, familiar smell. This odor and its coloring have caused people to use it in magick for centuries.

During the height of ceremonial magick, sulfur was often burned to drive off "demons" and "devils." This related to the concept that positive forces were attracted by sweet scents, while negative forces abhorred foul odors and would flee from them. Later, sulfur was burned as a magickal fumigant to protect animals and dwellings from "fascination," or magickal enslavement.

Healing Charm

Sulfur was prescribed until quite recently for colds, rheumatism, and bodily pain. It was usually placed in a small red bag and worn around the neck.

Protection

Pieces of sulfur are placed on the altar during protective rituals or in the home as a general magickal "ward."

Name and the Alchemical Trinity

Alongside salt and mercury, sulphur forms the trinity of substances that early alchemists held to express the workings of nature, the three primes from which all bodies were thought to arise. A bright yellow chemical, it takes its name from the Arabic word for yellow, sufra. It is also called brimstone, a name found especially in the Bible, where the fire and brimstone of Hell awaits the unrepentant.

Smell, Smoke, and the Sick Room

Sulphur itself has no odor, yet it is highly flammable, and when it burns it gives off fumes that carry the particularly noxious smell of rotten eggs. For this reason it was burned in sick rooms to purify the air, the foul smoke believed to carry off infection.

Hot Springs and Health

Sulphur is often found near hot springs, its presence given away by that unmistakable smell. Despite the stench, such waters were prized for their beneficial effect on health. It was once common to give children doses of a sulphur tonic, though the practice has since died out.

Additional Correspondences

Planet: Saturn. Element: Earth.

Deities: Isis, Hecate, Nephthys, Demeter (Ceres), Saturn, Kronos, Nut, Cerridwen, Danu.

Powers: Protection, Exorcism.

Protector Against Evil

Sulfur guards against malevolent magick and drives off evil entities. In North African magick it is sometimes held to equal salt as a protector against evil. It is a primary ingredient in exorcisms and banishing spells, where it is traditionally burned. Burning it is not advisable, since the fumes are highly irritating to breathe, but sulfur can be used in many other ways, several of them especially well suited to protective magick.

Funerary Protection

Sulfur placed inside a coffin before burial is a traditional working to shield the deceased from evil forces and danger during their passage to the next realm.
