Lore

Tanzanite comes from a village at the base of Mount Kilimanjaro in Tanzania. Legend holds that in the twentieth century, a torrential storm drove the villagers from the region; when they returned, they found tanzanite crystals embedded widely in the earth, exposed by lightning that had struck the mountain's base. The villagers saw tanzanite as a blessing from God. Today, the crystal remains a vital supporter of Tanzania's economy and is the number one crystal export from Africa. Mining natural tanzanite is incredibly difficult, and this stone is now considered rarer than diamonds. When tanzanite was first discovered, it was mistaken for sapphire, but its multicolored inner fire and unique terminations revealed it to be an entirely different mineral. Its orthorhombic crystal structure produces the distinctive lilac-blue hues that have made it one of the most sought-after gems in both the jewelry and metaphysical worlds.
