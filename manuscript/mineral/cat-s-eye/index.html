Appearance

Translucent to opaque with a chatoyant band resembling a cat's eye, various colors including yellow, brown, green, and gray

Origin

Sri Lanka, India, Myanmar, Brazil, Madagascar

Attributes

Cat's Eye is a powerful stone that dispels unwanted energy from the aura. It amplifies good luck and fortune. This protective stone guards against the evil eye and negative energies, and brings happiness and serenity along with optimism and generosity.

Psychologically, Cat's Eye stimulates intuition and enhances awareness. It promotes concentration and helps one to be more understanding and forgiving. This stone brings unconditional love and aids in seeing both sides of a situation.

Emotionally, Cat's Eye balances emotions and encourages self-forgiveness. It helps to reduce stress and increase feelings of well-being.

Spiritually, this stone facilitates spiritual growth and aids in understanding your life's purpose.

Healing

Cat's Eye is beneficial for the eyes, nervous system, and kidneys. It supports recovery from illness and improves night vision. It may assist with disorders related to the heart, liver, pancreas, and adrenaline production.

Position

Wear as jewelry, especially as a ring or pendant. Place as appropriate on the body during healing work.

Spellworking

Cat's-eye is a name applied to several different stones, usually a quartz containing olive-green asbestos. The ancient Asian cat's-eye was a form of chrysoberyl. This stone exhibits a moving luminous opalescence.

Beauty

Worn or carried to increase beauty and preserve youthfulness. A beauty tincture can be made by filling a green glass jar with fresh spring water, adding a cat's-eye, and leaving it in the Sun for three to six hours. Remove the stone and wash your face with the water every day until it's gone while wearing the stone.

Wealth and Gambling

Cat's-eye is used in wealth and money spells. Possession guards the owner's wealth and increases it as long as the stone is retained. Often kept with money for this purpose. It prevents financial ruin and restores wealth lost prior to ownership. An excellent talisman for gamblers. Cat's eye is believed especially fortuitous for lottery players and speculators in the stock market. Carry one in your pocket or charm bag, or keep one on your desk, by your telephone, or near your primary method of financial transaction.

Cat's-eye Money Spell

Take a bill of the highest denomination you have. Rub it completely with the cat's-eye, then tightly wrap the bill around the stone. Tie the bill firmly to it with green thread and carry in your pocket to increase your money. Don't spend the bill until the spell has worked.

Protection and Mental Health

Set in a silver ring, cat's-eye can be worn for mental health, protection, insight, and luck. It dispels depression, gives pleasure, and should be worn during financial speculation.

Healing

Because of its eyelike appearance, this stone is worn to help with diseases of the eyes.

Invisibility

The Assyrians believed cat's-eye caused invisibility, probably because of its dazzling appearance.

The Eye in the Stone

Any stone holding an internal occlusion that refracts light in the manner of a cat's eye can carry this name, an effect known as chatoyancy. The true cat's eye is Chrysoberyl, formed of fine fibres running in a single direction that reflect light in this way.

Wherever the shape of an eye appears in a natural stone, the object is regarded as protective, and the cat's eye is no exception. It is carried as a charm to counter spells and sorcery, in particular the malice of the evil eye. In Arab regions it was held that wearing the stone could render the bearer invisible, an effect attributed to the eye of the stone working upon the sight of any who looked on it.

The Nine Gems

In a traditional Indian belief, each of the nine planets is paired with its own precious gem, the set known together as the navaratna. Cat's eye is the gem of Ketu, one of the two lunar nodes. The full set assigns the pearl to the Moon, the ruby to the Sun, topaz or yellow sapphire to Jupiter, the diamond to Venus, the emerald to Mercury, red coral to Mars, blue sapphire to Saturn, hessonite garnet to Rahu, and cat's eye to Ketu. Rahu and Ketu are the northern and southern lunar points that come into play during eclipses, which is why they are also called the eclipse planets.
