Black Tourmaline is the most common member of the tourmaline family, crystallizing in the Hexagonal system with a Mohs hardness of 7. Also known as Schorl, it is an iron-rich variety generally found in granite, metamorphic rocks, and hydrothermal vents, and sometimes, albeit rarely, forms as double-terminated points. According to Egyptian legend, tourmaline formed in the center of the Earth before passing through a rainbow on its way to the sun, which accounts for the full-spectrum growth patterns seen across the tourmaline family. African and Australian traditions speak of tourmalines as seer stones capable of helping the bearer predict the future. The name "tourmaline" derives from the Sinhalese word "toramalli," originally used for yellow zircon, but a shipment of schorl was mistakenly labeled turmalin and the name stuck. Rubbing or heating Black Tourmaline creates a modest electrical charge that attracts dust particles, a property Dutch traders exploited to pull leftover ashes from their smoking pipes.

Appearance

Black Tourmaline presents as black, opaque, striated prismatic crystals with a vitreous luster. It may be found in long, thin crystals or in massive form. Rare double-terminated specimens are especially prized for energy work.
