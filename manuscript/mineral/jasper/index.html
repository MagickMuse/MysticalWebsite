Lore

The name jasper comes from the Greek "iaspis," meaning "spotted stone," which has been traced back to the ancient Akkadian word "yashupu." Akkadian was spoken in Mesopotamia from roughly 3000 to 1000 BCE. Jasper is a variety of opaque chalcedony that comes in a range of forms and colors, from gray and brown to red and orange or even blue, generally containing bands of different colors or patterns composed of mineral or fossilized inclusions.

Spellworking

American Indians utilized jasper in rain-attracting ceremonies, hence its name "rainbringer." It was also used by the early inhabitants of the United States in divination.

A green jasper engraved with the image of a dragon surrounded by rays was worn by ancient Egyptian King Nechepsus to strengthen his digestive tract.

Jasper is a common stone, an opaque variety of chalcedony, which is a form of quartz. Found in a wide range of colors, it has been used in magic since the earliest times.

General Uses

Generally, jasper is worn or carried to promote mental processes and to restrain dangerous desires or whims that could lead to hazardous situations. It is also a protective stone against both physical and nonphysical hazards.

A piece of jasper held in the hand during childbirth guards the mother and her child. It is also worn to relieve pain, especially during childbirth.

Perfectly carved arrowheads of jasper are worn to attract luck to its wearer.

Amulet History

Jasper carves easily, which made it a favored amulet stone, and the image engraved upon it was believed to direct its power, as though the stone itself understood the design it carried. An engraved hare, for example, guarded against harmful influences. Egyptians cut red jasper into the tyet, the Knot of Isis, because its deep dull red recalled the blood of the Goddess. This amulet was laid at the throat of a mummy to protect the soul on its passage to the Afterlife. The physician Galen carried a jasper amulet engraved with a figure bearing a bundle of herbs and trusted it to sharpen his medical judgment. Hildegard of Bingen taught that jasper held in a woman's hand through childbirth shielded both mother and infant from harmful spirits, and the stone was thought to ease the birth itself through its bond with blood. Jasper also numbered among the stones set in the breastplate of the High Priest of the Temple in Jerusalem.
