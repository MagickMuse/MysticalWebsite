Larvikite is a relatively coarse-grained igneous rock of the feldspar family, rich in anorthoclase. It has a triclinic crystal structure and registers 6 to 6.5 on the Mohs scale. Its silver-blue sheen comes from the presence of alkali feldspar and plagioclase.

Appearance

Opaque dark stone with blue-silver flashes and a distinctive gray sheen with flashes of silver light. Like moonstone and labradorite, it is a member of the feldspar family, distinguishable by its luminous play of light.

Origin

Named after Larvik, Norway, the town where it was discovered. Also found in Canada, France, Mexico, Russia, Ukraine, and the United States.

Lore

Larvikite is believed to have formed nearly 300 million years ago, at an estimated depth of eighteen miles below Earth's surface. It takes its name from the town of Larvik, Norway, where it was first identified. Like moonstone and labradorite, it belongs to the feldspar family, distinguishable by its gray sheen and flashes of silver light.
