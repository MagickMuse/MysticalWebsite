Tremolite is an amphibole mineral crystallizing in the Monoclinic system with a Mohs hardness of 5 to 6. Named after Val Tremola in Switzerland, where it was first discovered in the late eighteenth century, this stone occurs in colors ranging from gray and green through to pink. It is often seen as an amphibole inclusion in quartz and emerald. Tremolite has proven valuable in paleontological research, helping scientists distinguish between metamorphic layers for site dating, particularly around the Triassic period during which it first began to form. It is one of the six currently identified types of asbestos, and over 200 tons are mined annually in India.

Appearance

Green, grey, white, pink, brown opaque stone in raw crystal in matrix form. Monoclinic crystal system with a Mohs hardness of 5 to 6.

Found globally. The most important localities are Australia, Austria, China, India, Madagascar, Pakistan, Tanzania, and the United States.
