Lore

Hiddenite takes its name from William Earl Hidden, the geologist who first discovered it in Alexander County, North Carolina while on an expedition to locate platinum on behalf of Thomas Edison, who believed the metal could advance his experiments with electric light. Instead of platinum, Hidden unearthed this luminous green gem, and the mineral was named in his honor. Hiddenite is the green variety of spodumene, rarer than its pink sibling Kunzite but not as scarce as the golden variety known as triphane. As a spodumene, Hiddenite is a lithium-rich mineral often found in the same geological regions as tourmaline, quartz, and kyanite. The largest known spodumene specimen ever discovered measured an astonishing 42 feet in height. Hiddenite displays pleochroism in direct sunlight, revealing an array of hues from soft green to shades of blue and purple depending on the angle of observation.
