Handling family problems

Appearance

Reddish-brown chalcedony, similar to carnelian but darker and harder. Often banded or solid colored, translucent to opaque.

Origin

Brazil, India, Turkey, Namibia, Uruguay, Australia, Madagascar, United States.

Attributes

Sard is a darker, harder variety of chalcedony closely related to carnelian. It shares many properties with carnelian but has a deeper, earthier energy. This stone grounds and stabilizes while promoting courage and protection.

Healing

Sard was traditionally used in childbirth as a protective talisman for both mother and child. It is said to promote quick healing of wounds and cuts.

Position

Wear as jewelry, especially rings or pendants. Hold during meditation or carry for protection.

Spellworking

Protection

Sard is worn for protective purposes. It guards against incantations and sorcery, returning negative magic to its sender. In the Middle Ages, sard set in gold was particularly valued for shielding against witchcraft and enchantments. Keep a piece near you when you suspect magical interference.

Love and Fidelity

Anciently associated with Cupid, sard was believed to attract love and ensure fidelity in relationships. Roman women wore sard to attract passionate partners. Exchange sard between lovers to strengthen bonds.

Courage and Victory

Like its relative carnelian, sard imparts courage to the wearer. Soldiers carried it into battle for bravery and protection. Use it before difficult confrontations or when you need to stand your ground.

Binding and Banishing

Sard has been used to bind or banish malevolent spirits and entities. Place it at thresholds or incorporate into banishing rituals. Its dark, earthy color grounds negative energies and prevents them from taking hold.

Childbirth

Traditionally placed near or on a woman during labor to ease delivery and protect both mother and child. It was believed to prevent hemorrhaging and speed recovery.
