Mabon

Amber is fossilized tree resin, not a true crystal, forming in an amorphous structure with a Mohs hardness of 2 to 2.5.

Magickal Powers

Draws off negative energy

Appearance

Golden to amber-brown in color, ranging from transparent to fully opaque, with a warm, waxy luster. Lightweight to the touch. Often contains ancient inclusions of insects, plant matter, or air bubbles dating back over 120 million years.

Colors

Most often a deep tawny yellow, the color for which it is named. It also occurs in blue, violet, and black. Green amber is colored artificially.

Origin

Britain, Poland, Italy, Romania, Russia, Germany, Myanmar, Dominican Republic

Formation

Amber begins as soft, sticky resin seeping from ancient trees. Over millions of years, intense heat and pressure trigger a process of molecular polymerization, first transforming the resin into copal, a younger, intermediate form, and then into true amber as all remaining plant terpenes are eliminated. This extended alchemical process is said to fill amber with the sustained warmth of the sun, clearing away low vibrations and instilling energies of vitality and optimism.

Magickal Properties

Amber is not a crystal at all but fossilized tree resin that solidified over geological time. It has strong connections with the earth and is a grounding stone for higher energies. Amber is a powerful healer and cleanser that draws disease from the body and promotes tissue revitalization. It also cleans the environment and the chakras. It absorbs negative energies and transmutes them into positive forces that stimulate the body to heal itself. A powerful protector, it links the everyday self to the higher spiritual reality.

Psychologically, Amber brings stability to life but also motivates by linking what is wished for to the drive to achieve it. Its warm, bright energies translate into a sunny, spontaneous disposition that nevertheless respects tradition. It can help counteract suicidal or depressive tendencies.

Mentally, Amber stimulates the intellect, clears depression, and promotes a positive mental state and creative self-expression. It brings balance and patience and encourages decision-making, being a useful memory aid. Its flexibility dissolves opposition. Emotionally, Amber encourages peacefulness and develops trust. Spiritually, Amber promotes altruism and brings wisdom.

Healing

Amber is a powerful chakra cleanser and healer. At a physical level, it imbues the body with vitality and has the power to draw disease out of the body. By absorbing pain and negative energy, Amber allows the body to rebalance and heal itself. Amber alleviates stress. It resonates with the throat, treating goiters and other throat problems. It treats the stomach, spleen, kidneys, bladder, liver, and gallbladder, alleviates joint problems, and strengthens the mucus membranes. As an elixir and for wound healing, it is an excellent natural antibiotic. It can stimulate the navel chakra and help in grounding energies into the body.

Chakra Work

Amber works most powerfully with the Solar Plexus Chakra, where it ignites life force energy, stimulates inner power, and fosters courage and confidence. It also detoxifies stagnant or negative energy from the auric field and physical body, supporting the metabolism and regenerative healing. Through the Crown Chakra, it opens a connection to collective and universal wisdom, ancestral knowledge, and awareness of past lives. For those who struggle with seasonal affective disorder or a loss of purpose, amber's solar energy can help restore a sense of direction and inner strength.

Position

Wear for prolonged periods, especially on the wrist or throat. Place on the Solar Plexus or Crown Chakra as appropriate. Placing amber on an altar purifies the energy of the space and reinforces protective boundaries. If working with babies or children, it is helpful for the mother to wear the stone first.

Spellworking

Amber is perhaps the oldest substance used for human adornment, with beads and pendants found in northern European gravesites dating to 8000 BCE. It is not a stone but fossilized resin of coniferous trees from the Oligocene epoch. Because it is warm to the touch and often contains insect fragments, it was thought to possess life itself. The early Chinese visualized tiger souls transmuting into amber upon death. It was sacred to worshippers of the Mother Goddess, believed to contain the very essence of life.

As a fossil, amber has associations with time, cycles, and longevity. Since it was once a living substance, it connects to Akasha, the fifth element that governs and binds the four elements together. In contemporary Wiccan covens, high priestesses often wear necklaces of alternating amber and jet beads, representing the Goddess and God, feminine and masculine principles, projective and receptive forces. The Greek name elektron gives us the modern word electricity.

Protection

Amber necklaces are potent amulets against negative magic, especially effective for safeguarding children. Have children wear amber beads or place some in their bedroom. If subjected to heavy negativity, light a white candle, sit before it, and create a circle around yourself with small amber beads. Sit within the circle while restoring your energy. Alternatively, place nine small pieces in a warm bath, soak until the water cools, then carry one bead until your next bath.

Strengthening Magic

Witches, wise women, and shamans wear amber beads to strengthen their spells. A large piece placed on the altar increases the effectiveness of magic. Powdered amber added to any incense will increase its power.

Beauty and Attraction

Amber enhances beauty and general attractiveness. It magnifies the wearer's natural beauty, attracts friends and companions, and stimulates happiness. Highly sensual and magnetic, it is worn to attract love and increase enjoyment of pleasurable activities. Small pieces can be added to herbal love mixtures or worn near the heart to attract a mate.

Fertility

Women wore amber images of fish, frogs, and rabbits to ensure conception. Men wore amber figures of lions, dogs, and dragons to combat impotency and ensure fertility.

Health and Healing

Amber beads worn around the neck serve as general health protectors. Amber beads carved into shapes reminiscent of genitals, whether very literally or only vaguely, are said to provide special protective benefits for health specifically. Wear or carry as needed. Traditionally used for convulsions, deafness, sore throat, earache, headache, toothache, asthma, rheumatism, and digestive troubles. A ball of amber held in the hand reduces fever. Worn or carried to strengthen the eyes. Amber powder burned during childbirth assists labor; the smoke sniffed halts nosebleeds.

Prosperity

Amber is worn to increase strength, for business success, and to stimulate the flow of money. It plays a part in attraction spells for love, money, power, and success.

Folklore

Like jet, amber becomes charged with energy when it is rubbed. Before its true origin was understood, many explanations arose for where it came from. Some called it lynxurine, while others held that it was made of the Sun's own rays, hardened in the sea and then washed up along the shore. The link with the Sun is the most powerful symbolic association of amber, and it does resemble a trapped sunbeam, especially when the flaws and inclusions within it catch the light. For this reason it is said to cheer and revitalize the spirit.

Amber was also believed to cure many ailments, in particular hay fever, asthma, and other infections of the throat and respiratory tract. It was taken as a powder or worn as an amulet. To treat ear infections and even deafness, it was ground to a fine dust, mixed with honey and rose oil, and poured into the ear.

Because amber burns readily, it was once called Bernstein, the burn stone. The scent released by burning amber was believed to drive away evil spirits.

Burning Amber

When a spell or formula calls for burning amber, it sometimes means true amber rather than ambergris. Amber is technically a plant resin, not the mineral many take it to be. Ancient Greeks buried amber as a tribute to Helios and Apollo. Burned with copal, it brings the wisdom of the pines and the radiance of the sun (see Amber and Copal Sun Fumigation).
