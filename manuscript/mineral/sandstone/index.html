The Stone of Destiny

The Stone of Destiny is a heavy block of sandstone, weighing 152 kilograms and carved with a Latin cross. Its sacred standing rests not on rarity but on its provenance and great age. Several legends compete to explain it. One names it the pillow stone of Jacob, once kept in the Temple at Jerusalem. Another holds that it belonged to early Gaulish settlers who carried it with them to Scotland. A third claims it served as a portable altar for St. Columba.

Whatever its true origin, the reputation of the stone made its ownership a contentious matter across the centuries. It was kept at the now ruined Abbey of Scone in Perthshire and used in the coronations of the kings of Scotland and of England. After Edward I of England defeated Scotland in the thirteenth century, the stone was carried off from its Scottish home and set in Westminster Abbey as a token of Scotland's defeat and England's new sovereignty, though some say the canny Scots hid the original and the conquerors took only a replica. Real or not, it was placed on a bracket beneath the seat of the Coronation Chair, and every monarch since has been crowned seated above it. In 1996 the Stone of Destiny was returned to Scotland, and the journey of this simple block of sandstone made national news, such was its symbolic meaning. It now rests behind armored glass at Edinburgh Castle, and should the occasion arise, it will be carried back to Westminster Abbey for future coronations.
