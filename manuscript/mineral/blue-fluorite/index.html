About Blue Fluorite

Fluorite as a mineral family formed approximately 200 million years ago, created when superheated water carrying fluorine and other dissolved minerals moved upward through fissures in the earth and reacted with calcium-rich limestone bedrock. The first recorded fluorite mining operations began in Illinois in 1842. Historically, fluorite was used industrially as a flux in metal smelting to remove impurities, and the fluoride extracted from it found its way into products ranging from contact lenses to toothpaste. Blue fluorite specifically ranges in color from teal to deep indigo, and the variation in tone corresponds to shifts in trace mineral composition.
