Lore

Blue Tiger's Eye is the rarest variety of tiger's-eye, also known as eagle's-eye, ox-eye, or hawk's-eye. It gets its distinctive blue color when silica enters the crocidolite fibers during formation before the iron inclusions have had the chance to oxidize; this oxidation process is what normally turns tiger's-eye its widely recognizable golden hue. Tiger's-eye itself is a pseudomorph variety of quartz, formed through a metamorphosis process when parallel veins of crocidolite fibers are altered into iron oxide and then replaced by silica. The tiger's-eye family has long been associated with the Greek goddess Athena and was used to adorn the shields of warriors for protection on the battlefield and the sarcophagi of ancient Egyptian pharaohs to guide them through the afterlife. In ancient Egypt, blue tiger's-eye was specifically referred to as the stone of Horus and was believed to invoke his omniscient wisdom. Roman soldiers adorned their battle shields with this gem to deflect the gaze of their enemies and render attacks ineffective. Blue Tiger's Eye is also thought to return any envious or jealous thoughts directed at you back to the sender.
