Appearance

Contained within an outer form. When opened, it is hollow with many crystals pointing inward.

Attributes

Holds and amplifies energy within themselves. Due to their rounded, cavelike shape and numerous terminations, they diffuse the amplified energy, softening the energy but not neutralizing it, allowing it to flow out slowly if required.

Useful for protection and aid spiritual growth. Assist in breaking addictions and are beneficial for an addictive or overindulgent personality.

In the Middle Ages, geodes were thought to be favored by eagles, who placed them in their nests. Round and containing crystals, geodes are egg symbols. They also relate to the Great Mother Goddess.

Position

Place on altar, hold during meditation, or place in bedroom for fertility magic.

Spellworking

Geodes are hollow concretions containing crystals. All quartz crystals form within geodes, which may be a quarter-mile long or small enough to fit in your palm. Other geodes don't contain separate crystals but, when sliced, reveal intricate patterns of minerals.

Amethyst geodes are some of the most beautiful objects on Earth. When sliced or broken open, they reveal a mass of purple crystals growing in toward the center. Sunlight shining on them is dazzling.

Meditation

An amethyst geode, or any geode containing isolated crystals, can be held and used in meditation as a contemplative object.

Concentrating Power

Placed on the altar or held, geodes can be utilized to concentrate the powers of the specific type of stone contained within them. During magic, use your visualization to release these powers toward the magical goal.

Fertility

They can be placed in the bedroom and charged with energy to increase fertility and to promote conception.

Love and Safe Pregnancy

The pseudo-Albertus Magnus recommended carrying or wearing geodes to attract love and to avoid untimely birth (miscarriage).

Eagle Stone

Known also as the aquileus and the praegnus, the eagle stone was said to be found in the nest of an eagle or lodged in the bird's stomach or neck, which made it very hard to obtain and earned it a reputation for many magickal powers. It was hollow and held a smaller stone within it, called the calimus, that rattled when shaken. Because it seemed to carry a stone inside itself, it was thought to be pregnant, which is the source of some of its names. As an example of sympathetic magick, it was tied to the thigh of an expectant mother during childbirth to coax the baby from the womb and ease the delivery. The timing mattered, for if the stone was left in place too long it was believed to cause a prolapse.
