Magickal Powers

Regains balance in life; settles conflicts; courage, good luck

Appearance

Waxy and soft, usually banded, sometimes translucent with small crystals, various sizes. Often sold as artificially colored slices that have no additional therapeutic properties.

Origin

United States, India, Morocco, Czech Republic, Brazil, Africa

Attributes

Formed from microscopic crystals of quartz laid down in bands, this is a very stable crystal. Agates are grounding stones, bringing about an emotional, physical, and intellectual balance. They aid in centering and stabilizing physical energy.

Agate has the power to harmonize yin and yang, the positive and negative forces that hold the universe in place. A soothing and calming stone, Agate works slowly but brings great strength. Its multiple layers can bring hidden information to light.

Psychologically, Agate gently facilitates acceptance of one's self. This builds self-confidence. It aids self-analysis and perception of hidden circumstances, bringing to your attention any dis-ease that is interfering with your well-being.

Agates enhance mental function as they improve concentration, perception, and analytical abilities, leading to practical solutions. Agate's love of truthfulness encourages speaking one's own truth. Agates with clear crystals can stimulate memories.

Emotionally, this crystal overcomes negativity and bitterness of the heart. It heals inner anger, fostering love and the courage to start again. It is useful for any kind of emotional trauma. It creates a sense of safety and security by dissolving internal tension.

Spiritually, Agate raises consciousness and links into collective consciousness and awareness of the oneness of life. It encourages quiet contemplation and assimilation of life experiences, leading to spiritual growth and inner stability.

Healing

Agate stabilizes the aura, eliminating and transforming negative energies. Its cleansing effect is powerful at the physical and emotional levels. Placed on the heart, it will heal the emotional dis-ease that prevents acceptance of love. Placed on the abdomen or taken as an elixir, Agate stimulates the digestive process and relieves gastritis. It heals the eyes, stomach, and uterus; it cleanses the lymphatic system and the pancreas; it strengthens blood vessels and heals skin disorders.

Position

Hold or place on appropriate body location.

Specific colors have additional properties. Blue-Green Agate is usually artificially made glass and has no therapeutic properties. Green Agate enhances mental and emotional flexibility and improves decision-making. It is useful in resolving disputes. Pink Agate promotes love between parent and child. Position over the heart for optimum effect.

Spellworking

Agate is utilized in spells and rituals involving strength, bravery, longevity, gardening, love, healing, and protection. A grounding stone, it helps stabilize situations. Add it to a money mojo if you're having trouble holding on to money.

Gardening Magic: Worn on the arm or carried while gardening, agate increases your plants' fertility and ensures a bountiful crop. Charged agates can be "planted" in the garden to promote abundance, and small agates hung on trees increase their yield. In ancient Rome, an agate worn in a ring or bound to the left arm ensured the favor of vegetative deities.

Love and Social Magic: Often utilized in love spells, agate is worn to avoid envious thoughts and remove spite, making its wearer amiable and agreeable. It also serves as a truth amulet to ensure pure words and to gain favor from powerful persons.

Protection: Agate jewelry given to children serves as a protective amulet, particularly useful in preventing falls. Adults wear it to avoid stumbling. In ceremonial magic, agates engraved with serpents or men riding snakes prevented bites and stings from snakes, scorpions, and insects. It was thought to guard against sorcery, demons, and possession.

Healing Uses: Held in the mouth, agate relieves thirst. Applied to the forehead, it reduces fevers. Held in the hand, it calms the body and helps heal minor health problems. In the Middle East, agates were talismans for healthy blood. In ancient Britain, they guarded against skin diseases. Triangular agates averted intestinal problems in Syria.

Divination: In Asia, agates were used for scrying. Gazing at the markings on the stone allows the deep mind to project psychic impulses to conscious awareness.

Lore

A variety of quartz known as chalcedony, agate occurs in many colors and patterns, from the soft blue of blue lace agate to the small tree-like figures held within moss and dendritic agate. Some stones form in concentric rings of different color, and when one is cut and polished to reveal a black or brown center ringed in white, it is read as a symbol of the eye and worn, like other eye talismans, to guard against the evil eye.

Followers of Islam held that agate carried protective power, made stronger when the names of Mohammed's grandsons, Hassan and Hussein, were engraved upon it. In Jewish tradition the stone was believed to keep its wearer from falling or stumbling, so it was worked into horses' manes and bridles to keep the rider safe. European farmers later borrowed the practice, setting agates in their animals' bridles in the belief that doing so would bring a bountiful harvest.

Agate was also thought to shield people and buildings from storms and lightning. A stone with a naturally formed hole is still nailed above the doorways of some rural dairies, so that lightning will not sour the cows' milk and so that witches cannot steal the cows away to ride them through the night. Sailors carried agate to stay safe at sea and to keep off seasickness. Just as it was said to calm tempests, thunder, and lightning, agate was believed to soothe human nature, and a stone worn close to a sleeper would grant restful sleep and peaceful dreams.

The stone of Sylvester, also called the stone of St. James, is a banded agate that is dark on one side and light on the other. The light side stands for the past and all that is known, while the dark side stands for the unknowable future. Such stones were once given as New Year gifts to mark what has passed and what is to come, and the layered colors of agate were taken as a sign of hidden knowledge about to surface. Agate was believed to draw divine energies, so its wearer might attract divine favor. Powdered agate mixed with fruit juice was once used as a remedy for insanity and to ease kidney disease and boils.

Dark Magick Applications

Inscribing a curse into carnelian agate in a specific manner binds the working permanently. This technique is most commonly employed to hex those who disturb graves or trespass into spaces a witch has warded. It can also be directed to summon a plague of rats to a chosen location, sometimes disguised beneath the guise of warding an area against serpents.

Gzi Stone (Dzi Bead)

The gzi stone is a carved, etched, or naturally colored agate from Tibetan Buddhism, possibly prehistoric in origin. These beads are often turned up when fields are ploughed or found near burial sites. Many beliefs surround their origin. Some hold that they are jewels the deities discard, dropping them to earth once they become damaged or chipped. Others say they are the droppings of the mythological garuda bird, or the petrified remains of a living creature such as a worm.

Gzi stones appear in two main forms. One is cylindrical, made from black, brown, and white agate marked with circular eye symbols. The other is rounded, made from chalcedony or carnelian with spiralling bands. The most valuable is the nine eyed gzi stone, whose worth has been likened to a small farmhouse. The beads are now widely copied, often in materials unknown to earlier makers, and some appear manufactured from china or plastic.

⚠️ The gzi stone is sacred within Tibetan Buddhism and should be approached with respect for that living tradition.
