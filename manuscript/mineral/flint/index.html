Spellworking

Flint, a term vaguely applied to varieties of opaque quartz, was widely used in religio-magical rites by American Indians. Among the Cherokee, flint was invoked by shamans prior to medicinal treatments.

One of the first trade articles of early peoples, flint was used extensively to make blades. Ancient flint knives, found throughout Europe, were and still are used as protective amulets. They were known as "thunderstones" and "elf-shot," revealing that their origins long remained unknown.

Protection

The Irish set flint knives in silver and carried them to guard against mischievous fairies. In Scandinavia, flint knives were sometimes honored as family "gods"—beer and melted butter were poured over them, much as holy statues are revered in contemporary India.

Ancient flint implements are protective amulets, particularly potent when placed above the door. If you obtain an ancient flint knife (or a modern replica), place it on the altar or hold during protective rituals.

Divination

Flint is used in modern-day Brazil for divining gold, water, gemstones, and other underground treasures.

Headache Spell

To cure a headache, strike a flint several times. As the sparks fly, visualize the pain traveling out from your head, into the sparks, and dissipating with them.

Dark Magick Applications

Adder Stone

The adder stone is one of the curious mythological stones long tied to flint folklore. One tale holds that it forms from the gathered saliva of a writhing mass of adders, which they shoot into the air where it hardens into stone. Another says it comes from the head of a serpent. Such stones appear mainly across the British Isles, though they have also been recovered in Egypt, and they are linked to pagan ceremonial days like the Summer Solstice and May Day. The stone carries several other names, among them the Druids' egg, the snake egg, and the magician's glass.

Like other stones believed to be born from venomous creatures, the adder stone is said to hold a homoeopathic power that neutralizes toxins and poisons. A genuine adder stone is usually glassy in appearance, with a naturally occurring hole running through it. To prove one is real, place it in water: a true adder stone cannot be sunk no matter how much weight is attached to it, and it will float against any current.

Fire, Lightning, and Thunder

The words flint and flinty describe a person or object that shares the hard, tough steeliness of the stone. Struck against a piece of steel, flint throws a spark strong enough to set a fire smouldering, one reason it is bound to the element of fire.

Flint was a prized commodity for prehistoric peoples, who shaped it into tools such as arrowheads and hammerheads. When these worked pieces were unearthed in the Middle Ages, they were taken for the fossilized tongues of serpents. Set into silver mounts, they served as charms to protect cattle from bewitchment by malicious elves and fairies. Encased in silver, the arrowheads resembled lightning flashes, and so their meaning slowly shifted from practical tool to protective talisman that worked through sympathetic magick, guarding homes and byres against storms, lightning, and fire.

Elsewhere in Europe, excavated flint hammerheads were read as a physical manifestation of thunder, since Thor, god of storms, carried a hammer. In this way the bond between flint, fire in the form of lightning, and storms became fixed in the medieval imagination.

Healing and Folklore

The buried arrowheads were also believed to hold healing power, able to cut into the body and remove kidney stones. In medieval Ireland, a woman who found such an arrowhead was granted special status because of the magickal power of this sacred stone. The arrowhead would be soaked in water, and the water given to a sick person to drink as a kind of medicine.

The ancient Egyptians prized flint as a blade, using it to make the ritual knife that opened the first incision into a body at the start of embalmment. In some places, flints turned up out of the soil by the plough and were cleared away by hand, yet more would be found on the surface the next morning, leading to the belief that the stones bred overnight.
