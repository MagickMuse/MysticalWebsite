A rare pseudomorphic combination of albite, epidote, and zoisite, Saussurite is a multi-crystal system stone (albite: triclinic, epidote: tetrahedral, zoisite: orthorhombic) that brings together three distinct mineral energies into a single powerful healing ally.

Origin

Antarctica, Australia, China, India, Italy, Poland, Russia, Switzerland, United States (California, Virginia). Saussurite typically forms in large crystallized masses, making it highly sought after by stone carvers. Smaller individual pieces are so rare that collectors tend to acquire them quickly when they become available.

Appearance

A rich greenish-blue stone with a complex, layered appearance reflecting the interplay of its three component minerals. The coloring alone is considered healing to gaze upon, evoking a sense of calm and transport. Specimens may show veining or banding where the albite, epidote, and zoisite blend into one another.

Magickal Properties

Each of Saussurite's three component minerals contributes unique properties. Zoisite anchors you to the earth, keeping you centered while inspiring creativity, focus, and discipline. It radiates an energy of growth and richness in all aspects of life, providing energy for the soul's rebirth and the expansion of life-force energy. Zoisite magnifies happiness, appreciation, abundance, vitality, and growth, and helps overcome lethargy by boosting internal strength and motivation.

Epidote, whose name comes from the Greek word for "increase," is a protective and grounding stone that doubles as a powerful manifestation gem. It helps you manifest your intentions and clears whatever stands in the way of creating the life you desire. Epidote promotes personal freedom and helps you let go of the past.

Albite is a form of feldspar that opens the connection to the higher chakras, specifically activating the Third Eye and Crown, awakening the psychic and intuitive mind.

For beginners, Saussurite helps shift from a space of unease and restlessness to one of calm, trust, and harmony. It connects you with both the earth and the higher realms, channeling vitality, abundance, and divine wisdom into every aspect of your spiritual practice.

For advanced practitioners, Saussurite helps transform negative patterns and behaviors by addressing the root issues behind emotional disharmony. It encourages spiritual development, increased self-understanding, and a deeper comprehension of the world around you.

Chakra Work

Saussurite is a multi-chakra stone, resonating with the Root, Heart, Third Eye, and Crown Chakras. At the Heart Chakra, it is a calming stone that eases emotional disturbances and ushers in emotional healing and balance when held against the heart space. Through its zoisite component, it grounds energy through the Root Chakra, keeping you anchored and centered. The albite within activates the Third Eye and Crown Chakras, awakening psychic sight and intuitive awareness, creating a full energetic bridge from earth to spirit.

Healing

Saussurite is primarily known as a physical healing stone. Its rare combination of minerals works synergistically to address the body's energetic imbalances. It provides energy for the soul's rebirth and the expansion of human life-force energy, helping overcome lethargy and restoring vitality. Its calming influence on the Heart Chakra also supports emotional healing by soothing disturbances and restoring balance.

Spellworking

Saussurite is an excellent stone for abundance workings, manifestation rituals, and any magick aimed at personal transformation and growth. Use it to address root causes of emotional disharmony or to amplify intentions related to prosperity and vitality. It partners well with Blue Kyanite for chakra alignment and psychic communication, Cavansite for higher consciousness and channeling, Covellite for deep psychic vision, Iolite for intuition and inner journeying, Jade for prosperity and heart healing, and Ruby for life-force energy and passion.

Lore

Saussurite was first discovered in the 1760s by the Swiss naturalist Horace Benedict de Saussure, who initially misidentified it as a form of jade. It was only after his death that his son established that the stone was actually a new form of feldspar, a pseudomorph in which one mineral had replaced another during formation. The son had the stone formally named in honor of his father. The initial confusion with jade speaks to Saussurite's rich greenish-blue coloring and the high regard in which it has been held since its discovery.
