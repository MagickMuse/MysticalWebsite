Angelite is a type of blue anhydrite forming in the Orthorhombic crystal system with a Mohs hardness of 3.5.

Appearance

Pale blue to blue-white in color, translucent to opaque, fine-grained, soft stone with a pearly to vitreous luster, typically found in nodular masses.

Origin

Britain, Egypt, Germany, Mexico, Peru, Poland, Libya, United States, India, Madagascar, Chile, Slovakia

History and Formation

Angelite was first discovered in 1987 near Machu Picchu in Peru, the day before the Harmonic Convergence, the first synchronized global meditation event organized by José Argüelles, in which participants gathered at sacred sites and energy vortexes around the world to call in a new era of universal peace. Many believe angelite still carries the vibrational imprint of that event.

Its name derives from the Greek anhydros, meaning "waterless," a reference to how it forms: seawater evaporates in sedimentary basins, leaving behind anhydrite crystals. Angelite shares nearly the same chemical composition as gypsum, the main difference being that gypsum retains a much higher water content. Under certain conditions, anhydrite can absorb water and transform into gypsum, and rarely, with extreme heat, the reverse can occur. Because of this relationship, angelite shares vibrational qualities with selenite, carrying energies of peace, purification, and connection to celestial realms.
