Indicolite is a variety of Tourmaline crystallizing in the Trigonal system, and it is the rarest color of the Tourmaline family. It gets its distinctive blue coloring from iron, magnesium, and aluminum rather than copper like other blue minerals. Named in the early 1800s by Brazilian mineralogist Jose Bonifacio de Andrada e Silva for its indigo hue, this stone carries the energy of alignment, psychic development, and spiritual awareness.

Appearance

Blue, translucent to opaque, striated prismatic crystal. Shades range from light sea blue to deep indigo. Indicolite is strongly pleochroic, displaying a shifting range of hues when viewed from different directions or under various light sources. Trigonal crystal system with a Mohs hardness of 7 to 7.5.

Origin

Afghanistan, Brazil, Madagascar, Mozambique, Myanmar, Namibia, Pakistan, Sri Lanka, United States (California, Maine, Massachusetts, New Hampshire).

Attributes

Blue Tourmaline (Indicolite) activates the throat and third eye chakras. It aids clear communication, especially of psychic or spiritual information. Blue Tourmaline promotes peace, serenity, and tolerance. It aids in developing psychic gifts while maintaining a balanced perspective.

This stone assists in resolving grief and assists with coming to terms with loss. It supports the exploration of the spiritual realms and develops intuition.

Healing

Blue Tourmaline benefits the throat, thyroid, eyes, and brain. It aids with headaches and migraines.

Position

Lore

Exceptional specimens of Indicolite Tourmaline can command prices of up to $10,000 per carat, making it one of the most valuable members of the Tourmaline family. Its rarity and striking range of blues have made it highly sought after by collectors and practitioners alike.
