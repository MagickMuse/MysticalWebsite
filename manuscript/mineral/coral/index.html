Appearance

Skeletal remains of sea creatures, typically red, pink, white, or black. Found naturally on beaches or commercially harvested.

Origin

Australia, Philippines, Japan, Italy, Egypt, Pacific Islands, Mediterranean.

Attributes

Coral has played an important role in religious and magical rites throughout the Pacific Islands. It is often placed on graves to guard the deceased, and temples were sometimes built of lava rocks and coral. In the Mediterranean, coral was thought to contain the "life essence" of the Mother Goddess, who dwelled in the ocean. Coral comes from two Greek words meaning "daughter of the sea."

Note: Because coral is the skeletal remains of a sea creature, some object to its use in magic. Commercial harvesting of living coral is harmful, but naturally found beach coral can be used ethically.

Healing

Red coral was used to cure indigestion, all pains in the digestive tract, eye complaints, and to staunch blood. When worn, it alerts its wearer of ill health by paling in color.

Position

Wear plainly visible as a protective amulet. Place beneath the pillow for peaceful sleep.

Spellworking

To be powerful in magic, ancient people used coral that hadn't been processed. If a piece of coral used in magic breaks, it has lost its power—return the broken pieces to the ocean.

Protection

When worn visibly, coral is a protective amulet against "the evil eye, demons, furies, succubi, incubi, and phantasma." It guards against accidents, acts of violence, poison, theft, possession, and sterility.

Inner Changes

Coral dispels foolishness, nervousness, fear, depression, murderous thoughts, panic, and nightmares. It confers reason, prudence, courage, and wisdom upon its bearer. Placed beneath the pillow, it produces peaceful sleep.

Children

Coral has been used in magic for children for thousands of years. If presented as a gift to a child, it ensures their future health. Infants wear coral pendants or beads to ease the pain of cutting teeth. Place coral in a child's room for magical protection.

Smooth pink coral beads worn as a necklace provide a child with ongoing magickal protection. A Romany variation threads pieces of red wool or cotton alongside the coral beads on the same necklace, combining the protective power of coral with the warding properties of red thread.

Women's Magic

Italian women wore coral near the groin to regulate menstrual flow, recognizing the link between coral, the sea, the Moon, and their cycles. The coral would grow pale during the flow, then brighten afterward. This coral was carefully hidden from men, for if seen by them, it lost all its magic power.

Agriculture

In ancient Egypt and Greece, powdered coral was mixed with seed and scattered over newly planted fields. This protected growing crops from inclement weather and insects. Coral was also hung on fruit trees to increase their yield.

House Luck

Take a piece of coral and touch it to every door, window, and wall in the house while moving clockwise. Then put it in a place of prominence and let it do its magic.

Love

Coral earrings were worn by women in ancient Rome to attract men. Powdered coral was used in sixteenth-century Venusian incenses. Red or pink candles ringed with pieces of coral are burned to draw love.

Sea Protection

Due to its associations with the sea, coral is worn as a protectant while sailing or traveling over water, and guards boats against shipwreck. It is also worn as protection against shark attack.

Color Symbolism

The color of a piece of coral was thought to signify its power, so the user had to choose carefully. Pink and red coral guarded against the evil eye and drove malicious spirits away, while brown or darker coral was believed to attract malevolent spirits and demons. In some Eastern regions it is still held to be very unfortunate to wear or even own dull or discolored coral. Plato said that coral changed color to reveal poor health, turning pale if its owner fell ill.

Witch Stone

In parts of Italy coral is called the witch stone and is carried to keep witches and wizards at a distance. It is considered even more effective when carved into the shape of the cornus, a hand with the fingers curled in except for the first finger and the little finger, forming the sign of the horns. As an object linked to the Goddess, coral is also a fertility symbol, tied to trees and vines to ensure a good harvest and worn by women to encourage their own fertility.
