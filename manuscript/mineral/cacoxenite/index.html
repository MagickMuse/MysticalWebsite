Cacoxenite's Journey to You

Cacoxenite is a mineral composed of iron, aluminum, and phosphorus, classified as the Stone of Ascension and recognized as one of the seven inclusions found within Super Seven. It typically forms as thin, needle-like clusters on the surface of or within other stones, most commonly quartz and amethyst. Its distinctive appearance can sometimes be confused with rutile, though where rutile tends to form in straight lines or bars, cacoxenite produces what looks like tiny radiating arrows of golden light. It was first identified in a Bohemian iron ore mine in what is now the Czech Republic, and its name derives from the Greek for "bad guest," a reference to its high phosphorus content, which degrades the quality of the iron in which it forms. As a stone of ascension, cacoxenite supports spiritual evolution and the expansion of consciousness, making it a sought-after stone for those on an accelerating spiritual path.

Did You Know?

Cacoxenite takes its name from the Greek for "bad guest." Its high phosphorus content diminishes the quality of the iron ore in which it forms, earning it this unflattering title despite its considerable spiritual reputation.
