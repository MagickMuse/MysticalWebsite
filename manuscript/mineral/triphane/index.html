Triphane is the golden variety of spodumene, the rarest of the three spodumene siblings alongside green Hiddenite and pink Kunzite. It is a lithium-rich mineral with a monoclinic crystal structure, known for its air-element energy and its ability to clear pathways for new visions, possibilities, and experiences.

Origin

Afghanistan, Brazil, Madagascar, Sri Lanka, United States

Lore

The name "triphane" derives from the Greek word triphanes, meaning "appearing threefold," a reference to this gem's ability to display multiple colors when viewed from three different angles, as well as its natural threefold cleavage. Golden spodumene is the newest variety of this mineral family to receive its own name, following years of debate among geologists over the distinction between yellow and green spodumene. As a spodumene, Triphane is a lithium ore often found in the same geological regions as tourmaline, quartz, and kyanite. The largest known spodumene specimen ever discovered stood an astonishing 42 feet tall.
