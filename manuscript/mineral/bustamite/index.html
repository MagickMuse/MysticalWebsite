Bustamite is a rare Triclinic mineral composed of rhodonite and johannsenite with a Mohs hardness of 6, displaying a characteristically pale pink color that can cause it to be confused with rhodonite. Its name is debated, with some attributing it to Miguel Bustamante y Septien, a Mexican mineralogist, and others to General Anastasio Bustamante, a three-time president of Mexico. Gem-quality specimens are exceptionally rare, with faceted stones above 1 to 2 carats considered prized collector finds.

Appearance

Pale to medium pink, occasionally red-toned, with a vitreous luster and opaque to translucent quality. On rare occasions it is fibrous enough to produce a cat's-eye effect when polished.

Origin

South Africa, Sweden, Russia, Peru, Argentina, Austria, Bulgaria, Germany, Honduras, Italy, Japan, New Zealand, Norway, Britain, Brazil

It kindles joy, playfulness, and a renewed sense of wonder, calling forward the inner child and granting permission to pursue passions without the weight of self-imposed limitation. Where analysis paralysis has set in or daunting tasks have become overwhelming, bustamite clears the fog and restores the creative impulse that makes action feel possible again.

Carrying bustamite encourages connection, both with the people in your immediate circle and with the broader web of living beings that make up the natural world. Its energy is expansive and life-affirming, helping you appreciate the richness of physical existence. For those working creatively or sexually, bustamite opens imaginative space, supports the exploration of sensual boundaries, and promotes fertility across all domains, including the artistic and the physical. It is also a reliable enhancer of dream vividness and lucidity.

Emotionally, Bustamite removes old pain, harmonizing the emotional energy system and healing cellular memory. Physically, this stone realigns the energy meridians of the physical and subtle bodies. When Bustamite assists with healing, you follow your life path with great vitality.
