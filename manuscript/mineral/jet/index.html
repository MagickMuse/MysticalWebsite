This gem carries light energy and is ideal for purifying the auric field, keeping you connected to the physical world and comfortable in your own body. It has been used as a talisman since Stone Age times, drawing out negative energy and easing unreasonable fears.

Jet stabilizes finances and protects businesses when placed in a cash box or the wealth corner of a home or workspace. Psychologically, it promotes taking control of your life, balances mood swings, and eases depression. It cleanses the base chakra and stimulates the rise of the kundalini force, directing it toward the crown chakra when placed at the chest.

For more advanced work, jet is a powerhouse for removing curses, hexes, and dark magick that may stem from previous ages or lifetimes. It guides you through shadow work, helping you own your perceived shortcomings while exploring your true inner power and integrating the lessons of negative past experiences. Jet illuminates the areas of your life most worth focusing on to propel you toward your full potential, all while encouraging patience with yourself. Those drawn to this stone are said to be old souls with deep experience of earthly incarnation.

Traditionally, jet jewelry was believed to become part of the wearer's body, so inherited or purchased pieces should be cleansed with particular care. Jet used in healing should be thoroughly cleansed after each application.

Spellworking

Jet is fossilized wood millions of years old. Because it is black, it is associated with the element of earth, but due to its organic origins, it is also related to Akasha. Jet shares with amber the property of becoming electrically charged when rubbed.

When jet is continuously worn on the body, it is thought to absorb part of the wearer's "soul." Such stones were carefully guarded, for in the wrong hands they could be used to manipulate their original wearer.

Ancient Greek worshippers of Cybele, the goddess of growth and plants, wore jet to obtain her favors. Modern-day gardeners also wear jet to make their plants flourish.

Along with amber, jet was found in prehistoric gravesites. Contemporary Wiccan high priestesses often wear necklaces of alternating amber and jet beads.

Protection

Jet is receptive and absorbs energies, especially negativity. Wear as beads, carry, or place beside white candles during protective rituals. It is a fine household protectant.

Sea Witches and fishermen's wives in old Britain prized jet as a potent magical protectant. They burned it in household fires as incense to guard their absent husbands.

A small piece of jet is sometimes placed momentarily on a newborn baby's stomach to guard it. It is also a special traveler's amulet. During the Middle Ages, jet was carved into images of beetles and worn for protection.

Against Nightmares

To guard against nightmares and ensure a good night's rest, wear jet to bed, place a piece under the pillow, or hang it on the bedpost.

Tiny amounts of powdered jet are added to psychic-type incenses. Or, pour powdered jet onto a glowing charcoal block, still your mind, and scry in its fumes.

Health

Jet is worn to maintain proper energy flow within the body to avoid ill-health. It is combined with blue candles during healing rites, or fumed with lavender and sage to promote health.

Whitby and the Victorian Trade

Jet is fossilized wood compressed over millions of years until it hardens into a stone that carves easily and polishes to a brilliant shine. Its most famous source is Whitby, a town on the northeast coast of Yorkshire, though it is found elsewhere too. Its black color tied it to death and mourning, and pieces have been recovered from burial chambers dating back ten thousand years. The stone became hugely fashionable when Queen Victoria wore quantities of jet jewelry while mourning Prince Albert, and demand grew so vast that by 1870 the Whitby trade alone employed around fifteen hundred people. Once the fashion for mourning jewelry faded, the stone's many other virtues were largely forgotten.

Serpents, Toothache, and Demons

Burned jet was said to drive serpents away. An elixir made by boiling jet in wine was applied to the mouth to treat toothache. Like amber, jet builds a static charge when rubbed with cloth and will lift small scraps of tissue or lint, which led to the belief that it draws good fortune. Rubbed briskly it can give off smoke, and that smoke was thought to banish demons as black as the stone itself. A jet cross nailed to a door kept evil spirits out, and jet talismans were carried as a guard against devilish forces.
