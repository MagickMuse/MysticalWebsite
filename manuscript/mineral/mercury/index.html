Appearance

Liquid metal, silvery and reflective, never solidifies at room temperature.

Origin

Spain, China, Peru, United States, Slovakia, Russia.

Attributes

Mercury is a strange, shining, molten "silver" that never solidifies. Mystically and magically, mercury is a complex metal possessed of a dual nature—projective and receptive, yang and yin, metal and liquid.

Spellworking

Energy: Projective, Receptive | Planet: Mercury | Elements: Water, Earth, Air

Magical Lore

Due to its dense weight, mercury is ruled by the element of earth. Because it appears in a liquid state, it is also ruled by water, and its rapid movements signify air. Since Mercury is so poisonous, this aspect could perhaps be ruled by fire.

Mercury has been used in magic partly because of its unique appearance and properties. It defies easy categorization—neither fully solid nor truly liquid in the conventional sense, representing the liminal spaces between states of being.

Historical Uses

Scrying: Pools of mercury were once held in the hand and used as vehicles for scrying. Also used for this purpose were clear glass spheres filled with mercury and tightly corked, then placed upside down on a stand.

Gambling Talisman: A gambling talisman popular to this day consists of a hollowed nutmeg filled with mercury and sealed. This is carried for good luck with cards, dice, horses, and numbers.

Witches' Bottle: A modern version of the Witches' bottle, an old protective charm, involved three bottles. The smallest bottle was filled with mercury and placed inside another bottle. The second bottle was filled with water and then put into an even larger jar, and covered with sand, rocks, and shells.

⚠️ Safety Warning

Mercury is extremely dangerous. It is hazardous to breathe, ingest, or even touch for prolonged periods of time. Its magical uses are therefore limited and needlessly risky.

There are safer metals to use in magic—safer and cheaper as well. Do not use mercury. Consider alternatives like silver for lunar work, or other metals aligned with your intention.

Alchemy and the Soul

Alchemists counted mercury, or quicksilver, as one of the three elements essential to all life, the other two being salt and sulphur, and they saw it as a symbol of the soul. In India the art of alchemy is called rasavatam, meaning the way of mercury. Because the metal was thought able to take on the nature of any other, it was treated as a vital ingredient in the transmutation of base matter into gold, the goal that so many practitioners pursued. Mercury is at once a metal, a planet, and a god, and these three faces of its symbolism are not easily separated even when only the mineral is in view.

Quicksilver and Its Perils

Mercury is one of only four metals that remain liquid at room temperature. The name quicksilver suits it well, suggesting something hard to hold, whimsical, and swift, the very sense carried in the word mercurial. In its insoluble form the metal does little harm, but in soluble form, as methyl mercury, it becomes extremely toxic. Lighthouse lanterns once floated in a circular vat of mercury, and the rising vapors drove some keepers to madness long before the cause was understood. In the same way the phrase mad as a hatter arose from the insanity that struck milliners who used mercury nitrate to mat and strengthen felt. The symptoms of mercury poisoning include hallucinations, tremors, and dementia.

The Seven Magickal Metals

Mercury is the metal of the planet Mercury, one of the seven magickal metals that the ancients matched to the first seven planets. The full set pairs silver with the Moon, mercury with Mercury, copper with Venus, gold with the Sun, iron with Mars, tin with Jupiter, and lead with Saturn. In the Dharmic religions, sacred artifacts such as singing bowls and temple statuary are made from a blend of all seven metals.
