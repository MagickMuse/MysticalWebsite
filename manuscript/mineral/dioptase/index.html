Dioptase's Journey to You

Dioptase was first identified in 1793 by copper miners in Kazakhstan, who initially mistook it for emerald due to its intense green color. Further analysis revealed it to be an entirely distinct copper-based silicate mineral, and its name was drawn from the Greek phrase meaning "through visible," a reference to the internal cleavage planes visible through high-quality crystals. It forms as a secondary mineral in oxidized copper deposits, typically in warm, arid climates, where it develops within limestone or calcite matrix. Its abundance in the Congo region and its saturated green hue earned it the trade name Congo Emerald.
