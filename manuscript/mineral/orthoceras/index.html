Orthoceras is a fossilized cephalopod, not a true crystal or mineral, with no defined crystal structure. These ancient marine creatures lived over 400 million years ago when Earth was predominantly covered in water. Resembling modern squid and octopus, orthoceras could grow up to 14 feet in length. After their extinction, the hard aragonite shells they left behind were gradually replaced with calcite through millions of years of fossilization. These hardened shells are their only remaining earthly imprint, primordial matter carrying the energy of timeless tides. Mexico has been the world's most prolific source of these fossils, with the Aztecs first discovering them around 1345 CE. Historians trace the gem's use to the Aztecs, who referred to it as vitzitziltecpatl, meaning "hummingbird stone."

Origin

The most significant deposits of orthoceras fossils are found in Morocco and Sweden, though they can be found globally. Vast quantities are concentrated in the Atlas Mountains of Morocco, along the border of the Sahara Desert, a striking reminder that what is now an arid landscape was once a rich prehistoric ocean teeming with life. Sweden is another important locality for these ancient fossils.

Appearance

Orthoceras fossils typically appear as elongated, cone-shaped forms embedded in dark matrix stone. The fossilized shells display a distinctive segmented pattern of chambers, often appearing in shades of brown, black, gray, and cream. When polished, the calcite replacement creates beautiful contrast between the pale fossil and the surrounding dark stone. Some specimens reveal intricate internal structures and suture patterns that speak to the creature's ancient biology.

Colors

Orthoceras fossils display a palette of earthy, ancient tones. The fossilized shells range from pale cream and ivory to warm brown and golden amber, often set against a dark black or deep charcoal matrix stone. When polished, the contrast between the light-colored calcite fossil and the surrounding dark sedimentary rock creates a striking visual effect. Some specimens show subtle gradations of gray, tan, and chocolate brown within the segmented chambers.

Care

With a Mohs hardness of 3.5 to 4, orthoceras is relatively soft and should be handled with care. It is best cleansed with smoke, sound, or moonlight. Avoid prolonged water exposure or harsh chemicals. Store wrapped in a soft cloth away from harder stones to prevent scratching. Due to its fossilized nature, it is sensitive to extreme temperature changes.
