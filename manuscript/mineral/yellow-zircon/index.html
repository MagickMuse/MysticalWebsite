Jacinth

Jacinth is a yellow form of zircon. The Crusaders and other travelers prized it as a talisman to keep them safe on their journeys, trusting it to guard against both robbers and lightning. It also featured in a working against trickery. A loaf of bread was marked with the stone, and once eaten by someone who had been bewitched, the bread was believed to lift spells and enchantments from them.

Jacinth was counted among the stones that change color as a warning or omen. It was said to turn red when stormy weather was approaching. There is a kernel of truth in this, since the stone clouds in damp air and regains its brilliance in hot, sunny weather, acting as a natural barometer.

The stone was regarded as a symbol of all things good, making its wearer beloved by both the divine and other people. It was believed to lift melancholy, cheer the spirit, and encourage restful sleep. Its one reputed drawback was a belief that it could cause miscarriage.
