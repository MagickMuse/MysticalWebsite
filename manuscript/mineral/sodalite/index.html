Sodalite is a mineral in the Isometric crystal system with a Mohs hardness of 5 to 6, first discovered in Greenland in 1811 by the Scottish mineralogist Thomas Thomson and named for its high sodium content. Also known as the Dreamcatcher Stone.

Appearance

Sodalite displays mottled dark and light blue-white coloring, often tumbled, and is available in all sizes. It did not become widely popular until much larger deposits were found in Ontario, Canada, after which it became a favored material for ornamental carvings and jewelry. It forms deep within the Earth and reaches the surface through volcanic activity. Extremely rare 12-sided crystals have been discovered in hardened lava flows around Mount Vesuvius, though in sizes too small for jewelry. It is often mistaken for lapis lazuli but lacks the golden flecks of iron pyrite found in the latter.

Colors

Deep royal blue, light blue, blue-white mottled, occasionally grey or green

Origin

Found globally, with the most important localities in Afghanistan, Canada, Czech Republic, Greenland, Myanmar, Norway, Russia, United States, Brazil, Namibia, Romania, India
