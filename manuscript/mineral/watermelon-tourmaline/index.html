Watermelon Tourmaline is a variety of Tourmaline crystallizing in the Trigonal system with a Mohs hardness of 7 to 7.5. First discovered in Maine in 1902, it was named for its unmistakable coloring: a vivid pink center surrounded by deep, lush green, echoing the fruit it resembles. This unique layering occurs when pink tourmaline forming at the crystal's core becomes exposed to trace minerals such as manganese and lithium, causing the outer growth to shift to green over time. The Minas Gerais region of Brazil is renowned for producing some of the most stunning specimens in the world.

Appearance

Pink center surrounded by green outer layer, or vice versa. Translucent striated prismatic crystal. Trigonal crystal system with a Mohs hardness of 7 to 7.5.

Origin

Afghanistan, Brazil, Madagascar, Myanmar, Namibia, Pakistan, Sri Lanka, United States (Arizona, California, Colorado, Connecticut, Maine).
