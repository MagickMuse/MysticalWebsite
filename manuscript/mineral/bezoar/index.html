Bezoar

◇Notes/Warnings
Bezoar is not a true mineral but a hardened mass formed inside the bodies of animals. Its historical use as a poison antidote and ground elixir is unsafe and of historical interest only.

Bezoar is the generic name given to stones recovered from inside the bodies of animals, each believed to hold talismanic power matching the creature it came from.

Origins of the Name

Its most celebrated use also gave it its name. Bezoars were believed to dispel poisons, or at least to act as an antidote to them. The Arabic pad-zahr means "poison removing," and over time this became bezoar. The stone reached Europe through Arab physicians during the Great Plague, though it was already known to the Chinese, the Malays, and the peoples of Peru and India.

Magickal and Healing Uses

Bezoars were carved and set into knife handles to guard their owner against poisoning. The stone was also believed to confer youth and vitality, to prevent asthma, and to treat the kidneys and bladder when ground into a fine powder and made into an elixir.
