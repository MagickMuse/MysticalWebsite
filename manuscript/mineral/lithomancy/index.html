Lithomancy

What is Lithomancy?

Lithomancy is crystal divination, one of the oldest documented methods of seeking guidance through stones. Historical sources, including biblical texts, reference the use of sacred objects like the Urim and Thummim carried by priests and prophets for divination purposes.

The practice centers on selecting crystals intuitively to answer questions or gain insight. The colors, properties, and correspondences of chosen stones provide the framework for interpretation.

Methods of Practice

Single Stone Selection

Place a crystal where light can interact with it. Gaze at the stone and remain open to telepathic impressions or visual messages. This method treats the crystal as a scrying surface, similar to working with a crystal ball.

Past, Present, Future Reading

Display your collection and have the querent select three stones in sequence:

  • First stone: represents the past
  • Second stone: represents the present
  • Third stone: represents the future

Interpret each stone based on its color correspondences and symbolic associations. The combination reveals patterns across time.

Blind Selection Method

Place all stones in a cloth bag. Mix thoroughly, then draw stones without looking. This method works well for self-readings and demonstrates how intuitive selection surfaces needed guidance.

The stones you draw, regardless of method, will reflect what needs attention.

Twelve House Reading

For comprehensive life readings, use the twelve astrological houses as a framework. This method covers every major life area and provides detailed guidance.

The Twelve Houses:

  • 1st House: Self-image, ego, identity
  • 2nd House: Resources, money, possessions
  • 3rd House: Communication, short journeys
  • 4th House: Home, parents, domestic life
  • 5th House: Love, children, creativity
  • 6th House: Career, health, daily routines
  • 7th House: Partnerships, marriage, contracts
  • 8th House: Shared resources, sex, death, transformation
  • 9th House: Philosophy, higher education, long-distance travel
  • 10th House: Public image, reputation, authority figures
  • 11th House: Friends, groups, aspirations, ideals
  • 12th House: Secrets, hidden enemies, karma, unconscious patterns

Linear Selection Method: Have the querent select twelve stones one at a time. Place them in a line, with each position corresponding to a house in order (1st through 12th). Interpret each stone's color correspondence in relation to that house's life area.

Note: Selection often slows as the reading progresses. Early picks come quickly, later ones require more deliberation as the querent tunes into subtler aspects of their life.

Horoscope Wheel Layout: Draw a circle divided into twelve equal sections. From the querent's perspective, the 1st House occupies the 8-9 PM position. Remaining houses proceed counterclockwise (2nd House: 7-8 PM, 3rd House: 6-7 PM, continuing around the wheel).

The querent selects stones as before, placing one in each house section.

Casting Method: The querent holds all nine colored stones several inches above the center of the horoscope wheel. They focus on their question, then release the stones and let them fall naturally.

Discard any stones landing outside the wheel. Interpret only those inside the circle. Some houses will contain multiple stones, others none. Empty houses indicate areas requiring less attention currently. Houses with multiple stones signal areas of complexity, conflict, or heightened importance.

Interpret each stone's color meaning through the lens of the house it occupies.

Developing Your Practice

Lithomancy improves significantly with practice. Individual stones develop personal associations beyond their standard color correspondences. Like tarot cards, each stone accumulates layers of meaning through repeated use.

Your intuition strengthens as you work with the same set over time. Eventually, readings flow naturally without conscious reference to correspondences or layouts. The stones themselves become teachers.

Building Your Collection

A functional lithomancy set requires at least five stones of nine colors (45 stones minimum). Ten of each color creates better visual display and gives querents more choice. Tumbled stones work well and remain affordable.

Suggested Color Palette

Red: Red Garnet, Red Jasper, Rhodonite

Orange: Carnelian, Orange Calcite

Yellow: Citrine, Yellow Beryl

Green: Aventurine, Malachite

Blue: Howlite, Lapis Lazuli, Turquoise

Indigo: Iolite, Sodalite

Violet: Amethyst, Purple Garnet

Rose/Pink: Rhodonite, Rose Quartz

Gold: Goldstone, Gold Tiger's Eye

Nine colors align with numerological completion. You can adapt this palette or add colors based on your practice, but nine provides a solid foundation.

Display and Presentation

Arrange stones on a tray covered with quality white or black cloth. Organize by color in rainbow order from the querent's perspective (red on the left, progressing through orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet, rose, gold).

This creates an attractive, professional display that invites intuitive selection.

When multiple stones are selected, layer their meanings. Look for patterns, contrasts, or progressions across the reading.

For example, a past/present/future reading showing orange, violet, and red might indicate:

  • Past (orange): Hesitation or excessive caution limited opportunities
  • Present (violet): Current learning phase focused on spiritual development and hidden knowledge
  • Future (red): Independence and achievement through focused effort and determination

Always connect the colors back to the querent's specific question and circumstances.
