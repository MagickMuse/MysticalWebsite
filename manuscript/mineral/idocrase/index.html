Vesuvianite is a sorosilicate mineral crystallizing in the Tetragonal system with a Mohs hardness of 6.5. First discovered near Mount Vesuvius in 1795, it is also known as Idocrase and Vasonite. Vesuvianite has three popular subvarieties categorized by color and locality: Californite is a green variety from California, also called California jade; Cyprine is copper-rich with a blue hue, found in Sweden and South Africa; and Fluorvesuvianite is a white form originating in Russia. All three share the same mineralogical, metaphysical, and geological properties. Vesuvianite is related to both epidote and zoisite, which are known for manifesting higher levels of human life-force energy.

Appearance

Transparent to translucent crystal. Occurs in green, blue, white, and yellow varieties depending on trace mineral composition. Tetragonal crystal system with a Mohs hardness of 6.5.

Origin

Found globally. The most important localities are Australia, Canada, China, France, Italy, Japan, Kenya, Mexico, New Zealand, Norway, Pakistan, Romania, Russia, Tanzania, and the United States (Arizona, California, Colorado, Nevada, New Mexico, Utah).

Lore

Vesuvianite was first identified near the slopes of Mount Vesuvius in 1795, taking its name from the famous volcano. Its subvariety Californite earned the nickname California jade for its rich green color and was historically used as a jade substitute in carvings and ornamental work.
