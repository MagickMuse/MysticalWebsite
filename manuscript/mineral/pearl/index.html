Moon Magick

Key themes for lunar workings: Tranquility, transitions, sincerity, truth, wisdom, lunar connection, love, fertility.

Appearance

Round, lustrous, organic gem formed within mollusks. Various sizes, typically cream to white with iridescent sheen.

Origin

Australia, Philippines, Japan, Italy, Egypt, United States, China, Indonesia.

Attributes

Pearl symbolizes purity, spiritual transformation, and inner wisdom. As an organic gem born from the sea, it carries the energy of water and the moon. Pearl helps to balance emotions and promotes sincerity, loyalty, and truth. It enhances personal integrity and helps to provide focus.

Pearl is associated with faith, charity, and innocence. It enhances personal integrity and helps to create focus. Pearl symbolizes purity and is known for its calming effect. It represents the journey from irritation to beauty, teaching us that our challenges can become our greatest treasures.

Psychologically, Pearl helps you to see yourself clearly. It dissolves negative thought patterns and assists in recognizing patterns of behavior that no longer serve you. Pearl encourages self-acceptance and enhances feelings of contentment.

Emotionally, Pearl soothes and heals the emotional body. It promotes sincerity, loyalty, and truth in relationships. Pearl helps to bring out the best in people and can ease the strain of stressful situations.

Healing

Pearl treats digestive disorders and the soft organs of the body. It relieves conditions of bloating and biliousness. Pearl has been used to aid fertility and ease childbirth. It enhances the integrity of the skin and promotes a healthy glow.

Position

Wear as jewelry, especially close to the heart. Place on the solar plexus for digestive issues.

Spellworking

Pearl, like amber, jet, fossils, and mother-of-pearl, is the product of a living creature. Since the oyster must be killed to remove the pearl, some believe there is a heavy debt incurred by those who engage in trafficking pearls and by those who wear and use them. The choice is yours. Popular folklore naming pearls as bringers of bad luck might be connected with the violence of their collection.

The pearl's dramatic, unexpected appearance within an oyster has long inspired religious and magickal lore. Mystically, pearls symbolize the Moon, water, the center of creation, and the universe.

Cultured pearls, made by inserting a bit of rounded shell into a living oyster, are mostly shell, not pearl, and aren't as magickally potent as naturally produced pearls. But their magickal uses live on. Freshwater pearls have basically the same qualities as sea pearls.

Mythological Lore

Mythologically, pearls were dedicated by the Romans to Isis after her worship was imported from Egypt. They were worn to obtain her favors. They were thought to be the congealed tears of Freya in early Saxon religion, and the Goddess in ancient Syria was named the Lady of Pearls. Throughout the Mediterranean region, pearls were associated with various manifestations of the Goddess.

Pearls were once believed to be raindrops swallowed by oysters. In early Chinese belief, pearls fell from the sky when dragons fought among the clouds (during storms). Dragons and pearls are closely associated in Chinese thought.

Lunar Connection

Pearls are intimately connected with the Moon, so much so that some will wear or use them in magick only at night, during the Moon's domain. Because of this connection with lunar energy they are usually worn by women and rarely by men.

Love

Pearls have long been used in love magick, either worn or carried to spread loving vibrations. In India, women wear pearls as a magickal insurance for a happy marriage.

Money Spell

A simple money spell involves purchasing a cheap pearl, the cheapest you can find. After attuning with the pearl and giving thanks for the oyster's sacrifice, hold it tightly in your hand and visualize money flowing into your life. See yourself as using it wisely. Money is energy, and squandered energy leaves you little in return.

Still visualizing, throw the pearl into a stream, the ocean, or any moving water. As the pearl contacts the element, it begins the process of bringing your need into manifestation.

This ancient spell was once performed differently. The pearl was thrown into a pile of rubbish as an act of sympathetic magick. Obviously, anyone who can throw away pearls is rich. The action magickally created the desired condition.

Protection

Throughout the South Pacific, pearls are used by swimmers and divers as a magickal protectant against shark attacks. It is also a potent household guardian against fire.

Luck

For a general luck or good fortune attractant, set pearls around a ruby and wear.

Pearl Colors

Each pearl color has specific magickal uses:

  • Black pearls and those of blue tints bring luck to the owner
  • Pink pearls are worn to manifest an easy, comfortable life
  • Yellow pearls bring wealth (Hindu tradition)
  • Red pearls promote intelligence

At various times, pearls have also been used to lengthen life, promote fertility, drive off demons, preserve health, instill courage, and lend physical strength.

Name and Symbolism

The word pearl comes from the Latin pilula, meaning a small ball, the same root that gives us the word pill. If the diamond stands as the epitome of the masculine gem, the pearl is its feminine counterpart, an association ensured by its softness, its rarity, its gentle luster, and its birth within the sea.

Sensitivity and Illness

Pearls are delicate objects in both a physical and a symbolic sense. Their high water content makes them prone to cracking or breaking, so they favor an even temperature. They also respond to changes in body warmth and to chemicals such as perfumes and hairsprays, and for this reason a pearl that turns dull is said to mark illness in the one who wears it.

Old Beliefs of Their Making

Pliny the Elder held that pearls formed when dewdrops fell into an open shell and slowly hardened, and he explained their color and luster as rainbows that had settled on the dew. The pearl fishermen of Borneo kept a darker custom, setting aside every ninth pearl. These were gathered in a bottle with two grains of rice for each pearl, stoppered with the finger of a dead man, and placed in a tree, in the belief that more pearls would be born as a result. In China both raindrops and pearls were thought to be the spittle of dragons, falling to Earth when the dragons battled in the heavens.

Fire, Lightning, and Desire

Because water and fire stand diametrically opposed and the pearl belongs to water, it was carried as an effective charm against harm from fire and lightning. Since the Moon rules over lovers and the pearl is closely bound to the Moon, pearl ground into a fine powder was once used to make an aphrodisiac potion.

The Nine Gems

In a traditional Indian belief, each of the nine planets is paired with its own precious gem, the set known together as the navaratna. The pearl is the gem of the Moon. The full set assigns the pearl to the Moon, the ruby to the Sun, topaz or yellow sapphire to Jupiter, the diamond to Venus, the emerald to Mercury, red coral to Mars, blue sapphire to Saturn, hessonite garnet to Rahu, and cat's eye to Ketu. Rahu and Ketu are the northern and southern lunar points that come into play during eclipses, which is why they are also called the eclipse planets.
