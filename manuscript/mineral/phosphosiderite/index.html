Phosphosiderite is an iron phosphate mineral with a monoclinic crystal structure, registering 3.5 to 4 on the Mohs scale. Its name derives from the Greek "phosphoros" (phosphate) and "sideros" (iron). "Phosphoros" also translates as "morning star," reflecting the stone's metaphysical role in illuminating past lives. Discovered in 1858, it was not given this name until 1890 because it was initially misclassified as a different mineral. It is sometimes marketed as purple turquoise, though it has no mineralogical relation to turquoise. Phosphosiderite generally forms in botryoidal masses, which can resemble a bunch of grapes joined together.

Appearance

Phosphosiderite may be obtained as a polished gemstone and may be veined. It appears in orchid, purple, red, or brown colors.

Origin

Argentina, Australia, Belgium, Brazil, Chile, Czech Republic, France, Germany, Italy, Morocco, New Zealand, Portugal, Spain, United States (California, Colorado, Maine, Nevada, Utah).
