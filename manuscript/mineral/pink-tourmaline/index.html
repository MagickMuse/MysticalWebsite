Pink Tourmaline is a member of the tourmaline family crystallizing in the Trigonal system with a Mohs hardness of 7. Also known as Rubellite, some of the earliest discoveries can be traced to Maine and California during the 1800s. The Maine deposits were known for their raspberry-colored hue, while California produced typically bright pink specimens. By the late 1800s, Tiffany & Co. had made Pink Tourmaline a popular choice for jewelry throughout the United States. China became one of tourmaline's biggest markets, and Chinese empress dowager Cixi was especially fond of the gem, often having it set in jewelry or carved into snuff bottles. Wearing Pink Tourmaline jewelry is said to activate the "higher heart" and open the keeper to the energy of universal love.

Appearance

Pink Tourmaline presents as translucent to opaque, striated prismatic crystals in shades of pink ranging from pale rose to deep raspberry. It is an aphrodisiac stone with a tender, luminous energy.
