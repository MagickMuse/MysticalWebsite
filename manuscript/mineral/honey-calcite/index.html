Honey calcite is a Trigonal variety of calcite with a Mohs hardness of 3, named by Pliny the Elder in 79 CE from the Latin calx meaning "lime." Also known as amber calcite, it is among the most abundant minerals on Earth and is found in shades ranging from pale gold to deep amber-brown, with color derived from iron impurities. The majority of specimens on the market originate from Mexico.

Appearance

Opaque to translucent, ranging from warm honey gold to rich amber and dark brown. Typically found in massive form with a waxy to vitreous luster.
