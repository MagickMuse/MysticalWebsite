Spellworking

Hematite is a strange stone. It is heavy, solid, and silvery black. Its name is something of a mystery - to ancients, "hematite" was what we now know as bloodstone, so virtually all magical information in old books refers to bloodstone. This hematite, when worked on a lapidary wheel, "bleeds" and produces stains that look much like blood.

In Italy and elsewhere, it is fashioned into necklaces sold as "volcano spit."

Hematite possesses the curious property of "healing" itself. Make a small scratch on the surface of the stone, then rub your finger over it. The scratch may disappear.

Healing

Hematite is said to be powerful in drawing illness from the body. Hold it in the hands while visualizing, then place on the skin directly over the afflicted area. A necklace of small stones can also be worn for healing.

Grounding

Hematite is worn for grounding and stabilizing purposes, and to focus the attention on the physical plane.

Protection

Hematite's iron-rich composition creates a powerful protective barrier against negative energies and psychic attack. Its reflective, mirror-like surface bounces harmful intentions back to their source. Wear hematite jewelry or carry a tumbled stone when you need to shield yourself from energy vampires, toxic environments, or hostile people. Roman warriors carried hematite as a protective amulet.

Scrying Ritual

In a darkened room, light a red candle. Settle before it and hold a large piece of hematite so that the candle's flame is reflected on it. Gaze at the reflection and visualize a question. The answer will come to you.

Dark Magick Applications

Hematite serves a range of underworld purposes that are not inherently negative. However, when powdered, charged, and either wiped or blown onto an enemy, it amounts to casting your intention to send them to the realm of Hel.

Blood, Ochre, and Rebirth

Hematite owes its name to its bond with blood, a tie drawn from both its iron content and its coloring. Fashioned into jewelry it shows a shiny gunmetal tone, yet it also carries red streaks, and ground to a fine powder it yields red ochre, a pigment the shade of dried blood. Ancient peoples treated this ochre as a sacred substance bound to life giving blood and to rebirth. Burial sites have been found where the body was daubed in the powder, most likely a symbolic rite meant to secure life after death. Olmec tombs have yielded highly polished hematite mirrors, always laid alongside female bodies.

Mars and the Warrior's Stone

The Romans held hematite sacred to Mars, the god of war, and warriors carried it as an amulet for protection and added strength on the battlefield. That old association found a striking echo when significant deposits of hematite were identified on the planet Mars in 2001. The stone is believed to grant courage, strength, and endurance, to improve the circulation of the blood, and to help staunch bleeding.

Warrior's Stone

Hematite is under the dominion of Mars. Carried, it is said to enhance courage and daring, protect against wounds, and stimulate the healing of wounds already received.
