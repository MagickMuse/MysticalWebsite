Opalite is a manufactured stone with an amorphous crystal structure, registering 5.5 to 6.5 on the Mohs scale. It combines the qualities of three gemstones, dolomite, clear quartz, and fluorite, which are compressed and tumbled together to form a stone with its own unique metaphysical properties. The name "opalite" once referred to a form of poor-quality common opal that lacked the iridescent play of color for which opal is renowned. The first opalite was created by the French chemist Pierre Gilson in 1972.

Appearance

Opalite has a milky, opalescent appearance with a soft, ethereal glow. It displays a subtle play of light, often shifting between cool blue and warm golden tones depending on the angle and background.

Origin

Man-made. Created by compressing and tumbling dolomite, clear quartz, and fluorite together.
