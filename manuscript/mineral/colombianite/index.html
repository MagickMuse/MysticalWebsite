Colombianite is an extraordinarily ancient stone, estimated to be over 30 million years old and found exclusively in Colombia. Whether it formed from the heat and pressure of a Pliocene-era meteorite impact or emerged as a rare variant of volcanic obsidian from the Paletara caldera remains scientifically contested. Both possible origins underscore its intense, compressed energy. The Indigenous Muisca people, who flourished in Colombia from roughly 600 to 1600 CE before Spanish colonization, held this stone as sacred. The Spanish named it piedra rayo, meaning lightning stone, a reference to the speed with which it illuminates what lies within.

Colors

In ordinary light, colombianite appears dark brown to black and opaque. Under direct light, specimens reveal additional tones of green, gray, and occasionally purple within the glass.

Did You Know?

The Muisca people are best known historically for the legend of El Dorado, the "gilded one," a ritual in which a chief covered in gold dust made offerings to a sacred lake. Colombianite was among the sacred materials within their spiritual tradition.
