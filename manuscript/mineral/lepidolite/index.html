Lepidolite is a natural, micanized form of lithium with a monoclinic crystal structure, registering 2.5 on the Mohs scale. It grows alongside tourmaline and kunzite and gets its name from the Greek "lepis," meaning scale, due to the scaly appearance caused by its flakes of lithium.

Appearance

Plate-like layers, slightly shiny, or grainy mass, all sizes. Ranges from lavender to purple. A much rarer variety, golden lepidolite, forms in pockets that are less magnesium rich, creating a beautiful golden glow.

Origin

First discovered by Robert Bunsen and Gustav Kirchhoff in 1861, who initially named it lilalite for its lavender color. Found in Afghanistan, Australia, Brazil, Canada, Czech Republic, Japan, Madagascar, New Zealand, Pakistan, Russia, Sweden, and the United States.
