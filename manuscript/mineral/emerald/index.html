Emerald's Journey to You

Emerald has been mined and revered since at least 1300 BCE in ancient Egypt, making it one of the oldest known gemstones in continuous use. To the ancient Romans it was a symbol of fertility and eternal youth, associated with Venus, goddess of love and beauty. You may also know of the Emerald Tablet — the legendary green stone said to hold the secrets of the universe inscribed upon its surface. Above all, emerald embodies eternal love and youthfulness, radiating the springtime energies of renewal, rebirth, and growth. It helps you channel hope and new beginnings into your relationship with yourself and with others.

Crystal Healing with Emerald

Emerald is the purest gemstone expression of the Green Ray, the color corresponding to the Heart Chakra. Associated with the element of water, it is a potent emotional healer. Hold it to your heart space to release the weight of old hurts and fill your auric field with compassion, love, hope, and encouragement. Though deeply heart-centered, emerald also sharpens the mind — enhancing memory, discernment, intuition, and insight. Keep it nearby during divination or meditation to access the wisdom of your heart and gain clarity in matters of love.

For the Advanced Practitioner

Emerald is a powerful tool for cultivating deep insight and intuition. In meditation, it aids decision-making and brings clarity to love by bridging the wisdom of the heart with the logic of the mind. It is especially effective at breaking unhelpful patterns that block intimacy — clearing stagnant heartache energy, soothing heavy and lethargic feelings, and gently discouraging self-victimization. Though it is a love stone at its core, emerald strongly encourages independence, teaching you to find wholeness within yourself first. When you radiate love from that place of inner fullness, you naturally attract new blessings and soul connections.

Did You Know?

Christian legends describe emerald as a stone of resurrection. In ancient times it was believed to alleviate illness, bring emotional and physical healing, and even bestow prophetic visions.

History and Lore

The emerald belongs to the Beryl family and counts among the most precious of all stones. A flawless emerald is rare and immensely valuable, though even the flawed kinds are costly. One of the most famous emerald mines lay in Upper Egypt and was said to belong to the Queen of Sheba, its workings guarded by evil spirits. Emeralds were credited with a life of their own, said to grow, flourish, or diminish with the seasons and to change in intensity with the phases of the Moon, a quality described as ripening. The Peruvians were said to keep a Temple of Esmerelda that held a massive emerald along with many smaller stones called her daughters, a temple that has never been found.

Sacred and Arcane Associations

The emerald was one of the twelve stones set into the breastplate of the High Priest of the Temple in Jerusalem, and it was named as one of the stones forming the foundations of the New Jerusalem. Thoth, also known as Hermes Trismegistus, Hermes, or Mercury, the god of writing, communication, and knowledge, was said to have written the secrets of the Universe upon a great emerald tablet called the Smaragdina Tabula. For this reason the emerald is a symbol of arcane knowledge and carries the wisdom of the Ancients. Because emerald green is the color of spring and renewal, the stone was placed at the throats of mummies during embalming so that the limbs of the body would keep their vigor in the next life.

Talisman and Healing

Dedicated to Mercury, the emerald was carried by travelers as a talisman to keep them safe on their journeys, and emerald talismans were also used to drive out demons. Given between lovers, as in an engagement ring, it promised a long and happy life together, but it would lose its brilliance if either partner was unfaithful, so the stone came to represent truth. As a healing stone it was believed to settle upset stomachs, to ease melancholy and a heavy heart, and to neutralize poisons such as those caused by scorpions.

Eye Healing

Ancient engravers and craftsmen whose work demanded intense focus kept green stones — preferably an emerald — on their workbenches to periodically rest and refresh their eyes. This practice may benefit anyone who labors long hours before a computer screen or in any profession that strains eyesight.

Dark Magick Applications

Emerald can be charged to provoke extreme greed, selfishness, and isolation in a target. It may also be wielded by someone who wants others to feel undeserved jealousy or to be eclipsed and overshadowed.

The Nine Gems

In a traditional Indian belief, each of the nine planets is paired with its own precious gem, the set known together as the navaratna. The emerald is the gem of Mercury. The full set assigns the pearl to the Moon, the ruby to the Sun, topaz or yellow sapphire to Jupiter, the diamond to Venus, the emerald to Mercury, red coral to Mars, blue sapphire to Saturn, hessonite garnet to Rahu, and cat's eye to Ketu. Rahu and Ketu are the northern and southern lunar points that come into play during eclipses, which is why they are also called the eclipse planets.
