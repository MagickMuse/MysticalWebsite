Qualities of Gold

Gold relates to success, abundance, and power, tempered by wisdom. It is positive, self-motivated, and generous.

Ancient Beliefs and Symbolism

Gold has always been valuable and valued. In ancient times, it was believed that gods were the color of gold. It was also believed to be a residue of the sun. Gold is considered a masculine symbol because of its association with the sun.

Norse Runic Symbolism

In the Norse runic tradition, gold represents the light of the sun and the spiritual radiance that shines from Ásgardhr. It embodies the force of önd pervading the universe and serves as a symbol of honor, reputation, and power across all realms.

Personality of Gold

People who favor gold are ambitious and crave life's finest offerings. They are exceptionally capable and charismatic. Their combination of drive, energy, inner strength, and practical thinking virtually ensures they will reach the pinnacle of their chosen field. Their creative thinking generates unconventional solutions to complex problems.

Aversion to Gold

Disliking gold reveals abandoned dreams of success. You have convinced yourself that your aspirations will never materialize, resigning yourself to failure and mediocrity. Rekindling hope and taking small steps toward your goals becomes critical.

Supportive Uses of Gold

Gold eliminates self-sabotaging thoughts about money and success. It provides confidence and self-discipline to persist until reaching your goal. Gold also supports seeking wisdom, knowledge, and truth. Wear gold whenever trust in your intuition wavers.

Magical Lore

Gold is intimately linked with divinity, particularly with gods associated with the Sun. Throughout the ages, gold was the material of choice for fashioning sacred images and decorating altars, and was considered the highest offering to the deities. Magicians working with solar energy wear gold ritual jewelry to attune with that power. In Wicca, high priests and those who revere the Sun as a symbol of the God often wear gold. Legend states that the Druids collected mistletoe with sickles of gold.

Power and Personal Energy

Gold, perhaps the most magically potent of all metals, lends its energy to rituals. Worn during magic, gold jewelry enhances the magician's ability to rouse and send forth power. Wearing gold in everyday life increases personal power, promoting courage, confidence, and willpower.

Herb Collection

Gold tools were traditionally used to collect herbs. Use gold-plated knives to gather projective (masculine, electric) herbs. Silver knives are better suited for receptive (feminine, magnetic) herbs.

Healing

Chains of gold worn around the neck preserve health, and gold bands alleviate arthritis. Gold worn habitually is said to ensure a long life.

Protection

Owing to its solar gleam, gold is a protective metal. Plain gold can be carried or worn as a guardian. Small children in India are guarded by tiny gold amulets. During protective magic, place gold objects on the altar. A gold chain placed around a white candle can be the focus of protective rituals.

Wisdom

Gold is utilized to promote wisdom. For this purpose it is not carried but given to another with no conditions, bringing illumination to the giver.

Money and Success

Gold often figures in money rituals. Even a fragment of gold leaf is sufficient. Gold-nugget jewelry is worn to bring a continuous flow of money, particularly potent for miners and those investing in precious metals. As a symbol of the Sun, gold is utilized in success rituals.

Magickal Properties

Gold is a native isometric metal with a Mohs hardness of 2.5 to 3, the rarest and most historically revered of all metals used in magickal practice. Every atom of gold on Earth was forged in the explosion of a dying star and arrived here through asteroid bombardment approximately four billion years ago, when the impacts were powerful enough to force gold from the planet's deep core up toward its crust. This stellar origin makes gold one of the most cosmically charged substances available to the practitioner. Its documented use in human civilization extends back to at least 2600 BCE in Mesopotamia, where it was fashioned into jewelry. In astrological symbolism gold corresponds to the Sun, and in alchemical tradition it represents perfection at every level of existence: physical, mental, and spiritual.

Gold purifies the physical body when worn against the skin, particularly as jewelry. It stabilizes the Heart Chakra by helping the wearer release over-responsibility and the emotional weight of carrying others' burdens. By activating the Third Eye and Crown chakras simultaneously, it amplifies the capacity to manifest thought into material reality, clearing stagnant or discordant energies from the entire chakric system and the auric field and replacing them with renewed vitality.

For beginners, gold works by raising the vibrational quality of the wearer's field, drawing happiness, peace, and good fortune simply through sustained contact. It functions as a spiritual preservative, preventing the accumulation of lower-frequency states such as tension, inferiority, fatigue, or anger from becoming entrenched in the spirit. It amplifies and broadcasts positive energetic states outward, including joy, composure, and beauty, in the same way it conducts electricity and heat through matter. For the advanced practitioner, gold supports the full actualization of potential by cultivating a steady orientation toward growth without self-judgment. It builds empowerment, self-esteem, and confidence from the inside out, dissolves feelings of inadequacy or futility, and sustains emotional equilibrium under pressure.

Chakra Work

Gold activates and balances the Heart, Third Eye, and Crown chakras in concert. At the Heart, it eases the strain of absorbing others' emotional and energetic weight, restoring the wearer's center of gravity to their own inner experience. At the Third Eye, it sharpens the capacity for intentional thought and foresight. At the Crown, it opens the channel between individual consciousness and higher awareness. The combined activation of all three creates a coherent upward current through which thought and intention move more readily into manifestation. Gold is best engaged through wearable forms, as sustained skin contact is the most effective delivery method for its purifying and energizing properties.

Colors

Gold ranges from pale yellow-gold to rich, deep amber-gold depending on the purity and trace mineral content of the specimen. Higher purity gold presents as a warm, saturated yellow. Native gold found in quartz matrix often shows a bright metallic luster against white or gray host rock.

Did You Know?

Scientists estimate that approximately 52,000 tons of gold remain to be extracted from Earth's crust. Because all gold on Earth arrived via ancient asteroid impacts and the planet is no longer producing more, the total terrestrial supply is permanently finite.

Symbolism and Myth

Gold is inextricably linked with the element of fire and with the Sun, which it mirrors in both appearance and meaning. In mythology the passage of the Sun across the sky is pictured as a golden chariot crossing the heavens. Because gold resists tarnishing and corrosion of every kind, and keeps its color and luster even when melted by heat, it became an emblem of immortality. The ancients believed it was formed from sunbeams buried underground. Egyptian mummies of the highest castes were encased in gold to denote the immortality of the soul, and gold was one of the three gifts the Magi carried to the infant Christ. Even now gold remains the favored metal for wedding rings, since it is held to confer a long and happy life on the married couple.

Though platinum surpasses it in monetary value, gold keeps the upper hand in symbolic meaning through its color and thousands of years of adoration.

Gold is a symbol of purity because of its physical incorruptibility, yet it carries strongly negative associations as well. The striving for gold can corrupt once greed takes hold, and as an object of desire it draws out the worst in people. The story of King Midas illustrates this. Midas wished that everything he touched would turn to gold, and once Dionysus granted the wish it became a terrible curse. Unable to eat because his food turned to gold and unable to embrace his daughter because she turned to gold as well, Midas begged Dionysus to lift the curse. Dionysus did so by having the king wash his hands in the Pactolus River, in what is now Turkey, a myth that explains the gold deposits in that river and echoes the later idea that all that glistens is not gold.

Alchemists used gold to symbolize the soul, and the search for the philosopher's stone signified their conscious effort to purify the spirit, since the material stone could be produced only alongside spiritual growth and development.

The Seven Magickal Metals

Gold is the metal of the Sun, one of the seven magickal metals that the ancients paired with the first seven planets. The full set links silver to the Moon, mercury to Mercury, copper to Venus, gold to the Sun, iron to Mars, tin to Jupiter, and lead to Saturn. In the Dharmic religions, sacred objects such as singing bowls and temple statuary are cast from a blend of all seven metals.
