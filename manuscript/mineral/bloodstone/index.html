Bloodstone is a jasper-based hybrid mineral forming in the Monoclinic crystal system with a Mohs hardness of 6.5 to 7, its dark green body marked by vivid red spots produced by iron oxide inclusions. Known before 1844 as heliotrope, from the Greek for "sun-turner," it received its current name from mineralogist J. D. Dana in reference to those bloodlike spots.

Magickal Powers

Spiritual cleanser; binds troublesome people

Appearance

Dark green chalcedony with red jasper spots resembling drops of blood. Opaque, waxy to vitreous luster, Monoclinic crystal system. Found as nodules in riverbeds and embedded in rock.

Origin

India, Australia, United States, Brazil, China, Czech Republic, Russia. Found in riverbeds and embedded in rocks as nodules.

It promotes healthy blood circulation and movement of energy through the body's tissues, helping to ease inflammation and physical pain.

For practitioners supporting individuals facing terminal illness, bloodstone brings a grounded acceptance of physical mortality alongside reassurance that one's vital life force continues beyond the body.

History and Lore

The bloodstone takes its older name, heliotrope, from the Greek words for sun and to turn. Though the term once covered many reddish stones, it generally refers to green jasper marked with red flecks. The ancient Egyptians believed it eased menstrual troubles and called it the blood of Isis. In Christian statuary it was often carved into the head of Christ, the red speckles arranged so that they seemed to be blood flowing from the wounds of the crown of thorns.

Healers and physicians knew the stone as lapis sanguinarius and turned to it chiefly for ailments of the blood. It was used to staunch bleeding, to cleanse the blood, and to clear bloodshot eyes. Dipped in cold water and laid on the body it was thought to aid circulation, and it was also believed to cure hemorrhoids.

The bloodstone's link with the Sun made it a tool for scrying. Placed in a bowl of water it stood as a symbolic solar eclipse, and the passage of the Moon across the face of the Sun was said to appear upon the immersed stone.

Spellworking

Bloodstone, a green chalcedony flecked with red spots, has been used in magic for at least three thousand years. In ancient Babylon, it was carried to overcome enemies. In ancient Egypt, it was used to open doors, break bonds, and even cause stone walls to fall.

Halting Bleeding

Its most famous use is to halt bleeding. Soldiers frequently carried it to either avoid wounds or as magical first aid. When pressed on wounds, bleeding stops—likely due to pressure and the stone's cool temperature. A bloodstone held to the nose stops nosebleeds.

Health and Longevity

Worn to keep the blood healthy and cure blood-related diseases. Also worn to cure fevers and as a general health talisman. Athletes wear it to increase physical strength and win competitions. It is also worn to lengthen the life span.

Courage and Legal Matters

Bloodstone lends courage, calms fears, and eliminates anger. It has long been used in spells designed to secure victory in court and legal matters.

Wealth and Agriculture

Because it is green, bloodstone is utilized in wealth, money, and business spells. Kept in the cash register, it draws money. Carried or worn, it attracts wealth. In the Middle Ages, it was a farmer's talisman worn during planting to increase crop yield.

Fertility

Bloodstone is among the gemstones most strongly associated with fertility. It is considered the talisman of Isis, and its fertility potency is at its peak when the stone is carved into the form of the Tjet — the Buckle of Isis — a knot-shaped Egyptian amulet representing the goddess's protective and generative power. Uncarved bloodstone still radiates fertility energy and may be worn, carried, or placed near the body to absorb its influence passively.

Women's Magic

Women hung a bloodstone on the arm to prevent miscarriage and, later, on the thigh to ease childbirth.

Invisibility

For invisibility, the bloodstone was smeared with fresh heliotrope flowers and worn or carried. This dazzled the eyes of beholders. Today, this ritual may be used for "magical invisibility"—when you wish to lie low and not attract attention.

Strengthening Magic

In the thirteenth century, bloodstones were engraved with the figure of a bat. These talismans were worn by magicians to increase the effectiveness of spells and magickal rites.

Did You Know?

According to Christian legend, the distinctive red markings on bloodstone formed from drops of Christ's blood that fell onto green jasper during the crucifixion. This lore is part of why the stone has centuries of use in devotional and healing contexts.
