Peridot is an orthorhombic silicate mineral and the gem-quality variety of olivine, registering 7 on the Mohs scale. It is the traditional birthstone for August and is said to attract whatever you desire. Most peridot forms deep inside Earth's mantle, beneath the crust, reaching the surface only through earthquakes and volcanic activity. Rarely, peridot is also found within meteorites that strike the Earth.

Lore

Peridot was prized in ancient Egypt for its ability to attract good fortune. The ancient Egyptians kept the location of their supply, the island of Zabargad, such a closely guarded secret that when the kingdom fell following Cleopatra's death in 30 BCE, it was not rediscovered until 1906. Peridot is sometimes called the "tears of Pele," the Hawaiian goddess of volcanoes, because of its volcanic origins. The ancient Greeks and Romans believed peridot had special powers as a wayseer's stone, thought to glow from within at night and lead seekers to their intended path. The Inca also valued peridot for its connection to the sun.

Spellworking

Peridot and olivine are so alike as to be nearly identical, the main distinction being that olivine is the name more often used for the Hawaiian material. All magickal uses associated with olivine apply equally to peridot.

Protection

To be most effective magickally, the peridot was once set in gold. This makes a fine, if costly, protective amulet, which the ancients said guarded against enchantments, night terrors and illusions, as well as the universally feared evil eye. This latter term is usually defined as being either envy or unconscious psychic attack.

Though long associated with the Sun, peridot seems more suited to Venus.

Healing

Peridot is worn or carried for general healing purposes. Cups and other vessels made of peridot were once used in healing, since medicinal liquids drunk from them were thought to be more effective.

Peridot is said to promote healing of insect bites and to help in liver ailments.

Love and Calming

The stone is used to attract love as well as to calm raging anger. It is also useful to soothe nervousness and to dispel all negative emotions.

Sleep and Depression

Because it is calming to the nervous system, peridot is also helpful in promoting sleep if worn to bed. Such usages date back to at least ancient Roman times, when rings of peridot were worn to relieve depression.

Wealth

Its green hue suggests peridot's use in wealth-attracting spells.

Name and Esteem

Like other green stones, peridot was called an emerald in earlier ages, though it is truly a variety of olivine. Its name is traced either to the French peritot, meaning unclear, or to the Arabic faridat, meaning gem, while the Greek peridona means to give richness. A stone matching its description sat on the breastplate of the High Priest of the Temple in Jerusalem, a mark of the esteem in which it was held.

Engraved Amulets

Both the ancient Egyptians and the Romans treasured peridot and often wore it as an amulet carved with figures and inscriptions. A peridot bearing the image of a vulture was believed to keep demons at a distance, while one engraved with a torch was thought to draw wealth to its owner. Worn as a necklace, peridot was said to lift melancholy and leave the wearer cheerful and hopeful. Its green color was held to preserve the owner from jealousy, a piece of sympathetic magick rooted in green being the traditional hue of envy.

Sacred to Pele

Peridot is sacred to Pele, the Hawaiian Goddess of volcanoes, an association drawn from the stone's volcanic birth. ⚠️ This belonging is part of a living Hawaiian tradition, a closed practice meant for those raised within that culture.
