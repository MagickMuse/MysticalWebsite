What Is Baneful Magick?

Hexes, curses, bindings, and the ethics of protecting yourself.

  • By Avelynn
  • Protection
  • Beginner
  • October 2026
  • 5 min read

Say the word "hex" in a room full of new witches and watch half of them flinch. Baneful magick has a reputation for being the forbidden corner of the craft, the thing "real" witches whisper about and "good" witches never touch.

The reality is a lot less dramatic and a lot more practical. Most baneful work is about stopping harm, not causing it: banishing what shouldn't be in your home, binding someone who won't stop hurting you, breaking what's been sent your way. It took me years to make peace with this side of my practice, and I wrote about that shift in Ten Years a Witch.

This post is the practical companion to that one. Here's what baneful magick is, what falls under it, and the questions worth asking before you ever consider it.

What Baneful Magick Means

"Baneful" comes from bane, something that causes harm or ruin. Baneful magick is the umbrella for workings meant to restrict, remove, or harm. It's also called dark magick, negative magick, or offensive magick, though none of those labels capture the whole picture.

Here's the part that surprises most beginners: a lot of baneful work isn't about attacking anyone. It draws on the darker, more destructive forces in nature, the same forces that clear dead growth so something new can take its place. Those energies can be transformative and even healing. They break old patterns that gentler magick can't touch.

Magick itself is neutral, like fire. It can warm a home or burn one down. Your intention and your ethics decide which.

The Spectrum of Baneful Work

Baneful magick isn't one thing. It runs from defensive work most witches do regularly all the way to workings meant to cause harm.

Defensive and Protective Work

This is where most witches start, and many never go further.

  • Banishing: Forcefully removing a harmful influence, presence, or energy. (Protection Magick for Beginners covers banishing alongside shields and wards.)
  • Binding: Restricting someone's ability to cause harm. A binding stops an attack, but it isn't an attack itself.
  • Uncrossing and curse-breaking: Breaking or reversing negative magick that's been sent your way.
  • Return to sender: Reflecting harmful energy back to wherever it came from, rather than absorbing it.
  • Cord cutting: Severing an energetic tie to a person, belief, or situation you need to be free of.

Jinxes, Hexes, and Curses

These three get used interchangeably, but they're different things.

  • Jinxes: Low-energy, often unconscious ill will. Someone flips you off in traffic, and you stub your toe ten minutes later. Jinx energy can be sent without any magickal training at all.
  • Hexes: Harm worked through the practitioner's own will and intent. The witch takes direct, personal responsibility for the working.
  • Curses: Harm worked by calling on outside forces, like gods, spirits, or other entities, to carry it out. In folklore, curses are the dark mirror of blessings, and they're tied closely to the power of spoken words.

In traditional witchcraft, offensive magick falls under two planets. Mars is fast, hot, and direct. Saturn is slow, cold, and binding. That's why Tuesdays and Saturdays, the days those planets rule, are traditionally associated with this kind of work.

The Ethics

Witchcraft has no central authority and no universal rulebook. You decide your own ethics, and you carry the responsibility for them. Some traditions forbid harmful magick entirely. Wicca's Rede, "harm none," is one example. (Can You Be a Witch Without Being Wiccan? covers where that idea comes from.) Others permit baneful work in specific situations: self-defense, protecting the people you love, or seeking justice for a real wrong.

Wherever you land, own it. Decide your boundaries before you're angry, not in the middle of it.

It's also worth knowing where the line falls for a lot of witches. Cursing someone who stole your parking spot, or doing a revenge spell on a coworker who annoys you, is pettiness with a candle, not protection. Most baneful magick isn't done by villains. It's done by ordinary people acting on a bad day.

Questions to Ask Before Any Baneful Working

If you're even considering this kind of work, sit with these first, and write your answers down.

  • Is this warranted, or is emotion making the decision? Anger is a terrible spellcaster. If you're furious, wait.
  • Have I tried the mundane solutions? Talking, boundaries, blocking a number, walking away, reporting a problem.
  • Would defensive work solve this instead? A banishing, a binding, or stronger protection usually ends the problem without sending harm anywhere.
  • Am I sure about the target? Divination before baneful work is strongly recommended. Confirm who and what you're dealing with, and what the likely outcome is, so your energy doesn't land somewhere it shouldn't.
  • Am I prepared for the consequences? Depending on your beliefs, that might mean blowback or karmic return. Either way, this work affects you too.
  • Can I live with it? Not just tonight, but a year from now.

If you can't answer every one of these with clarity, you're not ready yet. Take a few hours, days, or weeks before deciding so that you can be certain.

When Magick Isn't the Answer

This matters enough to say plainly: if you're in danger, magick is not your first line of defense. If someone is threatening, stalking, or abusing you, reach out to the people and services that can physically protect you: the police, a domestic violence hotline, a lawyer, someone you trust. Protection magick can work alongside those things. It should never replace them.

The same goes for situations you're carrying alone. Talk to someone. A binding can stop a person's harmful behavior on the energetic level, but it can't get you a restraining order.

Myths Worth Letting Go Of

"Good witches never do baneful magick." Plenty of experienced witches see protecting yourself and your people as a responsibility, not a moral failing.

"Baneful magick always comes back on you." That's true in some traditions and belief systems, and not in others. What's universal is that the work affects you, so don't do it carelessly.

"Black candles are evil." Black is one of the strongest protective colors there is. It absorbs and removes negative energy, breaks hexes, and banishes. (Candle Magick for Beginners covers what each color does.)

"Baneful magick is only about hurting people." Most of it is about stopping harm: banishing, binding, breaking curses, and cutting ties.

Duality Is Part of the Craft

A full practice has room for both the soft work and the sharp work. Healing and defending come from the same place: caring about what's yours. What matters is that you choose it, if you ever do, with clarity, care, and full ownership of what you're doing.
