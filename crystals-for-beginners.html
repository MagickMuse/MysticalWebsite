Crystals for Beginner Witches

Which stones to start with, and how to care for them.

  • By Avelynn
  • Crystals
  • Beginner
  • October 2026
  • 9 min read

I carry amethyst more than any other stone. Right now I'm wearing amethyst, citrine, and emerald pendants on one necklace, and they don't come off.

Crystals are one of the most popular tools in the craft, and often the first thing a new witch buys. They're also surrounded by hype, fakes, and confusing advice. So let's talk about what crystals do in a magickal practice, which ones are worth starting with, and how to care for them so they keep working for you.

An Ancient Practice

Long before gems were prized for their monetary value, people used them as magickal charms. In many early societies, only rulers, royalty, and religious leaders wore them at all.

The ancient Egyptians used stones for healing and protection, wrapping their dead in gems and placing carved stone amulets throughout their tombs, each one with a protective purpose. Indian Ayurvedic medicine and Chinese herbal tradition both recorded the healing influence of stones on the body and spirit. The High Priest of the Temple in Jerusalem wore a breastplate set with twelve gems believed to hold the power of prophecy, and those twelve stones seeded many of the associations that still link certain gems to the months and signs of the year.

Witches have long seen crystals the way they see plants: as living beings. They just resonate at a rate too slow for most of us to notice. That slow, concentrated energy is exactly what makes them so useful, because it lets them keep working long after a spell is cast.

How Crystals Work in Magick

Every crystal carries its own correspondences, connecting it to certain intentions, planets, elements, and deities. When you use rose quartz in a working, you're calling on its associations with love, compassion, and healing, not just using a pretty rock.

One of the oldest ways to read a stone's purpose is through its color. Color has a real effect on mood, so a stone's hue becomes a direct signpost to the work it supports. It's the same idea behind the Law of Similars. Many stones also carry planetary rulers, which is why crystals and the planets are so often read together.

You can add crystals to almost any spell to increase, focus, or stabilize its energy. A few of the most common ways:

  • Wear them: Jewelry and pocket stones keep a stone's energy in your personal field all day.
  • Put them in charms: Add them to amulets, talismans, charm bags, and spell jars.
  • Protect your home: Set them near windows and doors.
  • Meditate with them: Hold a stone or place it near you to deepen your focus.
  • Build a grid: Arrange stones in a geometric pattern to amplify and direct one intention.
  • Offer them: Give a stone to a deity or nature spirit in return for their help.
  • Scry with them: Gaze into a crystal ball or polished stone to see what's hidden.

Choosing Your First Crystals

It's tempting to buy every pretty stone you see, but a small, intentional collection will serve you far better. Before you shop, grab your journal and ask yourself a few questions:

  • What kind of spellwork do I do most? If it's mostly abundance work, start with abundance stones.
  • Which crystals come to mind right away? Research them, and add at least two to your kit.
  • Is there a crystal I just don't like? Pay attention to that. A stone we resist is often the one with a lesson for us.
  • Which element am I most drawn to? Try to include a stone for each element, with extra focus on your favorite.
  • How much space do I have? Tumbled stones and small chips work just as well as big showpieces, so there's no need to start big.

And good news for your budget: the correspondence matters more than rarity. Some of the most useful stones, like clear quartz, black tourmaline, amethyst, and citrine, are also some of the easiest to find.

When you're choosing between stones, try one of these:

  • Your nondominant hand: Hold your nondominant palm over or toward the stone. Warmth or tingling is often read as a strong match, and no response may mean to wait. This works in person or even toward a photo online.
  • A pendulum: Ask whether the crystal is right for you and whether the timing is right, after setting which swing means yes and which means no.

If a stone keeps showing up in your path, pay attention. It may be meant for a future working, or even for someone else.

And if you dislike a stone, that's allowed too. Moldavite never did it for me. I thought it looked weird, and I never bought into the craze when everyone rushed to get some. Sometimes a stone you resist has a lesson for you, and sometimes it just isn't yours. Learning the difference is part of the practice.

A Few Starter Crystals

Here are eight beginner-friendly stones, along with the care notes you'll want to know.

  • Clear quartz: The universal stone. It holds and magnifies whatever intention you set with it, and because it's so easy to program, it can stand in for almost any other stone in a spell. It's sun and water safe. Some people find its energy too stimulating near the bed, so keep it out of your bedroom if it disrupts your sleep.
  • Amethyst: Calm, peace, mental clarity, and protection from negative energy. It's a natural fit for meditation and spirit work. Amethyst is water safe, but keep it out of the sun, since it fades with long exposure.
  • Rose quartz: The classic stone of love, compassion, and emotional healing. It's water safe but not sun safe.
  • Citrine: Abundance, prosperity, and sunny confidence. It's sun and water safe, which makes it very low maintenance. Most bright orange citrine on the market is heat-treated amethyst, so know what you're buying.
  • Black tourmaline: A go-to protection stone, often placed by doors. It's sun safe, but keep it away from water.
  • Smoky quartz: Grounding and protection with an earthy, steady energy. It's sun and water safe.
  • Carnelian: A fiery stone for manifestation, motivation, and courage. It's water safe and generally sun safe, though long exposure may fade it slightly.
  • Selenite: A cleansing stone that clears other crystals. It's very soft and must stay completely dry, since water dissolves it.

Connecting With Your Crystals

Just like plants, crystals are allies, not just tools. Getting to know how each one feels makes your spellwork far more effective, because you'll know which stone is right for which job.

  • Carry it: Keep a new stone in your pocket or bag and hold it often.
  • Talk to it: Speaking to your crystal opens the way for it to share messages during spellwork.
  • Sleep with it: Place it under your pillow or on your nightstand and ask it to speak to you in dreams.
  • Meditate with it: Sit with the stone and notice what it seems to offer you.
  • Journal about it: Track your thoughts, feelings, and messages so you know which stone to reach for later.

Once you know a crystal, you can give it a specific job in each spell. And sometimes a crystal won't want to take part. If a stone feels unusually cold or you sense resistance when you hold it, respect that and choose another. Check back after your working, since sometimes it just needs a good cleansing.

Cleansing, Charging, and Programming

Crystals absorb and hold energy, so most witches cleanse them when they first bring them home, after using them in a spell, and after other people handle them. A stone still carrying the vibrations of an old spell can interfere with a new one. That said, cleansing is a personal choice. Some practitioners never cleanse a stone whose journey holds meaning for them, and stones like selenite and black kyanite don't hold a charge at all.

There are plenty of ways to cleanse. Just make sure the method is safe for the stone.

  • Smoke: Pass the crystal through the smoke of cleansing herbs or incense. Rosemary works beautifully.
  • Moonlight: Set it on a windowsill or outside under the moon. Direct moonlight isn't required; your intention is what matters. The new moon is especially aligned with releasing old energy.
  • Sound: Use a singing bowl, bells, drumming, or even clapping your hands.
  • Earth: Bury the stone overnight, outside or in a flowerpot, and mark the spot so you can find it again.
  • Rice: Nestle it in a bowl of uncooked rice for as long as you can.
  • Selenite: Set your crystals on or in a selenite bowl, ask for them to be cleansed, and say thank you when you're done.
  • Water and salt: Many hard, clear stones can be rinsed in water or salt water. But check first, because salt can leach color from bright stones or damage fragile ones, and stones like selenite and black tourmaline shouldn't go in water at all.

Once a stone is cleansed, you can charge and program it. To charge it, leave it under the full moon while holding a clear intention for how it will serve you. To program it, hold it in your nondominant (receiving) hand, cover it with your dominant (projecting) hand, and visualize your intention flowing into the stone. When you need it for something new, cleanse it and reset it, ideally at the new moon.

Clear quartz is especially easy to program, which is why it can substitute for almost any stone. Hold it in sunlight or moonlight and say, "Crystal, for this spell you are given the powers of" and name the stone you need. After the spell, cleanse it to return it to its original state.

Crystal Shapes

Tumbled stones are perfect for beginners, but as your collection grows you'll notice crystals come in all kinds of shapes. Each one moves energy a little differently.

  • Points and wands: Send energy in one direction. Great for directing your intention toward a specific goal.
  • Generators: Six-sided points with flat bases that build and direct energy upward. They make excellent centerpieces for crystal grids.
  • Spheres: Radiate energy outward in every direction. Ideal for filling a whole room with a steady, harmonious energy.
  • Hearts: Carved to connect with the heart, they're used to calm, soothe, and support emotional healing.
  • Palm stones: Smooth, flat stones sized for your hand, perfect for meditation and energy work.

Spotting Fakes and Buying Well

Fake and treated stones are everywhere. Glass and dyed stones can still be used in your practice, but you deserve to know what you're paying for. A few common things to watch for:

  • Bright orange citrine: Usually heat-treated amethyst. True citrine forms as single points, not in small geodes.
  • Fruit-named quartz: Anything called cherry or strawberry quartz is likely dyed glass.
  • Opalite: Man-made glass, not a crystal, sometimes sold as sea opal or opal moonstone.
  • Aura crystals: Quartz electroplated with a thin layer of metal to create that rainbow sheen.
  • Dyed stones: Look for unnaturally bright colors, or cracks that look darker than the rest of the stone.
  • Amber and jet: Real ones sink in water. If yours floats, it's plastic.
  • Turquoise: Often faked with dyed howlite or magnesite. Fake turquoise glows under UV light, so a small blacklight is handy when shopping.

It's also worth asking where your stones come from. Reputable sellers usually know how and where their crystals were mined, and they're happy to tell you.

Crystal Safety

Crystals are natural materials, and some of them aren't safe in every situation. Always check a stone's safety notes before you put it in water, leave it in the sun, wear it against your skin, or make a crystal elixir.

Some stones are toxic. Malachite, for example, should never be cut, ground, or polished without protective gear, and toxic stones should never go directly into drinking water. If you want to make an elixir with a stone that isn't water safe, place the stone next to the water bottle instead of inside it.

And if you have small children or pets, keep your crystals on a high shelf or in a drawer.

Start With Three or Four

Start small. Choose three or four stones that fit the work you do, get to know each one, and keep notes on how they feel in your spells. A small collection you truly know will always outperform a shelf full of strangers.
